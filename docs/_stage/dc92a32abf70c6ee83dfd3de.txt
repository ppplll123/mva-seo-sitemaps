<p><strong class="df-k">Because it decides who can take the case and how long you have.</strong> Across 76 signed car cases a NJ lawyer later handed back, "it happened in another state" was the top reason, ahead of "already had a lawyer."<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>

<tr><th scope="row" data-label="Question">If you were partly at fault</th><td data-label="What applies">NJ: you recover unless you were more to blame. Maryland: its top court kept "contributory negligence" in 2013, so a small share of blame can bar you.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row" data-label="Question">Your lawyer</th><td data-label="What applies">Needs to be licensed where the case will be filed.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table></div>
</div>
<p>When the other driver is from out of state but the crash was in NJ, NJ rules apply instead; see the out-of-state driver notes on <a href="/new-jersey/route495-lincoln-tunnel-approach-crash-sequence">the Lincoln Tunnel approach page</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="this-week">
<h2>What should I do this week?</h2>
<p>Five steps, none of which needs a lawyer yet.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Write down the state, county and road, then get the crash report from that state's police.</li>
<li>Open a PIP claim with your own NJ insurer and ask, in writing, whether the crash state changes anything.</li>
<li>Note the other driver's name, home state and insurer from the report.</li>
<li>Ask every lawyer you call, "Are you licensed in the state where this happened?"</li>
<li>Put the crash state's deadline in your calendar, and the 90-day notice if a bus or public vehicle was involved.</li>
</ol>
<p>Passengers have extra questions about whose policy pays; see <a href="/new-jersey/passenger-accident-new-jersey">hurt as a passenger in New Jersey</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Will my car insurance cover an accident in another state?</h3></summary>
<p>Your own NJ policy's PIP usually still pays your medical bills first. What the other driver owes you is decided under the crash state's rules. More on PIP in <a href="/new-jersey/what-is-pip-nj">what PIP is in New Jersey</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What happens when you get into a car accident in another state?</h3></summary>
<p>Report it to that state's police, get treated, and open a claim with your insurer. The deadline and fault rules for suing are usually the crash state's.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can a NJ lawyer handle a New York or Pennsylvania crash?</h3></summary>
<p>Only if licensed there or working with a lawyer who is. Some NJ lawyers also hold New York or Pennsylvania licenses, so ask.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Which deadline applies if I am not sure?</h3></summary>
<p>Plan around the shortest one that could apply. The NJ clocks are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">NJ deadline table</a>.</p>
</details></section>
