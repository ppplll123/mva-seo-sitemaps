<h1>Route 17 NJ accident today: first steps, who holds the report, and what three years of crash records show</h1>

<p>We have no live traffic feed or breaking news here. Crashed on Route 17 just now? Call 911 if anyone is hurt, get medical attention, and ask the officer at the scene which department is writing the report. On this highway that is nearly always the police of the town where the crash happened, not the State Police.</p>
<p>The crash records point the same way, with one caveat. Of 3,855 police-reported Route 17 accidents in 2023 and 2024, only 10 name a State Police station, and the rest leave that field blank. The data project does not document the station field, so we read a blank as "not State Police" by comparison: on I-80 in the same years, 72 percent of records name a station. Route 17 runs entirely through Bergen County, and in those two years 769 of its accidents left someone hurt or killed: 1,077 people hurt and 7 killed.</p>

Data downloaded 2026-11-06, analyzed 2026-11-10.</p>

<h2 id="same-direction">Route 17's signature: crashes between cars going the same way</h2>
<p>Most Route 17 accidents where someone was hurt involve two vehicles traveling in the same direction. Rear-end crashes were 56 percent of them in 2023 and 2024, against 31 percent statewide. Add same-direction sideswipes and the share reaches 73 percent, nearly double the statewide 39 percent.</p>
<p>The crash types common elsewhere are rare here. Right-angle crashes, the classic intersection collision, were 5 percent on Route 17 against 22 percent statewide. Left-turn, U-turn and head-on crashes together were under 2 percent.</p>

<h2 id="by-mile">Two stretches hold half the injuries: Paramus and Hasbrouck Heights</h2>
<p>Paramus (266) and Hasbrouck Heights (120) together had 386 of the 769 Route 17 accidents where someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024. That is just over half, in two of the 20 towns the road's records cover.</p>

<h2 id="deaths">Fatal Route 17 crashes: mostly at night under street lights</h2>
<p>Eight people were killed in eight fatal Route 17 accidents from 2023 to 2025: 4 in 2023, 3 in 2024 and 1 in the preliminary 2025 records. Ramsey had two. Carlstadt, Hackensack, Hasbrouck Heights, Lodi, Maywood and Paramus had one each.</p>
<p>Of the 7 deaths in 2023 and 2024, 5 happened in the dark on lit stretches, coded "dark, street lights on, continuous." Yet only about a quarter of the accidents where someone was hurt happened in that light. Two of the 7 deaths were pedestrians, and two were fixed-object crashes.</p>
<p class="source">Source: lane2-content/data/profiles/route-17-nj-accidents.json (years, fatal_by_municipality_2023_2025, light_2023_2024, crash_type_mix_2023_2024). Fatal crashes are supplemented from the NJ State Police fatal feed. Seven deaths is a small number, so the light pattern can shift with one more year.</p>

<h2 id="pedestrians">North Arlington: most of Route 17's pedestrian crashes</h2>
<p>Route 17 had 19 pedestrian crashes in 2023 and 2024, and 13 of them were coded to North Arlington, at the road's south end. No other town had more than two. Two pedestrians were killed on Route 17 in those two years.</p>
<p class="source">Source: lane2-content/data/profiles/route-17-nj-accidents.json (two_year_2023_2024, by_municipality_2023_2024 ped_crashes). For what to do after being hit on foot, see our <a href="/new-jersey/pedestrian-crash-new-jersey">guide to pedestrian crashes in New Jersey</a>.</p>

<h2 id="when">Midday peaks, and a quiet Sunday in Paramus</h2>
<p>Route 17 accidents where someone was hurt peak in the middle of the day, not only at rush hour. The 1 pm hour had the most (64) in 2023 and 2024, and the noon to 3 pm window held 167 of 769. Tuesday (132) and Friday (124) were the busiest days.</p>

On the rest of Route 17, Sunday (71) sits inside the range of other days (59 to 88). The records do not say why, and we have not checked the reason against an official source.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 17. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<h2 id="method">Route 17 record selection and its limits</h2>
<p>Every figure here comes from NJDOT crash records for 2023 to 2025, in the cleaned-up form published by the open-source hudcostreets/nj-crashes project. That project adds fatal crashes from the NJ State Police feed; our copy was downloaded 2026-11-06.</p>
<p>We kept a record as Route 17 only when its route number is 17 and its road name carries a state prefix such as "NJ 17". That screens out Monmouth County Road 17, which shares the number. It also drops 51 Bergen records from 2023 to 2025 typed as plain "ROUTE 17" or "RT 17", 33 of them in parking lots, gas stations or private property. Only 4 of the 51 left someone hurt or killed, so every Route 17 count here is a slight floor.</p>
<p>The town, crash-type, death, pedestrian and time figures use 2023 and 2024, the two full years. The 2025 records are still coming in, so we mark them preliminary wherever they appear. The cross-street and junction tables instead pair 2024 with 2025, since the way streets were named in the export shifted after 2023. A crash counts as "someone hurt or killed" when police recorded at least one injury or death. None of this measures risk per driver, because we have no traffic volumes, and none of it shows who caused a crash.</p>
