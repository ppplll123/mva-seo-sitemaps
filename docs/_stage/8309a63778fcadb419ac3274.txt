<section id="how-dangerous">
<h2>How dangerous is the Pulaski Skyway?</h2>
<p>By injury share, more than average: 28.5 percent of Skyway crashes hurt someone, against 22 percent of all New Jersey crashes in 2023 and 2024.</p>
<p>Our count of NJDOT records finds 333 police-reported crashes on the Skyway in 2024, close to one a day. 95 of them hurt someone, injuring 130 people; none was fatal.</p>

<section id="crash-types">
<h2>What kind of crashes happen on the Skyway?</h2>
<p>Mostly same-direction crashes: rear-ends and sideswipes made up 76 percent of 2024 Skyway crashes.</p>
<ul>
<li>Rear-end: 161 crashes (48 percent), 58 with someone hurt. Statewide, rear-ends are 27 percent of crashes.</li>
<li>Sideswipe: 93 crashes (28 percent), 15 with someone hurt.</li>
<li>Right angle: 49 crashes, 15 with someone hurt.</li>
<li>Fixed object, such as the barrier: 18 crashes.</li>
</ul>
<p>34 percent happened in the dark, and 79 crashes fell between 10 pm and 6 am. The whole corridor, including Tonnelle Avenue and the Truck Route, is on our <a href="/new-jersey/route-1-nj-accidents">Route 1 crash data page</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="at-the-scene">
<h2>What to do immediately after getting rear-ended on the Skyway?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Stay in the vehicle unless responders say otherwise, and call 911.</strong> NJDOT describes a typical Skyway section without shoulders, and pedestrians are prohibited.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Call 911 and give the direction of travel and the nearest end (Jersey City, South Kearny or Newark).</li>
<li>Do not walk the deck to take photos. Photos from inside the car, a dashcam or a passenger are enough.</li>
<li>Ask the officer for the agency name and the case number before the tow arrives. Get checked the same day.</li>
<li>Keep the tow receipt and the storage yard address apart from the crash location.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></li>
</ol>

</section>
<section id="fault">
<h2>Who is at fault when you get rear-ended on the Skyway?</h2>
<p>Usually not you, but a Skyway rear-end or sideswipe is not automatically the other driver's fault; each driver gets a percentage.</p>
<p>How a split changes what you are paid, and how to appeal your insurer's percentage, is on our <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">partly at fault guide</a>. Rear-end specifics are on the <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">New Jersey rear-end crash page</a>.</p>
</section>
