<h1>Egg Harbor Township car accidents: a corrected count, and the road where people on foot die</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Our earlier Egg Harbor Township page showed 1,275 injury accidents for 2023 and 2024, because the state crash file lists hundreds of township police cases twice. With each case counted once, the township had 966 car accidents that left someone hurt or killed.</p>
  <p>That is out of 2,952 police-reported accidents over those two years, with 17 deaths. It still works out to more than one injury accident a day on average.</p>
  <p>The heaviest toll falls on people walking along the Black Horse Pike (Route 40). From 2023 to 2025, 6 of the 8 pedestrians killed in the township died on it. The 2025 figures are preliminary.</p>

<section id="double-counted">
  <h2>Why our count fell by a quarter</h2>
  <p>In the 2023 file, 777 Egg Harbor Township rows repeat another row. In 776 of those pairs, one copy writes the case number as "23-010017" and the other as "2023010017". The last one is a single case number entered twice, both rows at the same minute.</p>
  <p>In 2023 and 2024, every township case number that appears twice has the same date and minute on both rows. In 2024 there were 135 repeated rows, all of the two-format kind.</p>
  <p>2025 has one repeat, a case entered twice at the same minute, which we removed. Another 2025 case number appears twice with different times, and we kept both rows. With the repeats gone, 2023 and 2024 look alike instead of showing a steep drop.</p>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Three possible report sources inside one township</h2>
  <p>Egg Harbor Township contains local streets, county roads, the Black Horse Pike, a stretch of the Garden State Parkway and part of the Atlantic City Expressway. Which agency wrote your report decides where you ask for it.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Township streets, county roads and Route 40.</strong> Ask the department that responded; procedures and fees vary by department. The records cannot tell you which agency that was for 2023 and 2024: the station field was blank on 2,870 of the 2,952 township rows, including 282 of the 284 Parkway rows that State Police would have written.</li>
    <li><strong>Garden State Parkway.</strong> State Police reports for toll roads are requested on the official <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report portal</a>. That portal charges $5.00 for a toll road report and keeps reports for six years from the crash year. The station field filled in for 2025: 111 township Parkway records from that year name the State Police Galloway station.</li>
    <li><strong>Atlantic City Expressway.</strong> Reports go by written request to the Atlantic City Expressway Authority, P.O. Box 389, Hammonton, NJ 08037, (609) 965-7200 ext. 108. In 2023 and 2024, 82 township records name the Atlantic City Expressway station.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>If no police officer investigated, New Jersey's SR-1 form is the way to report the accident yourself. The NJTR-1 is the police form, and only police complete it (<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>When the report arrives, compare it with what you remember of the accident. The state insurance department, DOBI, puts it this way: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed... If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." (<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</p>
  <p class="source">Report routes: research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8 (NJSP/NJTA portal, Atlantic City Expressway Authority; retrieved 2026-11-10). Station counts: eht_station_2023_2024.csv and eht_station_2025_prelim.csv. NJDOT does not document the station field, so we read it as a clue about the agency, never as proof. For the other agencies in New Jersey, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">every New Jersey report route, agency by agency</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Egg Harbor Township. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
  <h2>How we counted Egg Harbor Township</h2>
  <p>Source file: NJDOT crash records for 2023 (NJDOT per-table archive) and 2024 to 2025 (NJDOT Crash Data Portal), normalized by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a> and downloaded 2026-11-06. Any other case-number format must match character for character, which is how the one other 2023 repeat and the 2025 repeat were caught.</p>
