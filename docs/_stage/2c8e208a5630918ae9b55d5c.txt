<h1>Sayreville NJ car accident: where to get the report, and what the crash records show</h1>

<p>After a car accident in Sayreville, the report usually comes from the agency whose officer came to the scene. For most crashes that is the Sayreville Police Department, whose Records Bureau handles accident report requests. For a crash on the Garden State Parkway, you request the report through the State Police online portal instead. That split matters here, because 138 of the borough's 748 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 were on the Parkway.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.sayrevillepolice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2025AnnualReportSPD.pdf">Sayreville Police Department 2025 Annual Report</a>, Records Bureau section (retrieved 2026-11-10); <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report portal</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10); NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 via hudcostreets/nj-crashes, our road-system cut (<a href="#method">how the figures were built</a>).</p>

<p>The crash records also show something less expected. Of the 17 people killed in Sayreville car accidents in 2023 and 2024, 9 were pedestrians. Seven of those 9 deaths happened in the dark, and 5 were on Route 9 or Route 35.</p>

<section id="report">
<h2>Borough officer or Parkway trooper: the two Sayreville report offices</h2>
<p>Only police fill out the NJTR-1, New Jersey's crash report form. A driver whose crash no officer investigated uses the SR-1 self-report form instead. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT Police Resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> In Sayreville, the uniform at the scene tells you where to send the request: a borough officer means Main Street, a trooper on the Parkway means the state portal.</p>

<h3>Borough crashes: the SPD Records Bureau on Main Street</h3>
<p>The Sayreville Police Department's Records Bureau "processes requests for police and motor vehicle accident reports," according to the department's 2025 Annual Report. Police headquarters is at 1000 Main Street, Sayreville, and the non-emergency number is (732) 727-4444. The department's website also links the Borough of Sayreville's Open Public Records Act (OPRA) request form, which goes to the Municipal Clerk at 167 Main Street.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.sayrevillepolice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2025AnnualReportSPD.pdf">SPD 2025 Annual Report</a>, "Records Bureau Function"; <a href="https://www.sayrevillepolice.org/">Sayreville Police Department website</a>; <a href="https://www.sayreville.com/FCpdf/NEW%20REQUEST%20FORM.pdf">Borough of Sayreville OPRA request form</a> (all retrieved 2026-11-10). We did not find a published fee or turnaround time for crash reports, so ask the Records Bureau.</p>

<h3>Serious crashes: the Traffic Safety Bureau and your car</h3>
<p>The department's Traffic Safety Bureau investigates serious accidents. Its listed numbers are (732) 525-5431, (732) 525-5429 and (732) 525-5430. If your vehicle was towed after a crash with a death or serious bodily injury, the department says you must first get clearance from the Traffic Safety Bureau before it can be released. For other towed vehicles, you contact the licensed towing company that removed it.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.sayrevillepolice.org/safety/">Sayreville Police Department, Traffic Safety and towing information</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). The 2025 Annual Report notes the traffic unit now sits within a Community Services Bureau.</p>

<h3>Parkway crashes through Sayreville: the NJSP and Turnpike Authority portal</h3>
<p>A Parkway crash report is ordered on the portal run by the State Police and the NJ Turnpike Authority. Once the report is ready and approved you can download the basic NJTR-1 there; if a search finds nothing yet, the portal says to try again in a couple of days. It charges $5.00 for a toll road report and keeps reports for six years from the crash year.</p>
<p>Fatal Parkway crashes follow a different path. The portal says the NJTR-1 for a fatal crash on the Parkway or Turnpike can only be requested through the OPRA form on the NJ Turnpike Authority website. That matters in Sayreville, where the Parkway carried 3 of the 17 deaths in 2023 and 2024.</p>
<p>For questions about a Parkway report, the portal lists the NJ Turnpike Authority at (732) 442-8600 ext. 2431. Help with using the portal itself goes by email to NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov. The State Police number on the portal, (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234, is for non-toll, boat and serious or fatal crash records.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report portal</a>, service information and "How do I get more information?" (retrieved 2026-11-10); research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8. The $5.00 fee is the portal's toll road fee; it does not apply to Sayreville police reports. Parkway deaths: route table below.</p>

<p>On Route 9 or Route 35, check who responded. If it was a State Trooper, use the portal; if it was a Sayreville officer, use the Records Bureau. Once you have the report, DOBI advises: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed... If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25)</span></p>
</section>

<section id="pedestrian-deaths">
<h2>More than half of the people killed were on foot</h2>
<p>In 2023 and 2024, Sayreville had 32 police-reported crashes involving a pedestrian. That is about 1 in every 98 crashes in the borough. Pedestrians were still 9 of the 17 people killed. Eight of those deaths were in the 32 pedestrian crashes, and one was in a crash police coded as a rear-end collision on the Parkway. In Middlesex County as a whole, pedestrians were 40 of 120 people killed. Statewide the figure was 409 of 1,294.</p>

<section id="method">
<h2>How the Sayreville figures were built</h2>
<p>Every Sayreville count here starts from NJDOT crash records as cleaned up by the open-source project hudcostreets/nj-crashes. The 2023 rows come from NJDOT's per-table archive, the 2024 and 2025 rows from the NJDOT Crash Data Portal, and fatal crashes are topped up from the State Police fatal feed. We downloaded that file on 6 November 2026. Its 2023 and 2024 years are complete, while 2025 is labelled preliminary throughout. A crash belongs to Sayreville when NJDOT places it in Sayreville Borough, Middlesex County, whichever police agency wrote it up, Parkway troopers included.</p>
<p>An injury accident on this page is any crash with one or more people hurt or killed. People hurt, people killed and pedestrians killed are tallied person by person, which is how a pedestrian death can sit in a Parkway crash police coded as rear-end. The crossing list draws only on 2024 and 2025 and leaves alternate spellings unmerged. Routes are grouped by NJDOT's route number field. Scripts: <code>data/build_place_profiles.py</code> and <code>data/sayreville-nj-car-accidents/sayreville_cuts.py</code>.</p>
<p>The file holds no traffic volumes, so a road can log more crashes simply because it carries more cars. A Sayreville tally cannot show who caused any single crash, and it has no bearing on anyone's claim.</p>
</section>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Sayreville. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
