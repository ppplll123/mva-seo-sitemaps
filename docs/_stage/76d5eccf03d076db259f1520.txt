<section id="left-turn">
<h2>Who is at fault when a car turns left across you?</h2>
<p>Fault turns on when the turn began, each car's direction of travel and how each driver reacted, in the crash type most likely to hurt someone.</p>
<p>Police code these crashes as Left Turn/U Turn or as Right Angle, so check which code your report carries. DOBI lists failing to observe and avoid the other car, and failing to brake, swerve or sound the horn, as fault factors.</p>
</section>
<section id="which-type">
<h2>Was it a T-bone or a sideswipe, and why does it matter?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">A T-bone turns on right of way at a crossing; a sideswipe turns on where each car was in its lane before contact.</strong> A side-panel dent alone does not tell you which one happened.</p>

<section id="fault">
<h2>Who is at fault in a T-bone or sideswipe in NJ?</h2>
<p>Usually the driver who lacked the right of way at the crossing, or the driver who left their lane; the insurer sets each driver's percentage from how both cars approached.</p>
<p>DOBI's own example of a "greater duty of care" is a driver pulling out from a stop sign. "Last clear chance" asks who could still have avoided the collision.</p>
<p>How a percentage cuts what you recover, and how to dispute it, is on our page on <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">real fault splits in New Jersey cases</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<section id="who-pays">
<h2>Does your PIP pay if the other driver ran the light?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes: your PIP pays eligible medical bills whoever ran the light.</strong> Your car is never a PIP claim; it goes to your collision coverage or the other driver's insurer.</p>
<p>The full split, from pain and suffering to uninsured drivers, is on our pages on <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">whether NJ is a no-fault state</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits for medical bills</a>. <section id="next-steps">
<h2>What should you write down after a side impact?</h2>
<p>Write down how both cars approached the point of contact, not just the damage.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Tell the clinician where you sat, which side was hit and any second impact. Keep the records listed in <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">medical records for an injury claim</a>.</li>
<li>Note your road, your direction and whether you or the other car was turning.</li>
<li>List each later contact in order, kept separate from the first hit.</li>
<li>Keep full-length videos, label photos taken after the cars moved, and do not ask witnesses to match accounts.</li>
</ol>
<p>Reporting the crash and the first calls are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-do-in-the-first-24-hours-after-a-new-jersey-car-accident">what to do right after a New Jersey crash</a>.
