<h1>Route 46 NJ accident: the police report, the towns and the spots where people get hurt</h1>

<p>Route 46 crash records come from 36 New Jersey towns in five counties: Warren, Morris, Passaic, Essex and Bergen. In 2023 and 2024 it had 1,327 police-reported car accidents in which someone was hurt or killed, with 1,833 people hurt and 14 killed. Clifton had the most of those accidents, 213.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>, fatal crashes supplemented from NJ State Police; our analysis of the NJDOT records. Retrieved 2026-11-06, analyzed 2026-11-10.</p>

<p>Only a small share of Route 46 records name a State Police station, so your police report usually comes from the police department of the town where the accident happened. The exception is White, Knowlton and Liberty townships in Warren County, where most records name a State Police station. The next section shows which stretch is which.</p>

<p>Everywhere else on Route 46, ask the police department of the town where the accident happened. That includes Hackettstown and Independence, the other Warren County towns on the road, where almost no records name a station. Each department sets its own request process and fees, and we have not checked them town by town. NJDOT confirms the NJTR-1 crash report is completed by police only. <section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 46. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<h2>Which records count as Route 46 here</h2>
<p>We kept NJDOT crash records coded with route number 46 whose road name starts with a state, US, interstate or Parkway prefix. Almost all of those names read "US 46," including "US 46 SECONDARY" and local names such as McFarland Street and Piaget Avenue. A handful name another highway, such as NJ 21 (McCarter Highway), NJ 23 or the Garden State Parkway, or spell it "US HIGHWAY 46." Those few stay in the counts. Roads that only share the number, such as a county road 46, are not included.</p>
<p>Unless a table says otherwise, totals on this page combine 2023 and 2024, the two complete years in the file. Anything from 2025 carries a preliminary label. The cross-street ranking uses only 2024 and 2025, since the 2023 export writes street names differently. A crash goes in the hurt or killed column when its police record lists at least one person injured or dead. No traffic volumes sit behind these figures, so they compare numbers of crashes, not the chance of a crash per mile driven.</p>
