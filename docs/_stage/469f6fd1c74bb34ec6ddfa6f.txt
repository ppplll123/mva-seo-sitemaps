<h2>Quick answers about East Orange</h2>
<h3>Is East Orange a dangerous place to drive?</h3>
<p>For people on foot, more than most of New Jersey. Pedestrian accidents were 10.9 percent of East Orange injury accidents in 2023 and 2024, against 6.6 percent statewide.</p>
<h3>How long do you have to report an accident in NJ?</h3>
<p>Tell the police right away if someone is hurt or damage tops $500. If no officer files a report, send one to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident reports, agency by agency</a>.</p>
<h3>How do I report an accident without a police report?</h3>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in East Orange. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup><a href="#src-12">12</a></sup></li>
</ul>
