<h1>Trenton NJ car accident: where people get hurt, and how to get the Trenton police report</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>If you were hurt in a car accident in Trenton, the local pattern is unusual: pedestrians.</strong> Trenton had 175 of Mercer County's 318 police-reported pedestrian accidents in 2023 and 2024, 55 percent, although it had only a quarter of the county's injury accidents. Overall, 1,182 accidents here left someone hurt or killed in those two years. 1,523 people were hurt and 20 died, 12 of them pedestrians.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by hudcostreets/nj-crashes, fatal crashes supplemented from the NJ State Police feed; our analysis.</p>

<section id="pedestrians">
<h2>Trenton has more than half of Mercer County's pedestrian accidents</h2>
<p>Pedestrian accidents are 12.8 percent of Trenton's injury accidents in 2023 and 2024. Across New Jersey the share is 6.6 percent. Twelve of Trenton's 20 deaths in those years were people on foot, 60 percent, against 32 percent statewide (409 of 1,294).</p>
<p>Trenton's share of the county's pedestrian accidents went up from 2023 to 2024, and the preliminary 2025 records point the same way. Its share of all injury accidents was one in four in both complete years (29% in preliminary 2025).</p>

<section id="crash-types">
<h2>Right-angle collisions and parked cars, fewer rear-ends</h2>
<p>Trenton's injury accidents look different from the state's. Right-angle accidents are 32 percent here, against 22 percent statewide. Rear-end accidents are 19 percent, against 31 percent. Accidents where a parked vehicle was struck are 7.6 percent of injury accidents, against 3.4 percent.</p>
<p>Counting every reported accident, not only injury accidents, Trenton had 1,454 where a parked vehicle was struck in 2023 and 2024. That is more than its right-angle (1,189) or rear-end (1,019) accidents.</p>
<p>Lighting differs too. 15.7 percent of injury accidents here happened in the dark on a street with spot lighting, against 5.1 percent statewide.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the NJDOT records for Trenton and statewide, 2023 and 2024. Crash type and lighting are coded by the reporting officer. These patterns describe where and how accidents happen in Trenton. They do not tell you who caused any one accident. For how right-angle accidents are handled, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-side-impact-collision-claim-support-help">side-impact and T-bone accident claims in New Jersey</a>.</p>

<section id="trenton-police-report">
<h2>Getting your Trenton police report</h2>
<p>If Trenton Police handled your accident, the City of Trenton says reports are available in person or by mail from the records office. It is at Trenton Police headquarters, 225 North Clinton Avenue, and open 9 am to 4:30 pm, Monday to Friday.</p>
<ul>
<li>In person, bring identification and the report number, or know the date of the report.</li>
<li>At the service window, a motor vehicle accident report costs $0.50 a page for pages 1 to 10, and $0.10 a page after that.</li>
<li>By mail, there is a flat $5 fee. The city accepts mail requests only from the person named in the report, their attorney or their insurance company. Individuals must include a $5 cashier's check.</li>
<li>Mail address as the city gives it: Trenton Police Headquarters, Records Office, 2nd floor, 225 N Clinton Avenue, Trenton, NJ 08609.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Source: City of Trenton, "Police Report," <a href="https://www.trentonnj.org/402/Police-Report">trentonnj.org/402/Police-Report</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. The city page does not give a waiting time.</p>
<p>If a New Jersey State Police trooper handled the accident, for example on a state highway the State Police patrol, the report comes from the State Police portal instead: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>. The portal says the basic report can be downloaded once it is "ready and approved", and to search again in a couple of days if it is not there yet. General questions go to NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov. The portal gives (609) 882-2000 ext 2234 for requests about serious or fatal crash records, and only after one month has passed.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report portal, and NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page, retrieved 2026-11-10. Police complete the NJTR-1 crash report; drivers do not (<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
<p>Not sure which agency came? The officer's name and department are usually on the paperwork you got at the scene. Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a> covers other agencies.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Trenton. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
