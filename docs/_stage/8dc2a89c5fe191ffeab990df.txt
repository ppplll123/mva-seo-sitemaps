<section id="answer" class="df-answer">
<p class="df-lead"><strong>Yes.</strong> New Jersey is a no-fault state: your own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) pays your eligible medical bills whoever caused the crash. Fault still decides any claim against the other driver, and your lawsuit option can limit only the pain and suffering part.</p>

<p><strong class="df-k">It is both: no-fault for your own medical bills, fault-based for everything you claim from the other driver.</strong> "No-fault" names a type of insurance. "Not at fault" is a finding about who caused the car accident.</p>

<section id="pip-mandatory">
<h2>Is PIP mandatory in NJ?</h2>
<p>Yes. PIP is part of New Jersey's minimum auto coverage, so every policy carries it and pays without anyone deciding fault first.</p>
<p>Even the Basic Policy, the cheapest legal option, carries $15,000 of PIP per person, with up to $250,000 for certain serious injuries. Most Standard Policy drivers buy the $250,000 medical limit, with a deductible and a co-pay. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">New Jersey minimum car insurance limits for 2026</a>.</p>
<p>What each policy pays you after a crash is compared in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-basic-policy-vs-standard-policy-after-a-crash">Basic Policy vs Standard Policy</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<p>The full menu of limits is on <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">New Jersey PIP limits, deductibles and co-pays</a>. If you made your health plan primary, see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">whether PIP or your health insurance pays first</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="above-pip">
<h2>Who pays medical bills above your PIP limit?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The at-fault driver, through a claim against their liability insurer.</strong> Future treatment that still fits inside your remaining PIP limit stays with PIP, and you cannot collect the same bill twice.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<p>How the 2026 ruling works with each PIP limit is explained on our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and medical bills page</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="can-you-sue">
<h2>Can you sue the other driver in a no-fault state?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes, but your own policy's lawsuit option sets the rules for pain and suffering.</strong> With no written choice, a Standard Policy carries the limitation option.</p>

</section>
<section id="fault-still-matters">
<h2>What happens if the other driver is at fault?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">You can claim from their liability insurer for losses PIP does not pay, reduced by any share of fault that is yours.</strong> The claim survives if your share is not greater than the other side's.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

<p>Worked fault splits from New Jersey appeals are on our <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">partly at fault fault splits</a> page.</p>
</section>
<section id="special-cases">
<h2>What if the other driver is uninsured or from out of state?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Your own uninsured or underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage pays you when the other driver has no insurance or too little.</strong> The Basic Policy has none; see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claims in New Jersey</a>.</p>
<p>An out-of-state driver whose insurer is authorized in New Jersey gets NJ minimum coverage, including PIP, here and is usually held to the limitation option. The exception: if the other driver's insurer is not authorized in New Jersey, that driver has no NJ PIP and cannot hold you to the limitation option.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="next-steps">
<h2>What should you do first after a car accident in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Open your PIP claim with your own insurer; the fault questions can wait.</strong> Three steps are specific to no-fault:</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Open the PIP claim with your own auto insurer, not the other driver's.</li>
<li>If you elected your health plan as primary, bill it first. Keep both reference numbers.</li>
<li>Find your lawsuit option and PIP limit on your Coverage Selection Form, or ask your insurer.</li>
</ol>
<p>The full first-week checklist, with deadlines, is in <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">what to do after a New Jersey car accident</a>. Every topic is listed in our <a href="/new-jersey">New Jersey car accident guides</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Who pays for car damages in a no-fault state?</h3></summary>
<p>In New Jersey, no-fault covers injuries, not cars. Damage to your car is claimed from the at-fault driver's property damage liability coverage, or from your own collision coverage if you carry it.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can you still sue if you have limited tort?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes, for economic losses such as bills above PIP. For pain and suffering, only if the injury is death, dismemberment, significant disfigurement or scarring, a displaced fracture, loss of a fetus, or permanent.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can someone sue you personally after a car accident in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes, people you hurt in an accident you cause can claim against you, and your bodily injury liability coverage pays those claims and lawsuits up to its limit. The Basic Policy does not include it as standard, and DOBI warns its insurer will not defend you without it.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long do you have to sue after a car accident in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Usually two years from the injury; see the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey car accident statute of limitations</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What is a deemer clause in insurance?</h3></summary>
<p>It is the New Jersey rule that adds NJ coverage to an out-of-state policy while the car is here; see <a href="/new-jersey/route495-lincoln-tunnel-approach-crash-sequence#deemer">what deemer state means for out-of-state drivers</a>.</p>
</details></section>
