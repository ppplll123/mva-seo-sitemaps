<h1>Old Bridge, NJ car accidents: Route 9 rear-end crashes lead, and 4 of 9 deaths were people on foot</h1>

<p>In Old Bridge Township (Middlesex County), police reported 863 car accidents where someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024, about 1.2 a day. They hurt 1,192 people and killed 9. Route 9 alone had 238 of those accidents, more than a quarter. Two in three of the Route 9 injury accidents were coded as rear-end collisions. Pedestrian crashes were rare, only 20, yet they account for 4 of the 9 deaths.</p>

<section id="route-9-share">
<h2>One highway, more than a quarter of the injury accidents</h2>
<p>NJDOT spells the same road several ways in its records. In Old Bridge, Route 9 appears as "US 9", "US 9 SECONDARY (JOANN H SMITH MEMORIAL HIGHWAY)", "US 9 (JOANN H SMITH MEMORIAL HIGHWAY)" and others. Counting by road name splits Route 9 into pieces. So we grouped crashes by the route number NJDOT attaches to each record instead.</p>
<p>Grouped that way, Route 9 had 1,014 police-reported car accidents in Old Bridge in 2023 and 2024. In 238 of them someone was hurt or killed, and 5 people died. That is 28% of the township's injury accidents and 5 of its 9 deaths. Half of all injury accidents in Old Bridge (433 of 863) happened on NJDOT state highways, all of them on Routes 9, 18, 34 and 35.</p>

<section id="rear-end-codes">
<h2>Rear-end collisions: a bigger share here than in the county or the state</h2>
<p>Police code each crash by type. In Old Bridge, 405 of the 863 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 were coded "Same Direction (Rear End)". That is 47%, against 37% in Middlesex County and 31% statewide. The gap comes from the state highways. On Route 9 in Old Bridge, 159 of 238 injury accidents were rear-end codes.</p>

They also have no traffic volume behind them, so a busy highway will show more accidents simply because more cars use it. Right-angle crashes were the next most common injury type in the township (151), then hitting a fixed object (117).</p>
<p>New Jersey insurers decide fault from all the facts of a claim, such as police reports and statements, not from one code. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, pp. 26 to 27, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> Our <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">rear-end car accident guide for New Jersey</a> covers the steps after this kind of accident.</p>

<section id="old-bridge-report">
<h2>Township police or the Parkway portal: which office holds your Old Bridge report</h2>
<p>An Old Bridge police report comes from whichever agency sent the officer. For a crash on a township street or a road Old Bridge police handled, that is the township's Records Bureau. A Parkway crash goes to a separate state portal.</p>
<h3>Old Bridge police: CRASHDOCS.org online, or free at the Records window</h3>
<ul>
<li>The Records Bureau page sends online requests to CRASHDOCS.org and states: "A fee of $5.00 will be charged." Picking the report up in person at Police Records costs nothing.</li>
<li>Questions go to 732-721-5600, ext. 3671. The Records Bureau keeps Monday to Friday hours, 8:15 a.m. to 4:15 p.m.</li>
<li>An OPRA request is handled by the Township Clerk's office, 732-721-5600, ext. 2200.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">(<a href="https://www.oldbridge.com/page/police-record-bureau">Old Bridge Township Police Records Bureau</a>; <a href="https://www.oldbridge.com/page/police-department2">Old Bridge Police Department page</a>, "Obtain a Crash Report" link; both retrieved 2026-11-10. Township address: 1 Old Bridge Plaza, Old Bridge, NJ 08857.)</p>
<p>We did not confirm which agency covers each state highway inside the township. The agency name on the officer's paperwork settles it, or the Records Bureau number above can tell you.</p>
<h3>Garden State Parkway: the State Police and Turnpike Authority portal</h3>
<p>The Parkway had 128 of Old Bridge's accidents in 2023 and 2024 (route table above). Parkway reports are requested online at the portal run for the State Police and the NJ Turnpike Authority. A toll-road report there is $5.00. The portal keeps each report for six years counted from the crash year. When a report is not posted yet, it suggests searching again in a couple of days. One exception: the police report (NJTR-1) for a fatal Parkway crash is requested through the Turnpike Authority's OPRA form instead. Portal questions go to NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov or (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234.</p>
<p class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10; NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page, retrieved 2026-11-10; research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8)</p>
<p>Only police fill out the NJTR-1. A driver whose crash police did not investigate reports it on the SR-1 form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT Police Resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> DOBI's 2026 consumer guide suggests reading the copy closely: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed... If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a> covers other agencies.</p>
</section>

<section id="pedestrian-deaths">
<h2>Few pedestrian crashes, yet 4 of the 9 deaths</h2>
<p>Only 20 of Old Bridge's 863 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 were coded as pedestrian crashes. That is 2.3%, against 5.1% in Middlesex County and 6.6% statewide. But 4 of the 9 people killed in Old Bridge in those two years were pedestrians. The other deaths were in rear-end (2), fixed-object (1), side-swipe (1) and head-on (1) crashes. Bicycle crashes added 12 more injury accidents (13 bicycle crashes in all).</p>
<p>The preliminary 2025 records list 16 pedestrian crashes in Old Bridge and no pedestrian deaths. Those 2025 figures can still change as records are completed.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Old Bridge profile (<code>data/profiles/old-bridge-nj-car-accidents.json</code>), county and statewide files; NJDOT crash records via hudcostreets/nj-crashes, fatal crashes supplemented from NJ State Police; bicycle injury count recomputed from the same parquet file (crash type "Pedalcyclist", 2023 and 2024). Our <a href="/new-jersey/pedestrian-crash-new-jersey">guide for pedestrians hit by a car in New Jersey</a> has the next steps.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Old Bridge. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
