<h1>Route 73 NJ accident records: 463 accidents hurt someone in two years, and six in ten were rear-end collisions</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Police-reported records show 463 car accidents on Route 73 in 2023 and 2024 in which someone was hurt or killed. In those accidents 642 people were hurt and 6 were killed.</p>
  <p>279 of the 463, or 60 percent, were coded as rear-end collisions, roughly double the New Jersey share shown in the collision table below. Mount Laurel and Maple Shade together had 199 of the 463, the stretch where Route 73 meets the Turnpike, I-295 and Route 38.</p>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Which agency wrote your Route 73 accident report</h2>
  <p>Only 46 of the 3,349 Route 73 records from 2023 to 2025 (2025 preliminary) name a New Jersey State Police station. 38 of those are in Folsom, at the Buena Vista station, out of 50 Folsom records. Elsewhere on Route 73, the records rarely name the State Police, so the accident was most likely handled by the town's own police department.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Folsom, or any accident the State Police handled.</strong> For a State Police report from Folsom, the non-toll road fee is $13.00 through the State Police <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">online crash report portal</a>. Reports stay there for six years. A Buena Vista report that does not show up yet may still be in process; the portal advises a fresh search a couple of days later. The Criminal Justice Records Bureau takes report questions by email at NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and by phone on (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. <span class="source">(NJSP crash reports portal and <a href="https://njsp.njoag.gov/identification-amp-information-technology-section/criminal-justice-records-bureau/">NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Mount Laurel, Maple Shade, Evesham, Voorhees, Winslow and the other towns.</strong> Ask the records unit of the police department that came to the scene. We could not verify the departments' procedures or fees, or confirm their online records pages, so we do not link them here.</li>
    <li><strong>No officer came.</strong> The police crash report (NJTR-1) is completed by police only. Drivers report an accident police did not investigate on New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source" data-file="lane2-content/data/route-73-nj-accidents/route73_state_police_station.csv">Source for the station counts: NJDOT crash records via hudcostreets/nj-crashes (retrieved 2026-11-10). NJDOT does not document the station field, so these counts only hint at which agency took a Route 73 report. The department named on the report itself settles it.</p>
  <p>When the report arrives, check it. The insurance regulator's advice: "Get a copy of the police report... Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed." If you find mistakes, tell your insurance company and the police department that responded. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> Every agency's route is on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey accident report</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 73. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="sources-method">
  <h2>Where these figures come from</h2>

All figures are counts, not risk rates, since there is no traffic-volume figure behind them.</p>
