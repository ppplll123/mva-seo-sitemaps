<h1>Route 23 NJ accident data, town by town from Verona to Montague</h1>

<p>Route 23 is really two roads under one number. Its southern miles through Essex, Passaic and Morris counties carry most of the car accidents, and rear-end crashes are the most common injury crash there: 184 of 405, about 45 percent. Its northern miles through Sussex County carry fewer crashes but a larger share of the deaths, head-on crashes and crashes on dark, unlit road.</p>
<p>The split shows in the totals. Of 2,167 police-reported car accidents on the road over 2023 and 2024, the southern stretch logged 1,579 and Sussex 588. Across all 16 towns, 559 left someone hurt or killed, with 775 people hurt and 10 killed. Wayne alone accounts for 119 of those 559.</p>

<h2>The southern suburbs and the Sussex highlands, side by side</h2>
<p>The four counties on Route 23 show very different crash patterns. In the Morris County stretch, 87 of the 143 accidents where someone was hurt were rear-end crashes, about 61 percent. Statewide, rear-end crashes are about 31 percent of such accidents.</p>
<p>In Sussex County, rear-end crashes drop to about 34 percent. Instead, Sussex holds 19 of the 26 head-on injury crashes on the whole road. It also holds 50 of the 94 Route 23 crashes that police coded as dark with no street lights.</p>

<p>Four of the 13 people killed were pedestrians: in Butler, Cedar Grove, West Milford and Montague. Alexander Avenue in Pequannock appears twice, with fatal accidents in May 2023 and April 2024 at the same recorded milepost.</p>

<h2>Deer crashes peak in October and November</h2>

<p>Most deer crashes hurt nobody: only 7 of the 172 injured someone. They are not only a Sussex problem. Cedar Grove recorded 31, West Milford 29, Wantage 23 and Pequannock 19.</p>

<h2>Town police for most of the road, the State Police Sussex station at the top</h2>
<p>Most Route 23 crash records do not name a State Police station. So for most towns on the road, start with the town police department. The far north is different. In Wantage, 192 of 238 records from 2023 to 2025 name the State Police Sussex station. So do 76 of 87 in Sussex Borough and 59 of 74 in Montague.</p>

A station name on a record is not proof of which agency came to a given Route 23 crash.</p>

<h2>How the Route 23 records were picked</h2>
<p>We kept NJDOT records with route number 23 whose road name carries the state prefix, such as "NJ 23", "NJ 23 SECONDARY" or "NJ 23 (POMPTON AVENUE)". A county road that happens to carry the number 23 is not in these counts. The 2023 records come from the NJDOT per-table archive, and 2024 and 2025 from the <a href="https://njdot.aashtowaresafety.net/njdot-crash-data-dashboard#/">NJDOT Crash Data Portal</a>, all normalized by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>. Fatal accidents that NJDOT lacks were added from the NJ State Police fatal feed.</p>
<p>The 2023 and 2024 years are complete and carry the totals on this page; 2025 is preliminary and labelled wherever it appears. The cross-street ranking leaves out 2023 because that export names streets differently. "Someone hurt or killed" means the police report lists at least one injured or killed person. Deer, light, crash type and milepost all come from police-coded fields. These are counts with no traffic-volume figure, so they are not risk rates, and they hold nothing on fault or on any claim.</p>
<p class="source">Script and outputs: lane2-content/data/route-23-nj-accidents/route23_cuts.py and its CSV files; method notes in lane2-content/data/README.md.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 23. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
