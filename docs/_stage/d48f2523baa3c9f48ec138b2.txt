<h1>Middlesex County car accident help: report routes, Route 27 corners and 25 towns compared</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <h2>After a car accident in Middlesex County, start with the agency that came</h2>
  <p>Medical attention comes before paperwork if anyone is hurt. After that, the question is which agency wrote the police report, because only that agency releases it. A town accident goes to that town's police department. A Turnpike or Garden State Parkway accident, or one the State Police handled, goes through the State Police portal.</p>
  <p>The State Police route matters in Middlesex. In 2023 and 2024 the county had at least 601 accidents where someone was hurt or killed on the Parkway and 252 on I-95, the New Jersey Turnpike (NJDOT crash records, county file, two Parkway record names added together).</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <code>lane2-content/data/counties/middlesex.json</code>, NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

  <h3 id="report-by-agency">Report routes for Middlesex agencies</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Perth Amboy Police Department</strong>, 365 New Brunswick Avenue. Perth Amboy hands out vehicle collision and incident reports at its Records window, and the city lists 732-324-3840 for them. Records and Services hours are Monday to Thursday 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. and Friday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Bring proper ID for pickup.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source: City of Perth Amboy, Police Department, <a href="https://www.perthamboynj.org/government/departments/police_department/records_and_services/">Records and Services</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>South Brunswick Police Department</strong>, 540 Ridge Road, Monmouth Junction, 732-329-4646. Its Online Reports page links to a LexisNexis police report search for accident reports already submitted to the department. Questions about getting a report go to PDRec@sbpdnj.net.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source: Township of South Brunswick, Police, <a href="https://southbrunswicknj.gov/police/online-reports/">Online Reports</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Edison, Woodbridge and Old Bridge</strong> publish their own records steps. Those are on our <a href="/new-jersey/edison-nj-car-accidents">Edison car accident page</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/woodbridge-nj-car-accidents">Woodbridge car accident page</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/old-bridge-nj-car-accidents">Old Bridge car accident page</a>.</li>
    <li><strong>Piscataway and North Brunswick</strong>: neither township website let us load its pages on 2026-11-10, and we list no steps we could not read. Ask each police department how its records bureau releases reports.</li>
    <li><strong>Turnpike, Parkway or a State Police case</strong>: all three go through one portal that the State Police operate together with the NJ Turnpike Authority. On that portal, the Turnpike and Parkway reports are $5.00 each, while a State Police report from a road without tolls is $13.00. If your report has not been posted, the portal suggests trying again after a couple of days.</li>
    <li><strong>Who answers questions</strong>: for a Turnpike or Parkway report, the portal names the NJ Turnpike Authority at 732-442-8600 ext. 2431. For a State Police report from a road without tolls, or a serious or fatal crash records request, it names the State Police at 609-882-2000 ext. 2234. Help with the website itself goes to njsprecords@njsp.gov. A fatal crash on the Turnpike or Parkway is different: the portal says its NJTR-1 comes only through an OPRA request filed on the Turnpike Authority's own website.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source: New Jersey State Police and NJ Turnpike Authority, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">Crash Reports portal</a> (fees, contacts and the OPRA note), retrieved 2026-11-10 by curl. The fees cover this portal only, not town police departments.</p>
  <p>Only police fill in the NJTR-1 police accident report. If no officer investigated, the driver reports it with the SR-1 self-reporting form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> Our guide on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">reading the boxes on a New Jersey police report</a> helps once you have it.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Middlesex County. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
  <h2>Where the Middlesex figures come from</h2>
  <p>Every Middlesex number above starts as a police crash record filed with NJDOT. The 2023 records come from NJDOT's per-table archive, and the 2024 and 2025 records from its Crash Data Portal. The open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">nj-crashes project</a> put them in one format, and its fatal records are filled in from <a href="https://www.nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/index.shtml">NJ State Police fatal statistics</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). We downloaded that file on 2026-11-06. On this page, "hurt" means police listed one or more people as injured or killed in the accident.</p>
  <p>The 2023 and 2024 records are complete; 2025 is preliminary, and each 2025 figure carries that label. Towns, roads, accident types and timing rest on 2023 and 2024. Corners and cross streets rest on 2024 and 2025, since street names were written differently before 2024. A misspelled street name splits one corner into two rows, so corner counts are minimums. Record names such as "US 1 SECONDARY" appear here as plain route names. These are counts with no traffic volumes behind them, so they cannot rank how risky a road or corner is. Full method: <code>lane2-content/data/README.md</code>; Middlesex recounts: <code>lane2-content/data/middlesex-county-car-accident-help/</code>.</p>
