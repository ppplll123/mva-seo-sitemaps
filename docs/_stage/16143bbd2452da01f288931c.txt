<h1>How to get your New Jersey car accident report</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>Your car accident report is held by the police agency whose officer took it, so you request it from that agency.</strong> If a New Jersey State Police trooper took it, request it on the state's NJ.gov Crash Reports Request portal for $13. For a crash on the New Jersey Turnpike or the Garden State Parkway, use the same portal's toll road option for $5. If a town's police department took it, use that town's records unit or the online portal its official page links to.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ State Police and NJ Turnpike Authority, Crash Reports Request help page, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help</a>, read 2026-11-06, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>The state portal does not cover the Atlantic City Expressway. Its help page sends those requests to the Atlantic City Expressway Authority, P.O. Box 389, Hammonton, NJ 08037, phone (609) 965-7200 ext. 108.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">Crash Reports Request help page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p><strong>Was the car accident today or this week?</strong> The report is probably not searchable yet. The State Police page says a report becomes searchable only after it is complete and approved, which can take 14 business days from the crash date. If the search says the report is not yet available, the portal says to "search again in a couple days."</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ State Police, non-toll road crash report search page, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Search/SearchReportsRequest?selectedType=Non-Toll%20Roads">njportal.com, Non-Toll Roads search</a>, read 2026-11-06, retrieved 2026-11-10; <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">Crash Reports Request portal home</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="which-agency">
<h2>Which agency investigated your car accident?</h2>
<p>The road name or the county alone does not tell you which records office has your report. A State Police trooper, a town officer or an authority police officer could have taken it. Work through these checks in order.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Look at what the officer gave you.</strong> If you have a case number or a card with a department name, that department holds the report.</li>
<li><strong>Turnpike or Garden State Parkway.</strong> Start with the state portal's toll road option. If the crash was fatal, the portal sends the basic report request to the Turnpike Authority's records request form instead.</li>
<li><strong>Atlantic City Expressway.</strong> Contact the Atlantic City Expressway Authority (details above). The state portal cannot fill these requests.</li>
<li><strong>A trooper responded on another road.</strong> Use the state portal's non-toll road option.</li>
<li><strong>Port Authority, NJ Transit, Palisades Interstate Parkway or Delaware River Port Authority police responded.</strong> Check that agency's official page, linked in the table below.</li>
<li><strong>A town officer responded, or you are not sure.</strong> Call the records unit of the police department in the town where the car accident happened. Ask whether their officers took the report, and give the date and the names involved.</li>
</ol>
<p>Did the crash happen more than 15 business days ago, and the state portal still finds nothing? The portal's help page says to email the State Police at njsprecords@njsp.gov.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">Crash Reports Request help page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. Fatal crash routing: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">Crash Reports Request portal home</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="portals">
<h2>The online portals, and what to expect from each</h2>
<h3>NJ.gov Crash Reports Request (State Police and Turnpike Authority)</h3>
<p>This is the state's own portal for State Police non-toll road reports and Turnpike and Garden State Parkway reports. You can search by case number, driver's license number, license plate number or the last name of someone involved. An account is optional. The portal takes major credit cards and debit cards processed as credit, and eCheck if you set up an account.</p>
<p>When the report is ready, you get an email with download instructions. The help page says the report then stays available for 14 days from that email, so download and save it as soon as it arrives. The portal only holds crashes from the last 6 years before the current year. For older crashes, the help page points you to the State Police.</p>
<p>The portal also takes a separate Records Request for serious or fatal crash investigation material. It costs $20. It asks for a case number, a court order docket number and a bar association number.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">Crash Reports Request help page</a> and <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Search/SearchReportsRequest?selectedType=Non-Toll%20Roads">non-toll road search page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>So it needs court papers. The portal says this material requires "a subpoena signed by an officer of the court or an order issued by a judge." It also says the basic report, the NJTR-1, "does not include additional investigative documents."</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">Crash Reports Request portal home</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>CrashDocs</h3>
<p>Elizabeth, Lakewood and Toms River link to CrashDocs from their official pages. Elizabeth's page gives the clearest steps. You enter the report or reference number, the date of the car accident and the last name of a driver or involved party. Elizabeth says its reports are free for the public. Our automated check of crashdocs.org was blocked on 2026-11-10, so we could not read the portal's own terms.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.elizabethnj.org/497/Car-Fax-Reports">elizabethnj.org</a>, <a href="https://www.lakewoodpolicenj.com/records/">lakewoodpolicenj.com</a> and <a href="https://trpolice.org/administration-bureau/records/police-reports-investigation-and-accident-reports/">trpolice.org</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>BuyCrash and LexisNexis Police Reports</h3>
<p>The Palisades Interstate Parkway Police, Paterson and Lakewood point to LexisNexis. None of the three pages states a price. Paterson and Lakewood still link the older policereports.lexisnexis.com address. It did not respond to our checks on 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-10. If a link fails, call that department's records unit and ask for its current route.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://parkwaypolice.org/resources/">parkwaypolice.org</a>, <a href="https://www.njoag.gov/ppd/reports-and-resources/ppdresources/">njoag.gov Paterson PD resources</a> and <a href="https://www.lakewoodpolicenj.com/records/">lakewoodpolicenj.com</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10; link check recorded 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>CrashDocs and LexisNexis are run by private companies, not by the police. If nothing shows up after the stated wait, the report may not be approved yet. The state portal says a report "will not be searchable or located until the report is completed and approved." Check the agency, the road category and your details before you pay again.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">Crash Reports Request portal home</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<section id="after-you-get-it">
<h2>Once you have the report</h2>
<p>Save the file as soon as it arrives. The state portal's download link lasts 14 days from the notice email.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">Crash Reports Request help page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Read it for mistakes. The state insurance regulator, NJ DOBI, says to review the report for details such as how many passengers were listed. If you see inconsistencies, it says to let your insurance company know and to contact the police department that responded to the scene.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>The basic report is the NJTR-1, the New Jersey Police Crash Investigation Report. Only police complete it, and NJDOT collects these reports from police statewide. Most of it is codes. Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">how to read a New Jersey crash report</a> walks through the form.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT, <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">Police Resources page</a> (archived copy of 2026-02-22, retrieved 2026-11-10; the live NJDOT host blocked our check).</p>

<section id="fault">
<h2>Does the police report decide who was at fault?</h2>
<p>No. DOBI says insurance companies assign fault from all the facts, such as police reports and statements. Disagree with the fault share your insurance company gives you? DOBI says insurers must have an internal appeal process. After that, you can go to DOBI's Office of Insurance Claims Ombudsman.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, pp. 26 and 27, and <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Comparative Negligence Settlements FAQ</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Being partly at fault does not on its own stop you from recovering. Under New Jersey law, your own negligence does not bar recovery if it "was not greater than the negligence of the person against whom recovery is sought." Your damages are then reduced by your share. In DOBI's example, a driver 10% at fault with $1,000 of damage is paid $900, and a driver 80% at fault gets nothing from the other driver.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">statute text (Justia 2025 edition, archived copy)</a>; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, pp. 26 and 27; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Fault matters less for your medical bills. Your own policy's Personal Injury Protection (PIP) pays your medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident." That is what "no-fault" means in New Jersey. A claim against the other driver is separate and does depend on fault.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide (2026)</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>More on both: <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">how New Jersey no-fault insurance works after a car accident</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what partly at fault meant in real New Jersey cases</a>. This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

<section id="no-police">
<h2>If no police officer came to the car accident</h2>
<p>Then there may be no police report to request. Ask the police records unit in the town where the car accident happened whether a report was made.</p>
<p>New Jersey law still puts a duty on the driver when someone is hurt or killed, or when property damage to any one person is over $500. The driver must tell the local police, county police or State Police as fast as they can. Within 10 days, the driver must also send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission. That written report is not required if a police officer files a written report. A knowing violation carries a fine of $30 to $100.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:4-130, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260920182556/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-4-130/">statute text (Justia 2025 edition, archived copy)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>For a car accident the police did not investigate, NJDOT says the SR-1, the New Jersey Self-Reporting Crash form, "is the only form that can be used for this purpose." You can fill it in online and get an acknowledgment back within minutes. NJDOT says that receipt "can be submitted to an insurance company." A separate SR-1S form takes supplemental information.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT, <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">Self-Reporting Crash Form page</a> (archived copy of 2026-09-18, retrieved 2026-11-10; the live NJDOT host blocked our check).</p>
<p>For what to record yourself, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-use-photos-and-videos-after-a-car-accident">how to use photos and videos after a car accident</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="faq">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>

<h3>How do I look up an accident report in NJ?</h3>
<p>Find out which agency took the report, then use that agency's route. For State Police, Turnpike and Garden State Parkway reports, use the NJ.gov Crash Reports Request portal. You can search by case number, driver's license number, plate number or an involved person's last name. For town police, use the route in the table above or call the town's records unit.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">Crash Reports Request help page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>How long does it take to get an accident report in NJ?</h3>
<p>Only 5 of the 11 agency pages we could recheck state any timing, and they measure different steps. The State Police say completion and approval can take 14 business days from the crash date. The Turnpike Authority's portal requests may take up to 14 business days. Elizabeth says 3 to 5 business days after approval, and Edison says generally 7 to 10 business days. Toms River recommends allowing 3 business days before going in person. It says mail requests are usually processed within a few days. The other six do not say, so ask the records unit.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Search/SearchReportsRequest?selectedType=Non-Toll%20Roads">non-toll road search page</a>, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">help page</a>, <a href="https://www.elizabethnj.org/497/Car-Fax-Reports">elizabethnj.org</a>, <a href="https://edisonnj.gov/departments/public_safety/accident_police_reports.php">edisonnj.gov</a> and <a href="https://trpolice.org/administration-bureau/records/police-reports-investigation-and-accident-reports/">trpolice.org</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>Can I get an accident report online in NJ?</h3>
<p>Often, yes. 7 of the 11 agency pages we could recheck list an online route: the NJ.gov portal, CrashDocs or LexisNexis. Jersey City, Edison, Woodbridge and Trenton list in-person routes, and Trenton also takes mail.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://sitemap.injuriescases.com/data/nj-research/nj-crash-report-access-by-agency.csv">agency dataset</a>, pages retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>Can I file an accident report online in NJ?</h3>
<p>Yes, if the police did not investigate. NJDOT offers the SR-1 Self-Reporting Crash form online, with a paper PDF version as well. The portals in the table above are different: they give you a copy of a report a police officer already wrote.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT, <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">Self-Reporting Crash Form page</a> (archived copy of 2026-09-18, retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>

<h3>How long do I have to report an accident in NJ?</h3>
<p>If someone was hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell police by the quickest means. A written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission is due within 10 days, unless a police officer filed a written report.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:4-130, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260920182556/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-4-130/">statute text (Justia 2025 edition, archived copy)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Reporting the car accident is not the same as making an injury claim. An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years. A claim against a public body, such as a town, the state, NJ Transit or the Turnpike Authority, needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions, so check your own dates with a lawyer.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260930102816/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-14-2/">statute text (Justia 2025 edition, archived copy)</a>; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8, as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Authority, 236 N.J. 335 (2019)</a>; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>Are accident reports public record in NJ?</h3>
<p>The basic one is, according to the state portal: "The basic crash investigation report is a public record." It adds that "Serious/Fatal Investigation reports associated with a crash are not public records." Those need a subpoena or a judge's order.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">Crash Reports Request portal home</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Town agencies still set their own request rules. Trenton accepts mail requests only from the person named in the report, their attorney or their insurance company. Elizabeth says its reports are free for the public and asks you to confirm you are a party involved.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.trentonnj.org/402/Police-Report">trentonnj.org</a> and <a href="https://www.elizabethnj.org/497/Car-Fax-Reports">elizabethnj.org</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>How do I get a NJ State Police accident report?</h3>
<p>Use the NJ.gov Crash Reports Request portal and choose the non-toll road option. The fee is $13. The State Police page says a report can take 14 business days from the crash date to be complete, approved and searchable.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Home/Help">Crash Reports Request help page</a> and <a href="https://www.njportal.com/NJSP/CrashReports/Search/SearchReportsRequest?selectedType=Non-Toll%20Roads">non-toll road search page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<section id="method">
<h2>How we built this page</h2>
<p>On 6 November 2026 we read the public report pages of 16 agencies. Six are state and regional: the State Police, the Turnpike Authority, the Port Authority Police, NJ Transit Police, the Palisades Interstate Parkway Police and the DRPA Police. Ten are town police: Newark, Jersey City, Paterson, Elizabeth, Lakewood, Edison, Woodbridge, Toms River, Hamilton (Mercer) and Trenton.</p>
<p>Town size comes from the NJ Department of Labor's 2020 Census municipal tables. For each agency we recorded the request route, fee, timing, report number and ID rules and the page URL.
