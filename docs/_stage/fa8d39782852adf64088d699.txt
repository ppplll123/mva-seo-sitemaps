<h1>NJ uninsured motorist claim help: how UM and UIM coverage work after a car accident</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>If the driver who hit you had no insurance, your own New Jersey auto policy may pay what you would have claimed from that driver, through uninsured motorist (UM) coverage.</strong> If the driver had insurance but too little, underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage on your policy may pay the gap, up to your own UIM limit. Either way, your insurance company pays only if the other driver was legally at fault, so the evidence about the car accident still matters.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 3 and 8; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Claim</a>. Both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

<section id="quiz-mid" class="quiz-cta">
<p>Hurt in a New Jersey car accident by a driver with no insurance or too little? Your PIP pays your medical bills either way. The questionnaire is about whether the other driver was at fault, which is what a UM or UIM claim turns on. It asks about your injury, any lawyer you already have, fault, medical attention, the police report and the date. If your answers fit, a sponsoring lawyer or legal consultant may call you. It is free and there is no obligation.</p>

<section id="um-vs-uim">
<h2>UM or UIM: which one fits your car accident?</h2>
<p>The two coverages are sold together but answer different problems. DOBI defines them this way.</p>
<div class="table-wrap">

<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 3, 4 and 8; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, pp. 2 to 3; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/basicpolicy.shtml">Basic Policy</a>. All retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>DOBI's property damage guide adds a point readers often miss. A driver can count as uninsured even when a policy exists, if that driver's insurance company denies coverage for this car accident. DOBI's examples include a car driven without the owner's permission and a policyholder who will not cooperate with his insurer, once the insurer denies coverage in writing. A denial because the insurer says its driver was <em>not at fault</em> is different. That is a liability dispute, not missing coverage, and DOBI says UM does not apply to it.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Property Damage Claim</a>, FAQs 4, 7 and 8, retrieved 2026-11-10. This guide is written for vehicle damage claims; ask your insurance company how its injury coverage treats the same facts.</p>
</section>

<section id="uim-math">
<h2>How underinsured motorist coverage is counted</h2>
<p>New Jersey UIM does not stack on top of the other driver's limit. It fills the gap between the other driver's liability limit and your own UIM limit. DOBI gives this example:</p>
<div class="table-wrap">

</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">Jane's UIM limit</th><td>$100,000</td></tr>

<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 3, retrieved 2026-11-10. DOBI's 2026 "Everything You Wanted to Know" guide (p. 2) repeats the example and adds that UM and UIM claims "exclude the first $500 in damages."</p>
<p>Two practical results follow. First, UIM helps only if your UIM limit is higher than the other driver's liability limit. Second, you claim against the other driver's insurance company first. DOBI's property damage guide says you submit only what remains unpaid to your own company after the other insurer pays up to its limit.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Property Damage Claim</a>, "Things to note" and FAQ 1, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>These are DOBI's teaching examples, not a forecast of what any claim pays. A limit on your declarations page is the most the coverage can pay, not an amount you will be offered.</p>
</section>

<section id="fault">
<h2>Fault still decides a UM or UIM claim</h2>
<p>DOBI says your insurance company "stands in the shoes" of the uninsured or underinsured driver. It pays only if that driver was legally responsible for your damages. New Jersey's comparative negligence law applies too. You can recover if your share of fault is not greater than the other driver's, but the payment is reduced by your own percentage of fault.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Property Damage Claim</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10; N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1, text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>So keep the same evidence you would keep for any car accident claim: photos, any video, witness names and the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">police report for the car accident</a>. Being partly at fault does not end the claim on its own; see <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how New Jersey splits fault in real cases</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="pip-first">
<h2>Your PIP still pays medical bills first</h2>
<p>UM and UIM are not a replacement for personal injury protection (PIP). Your own PIP pays your medical expenses no matter who caused the car accident. A UM or UIM claim is a fault-based claim for losses PIP does not cover, the same kind of claim you would have made against an insured driver. Keep the two claim numbers separate if your insurance company opens both.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10. More on how PIP works: <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">is New Jersey a no-fault state?</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP coverage limits and medical bills</a>.</p>
<p>If no one in your household has auto insurance, PIP may be sought through the Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund run by NJ PLIGA, as the NJ Supreme Court described in 2026.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026)</a>, describing N.J.S.A. 39:6-86.1, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="steps">
<h2>What to do next</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Tell your insurance company early.</strong> Some insurers open a UM or UIM claim only after the other insurer confirms in writing that coverage is missing or too low. DOBI still says it is in your best interest to contact your company as soon as there is any sign the other driver may not have enough insurance. Your company tells you what documents it needs.</li>
<li><strong>Keep every letter in full.</strong> Save the other insurer's complete denial or limits letter, with its date, claim number and stated reason. A note from the other driver saying "no insurance" is not the same as an insurer's written position.</li>
<li><strong>Find your policy for the car accident date.</strong> Pull the declarations page and endorsements that applied that day, not a later renewal. Check that the UM/UIM limit shown applies to the driver of your car.</li>
<li><strong>Do not settle with the other insurer before your own company agrees.</strong> DOBI says most insurers require you to notify them and get permission before you settle with the other carrier. Ask before you sign any release.</li>
<li><strong>Keep your medical records and bills organized.</strong> See <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">records for a New Jersey car accident injury claim</a>.</li>
</ol>
<p class="source">Source for steps 1 and 4: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Property Damage Claim</a>, FAQs 2 and 3, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Two published appeals show why steps 3 and 4 matter. In <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4679346/raymond-nesby-vs-sheryl-fleurmond-l-1923-16-middlesex-county-and/">Nesby v. Fleurmond (App. Div. 2019)</a>, a driver who was hurt later claimed against her relatives' insurers. She was not covered under those policies and had released the at-fault driver without reserving her rights, so her later claims failed. In <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9491190/britney-motil-v-wausau-underwriters-insurance-company/">Motil v. Wausau Underwriters (App. Div. 2024)</a>, the court enforced the UM/UIM limit printed on the declarations page over a policy clause that would have reduced it for that driver. Each case turned on its own policy language.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: CourtListener opinion pages linked above, as coded in our UM/UIM appeals table (2026-11-05). For more decisions, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">New Jersey uninsured and underinsured motorist appeals decisions</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="disputes">
<h2>If your insurance company disputes the claim</h2>
<p>DOBI says that if you and your insurer cannot agree whether you are legally entitled to recover, or on the amount, the policy terms require arbitration, which either side can demand in writing. If the insurer refuses or keeps delaying arbitration, you can use its internal appeal panel and then ask the DOBI Office of the Insurance Claims Ombudsman to review the decision (1-800-446-7467).</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Property Damage Claim</a>, FAQs 10 and 12, retrieved 2026-11-10. Check your own policy for its arbitration terms.</p>
<p>Time limits apply. DOBI warns that you must settle or file suit within the statute of limitations. For an injury suit against the driver who caused the car accident, New Jersey's general rule is two years. If the crash involved a public vehicle, such as a town, state or NJ Transit vehicle, a notice of claim is due within 90 days. The deadline for a UM/UIM claim against your own insurance company is not covered here. Ask a lawyer which deadline applies to your facts.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Property Damage Claim</a>, FAQ 11; N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2, text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260930102816/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-14-2/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8 as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Auth, 236 N.J. 335 (2019)</a>. All retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="hit-and-run">
<h2>Hit-and-run and unknown drivers</h2>
<p>When the driver drove off, the rules depend on the facts and on the kind of claim. DOBI's property damage guide says a vehicle damage UM claim needs the vehicle identified, so a car that caused the car accident without touching you and then fled does not qualify there. Injury claims can follow different rules. Read the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-hit-and-run-accident-claim-help">New Jersey hit-and-run guide</a> and ask your insurance company what applies to your policy.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk3.htm">Filing an Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Property Damage Claim</a>, FAQ 5, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>
