<h1>Bergen County car accidents: police reports and crash data by town and road</h1>

<section id="report-contacts">
  <h2>Getting the police report after a car accident in Bergen County</h2>
  <p>Two Bergen police departments publish how to get a copy of a crash report. For any other Bergen town, call that police department's non-emergency line and ask for the records bureau.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Teaneck Police Department</strong>, 900 Teaneck Road. The Records Bureau is on the right side of the front lobby of Police Headquarters, staffed 8:30 am to 4:30 pm Monday to Friday, closed on holidays. Reports are picked up at the customer window. The department says an accident report generally takes 3 to 5 days before it is available. Records Bureau phone: (201) 837-2551.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source: Township of Teaneck, Police Department, Records Bureau, <a href="https://www.teanecknj.gov/living-in-teaneck/police-department/records-bureau/">teanecknj.gov/living-in-teaneck/police-department/records-bureau/</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Hackensack Police Department</strong>, Records and Evidence Management Unit. Report requests: (201) 646-7725 or records@hackensackpd.org. The unit runs a Community Access Center on the first floor of the police department where the public can pick up copies of reports. The page's "Crash Reports" link opens the LexisNexis BuyCrash search set to the Hackensack Police Department.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source: City of Hackensack, Records and Evidence Management Unit, <a href="https://www.hackensack.org/records-evidence-management-unit/">hackensack.org/records-evidence-management-unit/</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <p>Our guide on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey accident report, agency by agency</a> covers the statewide routes. Once you have the report, our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">reading the codes on a New Jersey crash report</a> explains the boxes.</p>

  <h3 id="bergen-turnpike-parkway-reports">Accidents on the Turnpike, the Parkway or a State Police case</h3>
  <p>A town police department does not hold the report for every Bergen accident. Reports for accidents on the New Jersey Turnpike and the Garden State Parkway, and for accidents New Jersey State Police investigated on other roads, are requested online from the State Police. Those two toll roads matter here: our road table below counts 808 accidents where someone was hurt on I-95, which includes the Turnpike lanes, and 342 on the Parkway in Bergen in 2023 and 2024. Much of that I-95 count is the George Washington Bridge approach in Fort Lee and Teaneck, so the report comes from whichever agency responded, not always the State Police.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>The basic police crash report (form NJTR-1) can be bought once it is finalized. If the portal says it is not yet available, it asks you to search again in a couple of days.</li>
    <li>A toll road report costs $5.00 and a non-toll road report $13.00. Serious or fatal investigation reports, photos and statements cost $20.00.</li>
    <li>Reports are available for six years from the year of the crash.</li>
    <li>The basic report is a public record. Serious or fatal investigation material is not public and needs a subpoena or a judge's order. For a fatal accident on the Turnpike or Parkway, the NJTR-1 is requested through the NJ Turnpike Authority's OPRA form.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source: New Jersey State Police, Crash Reports portal, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. These fees are for the State Police portal only. Teaneck, Hackensack and other Bergen town departments run their own process (see above).</p>

  <h3 id="bergen-reporting-duty">Do you have to report the accident, and what if no officer came?</h3>
  <p>Yes, if anyone was hurt or killed, or if property damage to any one person is over $500. The driver must tell the local police, county police or State Police as fast as they can. The driver must also send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files a written report on the accident.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:4-130 (statute text, 2025 edition, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260920182556/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-4-130/">archived copy</a>), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <p>If police did not investigate, NJDOT's SR-1 self-reporting crash form is the only form for reporting that accident. It can be filed online, and NJDOT says the acknowledgment you get back can be given to an insurance company.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: NJDOT, <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">Self-Reporting Crash Form</a> (read from the <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260918164037/https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">archived copy of 2026-09-18</a>), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <p>When the report arrives, the state insurance department suggests checking details such as how many passengers are listed. If something looks wrong, tell your insurance company and contact the police department that responded. For a Teaneck or Hackensack police report, that means the contacts above.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

By accidents where someone was hurt, Bergen ranks second of New Jersey's 21 counties, behind Middlesex and just 5 ahead of Essex (10,928), with about 10 percent of the state total.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Bergen County. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
