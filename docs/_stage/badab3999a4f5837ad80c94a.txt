<h1>Wrongful death after a New Jersey car accident: what families can do</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>When someone dies in a New Jersey car accident, two separate things usually matter to the family.</strong> First, the auto insurance on the policy that covered the person who died may pay a PIP death benefit and funeral expenses, whoever caused the crash. Second, if another driver caused the car accident, that driver's bodily injury liability insurance covers claims by people who die as a result of it. The second claim depends on fault, and the rules about who brings it and when are specific enough that a family should get advice from a New Jersey lawyer early.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2 (bodily injury liability "pays for claims and lawsuits by people who are injured or die as a result of an auto accident you cause") and p. 7 (death benefit and funeral expense benefit). Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice. Injuries Cases Autos is not a law firm.</p>
</section>

<section id="quiz-mid" class="quiz-cta">
<p>PIP death and funeral benefits come from the policy itself and do not depend on fault. The 30-second New Jersey questionnaire is about the other question: whether another driver's insurance company may also be responsible. It asks about the injury, any lawyer you already have, fault, medical attention, the police report and the date. It was written for people hurt in a car accident, so a family member can use it to describe what happened, but it does not decide who can bring a claim. If your answers fit, a sponsoring lawyer or legal consultant may call you by phone. It is free and there is no obligation.</p>

<section id="who-pays">
<h2>Which insurance can pay after a fatal car accident</h2>
<p>New Jersey's no-fault system covers more than medical bills. The Extra PIP Package on the standard policy includes a death benefit and a funeral expense benefit. "No-fault" here means these benefits are paid without regard to who caused the crash. It does not mean nobody was at fault.</p>

<section id="fault-claim">
<h2>The claim against the driver who caused the crash</h2>
<p>New Jersey has a Wrongful Death Act (N.J.S.A. 2A:31-1 and following sections). This page does not explain who may bring a claim under it, what it allows, or how long a family has. We have not verified those rules from an official source, so we will not guess. A New Jersey lawyer can answer them for your family's facts.</p>
<p>What we can confirm from official sources:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Death is on the lawsuit-option list.</strong> Under the "limitation on lawsuit" option, pain and suffering can be claimed only for listed injuries, and the first item on that list is death. Economic losses are not limited by that option.</li>
<li><strong>Shared fault reduces a recovery but does not always end it.</strong> Under New Jersey's comparative negligence law, damages are reduced by the injured person's share of fault. There is no recovery from a driver whose share of fault is smaller than that person's own.</li>
<li><strong>Uninsured and underinsured drivers.</strong> If the driver who caused the crash had no insurance or too little, UM/UIM on the family's own policy may matter. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claims in New Jersey</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8(a) and (b), statute text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">Justia 2025 edition (Wayback copy)</a>, consistent with DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/standardpolicy.html">Standard Policy page</a>; N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1 via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">Justia 2025 edition (Wayback copy)</a>; DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Comparative Negligence FAQ</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>A published New Jersey appeal shows how these pieces fit. In <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6487385/dutton-v-rando/">Dutton v. Rando (App. Div. 2019)</a>, an SUV struck a bicyclist who died. The mother, representing her son's estate, received separate jury awards for wrongful death and for survivorship (the son's own conscious pain and suffering), and the total was reduced for comparative fault. The Appellate Division affirmed. One case shows that the two claims are treated separately and that fault still counts. It says nothing about what any other family's claim is worth.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Dutton v. Rando, A-1049-16 (App. Div. 2019), as coded in our appeals table (coordination/gigradar-blueprint-20261105/articles/data/nj-comparative-fault-appeals.csv, built 2026-11-05). For fault splits in other New Jersey cases, see <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">partly at fault in a NJ car accident</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="time">
<h2>Time limits: what we can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p>We are not giving a wrongful-death deadline, because we have not verified it. Do not rely on the two-year rule for injury suits as if it were the answer for a death claim. Two related rules are confirmed:</p>
<ul>
<li>If a public body is involved (for example a municipal or state vehicle, NJ Transit or a toll road authority), New Jersey's Tort Claims Act generally requires a written notice of claim within 90 days after the claim accrues (N.J.S.A. 59:8-8).</li>
<li>Suits over PIP benefits have their own time limit, and the statute expressly applies it to a "survivor" as well as the injured person (N.J.S.A. 39:6A-13.1(a)).</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Auth., 236 N.J. 335 (2019)</a>, quoting N.J.S.A. 59:8-8 and 59:8-9; <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7403318/aponte-correa-v-allstate-insurance/">Aponte-Correa v. Allstate Ins. Co. (App. Div. 1999)</a>, quoting N.J.S.A. 39:6A-13.1(a). Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Because the time rules depend on facts, treat any time question as urgent and raise it at the first conversation with a lawyer.</p>
</section>

<section id="next-steps">
<h2>Practical next steps for the family</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Find the policies.</strong> Locate the declarations page and Coverage Selection Form for the person who died and for the household. They show whether the Extra PIP Package, the funeral limit and UM/UIM were chosen.</li>
<li><strong>Tell the insurance company about the death.</strong> Ask the PIP carrier in writing how to claim the death benefit and funeral expense benefit, and what documents it needs.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the funeral bills and records.</strong> Keep funeral invoices, any medical bills from before the death, and pay records separately. The <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">medical records guide</a> explains what to request.</li>
<li><strong>Do not sign a release from the other driver's insurance company</strong> before a lawyer has read it. A release can end claims, and who has authority to sign it is a legal question.</li>
<li><strong>Ask a lawyer about fees in writing.</strong> In New Jersey tort cases, Court Rule 1:21-7 caps contingent fees on a sliding scale and requires a signed written agreement. The rule sets maximums; lower fees are allowed.</li>
</ol>
<p class="source">Source: NJ Court Rule 1:21-7, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20211230044710/https://www.njcourts.gov/attorneys/assets/rules/r1-21.pdf">Rule 1:21 PDF (official text, Wayback copy dated 2021-12-30)</a>; current rules index at <a href="https://www.njcourts.gov/attorneys/rules-of-court">njcourts.gov</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If a family member survived the same car accident with injuries, the guide for <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-families-should-know-after-a-serious-car-accident-in-nj">families after a serious car accident in NJ</a> covers that side.</p>
</section>

<section id="statistics">
<h2>How many people die in New Jersey car accidents</h2>
