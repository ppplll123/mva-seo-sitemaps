<ul>
<li><strong>Low-speed electric bicycle:</strong> pedal assist only, up to 20 mph. Must be registered; no insurance required.</li>
<li><strong>Motorized bicycle:</strong> throttle models and faster pedal assist, up to 28 mph. Must be registered and insured.</li>
<li><strong>Every rider:</strong> at least 15, with an e-bike license or a driver's license.</li>
</ul>
<p>The insurance a motorized bicycle needs is liability insurance. It pays people the rider hurts, not the rider's own bills.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>
</section>

<section id="nobody-insured">
<h2>What if nobody has car insurance, or the driver drove off?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Check your household first.</strong> In NJ crash calls we reviewed, 42% of people did not know whether they had uninsured motorist (UM) coverage.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>
<ul>
<li>Ask the insurer of any car policy in your home, in writing, whether its UM part covers you as a rider. Our sources do not settle that.</li>
<li>If the driver fled and cannot be found, the state fund (UCJF) can sometimes be sued, but written notice is due within 180 days.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></li>
<li>A scooter rider with a broken leg and no insurance on either side waited over a year to call; his story is on <a href="/new-jersey/what-is-the-ucjf-nj">what the UCJF is</a>.</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="first-week">
<h2>What should I do in the first week?</h2>
<p>Build the record a car occupant would get without trying.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Get a police report. Only 28% of NJ callers we reviewed had a witness's name and number, so ask anyone who stopped.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></li>
<li>See a doctor and say how it happened.</li>
<li>Keep the scooter or e-bike unrepaired, with its model, motor size and top speed noted.</li>
<li>Photograph the spot, any wire, hole or broken pavement, and the car.</li>
<li>Find the declarations page of every car policy in your home.</li>
</ol>
<p>Plain bicycle crashes, where PIP does pay, are covered in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-bicycle-accident-claim-help">NJ bicycle accident claim help</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>What happens if you are hit by an e-scooter?</h3></summary>
<p>If you were on foot, your PIP pays only for injuries "caused by an automobile," and a scooter is not one. The claim is against the rider; a motorized bicycle rider must now carry liability insurance.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Do e-bikes have to be insured in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Only motorized bicycles, meaning throttle models and pedal assist faster than 20 mph. Low-speed pedal-assist e-bikes must be registered but need no insurance, per the Motor Vehicle Commission.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can I still sue the driver who hit me?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes. A claim against the driver who caused it does not depend on PIP. You have two years to sue, and a road-defect claim against a public body needs notice within 90 days.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Does a pothole crash on a scooter count?</h3></summary>
<p>It is a claim against the road's owner, not a car claim. Notice, proof and limits are in <a href="/new-jersey/pothole-claim-nj">can I sue the town for a pothole in NJ</a>.</p>
</details></section>
