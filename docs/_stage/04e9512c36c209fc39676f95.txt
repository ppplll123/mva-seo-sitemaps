<h1>Black Horse Pike accident today? Start with the town it happened in</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">No feed here shows today's accidents. What the police records do show is that the Black Horse Pike is one road name spread over four route numbers, and that a single town, Egg Harbor Township, holds a third of its injury accidents.</p>
  <p>Of the 3,112 police-reported car accidents on the Pike across 2023 and 2024, 972 left at least one person hurt or dead, close to 1.3 every day. Across the two years, the Pike's injury accidents left 1,463 people hurt and 27 dead.</p>
  <p>The four numbers run in sequence: Route 168 from Camden County into Washington Township, Route 42 and Route 322 through Gloucester County, then Route 322 and Route 40 across Atlantic County. Atlantic County accounts for 557 of the 972 injury accidents (57 percent), and Egg Harbor Township alone for 330.</p>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Who holds the report: town police on almost every stretch of the Pike</h2>
  <p>Along the Pike, the police department of the town where the accident happened usually wrote the report. In our 2023 and 2024 records, the station field is blank on nearly every record. Folsom stands out: 36 of its 49 records carry the value "Buena Vista" in that field. The field has no published definition, so we cannot say which agency that value stands for. Your own report names the agency that wrote it.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Town police.</strong> Get the report number from the responding department and ask its records unit for a copy. In Atlantic County, the Hamilton Township police page links a "Motor Vehicle Crash Reports" lookup. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.hamiltonatlnj.gov/police">Hamilton Township Police, Atlantic County</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> Fees and timing differ from department to department, and we could not reach the other towns' records pages for this page.</li>
    <li><strong>State Police.</strong> If a trooper wrote the report, the State Police crash report portal handles the request. The portal lets you download the basic report once it is "ready and approved," and keeps reports for six years from the crash year. Help with the online service goes to njsprecords@njsp.gov. The portal gives (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234 for questions about non-toll road reports, and asks anyone waiting on serious or fatal crash records to call that line only after one month has passed. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>No police at the scene.</strong> Only police fill out the NJTR-1. If no officer came out, drivers file their own account on the SR-1 self-reporting form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
  </ul>
  <p>Read the report closely once you have it. The state insurance regulator's consumer guide puts the advice this way: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed... If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <p>For agencies not covered here, see our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">guide to getting a New Jersey accident report, agency by agency</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on the Black Horse Pike. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
  <h2>Building the Pike from four route numbers and their mileposts</h2>
  <p>Because no single route number covers the Pike, we assembled it from four. The count takes each record on Route 168, Route 42, Route 322 or Route 40 that has a state or US road name and sits inside these milepost ranges: Route 42 from 0 to 6.34, Route 168 from minus 0.2 to 9.51, Route 322 from 24.27 to 50.16, and Route 40 from 50.00 to 63.48.</p>
  <p>Almost all records named "Black Horse Pike" in 2024 and 2025 fall inside those ranges. The exceptions are three Route 168 records in Camden City, at the northern end, which the ranges leave out. Records naming another road, such as "NJ 42 (NORTH-SOUTH FREEWAY)" or "US 40 (HARDING HIGHWAY)", are also left out. On these four routes, 11 records with a state or US road name carry no milepost and cannot be placed; one of them is named Black Horse Pike.</p>
  <h3>Why these totals are higher than our earlier page</h3>
  <p>Our earlier page counted only records whose road name contained the words "Black Horse." That missed most of 2023, when the records usually wrote just "NJ 42" or "US 322." It also made the road look as if accidents had risen sharply each year. That rise was a change in labels, not in accidents. Pages on this site that pick Black Horse Pike records by name show lower figures for the same reason; by the corridor count used here, Atlantic City had 24 injury accidents on the Pike in 2023 and 2024.</p>
