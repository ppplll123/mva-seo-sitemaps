<section id="report">
<h2>Township police, Galloway State Police or the Expressway: who has your report?</h2>
<p>It depends on the road: the responding department for local roads and Route 40, the State Police portal for the Parkway, and the Expressway Authority for the Atlantic City Expressway.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Township streets, county roads and Route 40.</strong> Ask the department that responded to the accident.</li>
<li><strong>Garden State Parkway.</strong> Use the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report portal</a>: $5.00 per toll road report. In 2025, 111 township Parkway records name the State Police Galloway station.</li>
<li><strong>Atlantic City Expressway.</strong> Request it from the Atlantic City Expressway Authority, not the state portal. In 2023 and 2024, 82 township records name the Expressway station.</li>
</ul>

<section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us.</strong> They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Egg Harbor Township. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></li>
</ul>
