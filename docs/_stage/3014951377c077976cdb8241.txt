<h1>Do you need a lawyer after a car accident in New Jersey? 7 signs, even for a minor injury</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>You do not need a lawyer for every car accident in New Jersey.</strong> A consultation is most useful when you were hurt and something is still open: symptoms that continue, missed work, a fault dispute, a release you do not understand, a driver with little or no insurance, or a deadline. If only your car was damaged and the repair payment is clear, you can usually handle the claim yourself.</p>
<p>Your own PIP coverage pays your medical bills no matter who caused the car accident. So the lawyer question is mostly about a separate claim against the other driver, for pain and suffering and for losses PIP does not cover.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p class="disclaimer">This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

<section id="when-not-needed">
<h2>When you may not need a lawyer</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Only the vehicle was damaged.</strong> Work with the insurance company's repair process and a written estimate.</li>
<li><strong>You got medical attention, recovered, and PIP paid the bills.</strong> With no fault dispute and no open treatment, you may choose to deal with the insurance company yourself. Still read any final release before you sign it.</li>
<li><strong>The problem is a PIP payment, not the other driver.</strong> Disputes over PIP benefits go through DOBI's PIP dispute resolution program, administered by Forthright. That is a separate process from an injury claim against the at-fault driver.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.A.C. 11:3-5, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/njac11_3_5_effect130104.pdf">DOBI posted text</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/aicrapg.htm">PIP information page naming Forthright as administrator</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Small vehicle damage does not prove a small injury. A dented bumper with a settled repair bill is a simple property claim. The same car accident with neck pain that continues, physical therapy and fewer shifts at work raises an injury question as well.</p>
</section>

<section id="fees">
<h2>How car accident lawyer fees work in New Jersey</h2>
<p>New Jersey court rules cap contingent fees in injury cases. The cap is a maximum, not a standard price, and a lawyer may charge less. The copy of the rule we read is an archived December 2021 version of the official court rules PDF, so check the current rule or ask the lawyer to point to it.</p>

<li>The fee is figured on the net recovery after case costs are deducted, but medical liens are not deducted first.</li>
<li>The lawyer must first offer to work on an hourly or reasonable-value basis instead.</li>
<li>The agreement must be in writing and signed. At the end you get a signed closing statement.</li>
<li>For a minor or a mentally incapacitated client, a settlement before a jury is chosen (or a bench trial starts) is capped at 25%.</li>

<section id="questions-to-ask">
<h2>Questions to ask before you hire anyone</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Who are you, and what is your role?</strong> Ask for the person's name, organization and role. An intake caller and a lawyer deciding whether to take your case are different roles. You can check a lawyer's license through an official New Jersey source.</li>
<li><strong>What would you handle?</strong> Ask which injury, insurance and property issues the representation covers.</li>
<li><strong>How do fees and costs work?</strong> Ask for the percentage, what costs are deducted, and what happens if there is no recovery. Read the written agreement.</li>
<li><strong>What is missing?</strong> A useful answer names the record, coverage question or medical question that matters.</li>
<li><strong>Who handles the next deadline?</strong> A phone call or a form does not stop a deadline or notify an insurance company. Confirm that the lawyer has accepted the matter and who will act.</li>
</ul>
<p>You do not need a perfect file to start. The date, the place, your role in the car accident, your treatment so far and the letter or offer that prompted the question are enough for a first call.</p>

<p>A lawyer does not replace your doctor. Get medical attention for symptoms, and call 911 in an emergency. Do not get treatment you do not need, or describe an injury as permanent, to fit a checklist.</p>

<section id="faq">
<h2>Common questions</h2>
<h3>Should I get a lawyer for a car accident that wasn't my fault?</h3>
<p>Not always. If you were hurt and the other driver's insurance company disputes fault, your injury or its offer, a consultation can help. In New Jersey you can also recover if you were partly at fault, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's. Your recovery is reduced by your percentage. See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how fault splits work in New Jersey</a>.</p>
<h3>What percentage do lawyers take from a car accident settlement in NJ?</h3>
<p>Court Rule 1:21-7 caps contingent fees in injury cases at 33 1/3% of the first $750,000 of the net recovery, with lower caps above that. A lawyer may charge less, must offer an hourly option, and must put the agreement in writing. The table above shows the full scale.</p>
<h3>How long do you have to sue after a car accident in NJ?</h3>
<p>The general rule for personal injury suits is two years after the claim arises (N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2). A claim against a public entity needs a notice of claim within 90 days. Exceptions exist, so confirm your own deadline with a lawyer rather than counting it yourself.</p>
<h3>Is it worth claiming for a minor car accident injury?</h3>
<p>There is no minimum injury size for a claim for unpaid economic losses, such as medical bills PIP did not cover or lost wages. A pain and suffering claim needs a qualifying injury if your policy has the limitation on lawsuit option (N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8(a)). PIP pays your medical bills either way. A claim against the other driver depends on fault, your losses and your lawsuit option. If symptoms continue, you miss work or the release is unclear, those questions are worth answering before you agree to anything final.</p>
<h3>Does PIP paying my bills mean I have no other claim?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. PIP pays your own medical bills regardless of fault. A claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering and other losses is separate. Read <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">what no-fault means in New Jersey</a>.</p>
