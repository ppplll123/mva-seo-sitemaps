<h1>Car accident in Clifton, NJ? More than half of all Route 3 injury accidents in New Jersey happen inside the city.</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>Clifton (Clifton City, Passaic County) had 2,195 police-reported car accidents in 2023 and 2024 in which someone was hurt or killed.</strong> Those accidents hurt 3,016 people and killed 9. Only five New Jersey municipalities recorded more, which puts Clifton 6th in the state. In Passaic County, only Paterson recorded more.</p>
<p>What sets Clifton apart is Route 3. Of the 674 accidents on Route 3 statewide in which someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024, 393 (58 percent) were inside Clifton. On that stretch, rear-end accidents make up 69 percent of the injury accidents.</p>

<section id="allwood-broad">
<h2>Allwood Road at Broad Street: the junction name may not be where it happened</h2>
<p>The records show why a precise location matters here. In 2024 and 2025, 41 crashes were recorded with Allwood Road and Broad Street named as road and cross street. Only 5 of them were flagged as at the intersection itself. Ten of the 41 left someone hurt.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <code>data/clifton-nj-car-accidents/allwood_broad_2024_2025.csv</code> (2025 preliminary). The record does not say whether the other 36 were at a driveway, in a lot entrance or in a queue.</p>
<p>If your accident was near a junction like this, write down what you actually saw. Note whether the other car was turning in, pulling out of a property, or moving through the junction. Note which opening it used, if you know. A photo that shows the road and the driveways, not just the damage, records the layout as it was that day.</p>
</section>

<section id="pedestrians">
<h2>People on foot: 148 pedestrian crashes in two years, mostly on county avenues</h2>
<p>Clifton recorded 148 crashes coded as pedestrian crashes in 2023 and 2024, and 136 of them left someone hurt or killed. Main Avenue had 18 of the 148, Clifton Avenue 12 and Lexington Avenue 11. Another 8 are recorded under "NJ 161 (Clifton Avenue)", a separate record string for part of Clifton Avenue.</p>
<p>Pedestrian crashes here skew toward darkness. Of the 136 pedestrian crashes in which someone was hurt or killed, 59 (43 percent) happened in the dark. Across all 2,195 Clifton injury accidents, the dark share was 29 percent (646). Five of the 12 fatal crashes in Clifton from 2023 to 2025 were coded as pedestrian crashes, and one more involved a cyclist.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <code>data/clifton-nj-car-accidents/pedestrian_by_road_2023_2024.csv</code>, <code>pedestrian_dark_ki_2023_2024.csv</code> ("dark" covers every light code starting with Dark), <code>road_system_2023_2024.csv</code> (99 of the 148 on county roads) and <code>fatal_2023_2025.csv</code> (2025 preliminary). What to do after a crash on foot is on our <a href="/new-jersey/pedestrian-crash-new-jersey">pedestrian crash in New Jersey</a> guide.</p>

<section id="method">
<h2>Reading the Clifton records: what was counted and what was not</h2>
<p>Every figure here comes from NJDOT crash records: 2023 from the NJDOT per-table archive, and 2024 and 2025 from the <a href="https://njdot.aashtowaresafety.net/njdot-crash-data-dashboard#/">NJDOT Crash Data Portal</a>. We used the copy normalized by the open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a> project, downloaded 2026-11-06, with fatal crashes added from the NJ State Police feed.</p>
<p>We kept each record whose NJDOT municipality is Clifton City in Passaic County, whatever the road: city street, county avenue, state highway or Parkway. A crash counts as an injury accident on this page when the police report lists at least one person injured or killed. 2023 and 2024 are full years; 2025 is still arriving and is marked preliminary wherever it appears.</p>
<p>Corners, cross streets and mileposts use only 2024 and 2025, since the 2023 export writes street names differently. Names are counted exactly as typed, so each of those figures is a minimum. None of this is a risk rate: Route 3 carries far more traffic than a side street, and traffic volume is not in these records.</p>
<p class="source">Scripts: <code>lane2-content/data/clifton-nj-car-accidents/clifton_cuts.py</code> and <code>clifton_factcheck_cuts.py</code>; each CSV carries its period and method in a header line. Full method: <code>lane2-content/data/README.md</code>.</p>

<section id="police-report">
<h2>Clifton police reports: City Hall Records Division, or the State Police portal for the Parkway</h2>
<p>If Clifton police came to the scene, the report comes from the Clifton Police Department. Its records page says an accident report "can be obtained after 3 business days of the incident" through the online report service it links to. You can also ask the Records Division, inside Clifton City Hall, at 973-470-5877.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://cliftonpolice.org/FAQ.html?submenuRecords">Clifton Police Department, "How Do I..." records page</a> and <a href="https://cliftonpolice.org/General_Info.html">General Info page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>The department's two pages list slightly different weekday hours (9:00 am or 10:00 am to 4:00 pm), so call first. Both say that since 6 February 2024 the Records Division is also open Tuesday evenings until 7 pm to pick up police reports. The non-emergency line is 973-470-5900 and the Traffic Division is 973-470-5904.</p>
<p>A crash on the Garden State Parkway through Clifton is a State Police matter. The Parkway is a toll road, and its crash reports are requested on the New Jersey State Police and Turnpike Authority portal at <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>, as is any crash a trooper handled on a road without tolls. The portal lists NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and (609) 882-2000 ext 2234 for help with a request.</p>
<p>If someone died in the Parkway crash, the NJTR-1 is instead requested on the NJ Turnpike Authority's OPRA form. Clifton's records include two fatal Parkway crashes in this period, one in December 2023 and one in June 2025 (preliminary).</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJSP crash report portal and NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page, per research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8 (including the NJTA OPRA route for fatal Parkway reports), retrieved 2026-11-10; <code>data/clifton-nj-car-accidents/fatal_2023_2025.csv</code>.</p>
<p>Clifton officers write their reports on New Jersey's NJTR-1 form, which only police may complete. Where no officer came out, NJDOT points drivers to the SR-1 self-reporting form (<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10). Once you have the Clifton report, check it against what you remember. DOBI's 2026 consumer guide puts it this way: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed... If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." (<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Clifton. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
