<h1>Route 206 NJ accident records: 1,086 accidents hurt someone in two years, on a road that runs from Hammonton to Montague</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Route 206 behaves like two different roads. Through Trenton it runs on city streets, and 27 of its 47 pedestrian accidents in 2023 and 2024 happened there. Through the Pinelands and Sussex County it is a rural highway, where deer start many accidents and State Police often write the report.</p>
  <p>Across its six counties and 37 towns, the road had 1,086 car accidents that hurt or killed someone in 2023 and 2024. Those accidents left 1,587 people hurt and 13 dead. A deer was the first harmful event in 10.5 percent of all Route 206 accident records, close to three times the 3.6 percent share statewide.</p>
  <p>Everything here comes from past police reports, from 2023 through a preliminary 2025, so it cannot tell you about traffic on the road right now.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: our count of NJDOT Route 206 crash records, 2023 to 2025, in the form cleaned up by the open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a> project (retrieved 2026-11-10), with deaths checked against <a href="https://nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/">NJ State Police fatal crash statistics</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). The method is described at the bottom of the page.</p>

  <p>Mercer County had the most Route 206 accidents where someone was hurt, 313 in 2023 and 2024. Somerset County had slightly more accidents overall, 1,241, but fewer of them hurt anyone.</p>
  <p>The share of a county's injury accidents that happened on Route 206 varies a lot. In Sussex County, Route 206 had 181 of the county's 1,168 injury accidents, about 15 percent. In Morris County it was 133 of 5,033, under 3 percent.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 206. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
