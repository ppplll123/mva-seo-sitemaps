<h1>New Jersey property damage claim: steps after a car accident</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>After a New Jersey car accident, you can claim for your car's damage in one of two ways.</strong> You can claim with your own insurance company if your policy has collision or comprehensive coverage, and it pays after your deductible. Or you can claim with the other driver's insurance company, which pays only to the extent its driver was legally responsible.</p>
<p>Your PIP (no-fault) coverage does not help here. New Jersey's Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI) says PIP pays only your own medical expenses and "does not pay for damage to a vehicle."</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">Filing an Auto Damage Claim with Your Own Insurance Company</a>, introduction and question 1; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">Filing an Auto Damage Claim with Another Insurance Company</a>, introduction and question 1. Both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

It asks about your injury, any lawyer you already have, fault, medical attention, the police report and the date. If your answers qualify, a sponsoring lawyer or legal consultant may call you. It is free and there is no obligation.</p>

<section id="steps">
<h2>Steps after a car accident damages your car</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Tell the police when the law requires it.</strong> A driver must notify the police by the quickest means when a car accident causes injury, death or property damage to any one person over $500. A written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days is also required, unless an officer files a written report. DOBI also says to report theft, vandalism and hit-and-run damage to the police right away.</li>
<li><strong>Report the claim right away.</strong> Tell your insurance agent or company, and the other driver's insurance company if you claim against it. Exchange insurance details with the other driver.</li>
<li><strong>Let the insurance company inspect before repairs.</strong> DOBI says you must make the car available for inspection before it is repaired. The insurer can only ask for a time and place that is reasonably convenient for you.</li>
<li><strong>Stop further damage and keep receipts.</strong> If you leave a broken windshield uncovered, the insurer may refuse the later rain damage. Save receipts for emergency repairs so you can submit them.</li>
<li><strong>Give the documents asked for.</strong> Your own insurer may ask for a proof of loss, repair estimates, the police report, and the title or bill of sale. Send it any legal papers you receive about the car accident.</li>
<li><strong>Cooperate with the investigation.</strong> Both DOBI guides warn that a claim can be denied if you do not cooperate. Your own insurer may require an examination under oath, and you may have a lawyer present.</li>
</ol>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 39:4-130 (duty to report), text via Justia 2025 edition, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260920182556/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-4-130/">Wayback snapshot 2026-09-20</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">own insurer guide</a>, questions 2, 3 and 4; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">other insurer guide</a>, questions 2, 3 and 7. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Need the police report for the claim? See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey car accident report</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="fault">
<h2>How fault changes what the other driver's insurance company pays</h2>
<p>New Jersey uses comparative negligence. You can collect from the other driver if your share of fault is not greater than theirs. What you get is reduced by your share.</p>
<p>DOBI's example: the other driver is 80% at fault and you are 20%, so their insurance company offers 80% of your damage. Rental payments follow the same split. If an insurer allows $30 a day and its driver is 60% at fault, it reimburses $18 a day.</p>
<p>A traffic ticket does not settle fault. DOBI says a citation, or even a conviction, does not by itself make a driver 100% responsible. DOBI also notes that only a judge or jury can finally decide fault and how much is owed.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1, text via Justia 2025 edition, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">Wayback snapshot 2026-10-09</a>; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">other insurer guide</a>, questions 4, 9 and 23. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>For more on split fault, see <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what happens when you are partly at fault in a New Jersey car accident</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="repairs">
<h2>Repairs: your shop, parts and rental car</h2>
<h3>Choosing the repair shop</h3>
<p>You can choose your own licensed repair shop. The insurance company has to try to reach an agreed price with that shop. If it cannot, it gives you names of licensed shops that will do the work at its price.</p>
<p>You can also ask the insurer to recommend a shop near the car. DOBI says that shop's work is guaranteed and the insurance company stands behind the guarantee.</p>
<h3>New parts and aftermarket parts</h3>
<p>The insurer only has to restore your car to its condition before the loss. That can mean original equipment (OEM) parts or aftermarket parts warranted as like kind and quality. You do not have to accept aftermarket parts, but you may pay the price difference for OEM parts. The appraisal must say in writing which parts are aftermarket.</p>
<p>The insurer does not pay for "betterment." DOBI's example: if a five-year-old transmission is replaced with a new one, you pay the difference in value.</p>
<h3>Rental car and storage</h3>
<p>With the other driver's insurance company, ask about a rental car when you first report. It must tell you how much it would allow, and must commit once its driver is 50% or more at fault. If it offers a flat daily amount, it must tell you where you can rent for that amount. Payment runs until the repair is done, or until a total loss claim settles. If your car is safe to drive, rental is paid only while the car is in the shop.</p>
<p>Storage fees add up daily. DOBI says the insurance company must give you 3 working days' notice before it stops paying storage, so you can move the car.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">own insurer guide</a>, questions 6, 7, 9 to 12; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">other insurer guide</a>, questions 9, 10, 12 to 15. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If the car you were driving was a rental, see the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-rental-car-accident-claim-support-guide">New Jersey rental car accident claim guide</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="total-loss">
<h2>When the insurance company says your car is a total loss</h2>
<p>A car is a total loss when the repair cost equals or exceeds its actual cash value (ACV) on the date of the loss. ACV is what the car would have sold for that day. DOBI notes insurers often total a car at 80% of ACV, because hidden damage often appears once repairs start.</p>
<p>Each insurance company must use one of three valuation methods approved by the Commissioner for all its total loss claims:</p>
<ul>
<li>the average retail value of substantially similar cars in two named used-car price guides;</li>
<li>a dealer quote for a substantially similar car within 25 miles of where your car is normally kept; or</li>
<li>an approved valuation service, such as a computer database of fair market values.</li>
</ul>
<p>The insurer must tell you which method it used. It must also give you an itemized list of all additions, deductions and sales tax.</p>

<section id="diminished-value">
<h2>After the repair: questions about diminished value</h2>
<p>Some owners feel a repaired car is worth less because of the car accident. Neither DOBI vehicle claim guide covers diminished value, and this page does not state a New Jersey rule for it. What DOBI does say is narrower. Your own insurer must restore the car to its condition before the loss. The other driver's insurer pays only to the extent its driver was legally responsible.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">own insurer guide</a>, introduction and question 11; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">other insurer guide</a>, introduction and question 14. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If you still plan to raise a value question, keep it specific and backed by records:</p>
<ul>
<li>Say exactly what the concern is. An unfinished repair, a total loss value and value after a finished repair are different questions.</li>
<li>Keep the estimate, the final repair invoice and any written assessment of the finished work.</li>
<li>Record the car's condition before the car accident: trim, mileage, options and any earlier damage.</li>
<li>Do not treat a listing price, an offer for your car and a completed sale as the same evidence.</li>
<li>Before paying for an appraisal, ask what method it uses, what it costs and which question it answers.</li>
<li>Keep the insurance company's written reply, and note whether it disputes fault, coverage, the evidence or the amount.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section id="disputes">
<h2>If you disagree with the offer or the claim is denied</h2>
<p><strong>With your own insurance company,</strong> either side can request an appraisal under your policy. Usually you each pick and pay an appraiser, and they choose a neutral umpire whose cost you may split. A decision by any two of the three is binding.</p>
<p><strong>With either insurance company,</strong> you can file an appeal with the company's internal appeals panel. If you are still unhappy, you can ask DOBI's Insurance Claims Ombudsman to review the decision: 1-800-446-7467, ombudsman@dobi.nj.gov, or 20 West State Street, PO Box 472, Trenton NJ 08625-0472. Against the other driver's insurer, DOBI also lists claiming on your own policy and getting legal advice.</p>
<p><strong>If the other driver's insurer says its policy does not cover the car accident,</strong> DOBI says a Standard Policy holder can make an uninsured motorist claim with their own company, less a $500 deductible. If the other driver had too little coverage, an underinsured motorist claim may cover the rest, also less $500. A Basic Policy has no UM or UIM coverage. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claims in New Jersey</a>.</p>
<p><strong>Do not let the claim sit.</strong> DOBI says you must accept a final settlement or file a lawsuit within the statute of limitations, or you may lose the right to any settlement. This page does not state that period for vehicle damage. Ask a New Jersey lawyer about your dates.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">own insurer guide</a>, questions 20 to 22; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">other insurer guide</a>, questions 5, 6, 22 and 23; NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a> (no UM/UIM on the Basic Policy). Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="if-hurt">
<h2>If you were also hurt: keep the car claim separate</h2>
<p>Your medical bills go through your own PIP, whoever caused the car accident. A claim against the other driver for an injury depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. A check for your car does not decide any of that.</p>
<p>Before you sign anything to close the car claim, read which losses and which people the paper releases. If it mentions injury claims, get advice first. The <a href="/new-jersey/nj-8-questions-to-ask-before-accepting-a-settlement-offer">questions to ask before accepting a settlement offer</a> can help.</p>
