<h1>How long car accident symptoms can take to show up, and what that means for a New Jersey claim</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>Some car accident symptoms start hours later, and some head injury symptoms can show up days or weeks later.</strong> There is no safe waiting period, so get medical attention when something new appears. In New Jersey, your own PIP coverage pays medical bills no matter who caused the car accident, so a late symptom does not need to wait for a fault decision.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NHS, <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/whiplash/">Whiplash</a> ("It can take several hours for the symptoms to start after you injure your neck"), page last reviewed 19 August 2026, retrieved 2026-11-10; MedlinePlus (U.S. National Library of Medicine), <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000799.htm">Traumatic brain injury</a> ("Some symptoms may not occur right away and show up a few days or weeks later"), retrieved 2026-11-10; NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice. It does not diagnose any injury. A clinician decides what your symptoms mean.</p>
</section>

<h3>Danger signs that need care right away</h3>
<p>After a head injury, MedlinePlus says to seek medical care right away for changes in alertness, confusion that does not go away, seizures, weakness on one or both sides of the body, unequal pupils, repeated vomiting, or walking and balance problems. After a neck injury, the NHS lists tingling or pins and needles, weak hands, arms or legs, and problems walking or sitting upright as signs that need urgent help. In an emergency, call 911. Do not wait for an insurance company to approve care.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000799.htm">MedlinePlus: Traumatic brain injury</a>, emergency symptoms list; <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/whiplash/">NHS: Whiplash</a>, "Urgent advice" list; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<section id="nj-claim">
<h2>Why a late symptom matters for a New Jersey car accident claim</h2>
<p>New Jersey has two separate tracks after a car accident. Your own PIP pays your medical bills regardless of fault. A claim against the driver who caused the car accident, for pain and suffering, depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. A late symptom touches both.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 2 and 10 to 11, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>PIP pays for the treatment, within your policy's choices</h3>
<p>DOBI calls PIP "no-fault" coverage because it "pays your own medical expenses no matter who caused the auto accident." The standard PIP medical deductible is $250, and you pay a 20% co-payment on expenses between the deductible and $5,000. Many drivers carry $250,000 of PIP medical coverage, but lower limits of $150,000, $75,000, $50,000 or $15,000 can be chosen.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 2, 5 to 7 and 12, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If you chose your health insurer as primary for car accident injuries, your health plan pays first. Its deductibles, co-pays, limits and pre-certification rules then apply to the late visit, and PIP pays second.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>The PIP time limit is tied to knowing the accident caused the expense</h3>
<p>A lawsuit for PIP benefits generally must start within 2 years after you have a loss or expense and know, or reasonably should know, that the accident caused it. The outer limit is 4 years after the accident, whichever comes first. If PIP has already paid benefits, a suit for more benefits can start within 2 years of the last payment. This PIP clock is separate from the clock for an injury lawsuit against the other driver.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-13.1(a), text as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7403318/aponte-correa-v-allstate-insurance/">Aponte-Correa v. Allstate Ins. Co. (App. Div. 1999)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. The current text was not read from the NJ Legislature site; the NJ Supreme Court's 2026 summary in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">Murray v. Punina</a> is consistent. Exceptions exist, including one for future treatment the insurer knew would probably be needed.</p>
<p>The injury lawsuit against an at-fault driver has its own rule: it must be filed within two years after the cause of action accrued. Claims against a public entity, such as a town or state vehicle, need a written notice within 90 days. These rules depend on your facts, so do not work out a date from this page alone.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260930102816/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-14-2/">2025 edition via Justia, archived 2026-09-30</a> (secondary host); N.J.S.A. 59:8-8 as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Auth., 236 N.J. 335 (2019)</a>; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>The lawsuit option decides what medical proof a pain and suffering claim needs</h3>
<p>If your policy has the limitation on lawsuit option, you can claim pain and suffering only for certain injuries. These include death, dismemberment, significant disfigurement or scarring, displaced fractures, loss of a fetus, or a permanent injury. Under the statute, an injury counts as permanent when the body part or organ has not healed to work normally and will not do so with more medical treatment. A physician must certify it based on objective clinical evidence. The option does not limit claims for economic loss, such as uncompensated medical bills or lost wages. With the no limitation option, you can claim pain and suffering for any injury.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8(a) and (b), <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">2025 edition via Justia, archived 2026-05-01</a> (secondary host); DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 10 to 11; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>No type of injury, such as a neck sprain or a disc injury, qualifies or fails automatically. Records still matter. In one 2025 unpublished Appellate Division decision, the claim failed "without any competent and admissible evidence showing permanence of his alleged injuries or causally linking them to the accident." A late symptom with a clear, honest record of when it started and when it was treated is easier for a clinician to assess.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/appellate-division-unpublished/2025/a-0609-24.html">Orlando v. Williams, A-0609-24 (App. Div. 2025, unpublished)</a>, quoted from the coded appeals table dated 2026-11-05. Unpublished opinions do not bind other courts; it illustrates the evidence point only. More rulings: <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">13 NJ verbal threshold rulings and why 7 failed</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="next-steps">
<h2>What to do when a symptom shows up days later</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Get medical attention.</strong> Tell the clinician the symptom started after a car accident, when you first noticed it, and any similar problem you had before.</li>
<li><strong>Keep four times separate in your own notes.</strong> Use the table below. Do not invent a precise time, and mark any entry you write later as written later.</li>
<li><strong>Tell your insurance company you are getting treatment.</strong> Your own PIP carrier handles the medical bills, whoever caused the car accident.</li>
<li><strong>Check your Coverage Selection Form.</strong> It shows your PIP limit, whether your health insurer is primary, and your lawsuit option.</li>
<li><strong>Get the police report.</strong> It records the date, place and people involved. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey car accident report</a>.</li>
</ol>

<h3>If you said you were fine at the scene</h3>
<p>Many people say "I'm okay" right after a car accident. Do not change that earlier statement. Explain what you meant at the time and what you noticed afterward. Do not claim a symptom started immediately if it did not. If an adjuster asks, the same rule applies: describe the timeline as it happened. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="whiplash">
<h2>If a clinician's note says "whiplash"</h2>
<p>The NHS describes whiplash as a neck injury caused by sudden movement of the head, most often after a traffic accident. A word in a visit note is the clinician's assessment. Your own notes are your description. Keep them separate, and do not label a symptom yourself before it is assessed.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/whiplash/">NHS: Whiplash</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
