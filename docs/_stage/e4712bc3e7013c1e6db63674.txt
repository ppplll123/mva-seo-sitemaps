<h3>Most of these deaths happened after dark</h3>
<p>Of the 18 pedestrians killed, 12 died in accidents recorded as dark, and in 11 of those the street lights were on. Five died in daylight.</p>
<p>Route 37 had 6 of the 18 deaths and Route 166 had 3, all in 2024 and 2025. Hooper Avenue (Route 549) and Fischer Boulevard (the Route 549 Spur) had 2 each.</p>
<h3>Does PIP cover you if a car hits you while walking?</h3>
<p>Usually yes. PIP on your own or a household member's auto policy pays, even though no car of yours was involved. <section id="rear-end">

<p>Yes. Rear end collisions were 36 percent of Toms River injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 (492 of 1,370), against 30 percent in Ocean County and 31 percent statewide.</p>

<section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us.</strong> They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Toms River. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></li>
</ul>
