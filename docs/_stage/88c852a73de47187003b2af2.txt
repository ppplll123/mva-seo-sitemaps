<h1>Car accidents in Toms River, NJ: where people are hurt and killed</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">This page is not live news, so it will not show an accident from today or last night. It covers police-reported car accidents in Toms River Township from 2023 to 2025.</p>
  <p>In 2023 and 2024, police reported 6,107 car accidents in Toms River. Someone was hurt or killed in 1,370 of them, about 1.9 a day. In total, 1,915 people were hurt and 17 died.</p>
  <p>What sets Toms River apart is who dies. Pedestrians were 18 of the 34 people killed here from 2023 to 2025. That is about half of all pedestrian deaths in Ocean County over the same years (18 of 35).</p>

<section id="pedestrian-deaths">
  <h2>Why are so many people killed walking in Toms River?</h2>
  <p>Pedestrian accidents are a small share of injury accidents here, but a large share of deaths. In 2023 and 2024, accidents involving a pedestrian were 4 percent of Toms River accidents where someone was hurt (53 of 1,370). Yet pedestrians were 8 of the 17 people killed.</p>

<section id="police-report">
  <h2>Getting the police report for a Toms River car accident</h2>
  <p>Which office has your report depends on which police agency came to the scene.</p>
  <h3>Toms River Police Department</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Reports come from the department's Records Division. It does not give out report information over the phone.</li>
    <li>You can get a copy in person with proper identification. The department suggests waiting 3 business days, because officers' reports are reviewed and approved first.</li>
    <li>Certain reports can be requested by email at PoliceRecords@trpolice.org, with a copy of your driver's license.</li>
    <li>By mail, send a written request with a $5.00 fee payable to "Toms River Township" and a self-addressed stamped envelope to: Toms River Police Department, Records Division, PO Box 876, Toms River, NJ 08754.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://trpolice.org/administration-bureau/records/police-reports-investigation-and-accident-reports/">Toms River Police Department, Police Reports / Investigation and Accident Reports</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). The department's site is linked from the <a href="https://www.tomsrivertownship.com/Directory.aspx">Toms River Township directory</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <h3>New Jersey State Police, including the Garden State Parkway</h3>
  <p>If State Police handled the accident, request the report through the NJSP crash report portal. It covers the Garden State Parkway and non-toll roads that State Police patrol. A toll road report costs $5.00 and a non-toll road report $13.00. You can download it once it is ready and approved, and reports stay available for six years from the year of the accident.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10), as summarized in research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8.</p>
  <p>For other agencies, our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">guide to New Jersey accident reports, agency by agency</a> covers the other options.</p>
  <p>Toms River makes up 23 percent of Ocean County's injury accidents. For the rest of the county, see <a href="/new-jersey/ocean-county-car-accident-help">Ocean County car accident help</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Toms River. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

  <h2>How we counted</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Data: NJDOT crash records (2023 from the NJDOT per-table archive, 2024 and 2025 from the NJDOT Crash Data Portal), normalized by hudcostreets/nj-crashes, with fatal accidents supplemented from the NJ State Police fatal feed. File downloaded 2026-11-06.</li>

    <li>2023 and 2024 are complete. 2025 is preliminary; Toms River's 2025 record count is 98 percent of 2024 (ratio 0.983), so it is close to complete but can still change.</li>

    <li>Intersection counts use 2024 and 2025, because street naming changed between the 2023 and 2024 exports. Spelling variants are not merged, so they are a floor.</li>
    <li>These are counts, not risk rates. There is no traffic volume in this data, so a busy road will show more accidents.</li>
