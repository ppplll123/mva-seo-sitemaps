<section id="deaths">
<h2>How many people have died in Route 17 crashes?</h2>
<p>Eight people died in Route 17 crashes from 2023 to 2025, two of them in Ramsey.</p>
<p>Five of the 7 deaths in 2023 and 2024 came after dark on lit stretches, against about a quarter of injury crashes. Two of the 7 were walking.</p>
<p>North Arlington had 13 of the 19 Route 17 crashes that hit someone on foot; no other town had more than two. <section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported crashes from NJDOT records, counted by us.</strong> They show where and when people were hurt on Route 17. They do not show who was at fault in any crash, and they cannot tell you whether you have a case or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own car insurance pays your doctor bills first, whoever caused the crash. This part of your policy is called PIP, and you will see the word on your bills. It pays up to the limit you picked, after your deductible and co-pay. If you picked your health insurance to pay first, it does, and PIP pays second. Being paid for your pain by the other driver is separate: it depends on who was at fault and on the choice about suing that you made when you bought your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></li>
<li>If they say it was partly your fault, you can still be paid, as long as you were not more at fault than the other driver (people call this the "51 percent" rule). What you get is cut by your share of the blame. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></li>
<li>If anyone is hurt or killed, or one person's car or property has more than $500 of damage, the driver must call the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless an officer writes one. If no officer came, the state's SR-1 form is the one to send. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></li>
<li>You usually have two years to sue for an injury. If a town, the state or NJ Transit was involved, you must send them a written notice within 90 days. There are exceptions, so check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></li>
</ul>
<p>Rules for the whole state have their own pages: <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">how no-fault PIP pays your medical bills</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what happens if you were partly at fault</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">the deadline to file an injury lawsuit</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">when and how to report a crash</a>. This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
