<section id="people-on-foot">
<h2>How often are pedestrians hit in Elizabeth?</h2>
<p>About every two days: 351 pedestrian crashes in 2023 and 2024, 41 percent of Union County's 847, far above the city's share of all injury accidents.</p>
<p>315 of those crashes hurt or killed someone, 13.8 percent of the city's injury accidents. The share is 8.2 percent in Union County and 6.6 percent statewide. 131 of the 351 were recorded in the dark.</p>
<p>198 were on city streets, 43 on Route 27 and 37 on Route 439.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<p><strong>Does PIP cover you as a pedestrian?</strong> Usually PIP on your own or a household member's auto policy pays the medical bills, even though no car of yours was involved. See <a href="/new-jersey/pedestrian-crash-new-jersey">what to do after a pedestrian crash in New Jersey</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/hit-by-car-pedestrian-cyclist-nj-appeals">NJ appeals cases on pedestrians hit by cars</a>.</p>

<section id="your-report">
<h2>How do I look up an Elizabeth, NJ accident report?</h2>
<p>Check the card or case number the officer gave you: Elizabeth police reports go on CrashDocs, State Police reports on the state portal.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>Elizabeth Police Department.</strong> Once a traffic supervisor approves the NJTR-1, it is free on CrashDocs in 3 to 5 business days. Enter your name and email, and the report is emailed to you.</li>
<li><strong>Turnpike, Route 81 and I-278.</strong> These are mostly State Police cases. Request them on the shared NJSP and Turnpike Authority crash report site with the case number from the trooper.</li>
<li><strong>A fatal Turnpike crash.</strong> That NJTR-1 comes through the OPRA form on the Turnpike Authority's website. Report questions go to 732-442-8600 ext. 2431.</li>
<li><strong>Other city records.</strong> The Records Division at 1 Police Plaza (908-558-2000) handles OPRA requests.</li>
</ol>
<p>The city's online reporting form is not for collisions: it requires "no injured persons" and excludes motor vehicle collisions.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

<p>The basic crash report is a public record. Fees, the SR-1 self-report, and what happens if you miss the 10-day deadline are on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="deaths">

<p><strong class="df-k">24 people died in Elizabeth crashes in 2023 and 2024, and 11 of them were on foot.</strong> Statewide, pedestrians were 409 of 1,294 deaths, about 32 percent.</p>
<p>Route 1 had 8 deaths, more than any other road, and the Turnpike 1. 15 of the 24 deaths happened in the dark, as did 37 percent of all injury accidents (854), against 28 percent statewide.</p>

<section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us.</strong> They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Elizabeth. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></li>
</ul>
