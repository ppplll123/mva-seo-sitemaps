<h1>New Jersey PIP Coverage Limits: Medical Bills After a Car Accident</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>In New Jersey, your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills after a car accident, no matter who caused it, up to the medical limit you chose.</strong> On a standard policy that limit can be $15,000, $50,000, $75,000, $150,000 or $250,000 (some insurers sell more), and most drivers pick $250,000. You pay a deductible ($250 unless you chose a higher one) plus a 20% co-pay on bills between the deductible and $5,000.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 7 and 12 (Policy Options Chart); DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If your bills go past your PIP limit, or PIP does not cover them, you can claim those unpaid medical expenses from the driver who caused the car accident. That is a separate, fault-based claim.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), quoting N.J.S.A. 39:6A-12 as amended in 2019, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">courtlistener.com</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>PIP pays your medical bills either way. If someone else caused the car accident, the questionnaire asks whether the other driver's insurance company may also owe you, for example for bills above your limit or for pain and suffering.</p>

<section id="what-pip-covers">
<h2>What New Jersey PIP covers</h2>
<p>PIP has two parts. The first is medical expense coverage: treatment from hospitals, doctors and other medical providers, and medical equipment needed to treat your injuries. The second is additional PIP coverage for some other losses because you are hurt.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<p>New Jersey also sets payment rules for PIP medical care. DOBI publishes an automobile medical fee schedule with separate exhibits for home care services, ambulance services and durable medical equipment. A fee schedule sets how much a listed service is paid. It does not mean every listed service is approved for every patient.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/aicrapg.htm">PIP Information for Health Care Providers</a> (fee schedule exhibits 3, 4 and 5), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 12 (Policy Options Chart), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Do not assume you have $250,000. Many people choose a lower limit to save on the premium. The limit that counts is the one on your policy on the date of the car accident.</p>
<h3>The $250,000 rule for certain serious injuries</h3>
<p>Even with a lower limit, the standard policy still pays up to $250,000 for certain injuries. These are permanent or significant brain injury, spinal cord injury or disfigurement. They also include medically necessary treatment of other permanent or significant injuries at a trauma center or acute care hospital right after the accident, until the patient is stable. Those payments reduce the lower limit you chose. The Basic Policy has a $15,000 PIP limit with the same up-to-$250,000 rule for these injuries.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 5 (Standard and Basic policy chart and footnote); DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/standardpolicy.html">Standard Policy page</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="deductible-copay">
<h2>How the PIP deductible and 20% co-pay work</h2>
<p>You pay the deductible first. Then, whatever deductible you chose, you pay 20% of medical expenses between the deductible and $5,000, and the insurance company pays 80%. Above $5,000, the insurance company pays the bills up to your limit. DOBI gives this example for $10,000 of medical expenses.</p>

</thead>

<tr><th scope="row">Jane</th><td>$2,500</td><td>$500 (20% of $2,500)</td><td>$3,000</td><td>$7,000</td></tr>

<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 7 (Personal Injury Protection example), retrieved 2026-11-10. DOBI notes Jane's $2,500 deductible came with a 25% PIP premium reduction in its example. That is DOBI's example, not a promised saving.</p>
<p>Under the co-pay rule, your share of covered bills inside your limit is your deductible plus 20% of the gap between it and $5,000. With the $250 deductible that is $1,200.</p>
<p>If you chose your health plan as primary, its deductibles and co-pays apply instead, and PIP pays second. Our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">guide on whether PIP or health insurance pays first</a> explains that choice.</p>
</section>

<section id="limit-vs-balance">
<h2>Your limit, what PIP has paid and your bill are three different numbers</h2>
<p>A provider balance does not prove your PIP has run out. One hospital visit can also produce several bills, for example from the facility, the doctor and imaging. Ask which number someone is quoting before you act on it.</p>
<div class="table-wrap">
<div class="l2-tablewrap" tabindex="0" role="region" aria-label="Scrollable table"><table>

</section>

<section id="treatment-review">
<h2>How PIP reviews treatment</h2>
<p>Your doctor asks the PIP insurance company to approve some treatment through decision point review and precertification. Providers treating PIP patients must use a standard form, the Uniform Attending Provider Treatment Plan Form, for these requests. DOBI also publishes uniform internal appeal forms.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/aicrapg.htm">PIP Information for Health Care Providers</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>An unresolved PIP dispute can go to binding dispute resolution under the policy. DOBI's current administrator is Forthright, which runs the <a href="https://www.nj-no-fault.com/">New Jersey No-Fault PIP Arbitration Program</a>. A provider filing for its own bill must first finish the insurance company's internal appeal. PIP arbitration is not the process for a claim against the other driver.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.A.C. 11:3-5 (2013 text posted by DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/njac11_3_5_effect130104.pdf">nj.gov/dobi</a>; N.J.A.C. 11:3-4.7B as described there; Forthright, <a href="https://www.nj-no-fault.com/">nj-no-fault.com</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Treatment decisions belong to your doctor, not to a payment record. Never delay emergency care while a claim is reviewed. Published appeal decisions are summarized in our page on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-pip-appeals-decisions-permanent-injury">New Jersey PIP denial appeals</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="above-the-limit">
<h2>Medical bills above your PIP limit</h2>
<p>Since a 2019 change to the law, medical expenses that go beyond your PIP limit, or that PIP does not cover, can be claimed from everyone legally responsible for the car accident. Before that change, the Supreme Court's Haines v. Taft decision had blocked this for people with lower limits. Amounts PIP has paid or can pay are kept out of the injury case, so you are not paid twice.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), quoting N.J.S.A. 39:6A-12 and describing L. 2019, cc. 244 and 245, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">courtlistener.com</a>; Haines v. Taft, 237 N.J. 271 (2019), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8269493/haines-v-taft/">courtlistener.com</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>In May 2026 the New Jersey Supreme Court added a limit to this. Future medical costs that will not go past your remaining PIP limit count as "collectible" under PIP. They must be pursued through PIP, not in the case against the other driver.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">Murray v. Punina (N.J. 2026)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>The lawsuit option on your policy does not stop this kind of claim. The limitation on lawsuit option only limits claims for pain and suffering. Claims for economic losses, such as unpaid medical expenses and lost wages, are not limited by it. Read more about the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">limitation on lawsuit option</a>.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8, 2025 edition via Justia (secondary), <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">archived copy</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/standardpolicy.html">Standard Policy page</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>A claim against the other driver depends on fault. In New Jersey you can still recover if your share of fault is not greater than the other driver's, but the award is reduced by your share. See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what happens when you are partly at fault</a>. If the other driver had no insurance or too little, read about <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claims</a>.</p>

<section id="next-steps">
<h2>What to do next</h2>
<ol>
<li>Find your declarations page and write down your PIP medical limit, deductible and whether you have the Extra PIP Package.</li>
<li>Give every provider your PIP claim number, and ask them to bill the PIP insurance company (or your health plan, if you chose it as primary).</li>
<li>Ask the insurance company for a written record of what PIP has paid so far.</li>
<li>If a bill is denied, get the written reason and ask your provider about the internal appeal.</li>
<li>Get a copy of your <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey car accident report</a>. You will need it for any claim against the other driver.</li>
</ol>

<section id="faq">
<h2>Questions people ask about New Jersey PIP</h2>
<h3>How much PIP coverage do I need in NJ?</h3>
<p>No single amount fits everyone. DOBI says most New Jersey drivers buy $250,000, and lower limits cost less. Before choosing your health plan as primary, DOBI suggests checking whether your health coverage pays for auto accident injuries.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>Is personal injury protection the same as bodily injury coverage?</h3>
<p>No. PIP pays your own medical expenses no matter who caused the accident. Bodily injury liability pays claims by other people who are hurt in an accident you cause, including pain and suffering and lost wages.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>What happens if you don't have PIP insurance?</h3>
<p>People with no auto insurance in their household may be able to seek PIP benefits from the Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund, run by NJ PLIGA. Eligibility rules apply.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">Murray v. Punina (N.J. 2026)</a>, describing N.J.S.A. 39:6-86.1, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>How long does PIP pay for treatment in NJ?</h3>
<p>PIP is capped by your dollar limit, and treatment goes through the insurance company's review rules. A lawsuit for PIP benefits has its own time rule. It is generally two years after you have the expense and know it came from the accident, or four years after the accident if that is earlier. If PIP has paid benefits, a suit for more benefits can be started within two years of the last payment. Courts also allow some claims for expected future treatment within a reasonable time. These rules depend on your facts, so do not count on a date without advice.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-13.1(a) as quoted in Aponte-Correa v. Allstate Ins. Co. (App. Div. 1999), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7403318/aponte-correa-v-allstate-insurance/">courtlistener.com</a>; <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">Murray v. Punina (N.J. 2026)</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10. Current statute text not read from the Legislature site.</p>
