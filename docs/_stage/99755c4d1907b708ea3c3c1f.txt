<h1>What to expect from an insurance adjuster after a New Jersey car accident</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>An adjuster investigates your claim and decides what their company will pay, so first find out which company they work for.</strong> Your own insurance company's adjuster handles your PIP medical bills no matter who caused the car accident. The other driver's adjuster works for their policyholder and pays only to the extent that driver was legally responsible.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2; DOBI Ombudsman, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">Filing an Auto Damage Claim with Another Insurer</a>, Q4. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>For a vehicle damage claim, New Jersey rules require the insurance company to contact you within 10 working days of being told about the loss. The adjuster will then ask for documents, look at the car, assign a percentage of fault and make a written offer or decision.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance</a>, pp. 24 to 25. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<p>If you were hurt, your PIP adjuster pays medical bills either way. The short quiz asks about your injury, fault, medical attention, police report and accident date. It checks whether a sponsoring lawyer may want to call you about a claim against the other driver's insurance company.</p>

<section id="which-adjuster">
<h2>Which adjuster is calling you?</h2>
<p>After one car accident you may hear from two or three adjusters. Each one handles a different coverage, and each has a different duty to you. Ask the caller for the company name, the claim number and which coverage the file is about.</p>

<section id="what-they-investigate">
<h2>What the adjuster investigates</h2>
<p>DOBI says the other driver's insurance company will investigate three things. It checks whether its driver is legally responsible and to what extent, how much your damages are, and whether your damages came from this car accident.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">Filing a Claim with Another Insurer</a>, Q3. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>To set fault, the adjuster reviews the police report, driver and witness statements, the motor vehicle laws that apply and the road layout. The result is a percentage, such as 80% or 50%. A traffic ticket for the other driver does not mean that driver is automatically 100% at fault.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Auto Comparative Negligence Settlement FAQs</a>, Q3 and Q4; DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">Filing a Claim with Another Insurer</a>, Q4. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>That percentage matters. You can collect from the other driver's insurance company only if your share of fault is not greater than that driver's share. In a two-car accident, 50% each still pays half, but 51% or more pays nothing from that insurer. Your payment is cut by your percentage, and that includes rental car costs.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1 (<a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">statute text, Justia 2025 edition, archived</a>); DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Comparative Negligence FAQs</a>, Q5. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>When your story and the other driver's story differ and there is no other persuasive evidence, DOBI warns that the insurer will defend its own policyholder. Evidence helps here. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-evidence-helps-prove-fault-after-an-nj-crash">what evidence helps prove fault</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how partial fault works in New Jersey</a>.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">Filing a Claim with Another Insurer</a>, Q4. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

<section id="recorded-statement">
<h2>Do you have to give the adjuster a recorded statement?</h2>
<p><strong>With the other driver's insurance company, no New Jersey law sets the information you must give.</strong> DOBI still says it is in your best interest to give enough information to support your claim. It warns that if you do not cooperate fully with the investigation, the insurer could deny your claim.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">Filing a Claim with Another Insurer</a>, Q3. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p><strong>With your own insurance company, your policy sets the rules.</strong> DOBI tells you to cooperate with your insurer's investigator and to read the "Conditions" or "Insured's Duties After a Loss" section of your policy. Your company can ask for a proof of loss. In some cases it can require an examination under oath, and you have the right to have a lawyer present.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">Filing a Claim with Your Own Insurer</a>, Q2 and Q3. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Before any statement, ask who will hear it, what it will cover and whether written answers or documents would do. Say only what you know. Do not guess about speed, distances or your injuries. If you have not had medical attention yet, say so rather than saying you are fine.</p>
</section>

<section id="injury-claim">
<h2>What changes when you were hurt</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Medical bills go to your PIP adjuster first.</strong> PIP pays your own medical expenses regardless of fault, up to the limit you chose. Disputes over PIP benefits go through DOBI's PIP dispute resolution program, run by Forthright.</li>
<li><strong>Bills above PIP can be claimed against the at-fault driver.</strong> Uncompensated medical expenses beyond or outside your PIP limit can be claimed against the liable parties. Amounts PIP will pay cannot.</li>
<li><strong>Pain and suffering depends on your lawsuit option.</strong> Under the limitation on lawsuit option, you need a qualifying injury, such as a permanent injury, to recover for pain and suffering. Economic losses are not limited this way.</li>
<li><strong>The other driver had no insurance or not enough.</strong> On a standard policy, you can make an uninsured or underinsured motorist claim with your own insurance company.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 2, 8, 10 to 11; N.J.A.C. 11:3-5 (<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/njac11_3_5_effect130104.pdf">DOBI text</a>) and <a href="https://www.nj-no-fault.com/">Forthright NJ No-Fault PIP Arbitration</a>; Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">opinion</a>; N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8 (<a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">statute text, Justia 2025 edition, archived</a>); DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk2.htm">Filing a Claim with Another Insurer</a>, Q5 and Q6. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Keep copies of every bill and record you send. See <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">medical records for an injury claim</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/nj-10-ways-to-prove-pain-and-suffering-after-an-accident">proving pain and suffering</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claims</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="offer-and-time">
<h2>When the offer arrives, and how long you have</h2>
<p>A car repair estimate is not an injury offer. If a written offer arrives, check which losses and which people it would settle. Use the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-8-questions-to-ask-before-accepting-a-settlement-offer">questions to ask before accepting a settlement offer</a>.</p>
<p>A claim does not stay open forever. DOBI says you must either accept a final settlement or file a lawsuit within the statute of limitations. For most injury lawsuits in New Jersey that is two years, but some deadlines are shorter. A claim against a public entity, such as a town vehicle, needs a notice within 90 days. Your own deadline depends on your facts.</p>

</section>

<section id="disagree">
<h2>If you disagree with the adjuster</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Raise it with the adjuster and their supervisor.</strong> Put your reasons and your evidence in writing.</li>
<li><strong>File an internal appeal.</strong> Every New Jersey auto insurance company must have an Internal Appeal Process. It must tell you about your right to appeal and where to send it.</li>
<li><strong>Ask the DOBI Office of the Insurance Claims Ombudsman to review it.</strong> After the appeal, the company must tell you about this right. Phone 1-800-446-7467, email ombudsman@dobi.nj.gov, or write to PO Box 472, Trenton, NJ 08625-0472.</li>
<li><strong>Know that a court has the last word.</strong> DOBI says only a judge or jury can finally decide fault and what is owed. An appeal or a complaint does not take away your right to sue.</li>
</ol>

<section id="faq">
<h2>Questions people ask about adjusters</h2>
<h3>Should I give a recorded statement to the other insurance company?</h3>
<p>No New Jersey law sets what you must tell the other driver's insurance company. DOBI says sharing enough to support your claim is in your interest, and that not cooperating could lead to a denial. You can ask to answer in writing and stick to facts you know.</p>
<h3>Why is my insurance asking for a recorded statement?</h3>
<p>Your policy requires you to cooperate with your own insurance company's investigation. DOBI says it can ask for a proof of loss and, in some cases, an examination under oath with your lawyer present. Check the duties section of your policy.</p>
<h3>Can I refuse a recorded statement to an insurance company?</h3>
<p>It depends on whose company it is. Refusing your own insurer can break your policy duties and risk a denial. With the other driver's insurer there is no set list, but DOBI warns that not cooperating fully could also lead to a denial.</p>
<h3>Should I give the other driver my insurance information?</h3>
<p>Yes. DOBI tells drivers to exchange insurance information after a car accident, including the company and policy number for each vehicle, and to report the crash to the police.</p>
