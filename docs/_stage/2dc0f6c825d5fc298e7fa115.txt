<h1>Hurt driving your own uninsured car in NJ: can you sue?</h1><nav class="df-jump" aria-label="Jump to" data-df="added"><span>Jump to:</span><a href="#sources">Sources</a></nav>
<p class="byline">By Sandra H. Brown, Injuries Cases Autos research. Updated <time datetime="2026-11-11">11 November 2026</time></p>
<section id="answer" class="df-answer">
<p class="df-lead">Usually not. If you were hurt while operating your own uninsured car, New Jersey's uninsured-owner bar stops an injury suit against the other driver, even one who caused the crash. The bar is narrow: in a 2023 appeal it did not apply because the owner was no longer operating the car.</p>
<aside class="key-facts"><ul>
<li data-origin="original"><strong>2</strong> of the 76 NJ appeals we coded asked whether a hurt owner was statutorily barred from suing: one barred, one allowed</li>
<li data-origin="original"><strong>2,934</strong> drivers hurt in NJ "struck parked vehicle" crashes in 2023 and 2024, 79 percent of the people hurt in them</li>
<li data-origin="original"><strong>93,593</strong> drivers hurt in NJ police-reported crashes in 2023 and 2024, insured or not</li>
<li><strong>$250,000</strong>: the most the state Fund pays in medical benefits per person per accident</li>
</ul></aside>
</section>

<section id="drive-without-insurance">
<h2>What happens if I drive without insurance in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">On the injury side, you lose the right to sue a driver who hits you.</strong> DOBI, the state insurance regulator, puts it in one line: "if you lack coverage and someone hits you, you cannot sue."</p>
<p>The rule sits in N.J.S.A. 39:6A-4.5(a). As the Appellate Division read it in 2023, it reaches injuries suffered while operating the uninsured car, not every injury an uninsured owner suffers.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>
<p>Fines, license suspension and registration penalties are set by the Motor Vehicle Commission, and our files do not hold those amounts. Check them with the <a href="https://nj.gov/mvc/">NJ Motor Vehicle Commission</a>.</p>
<p>The same law has a separate drunk-driving bar, covered on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-drunk-driving-accident-help">drunk driving accident help in New Jersey</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="appeals">
<h2>Does the bar apply if you were not driving at the time?</h2>
<p>Not always. The two appeals we found point in opposite directions, and the difference is what the owner was doing when hurt.</p>

Both cases sit in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-appeals-index">New Jersey car accident appeals index</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="no-pip">
<h2>What happens if you don't have PIP insurance?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">People with no auto insurance anywhere in their household may seek PIP from the Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund, run by NJ PLIGA.</strong> Its medical expense benefits reach up to $250,000 per person per accident.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>
<p>Our sources describe the Fund for households with no auto insurance. They do not say whether an owner hurt while driving his own uninsured car qualifies, so ask PLIGA before counting on it.</p>
<p>How the Fund fits next to health insurance is on <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP, health insurance and medical bills</a>. An uninsured person hit on foot is a different case, covered on <a href="/new-jersey/hit-by-car-pedestrian-cyclist-nj-appeals">NJ appeals where people were hit on foot or by bike</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="um">
<h2>Do you need uninsured motorist coverage in NJ?</h2>
<p>It is required on a Standard Policy, but it does not pay the uninsured driver, so it cannot help here; the coverage rules are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claim help</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>
<p>If you caused the crash and fear being sued yourself, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-basic-policy-vs-standard-policy-after-a-crash">Basic Policy vs Standard Policy after a crash</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="drivers-hurt">
<h2>How many NJ drivers are hurt in crashes each year?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">These counts cover every hurt driver, insured or not.</strong> Police recorded 93,593 drivers hurt in 2023 and 2024, about 47,000 a year and 63 percent of everyone hurt whose role was coded.</p>

<section id="steps">
<h2>What should you do if you were hurt driving uninsured?</h2>
<p>Pin down the facts the cases turned on before anyone tells you there is no claim.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Get the police report and check what it says you were doing. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your NJ accident report</a>.</li>
<li>Write down where you were when hurt: driving, parked, or outside the car, as in Memudu.</li>
<li>List every auto policy in your household. The Fund route depends on there being none.</li>
<li>Ask NJ PLIGA whether the Fund will take your PIP claim.</li>
<li>Note the two-year injury suit deadline; the exceptions are on the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">NJ car accident statute of limitations</a> page.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></li>
</ol>
<p>More guides are on the <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident help hub</a>.</p>
