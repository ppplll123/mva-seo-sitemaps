<section id="possible-injury">
<h2>Why do so many crash injuries look minor at the scene?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Because many symptoms have not started yet.</strong> Whiplash can take several hours to begin, and some brain injury symptoms appear days or weeks later.</p>
<p>In our count of NJDOT crash records, 80,089 of the 154,156 people recorded as injured in 2023 and 2024 got the mildest grade, "possible injury". Another 60,026, or 38.9%, were suspected minor injury, and only 7,399 suspected serious.</p>
<p>Rear-end crashes lean even milder at the scene: 60% of the people injured in them were graded "possible injury". Outside rear-end crashes the share was 48.1%: 50,831 of 105,738 injured people. Only 811 rear-end victims were graded suspected serious.</p>

<p>The grade records what the officer saw that day. A symptom that starts the next morning is not on the report, so your medical record carries the timeline.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>

<section id="code-4">
<h2>What is a code 4 injury on a New Jersey crash report?</h2>
<p>Code 4 in the physical condition box is "possible injury", the grade most injured people in our count received.</p>
<p>It is the officer's view at the scene, not a diagnosis. Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">reading the codes on a New Jersey crash report</a> explains the injury box and the other grades, and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">getting your New Jersey car accident report</a> covers fees and portals.</p>
</section>
<section id="feel-worse">
<h2>Is it normal to feel worse days after a car accident?</h2>
<p>Yes, it can be: the health sources above describe symptoms that start hours to weeks after the injury.</p>
<p>Get medical care at once for confusion that does not go away, repeated vomiting, or weak or tingling arms or legs:</p>
<ul>
<li>call emergency services or go to an emergency room for those signs;</li>
<li>otherwise see your doctor or urgent care, and say the symptom began after a crash;</li>
<li>keep the date you first noticed it.</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="pip-late">
<h2>Does PIP still pay if pain starts days after the crash?</h2>
<p>Yes. PIP pays eligible bills whoever caused the crash, and the clock for a PIP suit starts when you know the accident caused the expense.</p>
<p>The exact PIP and injury suit deadlines are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey car accident deadlines</a>. If your health plan is primary, see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP versus health insurance for medical bills</a>; for the no-fault basics, read <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">whether New Jersey is a no-fault state</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="still-claim">
<h2>How long after a car accident can you still claim in NJ?</h2>
<p>Usually two years for an injury lawsuit against the driver who caused the crash.</p>
<p>A late symptom does not buy extra time on that suit, so do not work out a date yourself. The rules and exceptions are on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="threshold">
<h2>Can a symptom that started later still support a pain and suffering claim?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">It can, but the record has to tie it to the crash.</strong> In our coded appeals, at least 3 of the 7 failed threshold claims lost on cause, not severity.</p>
<p>In those cases the court found no medical proof linking the injury, or the worsening of an old condition, to the accident.</p>
<p>One 2025 claim failed for want of evidence "showing permanence of his alleged injuries or causally linking them to the accident." A dated record of when a late symptom began is that link.</p>
<p>The full case set is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">NJ verbal threshold rulings and why most failed</a>. How the lawsuit option shapes proof is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-10-ways-to-prove-pain-and-suffering-after-an-accident">ways to prove pain and suffering in New Jersey</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
