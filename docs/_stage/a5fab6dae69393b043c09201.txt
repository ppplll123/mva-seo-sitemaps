<h1>Can You Claim Lost Wages After a Car Accident in NJ</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>Yes, often by two routes.</strong> Your own auto policy's PIP may pay lost wages through its income continuation benefit, whoever caused the car accident, if your policy includes it. You may also claim lost wages from the driver who caused the crash, and the "limitation on lawsuit" option does not block that part of the claim.</p>
<p>The two routes do not pay the same money twice. Wages your PIP paid, or could have paid, are not claimed again from the other driver.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 2, 7 and 10 to 11; Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), quoting N.J.S.A. 39:6A-12, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">courtlistener.com</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

<section id="pip-income-continuation">
<h2>Route 1: PIP income continuation from your own policy</h2>
<p>New Jersey PIP has two parts. One pays medical bills. The other reimburses some other costs you have because you are hurt, such as lost wages and paying someone to care for your home or family.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>The lost-wage part is called income continuation. DOBI describes it this way: "If you cannot work due to accident-related injuries, this coverage pays lost wages, less Temporary Disability Benefits you may receive if your disability prevents you from working, up to the amount you select."</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 7 ("Extra PIP Package Coverage"), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Three points in that wording matter for your car accident:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>It is an extra you may not have.</strong> Income continuation is part of the "Extra PIP Package" on the Standard Policy. DOBI says you can decline the package, though most people choose it. DOBI also says you may buy both parts of PIP or medical coverage only.</li>
<li><strong>It is capped at the amount you selected.</strong> The limit is on your policy, not a fixed state figure. DOBI says packages may be available in higher amounts.</li>
<li><strong>Temporary Disability Benefits are subtracted.</strong> If you receive Temporary Disability Benefits because you cannot work, PIP pays lost wages less those benefits.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 7 and Policy Options Chart p. 12; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, PIP section. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>PIP pays "no matter who caused the auto accident." That is what "no-fault" means in New Jersey. It is not the same as being "not at fault" for the crash.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p><strong>Next step:</strong> find your policy declarations page or Coverage Selection Form. Check whether the Extra PIP Package is listed and what income continuation amount you chose. Then ask your own insurance company how to send proof of missed work. For how PIP limits and deductibles work, see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">what NJ PIP covers and its limits</a>. If you named your health plan as primary for medical bills, see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">who pays first when health insurance is primary</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="claim-against-driver">
<h2>Route 2: a claim against the driver who caused the car accident</h2>
<p>The at-fault driver's bodily injury liability coverage "compensates others for pain, suffering and economic damages, such as lost wages." So lost wages can be part of a claim against the other driver's insurance company.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>Your lawsuit option does not block a lost-wage claim</h3>
<p>Many New Jersey drivers have the "limitation on lawsuit" option, sometimes called the verbal threshold. It is also the default if no written choice was made. That option limits suits for pain and suffering unless the injury is on a listed type, such as a displaced fracture or a permanent injury. DOBI states that choosing it "does not affect your ability to sue for economic damages such as medical expenses and lost wages."</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 10 to 11 ("The Right to Sue"); N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8(a) and 39:6A-8.1(b), statute text via Justia (secondary), <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">39:6A-8 archived copy</a> and <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20240515013024/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8-1/">39:6A-8.1 archived copy</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>For the pain and suffering side of the threshold, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-10-ways-to-prove-pain-and-suffering-after-an-accident">how the lawsuit option affects pain and suffering claims</a>.</p>
<h3>What PIP paid is not claimed twice</h3>
<p>New Jersey law keeps amounts "collectible or paid" under PIP out of the injury case against the at-fault driver. In practice, the claim against the other driver is for wage loss your PIP did not cover. That includes losses above your income continuation limit, or all of it if your policy has no income continuation.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), quoting N.J.S.A. 39:6A-12, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">courtlistener.com</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>Your share of fault can reduce it</h3>
<p>Being partly at fault does not automatically end a claim. Under New Jersey's comparative negligence rule, you can recover if your fault is not greater than the other driver's. Your damages are then reduced by your percentage of fault. DOBI's example: 10% at fault on $1,000 of damage means $900 is paid.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1, statute text via Justia (secondary), <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">archived copy</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, pp. 26 to 27. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how partly at fault worked in real New Jersey cases</a>. If the other driver has too little insurance, your own underinsured motorist coverage may matter: see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claims in NJ</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="disputes-deadlines">
<h2>If PIP refuses, and the time limits</h2>
<p><strong>PIP disputes.</strong> Disputes over PIP benefits, not only medical bills, go through DOBI's PIP dispute resolution program. Forthright currently administers it at <a href="https://www.nj-no-fault.com/">nj-no-fault.com</a>. This program is not the route for a claim against the other driver.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.A.C. 11:3-5, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/njac11_3_5_effect130104.pdf">DOBI posted text</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/aicrapg.htm">PIP information page</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p><strong>PIP benefits clock.</strong> A suit for PIP benefits generally must start within two years after the loss and its link to the accident are known, or four years after the accident, whichever is earlier. If PIP has paid benefits, a suit for further benefits may start within two years of the last payment. There are exceptions.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-13.1(a), as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7403318/aponte-correa-v-allstate-insurance/">Aponte-Correa v. Allstate Ins. Co. (App. Div. 1999)</a>; see also Murray v. Punina (N.J. 2026). Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p><strong>Injury suit clock.</strong> A suit against the driver who caused the car accident generally must be filed within two years. If a public entity is involved, such as a town vehicle or NJ Transit, written notice is due within 90 days. These are separate clocks, and your facts decide which apply.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2, statute text via Justia (secondary), <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260930102816/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-14-2/">archived copy</a>; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8 as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Auth. (N.J. 2019)</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
