<h1>New Jersey Car Accident Medical Bills: PIP or Health Insurance First?</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>After a New Jersey car accident, your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills first, no matter who caused the crash.</strong> The exception is if you chose the "health insurer for PIP" option on your auto policy. Then your health insurance pays first and PIP pays second, for necessary costs your health plan does not cover.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>So the real question is which option you picked. The answer is on paperwork from your auto insurance company. The steps below show how to check it and what changes either way.</p>
<p>Your PIP or health plan handles your medical bills whoever was at fault. Our short quiz asks a few questions to see whether the other driver's insurance company may also owe you for losses those do not cover.</p>
<p><a class="button" data-quiz-link="mid" href="https://quiz.injuriescases.autos/s/accident-newjersey?utm_source=injuriescases.autos&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_campaign=nj_mva_seo&amp;utm_content=pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">Start the New Jersey accident quiz</a></p>
<p class="cta-note">Advertisement. Opens the New Jersey questionnaire. Review its policies and contact consent before submitting your details. No legal advice or guarantee of acceptance or compensation.</p>
</section>

<section id="default-pip-first">
<h2>The default: PIP pays first, whoever caused the car accident</h2>
<p>PIP stands for Personal Injury Protection. DOBI says it "is sometimes called 'no-fault' coverage because it pays your own medical costs no matter who caused the accident." It covers you and other people covered under your policy who are hurt in a car accident.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>"No-fault" here is about your own medical bills only. It is not the same as being "not at fault" for the crash. Whether you can also claim against the other driver depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide (2026)</a>, p. 2; Zabilowicz v. Kelsey, 200 N.J. 507 (2009), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1480699/zabilowicz-v-kelsey/">CourtListener</a>; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>The other driver's bodily injury liability coverage does not replace PIP for your treatment bills. DOBI describes liability coverage as paying claims by people hurt in a car accident the policyholder causes, for pain, suffering and economic losses such as lost wages.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide (2026)</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

<section id="health-primary-option">
<h2>The health insurer for PIP option: when health insurance pays first</h2>
<p>On a standard New Jersey auto policy, you can name your health insurer as the primary payer for car accident injuries. If you did, your health insurer pays first. Your auto insurer then provides secondary coverage through PIP.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide (2026)</a>, p. 7 ("Health Care Primary"); both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>DOBI lists what changes when health insurance is primary:</p>
<ul>
<li>Your health plan's deductibles, co-payments and coverage limits apply to car accident treatment.</li>
<li>Your health plan's pre-certification rules apply, so treatment may need its approval first.</li>
<li>Your auto insurer "will only pay for necessary expenses not covered by your health policy or plan."</li>
<li>Medicare and Medicaid cannot be chosen as the primary payer. They may pay on a secondary basis, for example when costs go above your PIP limit.</li>
<li>If your health coverage had ended by the time of the car accident, your auto insurer pays PIP medical benefits. You owe an additional $750 deductible.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, questions 2 to 5, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Do not assume your health insurance will pay just because you have it. DOBI tells drivers to check that their health plan covers injuries from car accidents before choosing this option. The answer depends on your plan.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, question 1, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="check-your-election">
<h2>How to check which option you chose</h2>
<p>The choice is made on the Coverage Selection Form your auto insurance company gives you when you buy or renew a policy. If you chose health insurance as primary, you had to give your health plan's name and policy or group number on that form.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, question 5, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<ol>
<li>Find your latest Coverage Selection Form, or ask your auto insurance company which PIP option is on your policy.</li>
<li>If health insurance is primary, call your health plan. Ask whether it covers car accident injuries and what approvals it needs before treatment.</li>
<li>Give every provider both cards and tell them which one is primary, so the bill goes to the right insurance company first.</li>
<li>Keep every bill, explanation of benefits and denial letter in date order. Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">medical records for an injury claim</a> covers what to collect.</li>
<li>Get a copy of the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey car accident report</a>. Insurance companies use it with other facts when they handle the claim.</li>
</ol>
</section>

<section id="beyond-pip">
<h2>What happens to bills PIP and health insurance do not pay</h2>
<p>Since a 2019 change to the law, medical expenses that go beyond your PIP limit, or that PIP does not cover, can be claimed against the people who caused the car accident. Amounts PIP pays or could pay cannot be claimed against the at-fault driver a second time.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), quoting N.J.S.A. 39:6A-12, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">CourtListener</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. The 2019 amendment (L. 2019, cc. 244 and 245) reversed Haines v. Taft, 237 N.J. 271 (2019), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8269493/haines-v-taft/">CourtListener</a>.</p>
<p>In May 2026 the New Jersey Supreme Court added a limit. Future medical costs that will not go above your remaining PIP limit count as "collectible" under PIP. They must be pursued through PIP, not in the injury case against the other driver.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">CourtListener</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>A claim against the other driver depends on fault. New Jersey reduces what you can recover by your own share of fault, and bars recovery only if your share is greater than the other side's. See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">partly at fault car accidents in New Jersey</a>. If the other driver had no insurance or too little, read about <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claims</a>.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1, via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, pp. 26 to 27; retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Claims for pain and suffering also depend on the lawsuit option on your policy. Our page on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">limitation on lawsuit appeals rulings</a> shows how courts have applied it.</p>
</section>

<section id="pip-disputes">
<h2>If PIP refuses or reduces a medical bill</h2>
<p>Disputes over PIP medical benefits go through New Jersey's PIP dispute resolution program, which is binding alternate dispute resolution. The state's designated administrator is Forthright, which runs the <a href="https://www.nj-no-fault.com/">NJ No-Fault PIP Arbitration Program</a>.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.A.C. 11:3-5, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/njac11_3_5_effect130104.pdf">text posted by DOBI</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/aicrapg.htm">PIP Information for Treating Health Care Providers</a>; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>When a provider brings the dispute as the assignee of your benefits, it must first finish the insurance company's internal appeal process. PIP arbitration is only for PIP benefits. It is not how you claim against the driver who caused the car accident.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.A.C. 11:3-5 (citing N.J.A.C. 11:3-4.7B), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/njac11_3_5_effect130104.pdf">text posted by DOBI</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Lawsuits for PIP benefits also have their own time limit, separate from the injury lawsuit clock. Ask a lawyer about the timing in your case rather than counting days yourself.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-13.1(a), as quoted in Aponte-Correa v. Allstate Ins. Co. (App. Div. 1999), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7403318/aponte-correa-v-allstate-insurance/">CourtListener</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="faq">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<h3>Is personal injury protection the same as bodily injury coverage?</h3>
<p>No. PIP pays your own medical costs no matter who caused the car accident. Bodily injury liability pays people hurt in a car accident the policyholder causes, including for pain, suffering and lost wages.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide (2026)</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>What happens if you don't have PIP insurance?</h3>
<p>If no one in your household has auto insurance, you may be able to seek PIP through the Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund, which NJ PLIGA runs. Whether you qualify depends on your facts.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), describing N.J.S.A. 39:6-86.1, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">CourtListener</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
