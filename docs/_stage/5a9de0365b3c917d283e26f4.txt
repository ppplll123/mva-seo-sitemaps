<h1>Had a car accident on I-78? 1,667 people were hurt here in two years, and milepost 52 in Union Township is the worst spot.</h1>

<p>New Jersey crash records show 1,195 car accidents on I-78 in 2023 and 2024 in which someone was hurt or killed, about 1.6 a day. Those accidents hurt 1,667 people, and 21 people were killed on the road in the same two years. The busiest mile for injury accidents is milepost 52 in Union Township, with 91 in 2024 and 2025.</p>

<h2 id="deaths">Who died on I-78: many were outside a moving car</h2>
<p>The fatal records for I-78 tell a different story from the injury records. From 2023 to 2025, 28 fatal accidents on I-78 killed 31 people. Eight of those killed were pedestrians, people on foot on or beside an interstate. Five fatal accidents began with a moving vehicle hitting a parked vehicle, and they killed 6 people.</p>
<p>Counting each death once, 13 of the 31 people killed died either on foot or in an accident that started at a stopped vehicle. That is about four in ten. In 2024 alone, four of the 11 fatal accidents began at a parked vehicle.</p>
<p>Darkness stands out too. 22 of the 28 fatal accidents happened in the dark, against about 36 percent of the injury accidents in 2023 and 2024. Ten fatal accidents were on stretches coded "dark, no street lights".</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the NJDOT records (via nj-crashes) and the NJSP fatal feed. 2025 is preliminary. "Parked MV" is the record's first harmful event; the records do not say why the vehicle was stopped.</p>

<h2 id="when">When: the evening commute, and October</h2>
<p>The hour from 5 pm to 6 pm had the most injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 (101), and 3 pm to 6 pm held 260, about 22 percent. October had the most of any month (135) and February the fewest (72). The day of the week matters little here: every day fell between 152 and 189.</p>

<p>Rear-end accidents are 43 percent of the injury accidents on I-78 (518 of 1,195), sideswipes 24 percent and fixed objects such as guide rails 21 percent. <h2 id="report">Getting the report for an I-78 accident</h2>
<p>I-78 accidents are not all handled by one agency. The NJDOT records name a reporting station (Somerville, Perryville or Newark) for 66 percent of I-78 accidents in 2023 and 2024. In Hudson County that drops to 32 percent, and almost every Hudson County record (99.5 percent) codes the road as "State Authority" rather than an NJDOT interstate. Check the agency name on the slip or card the officer gave you.</p>
<ul>
<li>State Police or New Jersey Turnpike Authority report: order it online through the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report portal</a>. The basic report (NJTR-1) can be downloaded once it is approved; if it is not there yet, the portal says to search again in a couple of days. <span class="source">(NJ portal, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
<li>Fees on that portal: $13 for a non-toll road report and $5 for a toll road report. Fatal Turnpike reports go through the Turnpike Authority's records request form instead. <span class="source">(NJ portal, retrieved 2026-11-10</span></li>
<li>Local police report: ask the department of the town where it happened. Fees and procedures vary by department. Town pages: <a href="/new-jersey/union-county-car-accident-help#union-township">Union Township car accidents</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/newark-nj-car-accidents">Newark car accidents</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/jersey-city-nj-car-accidents">Jersey City car accidents</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/hudson-county-car-accident-help#bayonne">Bayonne car accidents</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="method">Method and data limits</h2>
<ul>
<li>Scope: every crash NJDOT assigns to route 78 with an interstate road name, both directions, express and local lanes and ramps. County roads that share the number are excluded.</li>
<li>2023 and 2024 are complete. 2025 is preliminary: I-78 already shows 3,313 accidents for 2025, 1.05 times the 2024 total, so it does not look visibly incomplete, but late reports can still be added.</li>
<li>Counts, not risk rates. A busier stretch has more accidents because more cars use it.</li>
<li>An earlier version of this page listed different milepost figures, with Jersey City first. This version groups by whole mile (52.0 to 52.99), the method in our data notes.</li>
<li>We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file (downloaded 2026-11-06); the method notes on this page describe the selection.</li>
</ul>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on I-78. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell police by the quickest means. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
