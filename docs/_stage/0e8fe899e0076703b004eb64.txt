<h1>Route 202 NJ accident today: deer, Wayne's mileposts and which police department has the report</h1>

<p><strong>If your car accident on Route 202 happened today, the police report will not be ready yet. Judging by the records, it will nearly always come from the police of the town where it happened, not the State Police.</strong> In 2024 and 2025, only 75 of 3,758 Route 202 crash records named a State Police station, and 50 of those were in East Amwell. That station field is undocumented and filled in on about 2 percent of records, so treat it as a pointer, not proof of which agency holds your report. Wayne says its reports are usually ready within three working days; Bridgewater asks you to allow five business days.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 via hudcostreets/nj-crashes, our cut in <code>lane2-content/data/route-202-nj-accidents/route202_njsp_station_by_county_2024_2025.csv</code> (2025 preliminary); <a href="https://waynetownship.com/public-safety/wayne-police/police-operations/">Wayne Police Operations Division</a> and <a href="https://www.police.bridgewaternj.gov/divisions/records">Bridgewater Police Records</a>, both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<p>Over the two complete years, 2023 and 2024, police recorded 3,505 car accidents on Route 202 in New Jersey. In 771 of them someone was hurt or killed, about one a day, with 1,048 people hurt and 6 killed. What sets Route 202 apart from most state highways is deer: one record in eight starts with a deer strike, more than three times the statewide share.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <code>lane2-content/data/profiles/route-202-nj-accidents.json</code> and <code>route202_deer_vs_statewide_2023_2024.csv</code> (NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes).</p>

<h2 id="deer">Deer: one Route 202 record in eight, and nearly half of them in October and November</h2>
<p>In 2023 and 2024, 426 of 3,505 Route 202 records (12.2 percent) list a deer as the first thing struck. Across all New Jersey roads the share was 3.6 percent (18,591 of 514,320 records). October had 104 deer records on Route 202 and November 91, so those two months hold 46 percent of the two-year total.</p>
<p>Deer strikes rarely hurt anyone on this road. Police coded 435 records as the "Animal" crash type, and only 21 of them had someone hurt. The share climbs toward the southern end: in Hunterdon County, 167 of 716 Route 202 records (23.3 percent) began with a deer, and in Readington Township it was 61 of 148.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <code>route202_deer_vs_statewide_2023_2024.csv</code>, <code>route202_deer_by_month_2023_2024.csv</code>, <code>route202_by_municipality_2023_2024.csv</code>; crash type mix from the profile JSON. "Deer" is the first harmful event field; "Animal" is the crash type field, so the two counts differ slightly.</p>

<h2 id="towns">Wayne holds one in five Route 202 injury accidents</h2>
<p>Route 202 runs through 25 municipalities in the records, but Wayne Township alone had 151 of the 771 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 (19.6 percent). Somerset County as a whole had 240 (31 percent), mainly in Bridgewater, Branchburg, Raritan Borough and Bedminster, which together account for 220 of them.</p>
<p>The share of accidents in which someone was hurt also varies a lot by town. In Wayne it was 29 percent of records; in Flemington Borough it was 17 of 201, under 9 percent. <h2 id="report">Which police department has your Route 202 report</h2>
<p>Route 202 has no single report office. The report sits with whichever agency investigated, which on this road is nearly always the town police. Only an officer can complete New Jersey's NJTR-1 crash report. For a crash no officer investigated, the driver files NJDOT's SR-1 self-report form instead.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3 id="report-wayne">Wayne: Records and Identification Bureau</h3>
<p>Wayne Police say: "Police reports are available within three working days after the officer takes the report, including accident reports." Its accident reports are also online "in partnership with CARFAX" at crashdocs.org, normally "approved and posted within 7 days from the date the accident occurred." Copies cost 75 cents a page for pages 1 to 10.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://waynetownship.com/public-safety/wayne-police/police-operations/">Wayne Township Police, Operations Division, Records and Identification Bureau</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. We did not test the CARFAX service.</p>

<h3 id="report-bridgewater">Bridgewater: Police Records Bureau</h3>
<p>Bridgewater Police ask you to "allow five (5) business days for a report to be completed" and to call ahead, with your case or incident number ready. The Records Bureau phone is (908) 725-4044, and other records requests go to records@bridgewaternj.gov. A "nominal fee" applies. The page also links to the LexisNexis BuyCrash site, which we did not test.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.police.bridgewaternj.gov/divisions/records">Bridgewater Township Police, Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3 id="report-state-police">East Amwell and other State Police records: the NJSP portal</h3>
<p>In East Amwell, 50 of 56 Route 202 records in 2024 and 2025 name the State Police Kingwood station, as do 7 of 43 in West Amwell. A trooper's report on Route 202 is ordered online from the NJSP crash report portal, not from the town. Once the portal marks the basic NJTR-1 ready, you can download it; a report that is missing may simply need a few more days. Route 202 counts as a non-toll road, so the fee is $13, and the portal keeps reports for six years after the crash year.</p>
<p>The same portal page lists the contacts. Questions about non-toll road crash reports go to the State Police at (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234, and help with the online service to njsprecords@njsp.gov. For records requests on serious or fatal crashes, which can take up to a month, the page asks you to call only after that month has passed.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <code>route202_njsp_station_by_municipality_2024_2025.csv</code> (the station field is undocumented and does not prove which agency holds a report); <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10, for the fee, availability and all contacts (research/nj-primary-sources.md, section 8).</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 202. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
