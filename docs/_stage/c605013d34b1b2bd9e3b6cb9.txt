<h1>Linden car accidents: where 12 people died and 1,475 were hurt in 2023 and 2024</h1>

<p>If you were hurt in a car accident in Linden (Union County), you are in one of the busiest crash towns in the state. NJDOT records show 1,089 accidents where someone was hurt or killed in Linden in 2023 and 2024. That puts Linden 18th among New Jersey towns in the NJDOT records, and third in Union County. Those accidents hurt 1,475 people and killed 12. Seven of the 12 deaths, including every pedestrian death, were on two state highways: Route 1 (Herbert Highway) and Route 27 (St. Georges Avenue).</p>

<section id="linden-route-1-route-27">
<h2>Route 1 and St. Georges Avenue: a third of Linden's injury accidents</h2>
<p>Route 1 carried 241 of Linden's accidents where someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024, 22% of the town total. Route 27 carried 113, another 10%. Together they held 27 of the town's 86 pedestrian accidents, and all 4 pedestrian deaths.</p>
<p>On Route 1 Linden is one of the heaviest towns on the whole road. In our Route 1 file, which counts the road's injury accidents town by town, Linden has 241, more than Elizabeth (236) next door. The rest of the road is on our <a href="/new-jersey/route-1-nj-accidents">Route 1 accidents in New Jersey page</a>.</p>
<p class="source" data-file="lane2-content/data/linden-nj-car-accidents/linden_route_corridors.csv; lane2-content/data/route-1-nj-accidents/route1_corridor_by_municipality.csv">Source: NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes, NJSP fatal supplement; our count by route number on state-prefixed road names, 2023 and 2024. <p>Route 27 has no stand-alone page on this site. South of Linden the road runs on into Middlesex County, which our <a href="/new-jersey/middlesex-county-car-accident-help#route-27">Middlesex County car accident guide</a> covers.</p>

<section id="linden-left-turns">
<h2>Left-turn accidents: twice the county's share</h2>
<p>Police coded 80 of Linden's injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 as "Left Turn/U Turn". That is 7.3% of the town's total, against 3.6% across Union County and 4.6% statewide. Linden had 24% of all such accidents in Union County (80 of 335), while it had 12% of the county's injury accidents overall.</p>
<p>St. Georges Avenue (Route 27) had the most, 18. Park Avenue (County Route 616) had 11 and Elizabeth Avenue (County Route 514) had 7. South Wood Avenue and South Stiles Street tie at 6.</p>

<section id="linden-report">
<h2>Which office holds a Linden crash report</h2>
<p>That depends on who handled the crash. When Linden police came to the scene, the Linden Police Department keeps the report, so ask the officer or the department for the report number and how to request a copy.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <code>research/nj-primary-sources.md</code> section 8 (municipal reports come from the investigating department; procedures and fees vary and were not verified).</p>
<p>The New Jersey Turnpike roadways numbered I-95 cut across Linden, with 95 injury accidents here in 2023 and 2024 and 2 deaths in the preliminary 2025 file. A report for a Turnpike crash comes from the State Police and Turnpike Authority crash report portal, not from Linden police. The fee for a toll-road report there is $5.00, and reports stay on the portal for six years after the crash year. If yours is not posted yet, it says to try again in a couple of days. After a fatal Turnpike crash, the NJTR-1 is requested through the NJ Turnpike Authority OPRA form instead. Send portal questions by email to NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov. The phone line, (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234, is for serious or fatal crash records still not delivered a month after you asked.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Linden. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
