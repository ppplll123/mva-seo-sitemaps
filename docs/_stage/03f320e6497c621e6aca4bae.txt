<section id="phase-in">
<h2>How did NJ minimum limits change from 15/30 to 35/70?</h2>
<p>A 2022 law raised the bodily injury floor in two steps, tied to the date a policy is issued or renewed.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup><sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<section id="old-limits-online">
<h2>Why do so many sites still say 15/30 or 25/50?</h2>
<p>Many repeat older floors: of 9 ranking New Jersey pages we checked that state a bodily injury minimum, 5 give only 15/30 or 25/50 and never mention 35/70.</p>
<p>We read the saved text of 67 New Jersey pages that ranked on Google in November 2026 for PIP, uninsured motorist, lawsuit, deadline and police-report questions. Two of the five outdated pages ranked first.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-13">13</a></sup></p>
<p>On 10 November 2026 the top Google result for "basic auto insurance policy nj car accident injury", a page dated 2021, still gave $15,000 per person and $30,000 per accident.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-14">14</a></sup> 25/50 is still right for a policy issued or renewed in 2023 to 2025; 15/30 is not the floor for any policy issued since.</p>
</section>
<section id="minimum-bodily-injury">
<h2>What is the minimum bodily injury coverage in NJ?</h2>
<p>$35,000 per person and $70,000 per accident, on a Standard Policy issued or renewed on or after 1 January 2026.</p>

<section id="mandatory-coverage">
<h2>What car insurance is mandatory in NJ?</h2>
<p>Every legal New Jersey policy pays some of your own medical bills; only the Standard Policy also requires bodily injury liability and UM/UIM coverage.</p>

<section id="what-limits-mean">
<h2>What does a $25,000 or $35,000 limit mean after a car accident?</h2>
<p>It caps what the at-fault driver's insurance company pays you for losses your own coverage leaves, such as pain and suffering.</p>
<p>Your own PIP pays your medical bills first, unless you chose your health plan as primary; then the health plan pays first and PIP second.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup> In a 2024 New Jersey appeal, Motil, the injury claim had settled for the at-fault driver's $15,000 policy limit.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup></p>
<p>The per-accident cap can also be shared. In 2023 and 2024, 29,479 New Jersey crashes hurt or killed 2 or more people, and 71,963 people were in those crashes.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-12">12</a></sup> Some were at-fault drivers and some crashes involved more than one policy, so not all of them share one limit.</p>
<p>If three hurt people claim against one 35/70 policy, $70,000 split evenly is about $23,333 each, below the $35,000 per-person figure. 8,449 crashes in those two years hurt or killed 3 or more people.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-12">12</a></sup></p>
<p>Other New Jersey settlements that stopped at a policy limit are on our <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">settlement figures from New Jersey appeals</a> page. How PIP and the claim against the other driver fit together is on our <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">guide to New Jersey no-fault insurance</a>, part of our <a href="/new-jersey">New Jersey car accident guides</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="limit-too-low">
<h2>What if the other driver's limit is too low?</h2>
<p>Your own underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage may pay the gap between your UIM limit and the other driver's liability limit.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></p>
<p>How that works, with DOBI's worked example, is on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claim help</a> page. Disputes over reduced UIM limits are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">UM and UIM appeals decisions</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>How do I know my bodily injury limit?</h3></summary>
<p>Start with the date your policy was issued or last renewed: it sets the lowest limit the policy can legally carry, using the tables above. A policy can carry more than the floor, so the amount you bought is set in the policy itself.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What is the lowest car insurance in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>The Basic Policy and SAIP. SAIP is only for people enrolled in federal Medicaid with hospitalization, and DOBI lists it at $360 paid up front or $365 in two installments.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How much liability coverage do I really need?</h3></summary>
<p>New Jersey law sets only the floor, and your liability limits also cap the UIM you can buy. A 50/100/50 policy, for example, is above every minimum: $50,000 per person, $100,000 per accident and $50,000 of property damage.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Is personal injury protection the same as bodily injury coverage?</h3></summary>
<p>No. PIP pays your own medical bills whoever caused the crash; bodily injury liability pays people you hurt when you are at fault. PIP limits are on our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">New Jersey PIP limits and medical bills</a> page.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What is the minimum PIP limit in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>$15,000 per person on the Basic Policy, which is also the lowest Standard Policy medical option. Both still pay up to $250,000 for certain serious injuries.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>
</details></section>
