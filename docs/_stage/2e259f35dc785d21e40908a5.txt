<section id="sue-driver">
<h2>Can a passenger sue the driver of the car they were in?</h2>
<p>Yes. If your driver caused the crash, your injury claim runs against their liability insurance, which handles the claim and their defense up to its limits.</p>
<p>The common belief that you cannot claim against the friend who drove you is wrong. In 2,739 passenger-injury crashes in 2023 and 2024, no other vehicle was involved, so the driver carrying you was the only one to claim against.</p>
<p>Report your own injury and say you were a passenger; you do not need to wait for the driver's claim. The driver signing with a lawyer does not sign for you, and the driver's negotiations do not stop your clock.</p>
</section>
<section id="how-many">
<h2>Which crashes hurt passengers most in New Jersey?</h2>
<p>Rear-end and right-angle crashes: together they caused two of every three crashes in which a passenger was hurt in 2023 and 2024.</p>
<p>Crashes with at least one injured passenger made up 29.0% of all police-reported crashes where anyone was hurt or killed.</p>

<section id="who-pays">
<h2>Who pays a passenger's medical bills in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">PIP pays regardless of fault, starting with your own auto policy, then the policy of a relative you live with.</strong> The car you rode in comes after both. The same order applies to <a href="/new-jersey/route46-little-falls-crash-road-and-connection">passengers hurt on Route 46 in Little Falls</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

<p>Health-primary elections and households with no auto policy are covered in <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP or health insurance first for medical bills</a>. Deductibles and limits are in <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">NJ PIP coverage limits</a>. Wage loss is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-can-you-claim-lost-wages-after-a-car-accident-in-nj">claiming lost wages after a New Jersey crash</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="limit-split">
<h2>Who pays when several passengers are hurt in one car?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">In 6,136 passenger-injury crashes in 2023 and 2024, nearly one in five, two or more passengers were hurt.</strong> They share the at-fault driver's bodily injury cover: each claim is capped at the per-person limit first, then everyone shares one per-accident limit.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<p>The floor depends on when the driver's policy was issued or renewed: see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026#phase-in">minimum limits by policy date</a>.</p>
<p>Worked example: three passengers each have $30,000 in losses, and the driver's 2024 minimum policy carries $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident. Assume no one else, such as a driver in another car, claims on that policy.</p>
<p>Each rider's claim is first capped at $25,000, so $75,000 is claimed. The policy pays $50,000 at most, leaving $40,000 of the $90,000 in losses unpaid by that policy.</p>
<p>On a 2026 minimum policy the same crash pays up to $70,000, still $20,000 short. A quick offer to one passenger can shrink what is left for the others. In 7,314 passenger-injury crashes, three or more people were injured, drivers included.</p>
<p>UIM pays only the difference between your own UIM limit and the driver's liability limit, so UIM set at the same limit as that policy pays nothing. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claims in NJ</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="lawsuit-option">
<h2>Can you still sue as a passenger if limited tort applies?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes, for qualifying injuries.</strong> A named insured's limited right to sue also binds resident relatives who have no auto policy of their own.</p>
<p>Under the limited option, pain and suffering needs an injury such as a displaced fracture, significant scarring or a permanent injury. Economic losses are not limited.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<p>The full list of qualifying injuries is on <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">whether New Jersey is a no-fault state</a>, and the household rule on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-families-should-know-after-a-serious-car-accident-in-nj">what families should know after a serious NJ crash</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="courts">
<h2>What have New Jersey courts said about passenger claims?</h2>
<p>One ruling speaks directly to passengers, and two more decide the release and step-down problems passengers run into.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Rashid v. Reed (2018):</strong> a car hit a jitney carrying passengers. Whether a fellow rider was hurt is not relevant to proving your own injury.</li>
<li><strong>Nesby v. Fleurmond (2019):</strong> a release signed without reserving rights ended later claims on relatives' policies. Check any release against <a href="/new-jersey/nj-8-questions-to-ask-before-accepting-a-settlement-offer">8 questions to ask before accepting a settlement offer</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Motil v. Wausau (2024):</strong> the higher UIM limit on the declarations page beat a step-down clause. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claims in NJ</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></li>
</ul>

</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions passengers ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Should I give the driver's insurer a recorded statement?</h3></summary>
<p>You may, but no New Jersey law sets what you must tell another driver's insurer, and DOBI warns that not cooperating fully could lead to a denial. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can someone sue you personally after a car accident in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Your claim against a friend is paid by their liability insurer, which also defends them, up to the limit: at least $35,000 per person and $70,000 per crash on a 2026 minimum policy. A Basic Policy without bodily injury coverage gives no defense.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What if the ride was an Uber or Lyft?</h3></summary>
<p>The trip stage decides which policy applies. See <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-rideshare-accident-help">rideshare accident insurance for passengers in New Jersey</a>, or <a href="/new-jersey/hudson-county-rideshare-accident-help">Uber and Lyft passenger claims in Hudson County</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long does a passenger have to sue in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Generally two years. If a bus or other public vehicle was involved, the notice of claim must reach the public body within 90 days. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">the New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Is 50k a good settlement for a car accident?</h3></summary>
<p>For a passenger it depends on the injury and the insurance: on a 2026 minimum policy, $35,000 is the most one person can get from the driver's bodily injury cover. See <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">what NJ appeals figures say about a $50,000 settlement</a>.</p>
</details></section>
