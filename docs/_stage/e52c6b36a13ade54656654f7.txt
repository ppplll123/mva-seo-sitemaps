<h1>Camden NJ car accident data: where city streets cross</h1>

<p>Camden City had 1,465 car accidents where someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024. That is the most in Camden County and the 9th most of any New Jersey municipality in the records. Those accidents hurt 1,991 people, and 8 people died.</p>
<p>What sets Camden apart is the street grid. Half of those accidents happened on city streets, and 38% were right-angle collisions, where two vehicles on crossing paths collide.</p>

<h2>Nearly two in five Camden accidents with someone hurt were right-angle collisions</h2>
<p>In 2023 and 2024, 552 of Camden City's 1,465 accidents where someone was hurt were coded "Right Angle." That is 37.7%, against 22.4% statewide and 28.4% across Camden County.</p>
<p>We compared the 50 New Jersey municipalities with at least 500 such accidents. Only Plainfield had a higher right-angle share, at 41.0%. Trenton came in at 32.0%.</p>

<h2>Half the injury accidents were on city streets, few on state highways</h2>
<p>NJDOT tags each record with the agency that owns the road. In Camden City, 51.0% of accidents where someone was hurt were on municipal streets. Statewide that figure is 27.7%, and across Camden County it is 21.8%.</p>
<p>State highways show the reverse. They carried 27.4% of such accidents statewide but only 7.4% in Camden. Interstates, mostly I-676, carried 9.1%.</p>

<h2>Parked cars are the most common thing hit in Camden</h2>
<p>Counting every police-reported accident, not only those with someone hurt, "Struck Parked Vehicle" is the largest category in Camden City. There were 1,059 in 2023 and 2024, slightly more than the 1,022 right-angle accidents.</p>
<p>That is 22.8% of all Camden accidents, double the statewide 11.4%. Among the 50 large towns, Camden ranks 6th, behind Perth Amboy, Passaic, Trenton, Plainfield and Bayonne. Most of these accidents hurt no one, but 106 did.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 and 2024 via hudcostreets/nj-crashes, collision type as coded by police, all severities.</p>

<h2>Ordering a Camden accident report: match the agency first</h2>
<p>Camden accident reports do not all come from one place. Start with the agency name on the card or paper the officer gave you, then use the route below that matches it.</p>
<h3>I-676 and I-76: often the State Police</h3>
<p>In 2024 and 2025, 78% of the I-676 accident records inside Camden City (428 of 548) name the State Police Bellmawr station. On I-76, 14 of 74 records do, about one in five. On the city's other roads it is fewer than 1% (31 of 4,100 records).</p>
<p>If a trooper handled your accident, request the report through the State Police crash report portal at <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>. That portal page gives NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234 for questions.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the NJDOT records (2025 preliminary; our reading is that the station field marks a State Police report); <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10, for the request route and contact details.</p>
<h3>Camden streets: the Camden County Police Department's report link</h3>
<p>The Camden County Police Department lists its headquarters at 800 Federal Street, Camden, NJ 08103, phone (856) 757-7400. Its chief's page says the department was set up in 2013. Its contact page has an "Order Accident Reports" button that links to the LexisNexis BuyCrash site, plus a link to an Open Public Records Act request portal.</p>
<p>The department pages we fetched do not spell out which streets it covers. Before ordering from BuyCrash, confirm that your card or paper names the Camden County Police Department.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://camdencountypd.org/contact-us/">Camden County Police Department, Contact Us</a> (report link: buycrash.lexisnexisrisk.com/search/; OPRA link: camdencountynj.mycusthelp.com) and <a href="https://camdencountypd.org/about-us/chief-of-the-department/">Chief of the Department page</a> (department stood up in 2013), both retrieved 2026-11-10. <h3>Camden accidents with no police response</h3>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Camden City. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
