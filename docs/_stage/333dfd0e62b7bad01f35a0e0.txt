<li><strong>Temporary disability (TDI).</strong> It pays 85 percent of your average weekly pay, up to $1,119 a week in 2026, for up to 26 weeks. It covers mental health conditions too, not only physical injuries.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></li>
<li><strong>Your own car insurance's lost-pay coverage</strong>, if you bought it (it is called PIP "income continuation"). The basic amount is up to $100 a week and $5,200 in all, and never more than your normal take-home pay. Some policies pay more.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></li>
<li><strong>A claim against the other driver</strong> for pay neither one covered. Our page on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-can-you-claim-lost-wages-after-a-car-accident-in-nj">claiming lost wages after a car accident in NJ</a> shows you how.</li>
</ol>
<p>PIP subtracts disability benefits you could collect, even ones you never applied for. So apply for TDI even if PIP is already paying.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>
<p>The first week of TDI is held back. You get it later only if you are out 22 days or more.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>
</section>
<section id="job-already-gone">
<h2>What if my job is already gone?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Temporary disability does not stop because the job ended.</strong> It is paid until you recover and go back to work, or reach the limit.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>
<ul>
<li>Could not work within 14 days of your last day on the job: apply for regular TDI.</li>
<li>Could not work more than 14 days after your last day, or while on unemployment: apply for Disability During Unemployment.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></li>
<li>Able to work again but no job: apply for unemployment at myunemployment.nj.gov.</li>
<li>Still can't work when TDI runs out: the state says to look at Social Security disability.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></li>
</ul>
<p>You qualify for TDI in 2026 if you earned at least $310 a week for 20 weeks, or $15,500 in total, in the base year (a set 12 months before you apply).<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>
</section>

<section id="this-week">
<h2>What should I do this week?</h2>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Ask your doctor to certify on your TDI claim how long you need to recover, up to 26 weeks.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></li>
<li>Ask your employer whether you are on the state disability plan or a private plan, then apply online at myleavebenefits.nj.gov.</li>
<li>Tell your PIP adjuster (the person at your car insurance company who handles your PIP file) in writing that you are out of work, and send the TDI award notice when it comes.</li>
<li>Save your pay stubs: TDI is 85 percent of your average weekly pay, so they show what you are owed. Keep the firing notice too: the date your job ended decides whether you file regular TDI or Disability During Unemployment.</li>
</ol>
<p>If you apply after 30 days, you must explain why, and benefits can be cut or denied.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Can you get fired after a car accident?</h3></summary>
<p>Sometimes, but they cannot fire you for taking or asking for New Jersey temporary disability. If you get TDI, you may also have the right to your job back when the leave ends.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can you collect unemployment if you are fired in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Only if you are well enough to work and looking for a job. If your injuries still keep you from working, apply for Disability During Unemployment instead.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can my employer make me use vacation days first?</h3></summary>
<p>Sometimes. Your employer may be able to make you use paid time off, like vacation days, first, but they can never make you use the sick days you earned under New Jersey's Earned Sick Leave law.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long after a car accident can you sue in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Usually two years from the crash, for lost pay and pain alike. See the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey filing deadlines page</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></p>
</details></section>
