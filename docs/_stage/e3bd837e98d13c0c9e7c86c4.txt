<h1>NJ hit and run accident claim help: who pays when the driver drives off</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>If you were hurt in a hit and run car accident in New Jersey, your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills, because PIP pays no matter who caused the car accident.</strong> A claim for pain and suffering usually goes through your own uninsured motorist (UM) coverage if your policy includes UM (the Basic Policy does not), or against the driver if police later identify them. Report the car accident to police first, because your insurance company may need that report.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2 (PIP pays "your own medical expenses no matter who caused the auto accident") and p. 10 (uninsured motorist coverage); DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/basicpolicy.shtml">Basic Policy page</a> (no UM/UIM coverage), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice. Your policy wording and the facts of your car accident decide what applies to you.</p>
</section>

<section id="report">
<h2>Report the hit and run to police right away</h2>
<p>New Jersey law requires the driver in a car accident that causes injury, or property damage over $500 to any one person, to notify police as fast as they can. If an officer writes a report, you do not also have to send your own written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:4-130, statute text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260920182556/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-4-130/">Justia 2025 edition (Wayback snapshot 2026-09-20)</a>, a secondary host, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>For a hit and run, the police report matters for your insurance claim too. DOBI tells drivers to report a loss to police immediately if a hit-and-run driver damaged the vehicle, and warns: "Without a police report your company could deny your claim."</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">Filing an Auto Damage Claim with Your Own Insurer</a>, question 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Write down what you saw while it is fresh: a full or partial plate, the vehicle's color and type, the direction it went, and the time and place. Ask witnesses for their names and phone numbers. Note any business or home cameras that faced the road, and tell the officer about them. Do not chase the car or confront someone you suspect. Give the police what you have and let them follow up.</p>
<p>Your report comes from the police agency whose officer took it. Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">getting your New Jersey car accident report</a> shows where to request it. If no officer investigated, NJDOT says the SR-1 self-reporting crash form is the only form for reporting a crash police did not investigate.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT, <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260918164037/https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">Self-Reporting Crash Form page (Wayback snapshot 2026-09-18)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="who-pays">
<h2>Who pays after a hit and run car accident in New Jersey</h2>
<p>Three different coverages can be involved. They pay for different losses, so it helps to keep them apart.</p>

<h3>Your PIP pays your medical bills first</h3>
<p>New Jersey is a no-fault state for medical bills. "No-fault" here means your own PIP pays regardless of who caused the car accident. It does not mean you were not at fault, and it does not depend on finding the driver. How much PIP pays depends on the limit, deductible and co-pay on your policy. Our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">guide to PIP limits and medical bills</a> explains those, and <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">what no-fault means in New Jersey</a> covers the difference between no-fault and fault.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2; Zabilowicz v. Kelsey, 200 N.J. 507 (2009), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1480699/zabilowicz-v-kelsey/">CourtListener</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If no one in your household has auto insurance, PIP may be sought through the Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund, which NJ PLIGA administers.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), describing N.J.S.A. 39:6-86.1, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">CourtListener</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>Your UM coverage stands in for the missing driver</h3>
<p>Ask your insurance company, in writing, whether your UM coverage applies to a car accident where the driver was never identified, and what notice and proof it requires. Your policy's UM section sets those terms. New Jersey courts have treated an unidentified "phantom vehicle" as a UM matter. In Carbajal v. Patel (App. Div. 2021, published), a jury split fault between an identified driver and a phantom vehicle. The injured person's UM carrier paid the phantom vehicle's share, up to its UM limit, and the identified driver paid the rest.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Carbajal v. Patel, A-1999-19 (N.J. Super. App. Div. June 2, 2021), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4888351/jose-carbajal-vs-nancy-v-patel-l-4317-17-middlesex-county-and/">CourtListener</a>, as coded in our UM/UIM appeals table (coded 2026-11-05). One appellate decision, not a prediction for any other car accident.</p>
<p>Some limits apply. The Basic Policy has no UM coverage. On the standard policy, UM can be bought at higher limits but not above your own liability limits. DOBI also notes that UM and UIM claims "exclude the first $500 in damages."</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, pp. 3 to 4; DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/basicpolicy.shtml">Basic Policy page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>A UM claim is a claim against your own insurance company, so it can still turn on proof of injury. In Koukounias v. New Jersey Manufacturers Insurance Co. (App. Div. 2025, unpublished), a driver said a phantom vehicle merged into the driver's lane and caused the crash. The jury found the driver did not prove a permanent injury caused by that car accident, and the appeals court affirmed. Whether your lawsuit option applies to a UM claim against your own carrier has case-law nuance, so ask about your policy.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: Koukounias v. N.J. Mfrs. Ins. Co, A-1446-23 (App. Div. 2025), <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/appellate-division-unpublished/2025/a-1446-23.html">Justia</a>, as coded in our verbal threshold appeals table (coded 2026-11-05); Zabilowicz v. Kelsey, 200 N.J. 507 (2009), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1480699/zabilowicz-v-kelsey/">CourtListener</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>More on these claims: <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">New Jersey uninsured motorist claim help</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">New Jersey UM and UIM appeals decisions</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="if-found">
<h2>If police find the driver later</h2>
<p>If the driver is identified and insured, you may have a claim against that driver's liability coverage. Bodily injury liability pays others for pain, suffering and economic losses such as lost wages after a car accident its driver caused. If that driver carries less coverage than your underinsured motorist (UIM) limit, your UIM may pay the difference between the two limits.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 2 and 10, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>A claim against the driver is a fault-based claim. Your recovery is reduced by your own share of fault, and it is barred only if your share is greater than the other side's. See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what happens if you were partly at fault</a>. Your lawsuit option also matters. Under the "limitation on lawsuit" option, pain and suffering is recoverable only for injuries in the listed categories, such as a permanent injury. Economic losses are not limited that way. Court rulings on that threshold are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">New Jersey verbal threshold appeals rulings</a>.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1, via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">Justia 2025 edition (Wayback 2026-10-09)</a>; N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8, via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">Justia 2025 edition (Wayback 2026-05-01)</a>, secondary hosts; DOBI <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Comparative Negligence FAQ</a>; all retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>A lawsuit for an injury against a person generally must be started within two years after the claim accrues. Other rules can be shorter. For example, a claim against a public entity needs a notice of claim within 90 days. Timing for a UM claim under your own policy is a separate question to raise with a lawyer.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2, via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260930102816/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-14-2/">Justia 2025 edition (Wayback 2026-09-30)</a>, secondary host; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8 as quoted in O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Auth, 236 N.J. 335 (2019), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">CourtListener</a>; retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<li><strong>Call your insurance company</strong> to open the PIP claim and tell them it was a hit and run. Ask what forms and proof they need. DOBI says your insurance company must contact you within 10 working days after it is notified of a loss.</li>
<li><strong>Ask in writing</strong> whether your UM coverage applies and what it requires from you.</li>
<li><strong>Request the police report</strong> and check it for mistakes. DOBI says to tell your insurance company and the police department if you see inconsistencies.</li>
<li><strong>Keep your notes, photos and witness contacts</strong> in one place, with the date you made each one.</li>
</ol>
