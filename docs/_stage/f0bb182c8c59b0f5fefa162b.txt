<h1>Route 3 NJ accident today: who handles it, and where Route 3 crashes happen</h1>

<section id="today">
  <p class="lead">If you were in a car accident on Route 3 today, the police report usually comes from the town where it happened: Clifton, Rutherford, East Rutherford, Secaucus or North Bergen. Clifton and Secaucus police both say crash reports are usually ready after about three business days.</p>
  <p>This page has no live traffic or news feed. It uses police-reported car accidents from 2023 to 2025 to show where on Route 3 people get hurt, and how those accidents differ from the rest of New Jersey.</p>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Getting the police report for a Route 3 accident</h2>
  <p>Only 43 of the 4,634 Route 3 crash records from 2023 to 2025 name a State Police station. So for most Route 3 accidents, the report sits with the local department. Check the agency name on the slip the officer gave you.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes, field "station", 2023 to 2025 (2025 preliminary); our count in lane2-content/data/route-3-nj-accidents/route3_cuts.py. This field suggests, but does not prove, which agency handled each crash.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Clifton (Passaic County).</strong> The police FAQ says an accident report "can be obtained after 3 business days" and links to an online report service. The Records Division number given is 973-470-5877. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.cliftonpolice.org/FAQ.html">Clifton Police FAQ</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Rutherford (Bergen County).</strong> The Records Bureau holds motor vehicle crash reports. Its front counter is open Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and crash reports requested by mail cost $5.00 each. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.rutherfordboronj.com/police-pages/330-records-bureau">Rutherford Police Records Bureau</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Secaucus (Hudson County).</strong> Accident reports are "usually available after three (3) working days," through the Records Bureau at police headquarters, 1203 Paterson Plank Road, or the online services the department links. <span class="source">(<a href="http://secaucuspolice.org/divisions/records">Secaucus Police Records Bureau</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>

A report can be downloaded once it is "ready and approved." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>

<section id="towns">

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 3. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
