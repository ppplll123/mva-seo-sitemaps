<h1>Irvington NJ car accidents: the 2024 and 2025 records, the Parkway, and a 2023 gap in the state file</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">In 2024, NJDOT records list 2,453 police-reported car accidents in Irvington. Someone was hurt or killed in 601 of them, 815 people were hurt and 2 people died. Read these as a floor: the file is missing almost all of Irvington's July 2024 local police records.</p>
  <p>Even with that hole, only 15 of the 570 New Jersey municipalities with 2024 records had more injury accidents than Irvington. The township held 10.4 percent of Essex County's injury accidents that year. The preliminary 2025 records look similar: 542 accidents where someone was hurt, 723 people hurt and 2 deaths.</p>
  <p>Two things set Irvington apart. Pedestrians make up a much larger share of injury accidents here than in the state as a whole. And the state file is missing most of Irvington's 2023 local police records, so older totals for the township, including ours, were too low.</p>

<section id="two-road-systems">
  <h2>The Parkway and the township streets are two different records trails</h2>
  <p>In 2024 and 2025 together, the records list 1,143 Irvington accidents where someone was hurt or killed. The Garden State Parkway holds 188 of them (16 percent), with 252 people hurt and 2 killed. The other 955 happened on county roads and township streets.</p>

<section id="police-report">
  <h2>Getting the police report for an Irvington car accident</h2>
  <p>Start with where the accident happened. If it was on the Garden State Parkway, the report is a State Police or New Jersey Turnpike Authority report. Request it online through the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash report portal</a>, which covers the Parkway and lists a $5.00 fee for toll road reports. A fatal Parkway crash is the exception: the portal says that NJTR-1 can only be requested with the OPRA form on the NJ Turnpike Authority website. That route applies to two of Irvington's recent deaths, in April 2024 and June 2025. If a Parkway request gets stuck, State Police records staff take email at NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and calls at (609) 882-2000, extension 2234.</p>
  <p class="source">Sources: NJSP and NJTA crash report portal (retrieved 2026-11-10); <a href="https://njsp.njoag.gov/identification-amp-information-technology-section/criminal-justice-records-bureau/">NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>If it was on Springfield Avenue, Grove Street or any other township or county street, the report usually comes from the Irvington Police Department. The department is at the Municipal Building, 1 Civic Square, and its main line is 973-399-6600. When we checked, its township web page did not post a crash report procedure or fee, so call ahead.</p>
  <p>The township's Municipal Clerk page lists opra@irvingtonnj.gov for public records requests and an online OPRA request portal. The Police Department's 2024 annual report counts 1,159 OPRA requests received by its Records and Evidence Division that year.</p>
  <p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://irvingtonnj.gov/police-division/">Township of Irvington, Police Division</a>; <a href="https://irvingtonnj.gov/municipal-clerk/">Township of Irvington, Municipal Clerk</a>; <a href="https://irvingtonnj.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2024-Annual-Report-Irvington-Police.pdf">Irvington Police Department 2024 Annual Report</a> (all retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>Whether it is Irvington Police or a State Police trooper, only the officer completes the NJTR-1; a driver cannot file one. When no officer came out to your accident, your own account goes on New Jersey's SR-1 self-report form.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT crash records, police resources</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>Once you have the report, DOBI's advice is to "review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed." If you see mistakes, DOBI says to tell your insurance company and "contact the police department that responded to the scene."</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25 (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Irvington. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
