<details class="plain-version" id="plain-version" open=""><summary><h2>The short, plain version</h2></summary>
<p>Ask the police who came to your accident. If a state trooper came, or it happened on the Turnpike or the Parkway, you can get the report online on NJ.gov. If a town officer came, call that town's police records office.</p>
<p>Look at what the officer gave you at the scene. A case number or a card with the department's name tells you who to ask. If you are not sure who came, try our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-crash-report-finder">report finder by town or road</a>.</p>
<p>The report can take a couple of weeks to show up online. If you can't find it, search again in a few days, and check you picked the right road type (toll or non-toll).</p>
<p>The police never came? You can report it yourself with the state's SR-1 form, a crash report the driver fills in online.</p>
<p>The report blames you? It is not the final word on who was at fault. Read it early, and ask the police in writing to fix any wrong fact.</p>

<section id="which-agency">
<h2>Which police agency has your accident report?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The police who came to your crash have it.</strong> For most crashes that is your town's police: 59.3% of 514,320 police-reported NJ crashes in 2023 and 2024 were on town or county roads.</p>
<p>Crashed on a state highway, interstate or toll road? That was only about 4 in 10 crashes (30.1% and 7.4%), and the road name alone won't tell you who took the report.</p>

<li><strong>Check what the officer gave you.</strong> A case number or a card with a department name tells you who has the report.</li>

<section id="not-searchable">
<h2>Why can't I find my accident report online yet?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">A report shows up online only after it is finished and approved, which the State Police say can take 14 business days.</strong> If nothing turns up, the website says to "search again in a couple days."</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Still nothing after 15 business days?</strong> Email the State Police at njsprecords@njsp.gov, as the help page says.</li>
<li><strong>Sent to CrashDocs or LexisNexis?</strong> Those are private companies, not the police. Elizabeth, Lakewood and Toms River use CrashDocs; Palisades, Paterson and Lakewood use LexisNexis.</li>
<li><strong>Dead link?</strong> Paterson's and Lakewood's pages still point to an old LexisNexis address that did not work when we checked, so call their records office.</li>
<li><strong>Before you pay twice,</strong> check the police department, the road type (toll or non-toll) and the details you typed.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong class="df-k">Use the NJ.gov Crash Reports Request website: the non-toll option for a trooper's report ($13), the toll option for the Turnpike or Parkway ($5).</strong> Crashed near Mount Laurel? <p><a href="/new-jersey/i-295-nj-accidents#over-the-bridge">Hit on I-295 over the bridge in Delaware?</a> Check which state it was first.</p>

<section id="no-police">
<h2>How do you report an accident without a police report?</h2>
<p>File NJDOT's <a href="/new-jersey/what-is-an-sr-1-form-nj">SR-1 Self-Reporting Crash form</a> online. If police didn't investigate, NJDOT says it "is the only form that can be used."</p>
<p>You get a receipt within minutes from the <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting page</a>, and NJDOT says it "can be submitted to an insurance company." Remember more later? Add it on the SR-1S form. We also cover <a href="/new-jersey/hit-a-deer-nj">reporting a crash after hitting a deer</a>.</p>
<p>Call police first if someone is hurt or killed, or the damage to one person's car or things is over $500, by the quickest way you can. If no officer files a report, you owe the Motor Vehicle Commission a written one within 10 days; our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-deadline-calculator">accident deadline checker</a> works out the date.</p>

<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<section class="faq-item">
<h3>What happens if I don't report an accident within 10 days?</h3>
<p>You owe the MVC a report only if no officer wrote one. If you knowingly skip it, you risk a $30 to $100 fine and losing your license or registration for a time.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section class="faq-item">
<h3>Can you file a police report after an accident in NJ?</h3>
<p>Yes, if police never came: file the online SR-1 yourself. Too hurt to call police? Then someone else in the car, or the owner, must do it.</p>
</section>
<section class="faq-item">
<h3>How do I look up an accident report in NJ?</h3>
<p>Ask the police who came: the NJ.gov website for State Police, Turnpike and Parkway crashes, the town's records office for the rest.</p>
</section>
<section class="faq-item">
<h3>Are accident reports public record in NJ?</h3>
<p>Yes, the basic report is public. Files on serious or fatal crashes are not, and getting them takes a subpoena or a judge's order.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section class="faq-item">
<h3>How do you read the codes on an NJ police report?</h3>
<p>The police report (its form name is NJTR-1) is filled in only by police, and most of it is number codes. Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">reading a New Jersey crash report</a> decodes the form and explains how to flag a mistake.</p>
</section>
