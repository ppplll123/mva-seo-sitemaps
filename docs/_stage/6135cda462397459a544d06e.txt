<h1>Medical records for a New Jersey car accident injury claim</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>Keep every record that shows when you were hurt, what care you received, what it cost and who paid.</strong> That means emergency room and follow-up records, test reports, itemized bills, insurance company payment explanations, receipts and your own dated symptom notes. In New Jersey these records do two jobs: they support your own PIP medical claim, and if you sue the other driver, they are the base for any physician certification and for claiming bills PIP does not cover.</p>
<p>You have a federal right to copies of your medical and billing records from your providers and health plan, with few exceptions. Ask your own auto insurance company early which forms and documents it needs.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: US Department of Health and Human Services, <a href="https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-individuals/medical-records/index.html">Your Medical Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25 "After an Accident", retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

If another driver caused it, a short free questionnaire asks about your injury, medical attention, police report and accident date. If you qualify, a sponsoring lawyer or legal consultant may call you about a claim against that driver's insurance company. No obligation.</p>

<section id="why-records-matter">
<h2>Why your records matter in New Jersey</h2>
<p>This is general information, not legal advice.</p>

<h3>For your PIP claim</h3>
<p>PIP on your own policy pays your medical expenses regardless of fault. That is why New Jersey is called a no-fault state. It is a different idea from being "not at fault" for the car accident.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide (2026)</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10. See also <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">is New Jersey a no-fault state</a>.</p>
<p>Your bills and payment explanations show what PIP has paid and what you still owe. Even after the deductible, you pay a 20% co-payment on medical expenses between your deductible and $5,000. DOBI's example: $10,000 of expenses with the $250 deductible means you pay $250 plus $950, and the insurer pays $8,800.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide (2026)</a>, pp. 6 to 7, retrieved 2026-11-10. More on <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and deductibles</a>.</p>
<p>If you chose your health insurance as primary for car accident injuries, your health plan's deductibles, co-pays and pre-certification rules apply first, and PIP pays second. Keep both sets of payment explanations.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">Selecting Your Health Insurer for PIP Option</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. See <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP or health insurance first</a>.</p>

<h3>For a lawsuit under the limitation on lawsuit option</h3>
<p>Every New Jersey policy with PIP carries one of two lawsuit options. If no written choice was made, the limitation on lawsuit option applies by default. Under it, you can claim pain and suffering from the other driver only for certain injuries: death, dismemberment, significant disfigurement or scarring, displaced fractures, loss of a fetus, or a permanent injury.</p>
<p>An injury counts as permanent when the body part or organ has not healed to function normally and will not heal with further treatment. If you sue, a physician certification based on objective clinical evidence must be served within 60 days after the defendant's answer. Your treating records are what that physician reviews.</p>
<p>The option does not limit claims for economic loss, such as medical bills PIP does not cover and lost wages.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8 and 39:6A-8.1, statute text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">Justia 2025 edition (archived copy)</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know (2026)</a>, p. 9 "Do You Want an Unlimited Right to Sue?"; all retrieved 2026-11-10. See how courts applied it in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">verbal threshold appeals rulings</a>.</p>

<h3>For bills above or outside PIP</h3>
<p>Medical expenses that exceed your PIP limit, or that PIP does not cover, can be claimed against the driver who caused the car accident. Amounts PIP paid or will pay cannot be claimed again in that lawsuit. In May 2026 the NJ Supreme Court held that future treatment costs that fit within your remaining PIP limit must go through PIP.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026)</a>, quoting N.J.S.A. 39:6A-12, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="get-copies">
<h2>How to get copies of your medical records</h2>
<p>Under the HIPAA Privacy Rule you can inspect and get copies of your medical and billing records held by covered providers and health plans, with few exceptions. Only you or your personal representative has that right.</p>
<ul>
<li>A provider may charge reasonable costs for copying and mailing, but not a fee for searching for or retrieving your records.</li>
<li>A provider cannot refuse you a copy because you have not paid for the care.</li>
<li>Psychotherapy notes kept separately by a mental health professional are not included.</li>
<li>If something in your record is wrong, you can ask for an amendment. If the provider disagrees, you can add a statement of disagreement to your record.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Source: HHS, <a href="https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-individuals/medical-records/index.html">Your Medical Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Ask each place for what you need by name and date range. The emergency room, the imaging center, your follow-up doctor and your health plan each keep their own files. A request for visit notes may not include the itemized bill, so ask for both. Keep the original of anything you receive and never edit a record yourself.</p>
</section>

<p>Write the answers down, or ask for them in writing. They become part of your record and help you follow the care plan.</p>

<p>After a blow to the head, the CDC says to call 911 or go to an emergency department right away for danger signs. These include a headache that gets worse and does not go away, repeated vomiting, seizures, weakness or numbness, slurred speech, confusion, or being very drowsy or impossible to wake.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/signs-symptoms/index.html">Signs and Symptoms of TBI</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. <section id="bill-log">
<h2>Keep one log for bills and payments</h2>
<p>A new statement is often the same bill again, not a new expense. Keep one line per service so totals are not counted twice.</p>
<div class="table-wrap">
<div class="l2-tablewrap" tabindex="0" role="region" aria-label="Scrollable table"><table>
<caption>Medical bill and payment log</caption>
<thead>
<tr><th scope="col">Column</th><th scope="col">What to enter</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">Service</th><td>Provider, date of service and account number</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Statement</th><td>Statement date, charge and balance shown; keep earlier versions</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Sent to</th><td>PIP carrier or health plan, and the date</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Result</th><td>Paid, reduced, denied or waiting, with the explanation's date</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">You paid</th><td>Date, amount and receipt, including the deductible and 20% co-payment</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Still open</th><td>The unpaid amount and who you contacted about it</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table></div>
</div>
<p>If a claim is denied, you are entitled to a written explanation. Keep it with the bill it concerns.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know (2026)</a>, p. 29 "You Have the Right to the Prompt and Fair Handling of Claims", retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Disputes over PIP payments go through a separate process from an injury claim against the other driver. See <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP coverage limits and medical bills</a> before you respond to a denial.</p>
</section>

<section id="symptom-notes">
<h2>How to keep your own symptom notes</h2>
<p>Keep three things separate: what you noticed, what a clinician recorded, and what the insurance company asked. A useful note has the date you wrote it, the date it happened, the ordinary task, and what changed.</p>
<p>For example: "Written on the 13th about the 12th, in the evening: reaching to my usual kitchen shelf hurt, so I asked for help. I will tell my doctor." Write what really happened, including days you felt better.</p>
<p>Tell your clinician your real history, including earlier problems with the same body part. If cost or transport delayed treatment, say so to the clinician rather than leaving an unexplained gap. Do not add visits to make a file look stronger. Treatment should follow medical need.</p>
<p>For how notes and records relate to pain and suffering, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-10-ways-to-prove-pain-and-suffering-after-an-accident">ways to show pain and suffering</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="deadlines">
<h2>Collecting records does not pause any deadline</h2>
<p>Waiting for a chart or bill does not extend a legal deadline. In New Jersey a personal injury lawsuit generally must be started within two years after the claim arises. A claim against a public entity, such as a town or state vehicle, needs a written notice of claim within 90 days.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2, statute text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260930102816/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-14-2/">Justia 2025 edition (archived copy)</a>; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8 as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Authority, 236 N.J. 335 (2019)</a>; both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Suits over PIP benefits have their own time limit, which runs from when you incur an expense and from the accident date, with an extension after the last PIP payment. The dates on your bills and payment explanations matter here. Exceptions apply, so ask a New Jersey lawyer about your own dates.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-13.1(a), as quoted in <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7403318/aponte-correa-v-allstate-insurance/">Aponte-Correa v. Allstate Ins. Co. (App. Div. 1999)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>
