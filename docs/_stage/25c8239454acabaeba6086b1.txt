<section id="no-report-or-blames-me">
<h2>What if the police never came, or the report says I was at fault?</h2>
<p>Police came? Then you probably have a report: about 9 in 10 people who asked for help after a NJ crash said police came (89%). What people often don't have is a witness's phone number (only 28% did) or a clear idea of who caused the crash (32% weren't sure), so get both while you can.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>
<p>Read the report before you call anyone. It is evidence, not the final word on fault: in New Jersey you can still get money if your share of the blame is not bigger than the other driver's. If no officer came, you file your own crash report online (the SR-1 form) within 10 days.</p>
<p>Blamed in the report? Our page on what to do <a href="/new-jersey/police-report-says-my-fault-nj">if the report blames you</a> shows how to push back with your insurance company, step by step.</p>
<p>Hurt in a fall, not a crash? You may think you have a police report when you don't: 20 of 53 people who fell said they had one, but police came to only 9 of 58 falls. A store manager's note or an ambulance sheet is not a police report, and it will not show up in this finder.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

<section id="look-up">
<h2>How do I look up an accident report in NJ?</h2>
<p>Find the police department first, then use its route: there is no single statewide search for every report.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>Read the card or case number the officer gave you.</strong> It names the department, so trust it over the finder above.</li>
<li><strong>State Police, Turnpike or Parkway:</strong> search the NJ.gov website by case number, license number, plate or last name.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></li>
<li><strong>Town or county officer:</strong> call that department's records office with the date, place and names of the people in the crash.</li>
<li><strong>Nothing found after 15 business days?</strong> Email the State Police records office, as the state website's help page says.</li>
</ol>
<p>Need the exact steps and what to bring? See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey accident report</a>.</p>

<section id="state-police">
<h2>How do I get a police report from the NJ State Police?</h2>
<p>Online, on the NJ.gov crash report website: $13 for a non-toll road, $5 for the Turnpike or Parkway.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
<p>Search by case number without dashes, or by license number, plate or last name. For a fatal crash on the Turnpike or Parkway, use the Turnpike Authority's records form instead. The Atlantic City Expressway is not on the portal: write to the <a href="/new-jersey/atlantic-city-expressway-accidents">Atlantic City Expressway Authority</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="how-long">
<h2>How long does it take to get an accident report in NJ?</h2>
<p>A State Police report can take up to 14 business days; the town reports we checked took 3 to 10.</p>
<p>In Clifton you can usually get it after 3 business days; in Elizabeth, Kearny and Wall, 3 to 5; in Millville and Nutley, about 5. In Randolph most are ready within seven working days, in Edison generally 7 to 10, and in Franklin Township (Somerset) about 3 to 10.</p>
<p>While you wait, other deadlines keep running: check your dates with our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-deadline-calculator">NJ accident deadline checker</a>.</p>
<p>Most departments publish no wait at all, so call the records office before you go. Your town's page, linked from the finder, has the phone number and where we found it.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Are accident reports public record in NJ?</h3></summary>

</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can I file an accident report online in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes, if no officer came. Fill in the state's online form (the SR-1), and you get a receipt you can send to your insurance company.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> If police came, there is nothing to file; you ask for their report.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What is the difference between a police report and an accident report?</h3></summary>
<p>The police report is the form the officer fills in (the NJTR-1), and this finder helps you find it. The SR-1 is the report you file yourself when no officer came.</p>
