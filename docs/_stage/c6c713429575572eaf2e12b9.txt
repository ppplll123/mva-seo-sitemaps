<section id="reversal-rate">
<h2>How hard is it to win a car accident appeal in New Jersey?</h2>
<p>Hard for the side that lost below: the court kept the ruling in 40 of 76 decisions and reversed outright only 21, with 8 more reversed in part.</p>
<p>Reversal is most common in cases that turn on liability, evidence and trial rulings. Those 42 decisions produced 13 reversals and 5 split results.</p>

<section id="published-unpublished">
<h2>Do unpublished appeals come out differently?</h2>
<p>Yes: 79 percent of the 24 unpublished 2025 rulings were affirmed, against 40 percent of the 52 published ones.</p>
<p>Published opinions reversed the ruling below 18 times; unpublished ones only 3 times. Unpublished rulings show how judges treat ordinary disc, neck and back claims, which is why we index them.</p>
</section>
<section id="threshold">
<h2>Can I sue for pain and suffering in NJ after a crash?</h2>
<p>On the limitation on lawsuit option, only if the injury clears the verbal threshold, and 7 of the 9 threshold rulings here with an outcome said it did not.</p>
<p>The two that cleared, Lamothe and Presbery, were both affirmed in 2025. The seven that failed mostly described neck, back, shoulder or leg pain.</p>
<p>How the two lawsuit options differ, and the statute's list of qualifying injuries, is on our page on <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">what New Jersey's no-fault system means</a>. Each ruling's medical evidence is in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">verbal threshold appeals rulings</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="herniated-disc">
<h2>Does a herniated disc get past the verbal threshold?</h2>
<p>Sometimes: both threshold wins were cervical disc herniations, but four of the seven losses also listed disc herniations.</p>
<ul>
<li>Cleared: Lamothe (herniations at C5-6 and C6-7, with radiculopathy) and Presbery (a permanent C6-7 herniation, with radiculopathy).</li>
<li>Fell short: Williams-Siraj (spinal disc herniations), Orlando (cervical and lumbar disc bulges and herniations), Zavis (herniations at C3-4 and C5-6) and Joo (four cervical disc herniations).</li>
<li>Reversed on other grounds: Morales-Hurtado (2018), a herniated-disc jury verdict that was reversed.</li>
</ul>
<p>The award amounts in those disc cases are compared on our <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">$50,000 settlement check against court figures</a>.</p>
</section>

<p>See how $50,000 compares with every figure in the set on our <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">appeals figures page</a>; the full rows are in the table below.</p>

<section id="pip-arbitration">
<h2>Why do so few PIP disputes reach the appeals court?</h2>
<p>PIP fights go to arbitration, so only 12 of the 76 reached a judge, and 8 of those were affirmed.</p>
<p>Each of the 12 is summarised in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-pip-appeals-decisions-permanent-injury">PIP appeals and the permanent-injury bar</a>. How PIP limits and the arbitration route work is on our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP coverage limits guide</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="counties">
<h2>Which counties produce the most car crash appeals?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Middlesex leads with 16 of the 76 decisions, then Essex (7), Camden (6), Burlington (5) and Bergen (5).</strong> Nine opinions do not state the county.</p>
<p>The county is where the trial court sat, not always where the crash happened. For Middlesex crash counts by town and road, see the <a href="/new-jersey/middlesex-county-car-accident-help">Middlesex County car accident hub</a>.</p>
