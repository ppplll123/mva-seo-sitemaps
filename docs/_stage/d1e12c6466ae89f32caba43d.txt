<h1>New Jersey side-impact car accident: T-bone and sideswipe claims</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>If you were hurt in a side-impact car accident in New Jersey, your own PIP coverage pays your medical bills no matter who caused it.</strong> A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering, or for losses PIP does not cover, depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. Fault in a T-bone or sideswipe car accident turns on how each car got to the point of contact, so get medical attention first and then preserve that record.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI), <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">New Jersey Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2 (PIP pays "no matter who caused the auto accident"; bodily injury liability pays for pain, suffering and economic damages caused by its insured); N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8 (lawsuit options). Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Call 911 for an emergency. This is general information, not legal advice.</p>
</section>

It is free and there is no obligation.</p>

<section id="which-type">
<h2>T-bone or sideswipe: which kind of side-impact car accident was it?</h2>
<p>"Side impact" covers two different events, and the questions that matter differ. A T-bone is a crossing collision: the front of one car hits the side of another, often at a signal, a stop sign, a driveway or a turn. A sideswipe is lateral contact between cars moving side by side, often during a lane change or a merge. A side-panel dent alone does not tell you which one happened.</p>

<section id="who-pays">
<h2>Who pays for what after a side-impact car accident in New Jersey</h2>
<h3>Your medical bills: your own PIP</h3>
<p>Personal Injury Protection (PIP) on your own policy pays your medical expenses whoever caused the car accident. That is why New Jersey is called a no-fault state. "No-fault" describes PIP. It does not mean nobody can be held responsible, and it is not the same as being "not at fault." Your PIP limit and deductible depend on what you chose on your policy.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 2, 5 to 7; Zabilowicz v. Kelsey, 200 N.J. 507 (2009), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1480699/zabilowicz-v-kelsey/">courtlistener.com</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>More on this: <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">what "no-fault" means in New Jersey</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and what PIP pays for medical bills</a>.</p>

<h3>Pain and suffering and other losses: a claim against the at-fault driver</h3>
<p>The other driver's bodily injury liability coverage pays people hurt in a car accident its driver caused, including pain, suffering and economic losses such as lost wages. If your policy has the limitation on lawsuit option, you can claim pain and suffering only for certain injuries. They are death, dismemberment, significant disfigurement or scarring, displaced fractures, loss of a fetus, or a permanent injury. The limitation does not stop a claim for economic loss.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 2, 10 to 11; N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8, text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Medical bills above your PIP limit, or not covered by PIP, can be claimed from the people responsible. Bills that your remaining PIP limit will still cover must go through PIP.</p>
<p class="source">Source: Murray v. Punina, A-51-24 (N.J. May 6, 2026), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10854078/lakita-d-murray-v-christopher-b-punina/">courtlistener.com</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>See how New Jersey appeals courts have applied the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">limitation on lawsuit (verbal threshold)</a>.</p>

<h3>Damage to your car: not PIP</h3>
<p>PIP does not pay for damage to a vehicle. You can claim car damage from your own insurance company if you have collision coverage, minus your deductible. Or you can claim from the other driver's insurance company, which pays only to the extent its driver was legally responsible.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI Office of the Insurance Claims Ombudsman, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">Filing an Auto Damage Claim with Your Own Insurer</a>, intro and question 1, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>If the other driver has no insurance or too little</h3>
<p>Uninsured motorist (UM) coverage pays you when the driver who caused the car accident lacked the required insurance. Underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage pays up to the difference between your UIM limit and the other driver's liability limit. The Basic Policy has no UM or UIM. Read more about <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claims</a>.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, pp. 3, 5, 8, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
</section>

<section id="fault">
<h2>How fault is decided in a T-bone or sideswipe car accident</h2>
<p>In New Jersey the insurance company investigates and assigns each driver a percentage of fault. State law gives no fixed formula. DOBI says fault is decided case by case, on the circumstances of the car accident.</p>
<p>DOBI lists factors an adjuster weighs that fit side-impact cases closely. They include failing to observe and avoid the other vehicle, failing to sound the horn, brake or swerve, and driver inattention. DOBI also names terms you may hear. "Greater duty of care" is its example of a driver pulling out from a stop sign, who must be sure no cars are coming. "Last clear chance" asks who could still have avoided the collision.</p>
<p>The adjuster may rely on the police report, driver and witness statements, motor vehicle laws, and a scene investigation of the road's design and layout. The police report is one input. It does not decide fault on its own.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Auto Comparative Negligence Settlement FAQs</a>, questions 1 to 4, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>You do not have to be 0 percent at fault. You can recover if your share of fault is not greater than the other driver's, and your recovery is reduced by your share. DOBI's example: at 50 percent fault with $1,000 of car damage, you are paid $500. At 10 percent fault, you are paid $900.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1, text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20261009074503/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-15-5-1/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Comparative Negligence FAQs</a>, question 5. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If you disagree with the percentage you were given, raise it with the adjuster or a supervisor first. Every auto insurance company must have an internal appeal process. If the appeal does not resolve it, you can ask DOBI's Office of the Insurance Claims Ombudsman to review it, and you keep the right to go to court.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">Comparative Negligence FAQs</a>, questions 6 and 7, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Related: <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how partial fault works in New Jersey car accident cases</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-evidence-helps-prove-fault-after-an-nj-crash">what evidence helps prove fault, and what each source cannot show</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="next-steps">
<h2>What to do next after a side-impact car accident</h2>
<h3>1. Get medical attention and describe the impact accurately</h3>
<p>Tell the clinician where you were sitting, which side of the car was hit and any second impact you remember. A repair estimate does not diagnose an injury. Keep your care instructions and follow-up records, and see <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">which medical records matter for a car accident injury claim</a>.</p>

<h3>2. Make sure the car accident is reported, then get the report</h3>
<p>A driver in a car accident that causes injury, death, or more than $500 of damage to one person's property must notify police by the quickest means. A written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days is needed only if no officer files a written report. Once the report is ready, check it for errors, such as the number of passengers listed. Tell your insurance company and the police department about any inconsistency.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 39:4-130, text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260920182556/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-4-130/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>; DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Here is <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey car accident report</a> from the agency that took it.</p>

<h3>3. Preserve both approaches, not just the damage</h3>
<p>Write down your road, your direction and whether you were turning or going straight. Note whether you saw the other car before contact or only learned its path later. Keep original photos and full-length videos, including the lead-up. Label photos taken after the cars were moved. A possible camera nearby is a lead, not proof that footage exists. Keep witness contact details, and do not ask witnesses to match their accounts.</p>

<h3>4. Let the insurance company inspect your car before repair</h3>
<p>For a claim on your own policy, you must make the damaged car available for inspection before it is repaired. New Jersey rules require your insurance company to contact you within 10 working days of notice. If it wants to inspect the car, it must do so within 7 working days.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/wysk1.htm">Filing an Auto Damage Claim with Your Own Insurer</a>, questions 2 and 4, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>5. Know which deadlines may apply</h3>
<p>A lawsuit for an injury generally must be filed within two years after the claim arises. If a government vehicle was involved, such as a town vehicle or an NJ Transit bus, a written notice of claim is due within 90 days. Your own facts decide which deadline applies, so check early rather than counting on two years.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2, text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260930102816/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2a/section-2a-14-2/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8 and 59:8-9 as quoted in O'Donnell v. N.J. Turnpike Auth, 236 N.J. 335 (2019), <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8267675/odonnell-v-nj-tpk-auth/">courtlistener.com</a>. Retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>6. Ask the other side to state its position</h3>
<p>If the other driver says you moved first or ran the light, ask the insurance company for its explanation and the facts it relied on. Then match each point to the source that may clarify it: a recording, a witness or the road layout. If your case is complicated, read <a href="/new-jersey/nj-7-signs-you-may-need-an-accident-lawyer-after-a-crash">signs you may need an accident lawyer</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="faq">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<h3>What does it mean when they say NJ is a no-fault state?</h3>
<p>It means your own PIP pays your medical bills after a car accident regardless of who caused it. A separate claim against the at-fault driver is still possible, subject to fault and your lawsuit option.</p>
<p class="source">Source: DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>Can you still sue if you have limited tort?</h3>
<p>Yes, but under the limitation on lawsuit option a pain and suffering claim needs one of the listed injuries, such as a permanent injury or a displaced fracture. A permanent injury must be supported by a physician's certification based on objective clinical evidence. Claims for economic loss are not limited.</p>
<p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:6A-8(a), text via <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260501025515/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-6a-8/">Justia 2025 edition (archived)</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>What is the statute of limitations for a personal injury claim in New Jersey?</h3>
<p>Generally two years under N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2. Claims involving a public entity need a notice of claim within 90 days, and other exceptions exist. Do not rely on a date you work out yourself.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: as in step 5 above, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
