<section id="danger">
<h2>Is Route 36 one of New Jersey's most dangerous roads?</h2>
<p>Not by volume: Route 36 carried 309 of Monmouth County's 6,952 accidents with someone hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024, about 4 percent.</p>
<p>Its sharper risk is to people outside a car. Seven of the eight people killed from 2023 to 2025 were walking or riding a bike. For the corners with the most injury crashes statewide, see <a href="/new-jersey/most-dangerous-intersections-nj">New Jersey's most dangerous intersections</a>, or compare a Monmouth town through <a href="/new-jersey/wall-township-monmouth-nj-car-accidents">Wall Township crash records</a>.</p>

<section id="crash-types">
<h2>What kind of accidents hurt people on Route 36?</h2>
<p>Mostly rear end collisions: 48 percent of Route 36 accidents with someone hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024, against 31 percent across Monmouth County and New Jersey.</p>
<p>Yet not one of those 148 rear end accidents killed anyone. What to do after one is covered in <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">our rear end accident guide for New Jersey</a>.</p>
<p>Timing follows the shore traffic. Of the 309 injury accidents, 145 happened between 3 pm and 9 pm, and 122 fell in May through August, about a third of the year.</p>
<p>Saturday had the most injury accidents of any day (56), and Sunday the fewest (34).<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

<section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us.</strong> They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 36. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></li>
</ul>
