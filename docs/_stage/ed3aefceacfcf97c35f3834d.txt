<h1>Route 22 car accidents in New Jersey: towns, cross streets and times</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Police reported 5,196 car accidents on Route 22 in New Jersey in 2023 and 2024. Someone was hurt or killed in 1,242 of them, about 1.7 a day, and 11 people died.</p>
  <p>Two thirds of those accidents where someone was hurt (839 of 1,242) were filed in five towns: Union Township, Bridgewater, North Plainfield, Springfield and Green Brook. Rear end collisions were 55 percent of them, far above the statewide 31 percent.</p>
  <p>This page does not show live traffic or today's Route 22 accidents. It covers police-reported accidents from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 still preliminary.</p>

<section id="next-step">
  <h2>After a car accident on Route 22: telling police and getting the report</h2>
  <p>The crash records file every Route 22 accident under a municipality and, when police note one, a cross street. Write down both, plus the nearest milepost sign if you saw it. Route 22 runs through 21 towns in these records, so the town tells you which police department to ask.</p>

  <h3>When New Jersey law says police must be told</h3>
  <p>A driver in an accident where someone is hurt or killed, or where one person's property damage is over $500, must notify the local, county or State Police as fast as they can. Within 10 days the driver must also send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission, unless a police officer files a written report on the accident.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: N.J.S.A. 39:4-130, statute text from the Justia 2025 edition (<a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260920182556/https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-39/section-39-4-130/">archived copy</a>, a secondary host, because the NJ Legislature site could not be reached), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <p>If police did not come out and investigate, NJDOT's SR-1 self-reporting form is the only form for reporting that accident. It can be filed online, and NJDOT says the acknowledgment it returns can be given to an insurance company.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT Self-Reporting Crash Form page</a> (read from an <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260918164037/https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">archived copy dated 2026-09-18</a>), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 22. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
