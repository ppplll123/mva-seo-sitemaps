<section id="how-many">
<h2>How many people are seriously hurt in New Jersey crashes?</h2>
<p>Police graded 7,399 people as suspected serious injury in 2023 and 2024, in 6,402 separate crashes: about 9 a day statewide.</p>
<p>A quarter of them were passengers, and that group grew fastest: from 852 in 2023 to 1,008 in 2024. That is why a family's first insurance question is often whose policy covers a rider.</p>

<section id="hospital-bill">
<h2>How much is a hospital bill after a car accident?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">For the worst injuries PIP covers up to $250,000, even on a lower limit, and it pays whoever caused the crash.</strong> That ceiling covers brain or spinal cord injury, disfigurement and trauma-center care until the patient is stable.</p>
<p>The injured person's own PIP pays first, or their health plan if they chose it as primary. Limits and co-pays are on <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">New Jersey PIP limits for medical bills</a>, and the primary choice on <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP or health insurance first in New Jersey</a>.</p>
<p>If the injured person cannot work, lost income is a separate question: see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-can-you-claim-lost-wages-after-a-car-accident-in-nj">claiming lost wages after a NJ car accident</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="household-policy">
<h2>Does a relative's car insurance cover the injured person?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Often only if the injured person lives in that household or was in that car.</strong> Being family on its own is not enough.</p>
<p>A named insured's lawsuit option also binds resident relatives who have no auto policy of their own. What that option limits is on <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">whether New Jersey is a no-fault state</a>.</p>
<p>A rider's full coverage order is on the <a href="/new-jersey/passenger-accident-new-jersey">New Jersey passenger accident guide</a>. Uninsured and underinsured driver claims are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claim help in New Jersey</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="report">
<h2>Who reports the crash if the driver is in the hospital?</h2>
<p>Another occupant, or the vehicle owner, must report it when the driver is physically unable to.</p>
<p>When police came to the scene, the family's job is to get a copy, not to file one. Fees and routes are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get a New Jersey car accident report</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="authority">
<h2>Can a family member get medical updates or make decisions?</h2>
<p>A hospital may share information with family involved in care or payment, but that is not legal authority to decide or sign.</p>
<p>HHS says sharing depends on the patient's agreement, lack of objection or the provider's professional judgment. Only a personal representative recognized under state law, such as a parent of a minor or a named agent, stands in the patient's shoes.</p>
<p>Seeing health records, making medical decisions and settling an insurance claim can each need a different authority.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="first-week">
<h2>What should the family do in the first week?</h2>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>Find the Coverage Selection Form</strong> for every car in the household. It shows the PIP limit, which insurer pays first and the lawsuit option.</li>
<li><strong>Tell the insurer and pick one person for adjuster calls.</strong> Reporting timing and what each adjuster may ask are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Sign no release</strong> for the injured person. In a 2019 appeals ruling (Nesby), a driver who released the at-fault driver lost her claims on her sister's and mother's policies too.</li>
<li><strong>Check for a public vehicle.</strong> If a bus, municipal or Turnpike Authority vehicle was involved, a notice of claim is due much sooner than a lawsuit. Both limits are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey car accident deadlines</a>.</li>
</ol>
<p>For everything else after a crash, start at the <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident help guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="pain-suffering">
<h2>What injuries let the family sue for pain and suffering?</h2>
<p>Under the limited right to sue, only listed injuries such as death, displaced fractures, significant scarring or a permanent injury a doctor certifies.</p>
<p>The full list is on <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">the no-fault lawsuit threshold page</a>, and building the proof on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-10-ways-to-prove-pain-and-suffering-after-an-accident">ways to prove pain and suffering</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="fees">
<h2>How much do lawyers take from settlement in NJ for a minor?</h2>
<p>At most 25 percent if a minor or mentally incapacitated client settles before a jury is picked or a bench trial starts.</p>
<p>Adult fee rules, and what to check before agreeing to any offer, are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-8-questions-to-ask-before-accepting-a-settlement-offer">questions to ask before accepting a settlement offer</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions families ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>What qualifies as a serious car accident?</h3></summary>
<p>In New Jersey crash records, one where police grade someone "suspected serious injury": 6,402 crashes in 2023 and 2024. For a pain and suffering claim, the injury categories in the <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">no-fault law</a> decide, not the label.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What constitutes catastrophic injury?</h3></summary>
<p>New Jersey PIP rules single out permanent or significant brain injury, spinal cord injury and disfigurement: those keep up to $250,000 of PIP cover even on a lower limit.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Should I give a recorded statement to the other insurance company?</h3></summary>
<p>You may, but no New Jersey law sets what you must tell the other driver's insurer. Give facts only and sign nothing for the injured person. The details are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">the insurance adjuster guide</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long do you have to report an accident to your insurance company in New Jersey?</h3></summary>
<p>Report promptly: the insurer's settlement clocks start only from notice. The <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">adjuster guide</a> covers what each insurer needs.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Is it worth suing for pain and suffering?</h3></summary>
<p>Only if the injury passes the lawsuit option the household policy chose. How to document it is on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-10-ways-to-prove-pain-and-suffering-after-an-accident">ways to prove pain and suffering after an accident</a>.</p>
</details></section>
