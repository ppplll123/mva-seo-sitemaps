<h1>White Horse Pike accident today? What the Route 30 crash records show, from Camden to Atlantic City</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">We have no live feed, so an accident from today will not appear on this page. What the records do show is a pattern: on the White Horse Pike, pedestrians were 18 of the 33 people killed in 2023 and 2024.</p>
  <p>That is more than half of all deaths on the road. Across New Jersey, people killed in pedestrian accidents were about 30 percent of all road deaths in the same two years (388 of 1,294).</p>
  <p>In 2024, the most reliable year in these records, police reported 1,576 accidents on Route 30. Someone was hurt or killed in 562 of them, 816 people were hurt and 18 died.</p>
  <p>For an accident today, expect the report to sit with the local police of the municipality you were in. The report section below shows why State Police reports are rare on this road.</p>

  <h2>Why pedestrian deaths stand out on Route 30</h2>
  <p>Police coded 17 of the 32 fatal accidents in 2023 and 2024 as pedestrian accidents, with 18 people killed. The next largest group was fixed-object accidents, with 6 deaths.</p>
  <p>Light is the clearest common thread. 16 of the 18 pedestrians killed were struck in the dark, under continuous or spot street lighting or none (6, 6 and 4). Two were killed in daylight.</p>

<section id="year-gap">
  <h2>The 2023 gap on the Camden County end</h2>

<section id="cross-streets">

<section id="police-report">
  <h2>Getting the report: local police on almost all of Route 30</h2>
  <p>A State Police station appears in just 9 Route 30 records out of 4,682 from 2023 to 2025, in Camden City, Barrington, Magnolia and Galloway. Our reading is that municipal officers wrote nearly all the others. That is an inference from a sparsely documented field, not an official statement.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: station field in NJDOT crash records via hudcostreets/nj-crashes (undocumented column, indicative only).</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Town police.</strong> Start with the officers who responded. Request methods and fees vary by town, and we have not verified them town by town. Absecon, for example, posts a <a href="https://abseconnj.gov/police-department/">police department page</a> and links an OPRA request form from its city homepage. <span class="source">(abseconnj.gov, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>State Police.</strong> If a trooper wrote it, order it on the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash report portal</a>; the fee there for roads without tolls is $13.00. Email questions to NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov; if a report has not arrived after a month, call (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. <span class="source">(NJSP crash reports portal, njportal.com, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Atlantic City Expressway.</strong> If your accident was on the Expressway rather than Route 30, the report holder is different. Write to the Atlantic City Expressway Authority for those: P.O. Box 389, Hammonton, NJ 08037, (609) 965-7200 ext. 108. <span class="source">(NJSP crash reports portal, njportal.com, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>No officer came.</strong> Drivers cannot file an NJTR-1 themselves; that form belongs to police. With no police investigation, each driver uses the SR-1 self-report. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
  </ul>
  <p>When you get the report, check the cross street and milepost against what you remember, and check the names of everyone in the car. New Jersey's insurance department advises telling your insurance company and the responding police department if you find mistakes. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25)</span></p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on the White Horse Pike. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Which Route 30 records this page uses</h2>
  <p>We kept NJDOT records with route number 30 and a road name starting "US 30".
