<section id="getting-the-report">
<h2>How do I look up an accident report for a Newark crash?</h2>
<p>It depends on who took it: 2,406 of Newark's 19,696 accidents in 2023 and 2024 were State Police cases.</p>
<p>Those came from the Newark, Totowa and Somerville stations, mostly on I-78, the Turnpike (see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-turnpike-accidents">NJ Turnpike crash data</a>) and I-280. The Turnpike and Parkway alone had 1,468 accidents inside the city, and their reports use the state portal's toll road option.</p>
<p>For city streets, Newark Police Division records go through the City Clerk's GovPilot open records form. The Clerk is at 920 Mayor Kenneth A Gibson Boulevard, Room 306 (973-733-6574); the Police Division is at 480 Clinton Avenue (973-733-6000).</p>
<p>Neither office publishes a crash report fee. The basic crash report is a public record; serious and fatal crash investigation files are not.</p>
<p>You can request State Police and toll road reports online. Newark's online police report program covers other incidents, not crashes, but if no officer came, its File a Police Report page links the state SR-1 form.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<p>State Police say a report can take 14 business days to become searchable. Fees, waits and NJ Transit Police steps are in the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide, agency by agency</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="pedestrians">
<h2>How often are pedestrians hit by cars in Newark?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">About once a day: 826 police-reported pedestrian accidents in 2023 and 2024.</strong> That is 9.7 percent of New Jersey's 8,521, in a city with 4.7 percent of the state's injury accidents.</p>
<p>Pedestrian accidents are 14 percent of Newark's injury accidents, against 7 percent statewide, and 290 more involved a cyclist. Right-angle accidents (25 percent) and sideswipes (12 percent) run above the state's 22 and 9.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<p>Does PIP cover you as a pedestrian? Usually yes, from your own or a household member's auto policy, even though no car of yours was involved. The <a href="/new-jersey/hit-by-car-pedestrian-cyclist-nj-appeals">New Jersey appeals on pedestrians and cyclists</a> show who pays when you have no policy, and <a href="/new-jersey/pedestrian-crash-new-jersey">what to do after a pedestrian crash</a> covers the first steps.</p>
</section>
<section id="after-dark">
<h2>What happened in fatal car accidents in Newark?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">63 people died in Newark accidents in 2023 and 2024, 30 and then 33.</strong> Two in three of those deaths, 42, came in accidents recorded as dark.</p>
<p>Statewide only 51 percent of deaths came after dark. Pedestrians were 24 of Newark's 63 dead, 38 percent, against 32 percent across New Jersey.</p>
<p>11 percent of Newark's injury accidents happened between midnight and 6 am, against 8 percent statewide. The busiest stretch is 3 pm to 6 pm, with 1,090 injury accidents.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

<section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us.</strong> They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Newark. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></li>
</ul>
