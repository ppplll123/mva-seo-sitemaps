<h1>Had a car accident on the Garden State Parkway? 4,763 people were hurt here in two years, and Union County's stretch has the most accidents where someone was hurt.</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Police reported 16,688 car accidents on the Garden State Parkway in 2023 and 2024. Someone was hurt or killed in 3,461 of them, about 4.7 a day. In total 4,763 people were hurt and 40 were killed.</p>
  <p>The two ends of the Parkway look very different in the records. The northern stretch through Union, Essex, Passaic and Bergen counties had 1,837 accidents where someone was hurt, but 8 deaths. The southern stretch through Ocean, Burlington, Atlantic and Cape May counties had a third as many, 628, yet 17 deaths.</p>
  <p>This page does not show live traffic or today's Parkway accidents. It covers police-reported accidents from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 still preliminary.</p>

<section id="crash-report">
  <h2>Getting the police report for a Parkway accident</h2>
  <p>Garden State Parkway crash reports are requested through the State's crash report portal for the New Jersey State Police and the New Jersey Turnpike Authority, at <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>The basic NJTR-1 report for a Parkway accident can be downloaded there once it is "ready and approved". If it is not there yet, search again in a few days.</li>
    <li>Toll road crash reports cost $5.00. Reports stay available for six years from the year of the crash.</li>
    <li>For a fatal accident on the Parkway, the NJTR-1 can only be requested with the OPRA form on the <a href="https://www.njta.com/forms-public-records/opra-form">NJTA website</a>.</li>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on the Garden State Parkway. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
  <h2>Method and data</h2>
  <p>An accident where someone was hurt is a police-reported accident in which at least one person was hurt or killed, as coded on the New Jersey crash report (NJTR-1). We counted NJDOT records for 2023, 2024 and 2025 with route number 444 and a state highway road name, so local roads that share the number are left out. Almost all of these records name "GARDEN STATE PARKWAY"; 61 records over the three years name a crossing highway instead, such as I-280.</p>
  <p>Source: NJDOT crash records (2023 from the NJDOT per-table archive, 2024 and 2025 from the <a href="https://njdot.aashtowaresafety.net/njdot-crash-data-dashboard#/">NJDOT Crash Data Portal</a>), normalized by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>, with fatal accidents supplemented from the <a href="https://nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/">NJ State Police</a>. File downloaded 2026-11-06; all retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
  <p>Coverage: 2023 and 2024 are complete. 2025 is preliminary; for the Parkway it holds 98 percent of the 2024 total. Headline counts use 2023 and 2024 only.</p>
  <p>Mileposts use 2024 and 2025 only, because NJDOT changed how it records locations between the 2023 and 2024 files. Nearly every Parkway record in those years has a milepost (16,242 of 16,256). Cross street names are not merged across spelling variants. The records have no usable direction field for the Parkway, so northbound and southbound are not split.</p>
  <p>Counts are counts, not rates. Busier stretches carry more cars and will show more accidents. None of these numbers says who caused any accident.</p>
  <p>We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file; the method notes on this page describe the selection.</p>
