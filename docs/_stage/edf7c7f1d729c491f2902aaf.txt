<section id="report">
<h2>How long do you have to report a car accident in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Call the police at once if anyone is hurt or one person's property damage tops $500.</strong> The driver also owes the Motor Vehicle Commission a written report within 10 days, unless an officer files one.</p>
<p>A knowing failure to report carries a $30 to $100 fine. If police did not investigate, NJDOT's SR-1 form gives an online acknowledgment you can send to an insurer.</p>
<p>The report stays with the agency that took it. The basic report is a public record; serious or fatal investigation files are not.</p>

<section id="medical-pip">
<h2>Who pays my medical bills after a car accident in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Your own PIP pays first, whoever was at fault, unless you made health insurance primary.</strong> So yes, New Jersey is a no-fault (PIP) state.</p>
<p>The common PIP limit is $250,000, and bills above it can be claimed from the at-fault driver. In May 2026 the NJ Supreme Court held that future bills within your remaining PIP limit still go through PIP.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

<p>Deductible, co-pay and limits are in <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and medical bills</a>, the primary choice in <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP or health insurance first</a>. What no-fault does not mean is in <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">is NJ a no-fault state</a>; pay you missed is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-can-you-claim-lost-wages-after-a-car-accident-in-nj">claiming lost wages</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="claim-other-driver">
<h2>What happens if the other driver is at fault?</h2>
<p>You claim from that driver's bodily injury liability insurer, which DOBI says pays pain, suffering and economic losses such as lost wages.</p>
<p>Pain and suffering depends on your lawsuit option. Under "limitation on lawsuit", the default if you never chose in writing, the injury must fit a listed type such as a displaced fracture or a doctor-certified permanent injury.</p>
<p>That filter bites in court: of 13 New Jersey appeals rulings on it since 2018, 7 found the injury did not qualify, and 2 upheld verdicts of $240,000 and $930,500. Every ruling is in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-appeals-index">index of NJ car accident court decisions</a> and the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">verbal threshold appeals rulings</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<h3>Can I still recover if I was partly at fault?</h3>
<p>Yes, if your share is not greater than the other side's. DOBI's example: at 10% fault you get 90%, at 80% nothing. Jury splits are in <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">real NJ fault splits</a>.</p>
<h3>What if the driver had no insurance, or someone died?</h3>
<p>Your own UM or UIM coverage may pay: see the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claim guide</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">UM and UIM appeals decisions</a>. After a death, start with the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-wrongful-death-accident-claim-support-guide">wrongful death claim guide</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-families-should-know-after-a-serious-car-accident-in-nj">what families should know after a serious crash</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="deadlines">
<h2>How long do you have to sue after a car accident in NJ?</h2>
<p>Two years for an injury lawsuit, but a claim against a public body needs written notice within 90 days.</p>
<ul>
<li>Injury lawsuit: two years after the claim arises, under state law.</li>
<li>Town, state, NJ Transit or Turnpike Authority: notice by day 90; late notice within one year only in extraordinary circumstances.</li>
<li>Unpaid PIP benefits: a separate clock, generally two years, tied to when expenses arose and PIP last paid.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></li>
</ul>

<p>All seven clocks, including suing the state, are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">every deadline after a NJ car accident</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="sued-personally">
<h2>Can someone sue you personally after a car accident in NJ?</h2>
<p>Yes: a person hurt in a crash you caused can sue you, and DOBI says bodily injury liability coverage pays for claims and lawsuits like that.</p>
<p>A Standard Policy issued or renewed from 2026 carries at least $35,000 per person and $70,000 per accident; one from 2023 to 2025, $25,000 and $50,000. The Basic Policy leaves this coverage out, and DOBI warns its insurer will not defend you.</p>
<p>Whether you owe anything above your limit depends on the claim. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">NJ minimum insurance limits in 2026</a> and the injured person's two-year clock in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">every deadline after a NJ car accident</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="insurer-contact">
<h2>What should I tell the insurance company?</h2>
<p>Only the facts you know, with gaps marked, and ask for separate claim numbers for medical bills, the car and the injury claim.</p>
<p>Read the full written release before signing, and treat a policy limit as a ceiling, not a value: there is <a href="/new-jersey/average-car-accident-settlement-nj-no-public-data">no public NJ settlement average</a>. Recorded statements and what not to say are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>.</p>
<p>To complain about an insurer, use its internal appeal first, then DOBI's Insurance Claims Ombudsman.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

</section>
