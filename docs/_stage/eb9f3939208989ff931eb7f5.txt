<h1>Route 9 NJ accident data: one route filed three ways, and the small towns where it holds half the injury crashes</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Police filed 2,158 Route 9 car accidents with someone hurt or killed across 2023 and 2024. The toll over those two years was 2,939 injured people and 27 deaths.</p>
  <p>Route 9 sits in the records under three filings. The main line runs from Lower Township in Cape May County to Woodbridge. Bergen County has its own Route 9W and "western alignment" records with separate mileposts, and the shared Route 1&amp;9 is filed under Route 1.</p>
  <p>In Tuckerton and Eagleswood Township, where police sometimes record Route 9 as Main Street, it holds 59 and 52 percent of all injury accidents. On Route 9W, it is 50 percent of Englewood Cliffs' total.</p>
  <p>Deaths skew toward people on foot: walkers were 14 of the 27 dead, against roughly a third statewide (409 of 1,294). With preliminary 2025 added, it is 19 of 36 since 2023.</p>
  <p>Nothing here is real time. The records run from 2023 to 2025, and the preliminary 2025 file already holds 99 percent of Route 9's 2024 count.</p>

<section id="three-roads">
  <h2>One route number, filed three ways</h2>
  <p>Most Route 9 records follow one line from Lower Township in Cape May County to Woodbridge, with recorded mileposts running from under 1 in Lower Township to about 136 in Woodbridge. <section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 9. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
  <h2>How we counted Route 9</h2>
  <p>Our Route 9 file comes from the hudcostreets/nj-crashes copy of NJDOT's 2023 to 2025 crash records, with State Police fatal data filled in (downloaded 2026-11-06). The 2023 and 2024 years are final; every 2025 figure carries a preliminary label.</p>
  <p>Selection: the route field must read 9, and the road name must begin with NJ, US, I- or a toll road name. That catches "US 9 SECONDARY", "US 9W" and "US 9 WESTERN ALIGNMENT" in one set. It drops county roads numbered 9 and the Route 1&amp;9 records filed under Route 1.</p>
  <p>Two small edge sets are left as filed. 21 records coded route 9 carry a different road name, mostly another route such as NJ 440, NJ 184, NJ 72, NJ 35, NJ 70, NJ 33 or NJ 83; they stay in the counts. 108 records named "US 9." but coded to another route or none, many of them parking-lot rows, are not counted.</p>
  <p>"Someone hurt or killed" means the police coded at least one injury or death. Cross streets use only 2024 and 2025, with spellings kept apart, so each is a floor. No traffic volumes are applied, so a high count here does not mean a high risk rate.</p>
  <p>We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file; the method notes on this page describe the selection.</p>
