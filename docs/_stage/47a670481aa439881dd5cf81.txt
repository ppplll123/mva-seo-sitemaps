<h1>Paterson NJ car accidents: 451 pedestrian accidents in two years, twice the state's share</h1>

<p>Paterson had 11,466 police-reported car accidents in 2023 and 2024. Those records count 3,967 people hurt and 18 killed, spread over 2,785 accidents with at least one injury or death. Only one of New Jersey's 573 municipalities had more accidents where someone was hurt.</p>
<p>People on foot are where Paterson breaks most sharply from the state pattern. Pedestrian accidents are 13.5 percent of the city's accidents where someone was hurt, against 6.6 percent statewide. Paterson alone holds 451 of Passaic County's 835 pedestrian accidents.</p>

<h2 id="pedestrians">People on foot: where Paterson differs most from New Jersey</h2>
<p>In 2023 and 2024, 451 Paterson accidents were coded as pedestrian accidents, and 376 of them hurt or killed someone. That is 54 percent of all pedestrian accidents in Passaic County, from a city with 35 percent of the county's accidents where someone was hurt.</p>
<p>Statewide, Paterson has 2.5 percent of New Jersey's accidents where someone was hurt but 5.3 percent of its pedestrian accidents.</p>

<h2 id="accident-types">Right-angle and parked-car accidents lead here, not rear-end ones</h2>
<p>Across New Jersey, rear-end accidents are the most common accident where someone is hurt. In Paterson, right-angle accidents lead, at 31.4 percent against 22.4 percent statewide.</p>
<p>A second pattern shows up in the full record count. Hitting a parked vehicle is Paterson's single most common accident type, 2,442 of 11,466 records. Few of those hurt anyone: 133 did.</p>

<h3>I-80 and other State Police reports</h3>
<p>Request State Police reports online through the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP accident report portal</a>. The State Police records bureau takes questions by email at NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and by phone at (609) 882-2000, extension 2234.</p>

<h2 id="after-dark">13 of the 18 deaths happened after dark</h2>
<p>Of the 18 people killed in Paterson in 2023 and 2024, 13 died in accidents the police coded as dark. Ten of those were on streets with continuous street lights. Dark accidents were 35 percent of the city's accidents where someone was hurt, against 28 percent statewide.</p>
<p>By day of the week, Friday and Sunday tie for the most accidents where someone was hurt, 449 each. The busiest three hours are 3 pm to 6 pm, with 602 of the 2,785.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Paterson. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<h2 id="method">The Paterson City records behind these tables</h2>
<p>An accident where someone was hurt is a police-reported accident in which at least one person was hurt or killed, as coded on the NJTR-1. Each table draws on the NJDOT records coded to "Paterson City" in Passaic County, whether the accident was on I-80, a state route, a county road or a city street.</p>
<p>2023 and 2024 are complete years, and the headline figures use only those. 2025 is preliminary and visibly incomplete for Paterson. The intersection ranking uses 2024 and 2025, because street names were written differently in the 2023 export. The station table uses the same two years for a different reason: in the 2023 file the station field is mostly empty for Paterson. Counts are counts, not rates: a busier road has more accidents because more people use it.</p>
