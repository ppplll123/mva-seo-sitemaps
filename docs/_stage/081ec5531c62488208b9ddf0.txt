<h1>Route 38 NJ accident records: injuries cluster in Cherry Hill, while 8 of 12 deaths came at milepost 13 and beyond</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Police reported 1,643 car accidents on Route 38 in New Jersey in 2023 and 2024. Someone was hurt or killed in 456 of them, more than four a week. In all, 635 people were hurt and 10 were killed.</p>
  <p>The injuries cluster at the western end. In 2024, 78 of the road's 232 accidents where someone was hurt were in Cherry Hill, a third of the total.</p>
  <p>The deaths do not follow that pattern. Eight of the 12 people killed from 2023 to 2025 died at milepost 13 or higher, the Hainesport to Southampton stretch. That stretch had only 22 percent of the injury accidents. Five of the 12 were walking or riding a bicycle.</p>
  <p>The 2025 records are preliminary. They already hold 935 accidents, 1.14 times the 2024 total, with 242 where someone was hurt and 2 deaths so far.</p>

<section id="two-stretches">
  <h2>Mileposts 0 to 4.4 and 13 onward: one road, two crash patterns</h2>
  <p>We split Route 38 by the NJDOT milepost on each record, not by town name. That avoids the town coding problems in the 2023 file described further down.</p>
  <p>The first 4.4 miles, through Pennsauken and Cherry Hill, had 193 accidents where someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024. Seven in ten of those were rear-end collisions. Three people died there from 2023 to 2025.</p>
  <p>From milepost 13 east, through Hainesport, Lumberton, Mount Holly and Southampton, the records show 101 such accidents. That is about half as many, yet eight people died. Rear-end collisions were under half of the injury accidents there, and right-angle collisions were more common than at the western end.</p>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Finding the Route 38 police report: town departments, and records that point to State Police in Hainesport and Southampton</h2>
  <p>The New Jersey crash report (NJTR-1) is written by the police agency that investigated. NJDOT describes it as a police-only form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> On Route 38, which agency that is depends on where the accident happened.</p>
  <p>The records carry a field that names a State Police station. On Route 38 it is filled for 161 of 187 accidents in Hainesport (Bordentown station) and 47 of 57 in Southampton (Red Lion station), 2023 to 2025. In Cherry Hill, Pennsauken, Maple Shade and Moorestown it is almost never filled. We read it as a pointer, not proof; the agency named on your report is what counts.</p>
  <p class="source" data-file="route-38-nj-accidents/route38_station_field_by_town_2023_2025.csv">Source: NJDOT crash records via hudcostreets/nj-crashes, "station" field. Lumberton has it on 7 of 148 records, Mount Laurel on 3 of 463, Mount Holly on 2 of 70.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Cherry Hill.</strong> Cherry Hill Township's police records page states that reports "are available to involved persons and can be picked up during regular business hours at the Police Records window." You will find that window on the Municipal Building's first floor, and the Records Unit answers at 856-488-7822. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.chnj.gov/138/Police-Records">Cherry Hill Township, Police Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Pennsauken.</strong> The police Central Records Division keeps motor vehicle accident reports. Its window is open Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., phone 856-488-0080. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.pennsauken.gov/320/Records-Forms-and-Reports">Pennsauken Township, Records, Forms, and Reports</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Lumberton.</strong> The police department's site links its "Crash Reports" button to the LexisNexis police report search for the department, and lists a records and reports line, 609-267-1111. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.lumbertonpolice.com/">Lumberton Township Police Department</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Hainesport and Southampton, where the records point to State Police, and any other State Police case.</strong> A State Police crash report is ordered through the NJSP online portal. On a non-toll road like Route 38 it costs $13.00. Once the report is ready and approved it can be downloaded, and the portal keeps it for six years counted from the year of the crash. No report showing yet? The portal's advice is to come back and repeat the search after a couple of days. For records questions, the bureau lists NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a> and the NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Maple Shade, Moorestown, Mount Laurel, Mount Holly.</strong> Their police records pages did not load for us on 2026-11-10, so this page gives no process or fee for them. Contact the township police department that handled your accident, or see our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a>.</li>
  </ul>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 38. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
