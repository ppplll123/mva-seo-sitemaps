<section id="severity">
<h2>What happens if a pedestrian gets hit by a car in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Most of the time someone is hurt.</strong> Police coded 7,439 of the 8,521 pedestrian crashes in 2023 and 2024 as injuring or killing a person, which is 87 percent.</p>

<section id="when">
<h2>When are pedestrians most likely to be hit by a car?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Late afternoon into evening, and in the darker months.</strong> Advice that stops at crosswalks is incomplete: just over half of these crashes happened away from an intersection, and 260 of 385 deaths came after dark.</p>
<ul>
<li>3,836 crashes, or 45 percent, happened between 3 pm and 9 pm.</li>
<li>December had 926 pedestrian crashes and July 563, so the darkest month ran 64 percent above midsummer.</li>
<li>October to December held 31 percent of the year's pedestrian crashes.</li>
<li>4,405 of the 8,521, just over half, happened away from an intersection.</li>
</ul>
<p>Darkness matters most for deaths. Only 37 percent of pedestrian crashes happened after dark, yet 260 of the 385 fatal ones did. One local pattern: <a href="/new-jersey/egg-harbor-township-nj-car-accidents">pedestrian deaths on Route 40 in Egg Harbor</a>.</p>
<p>The <a href="/new-jersey/most-dangerous-intersections-nj">ranking of the most dangerous intersections in New Jersey</a> shows which crossings collect the most injury crashes of every kind.</p>

<section id="report">
<h2>Is a pedestrian crash report public in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The basic crash investigation report is a public record, the state portal says.</strong> Ask the agency that responded; fees and waits for each agency are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">guide to getting a New Jersey crash report</a>.</p>
<p>When a pedestrian dies or is seriously hurt, the State Police investigation file is separate. It costs $20 on the portal and is not a public record, so release needs a subpoena or court order.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="first-week">
<h2>What should a pedestrian write down after being hit?</h2>
<p>Write down the details that only a walker can give, while they are fresh:</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Where exactly you were walking: a marked crossing, a sidewalk across a driveway, or a gap between parked cars.</li>
<li>That you kept the clothes and shoes you wore.</li>
<li>Which agency responded and the case number.</li>
<li>Whether a bus, town truck or other public vehicle hit you, because the notice clock against a public body is 90 days.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></li>
</ol>

<p>The 90-day notice steps are set out in our <a href="/new-jersey/hit-by-car-pedestrian-cyclist-nj-appeals">pedestrian and cyclist appeals guide</a>. Pain that starts days later is covered in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-long-car-accident-symptoms-can-take-to-show-up">how long crash symptoms can take to show up</a>.</p>
