<h1>Route 130 accident records in New Jersey: 2024 counts, deaths after dark, and who has your report</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">In 2024, police reported 2,537 car accidents on Route 130 in New Jersey. In 719 of them someone was hurt or killed, about two a day. 1,042 people were hurt and 18 were killed.</p>
  <p>Pennsauken in Camden County had more of these accidents than any other town on the road in 2024, with 94. Seven of the road's 18 deaths that year were in Pennsauken.</p>
  <p>The 2025 records are preliminary, but they already hold 2,740 accidents, slightly more than 2024. They count 721 accidents where someone was hurt and 8 deaths so far.</p>

  <h2>Why we lead with 2024: thin 2023 records in Pennsauken, Haddon and Woodlynne</h2>
  <p>The data file treats 2023 as a complete year statewide. For three Camden County towns on Route 130, it is not. Pennsauken has 66 police-reported accidents of any kind in 2023, against 1,418 in 2024. Haddon Township has 12 against 216, and Woodlynne has 12 against 29.</p>
  <p>On Route 130 itself, Pennsauken has 18 accidents in 2023 and 359 in 2024. So any two-year total for 2023 and 2024 leaves out most of Pennsauken's 2023 accidents.</p>
  <p>That gap changes the ranking. Over 2023 and 2024 combined, North Brunswick looks like the town with the most accidents where someone was hurt, 132 against Pennsauken's 101. In 2024 alone, the only year complete for both, Pennsauken leads with 94 against North Brunswick's 71.</p>
  <p>The death counts hold up better, since the file adds fatal accidents from State Police records. Even the thin 2023 Pennsauken file shows two Route 130 deaths that year.</p>
  <p>We cannot vouch for 2024 in every town either. Haddon's town-wide count jumps from 216 in 2024 to 358 in preliminary 2025, and Woodlynne's from 29 to 62. The records do not show whether those 2024 counts are short or accidents really rose.</p>
  <p>Two Burlington County towns show the reverse. The records place 24 Route 130 accidents in Westampton and 10 in Lumberton in 2023, then none in 2024 or 2025. <section id="method">
  <h2>How the Route 130 records were counted</h2>
  <p>We kept a record when its route number is 130 and its road name has a state or US prefix; on this road that means "US 130" and "US 130 SECONDARY". That takes in traffic in both directions and leaves out any county road numbered 130. On this page, "someone hurt or killed" means the police listed at least one injured or dead person on the report.</p>
  <p>The records are NJDOT's: the 2023 file from its per-table archive, and 2024 and 2025 from its Crash Data Portal. The open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">nj-crashes project</a> cleaned them and added fatal accidents from the <a href="https://www.nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/index.shtml">NJ State Police fatal crash statistics</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). We used the copy we downloaded on 2026-11-06.</p>
  <p>The 2023 town gaps are set out above. The 2025 Route 130 count is preliminary, at 1.08 times the 2024 count, so the year does not look short yet; reports filed late can still be added. We rank cross streets on 2024 and 2025 because the 2023 export writes street names differently.</p>
  <p>We have no Route 130 traffic counts, so no figure here is a risk rate. A town with more Route 130 accidents may just have a longer or busier piece of the road. Nor can any count show who caused a particular accident.</p>
  <p class="source" data-file="lane2-content/data/README.md; lane2-content/data/route-130-nj-accidents/route130_cuts.py">Counting script route130_cuts.py (run 2026-11-10); shared data rules in lane2-content/data/README.md.</p>
</section>

<section id="after-dark">

  <p>14 of the people killed were pedestrians, and all 14 were hit in the dark. In nine of those accidents the police recorded street lights on. In four there were no street lights, and in one the street lights were off.</p>
  <p>Pedestrian accidents are rare on this road: 51 in 2023 and 2024, about 1 percent of all accidents. Yet pedestrians were 11 of the 30 people killed in those two years. Statewide in the same years, pedestrians were 409 of 1,294 people killed.</p>

<section id="your-report">
  <h2>Who wrote the report for an accident on Route 130</h2>
  <p>Of 7,387 Route 130 records from 2023 to 2025, 57 name a State Police station, most often Woodstown in Salem County (29). The station field is undocumented and blank on the other records, so it cannot tell you which agency wrote your report. Two report routes exist, depending on who came to the scene.</p>
  <p class="source" data-file="lane2-content/data/route-130-nj-accidents/route130_state_police_station.csv">Source: route130_state_police_station.csv, a tally of the undocumented "station" column in the NJDOT records as cleaned by hudcostreets/nj-crashes.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Town police responded:</strong> ask that department's records unit for the New Jersey police accident report (NJTR-1). Only police complete that form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Pennsauken:</strong> the police department's Central Records Division keeps reports of motor vehicle accidents. Its phone is 856-488-0080, with window hours Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.pennsauken.gov/320/Records-Forms-and-Reports">Pennsauken Township Police, Records, Forms, and Reports</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>State Police responded:</strong> request the report through the State Police online portal. The portal charges $13.00 for a report from a non-toll road such as Route 130 and keeps reports for six years from the year of the accident. The portal's email for trouble with the online service is njsprecords@njsp.gov. For questions about non-toll road crash reports, it gives (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. If you filed a records request for a serious or fatal accident, the portal asks you to call that number only after one month has passed. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
  </ul>
  <p>Read the report through once you have it. The state Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI) puts it this way: "If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <p>We could not verify records pages for the other Route 130 towns. Their fees and steps vary by department.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 130. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
