<h1>Route 31 NJ accident today: animal strikes, curves and where to get your report</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">This page does not track live Route 31 accidents. It covers police-reported car accidents on Route 31 from 2023 to 2025, and tells you which police agency is likely to hold your report.</p>
  <p>In 2023 and 2024, police reported 1,850 car accidents on Route 31, which runs through Mercer, Hunterdon and Warren counties. Someone was hurt or killed in 436 of them, 656 people were hurt and 8 people died.</p>
  <p>Two things set Route 31 apart. About one in seven of its accidents involved an animal, about four times the statewide share, and most of those came in October and November. And half of its deaths came from head-on collisions, all three of them on curves.</p>
  <p class="source">Counts: our tally of NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025, using the cleaned files published by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10), with fatal accidents filled in from <a href="https://nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/">NJ State Police fatal crash statistics</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). How Route 31 records were picked is under "How the Route 31 records were selected".</p>

  <h2>Animal strikes: 281 accidents, most of them in October and November</h2>
  <p>Route 31 had 281 animal accidents in 2023 and 2024, 15.2 percent of all its police-reported accidents. Statewide, animals were 3.9 percent (20,082 of 514,320). The records do not name the animal.</p>
  <p>October (81) and November (72) together held 153 of the 281, or 54 percent. Most animal accidents damaged vehicles without hurting anyone: 15 of the 281 had someone hurt or killed.</p>

  <h2>The 8 deaths: head-on collisions on curves stand out</h2>

  <p>All three fatal head-on records were coded on a curve. Two were on wet, downhill stretches in daylight; one was at night on a road with no street lights. With numbers this small, treat this as a description of what happened, not a measure of risk.</p>

<section id="report-route">
  <h2>Who holds a Route 31 report: the station field, town by town</h2>
  <p>Route 31 is a state highway, yet most of its accidents are written up by local departments rather than troopers. Ewing, Flemington, Pennington, Readington and Trenton had no Route 31 record naming a State Police station in 2024 and 2025.</p>

  <h3>Ewing, Hopewell and Raritan: the three town departments we checked</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Ewing Township</strong> (Route 31 is Pennington Road here): the Records Bureau says most accident reports are public information and are available in person for a nominal fee. Phone 609-882-1313 ext. 7553 or 7543, email policerecords@ewingnj.org. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.ewingpolice.com/divisions/records">Ewing Police Records Bureau</a>, linked from ewingnj.org, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Hopewell Township</strong>: crash report copies can be requested from the records clerk by phone or in person, or obtained online for $7 by searching by date, name or case number. Records clerk (609) 737-3100 option 2, Monday to Friday 8:30 am to 4:30 pm. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.hopewellpolicenj.gov/divisions/records">Hopewell Township Police Records Bureau</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Raritan Township</strong> (around Flemington): a party listed in the report can get a copy in person at the police station or through crashdocs.org with the details the officer gave. If officers did not investigate, the department points drivers to the state SR-1 self-report form. Phone 908-782-8889. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.raritantownshippolice.com/accident-reports">Raritan Township Police, Accident Reports</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>

<section id="method">
  <h2>How the Route 31 records were selected</h2>
  <p>We kept NJDOT crash records with route number 31 and a road name starting with "NJ", "US" or "I-". That leaves out county and local roads that also carry the number 31. The main roadway is recorded as "NJ 31". The Flemington cross street called Route 202 above is recorded as "US 202 Secondary".</p>
  <p>Headline counts use 2023 and 2024, the two complete years. Tables of mileposts, cross streets and State Police stations start in 2024: the 2023 export names streets differently from the later files. In every table, an injury accident is a record where the officer listed one or more people as hurt or dead.</p>
  <p>No traffic volumes are used, so these counts cannot show whether one stretch is riskier per car than another. Nor do they reveal fault in a single Route 31 accident, or anything about a possible claim or its value.</p>
  <p class="source">We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file, downloaded 2026-11-06; the method notes on this page describe the selection.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 31. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
