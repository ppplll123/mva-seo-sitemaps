<section id="how-common">
<h2>How common are rear-end crashes in New Jersey?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">They are the state's most common injury crash.</strong> In 2023 and 2024, 34,717 rear-end crashes hurt or killed someone: about a quarter of all 139,205 rear-end crashes, and 30.7 percent of every injury crash.</p>
<p>They are rarely fatal. Rear-end crashes killed 86 people in those two years, against 295 in crashes into fixed objects. What gets argued afterwards is how lasting a neck or back injury is.</p>
<p>The pattern is commuter traffic. 78 percent of rear-end injury crashes happened on a weekday, and Friday had the most (5,714). Only 14.7 percent were on wet pavement, below the 16.5 percent for all injury crashes.</p>
<p>Busy corridors run higher still: see our <a href="/new-jersey/route-36-nj-accidents">Route 36 rear-end crash data</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/paramus-route-4-route-17-shopping-trip-crash">rear-end crashes on Paramus shopping trips</a>.</p>

<section id="fault">
<h2>Who is most at fault in a rear-end collision?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Not always the driver behind.</strong> New Jersey splits fault by percentage among every driver whose conduct caused the crash, and your damages shrink by your own share. Queues and driveways complicate this, as with <a href="/new-jersey/route-70-cherry-hill-crash-guide">Route 70 rear-end crashes in Cherry Hill</a>.</p>
<p>In the 2025 Cain case, a lead car's sudden stop after an aborted illegal U-turn shifted fault. The jury split it 65 and 35 percent between the two other drivers, and the $500,000 award stood.</p>
<p>Fault is rarely the real fight. In the rear-end threshold appeals below, nobody won or lost on who hit whom: Wallace lost on the injury, while Lamothe and Presbery won on it.</p>
<p>For the payout math at 10, 50 or 80 percent and how to appeal an insurer's split, see <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how fault splits work in New Jersey</a>. If you were pushed into the car ahead, our <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/describe-multiple-vehicle-crash-nj">chain-reaction crash guide</a> covers several impacts.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>

<section id="medical-bills">
<h2>Who pays your medical bills after being rear-ended?</h2>
<p>Your own PIP pays first, whoever caused the crash: that is what <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">New Jersey no-fault insurance</a> means. Bills above your limit can be claimed from the driver at fault.</p>
<p>Deductibles, co-pays and PIP disputes are in the <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and medical bills guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<section id="police">
<h2>Do I need to call the cops if I get rear-ended?</h2>
<p>Yes, if anyone is hurt or any one person's property damage tops $500.</p>
<p>If police did not come, the SR-1 self-reporting form is the way to report it, and copies are covered in <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey car accident report</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

<section id="first-steps">
<h2>What to do immediately after getting rear-ended?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Get medical help, then keep two files apart: one for your injury, one for your car.</strong> Three steps matter most in a rear-end crash:</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>If it is safe, photograph both bumpers and the lane before the cars move.</li>
<li>Write down when neck or back pain started, even if it began the next day, and tell the clinician.</li>
<li>Keep repair estimates apart from medical records, and read any release that comes with a repair check.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>How long after a car accident can you sue in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Generally two years against the driver, and a 90-day notice applies if a public vehicle hit you. Exceptions are in our <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What not to tell insurance company after accident?</h3></summary>
<p>Do not call your injuries minor or healed while you are still treating. The other driver's insurer owes its first duty to its own policyholder, so give the facts your claim needs and stop. More in <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long can whiplash symptoms be delayed?</h3></summary>
<p>Whiplash can take several hours to begin. In our count, 60 percent of people hurt in rear-end crashes were graded only "possible injury" at the scene. PIP still pays for later treatment; see <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/nj-how-long-car-accident-symptoms-can-take-to-show-up">how long crash symptoms take to show up</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How much compensation can I expect for a herniated or bulging disc?</h3></summary>
<p>No public New Jersey average exists. The two 2025 rear-end herniation verdicts in the table were upheld on appeal: $240,000 and $930,500. Our <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">appeals settlement figures</a> compare others.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Is it worth claiming for a minor accident?</h3></summary>
<p>Your PIP pays treatment whoever caused it, so get checked. If an insurer calls it a minor impact, ask which issue it means: car damage, cause of injury, coverage and fault are separate questions.</p>
</details></section>
