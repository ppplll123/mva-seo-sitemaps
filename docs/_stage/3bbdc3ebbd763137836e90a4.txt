<section id="seven-signs">
<h2>What are the 7 signs you may need a lawyer after a New Jersey crash?</h2>
<p>You likely need one if any of these apply: unhealed injury, a threshold denial, disputed fault, losses beyond PIP, a low-limit driver, a public agency, or a release.</p>

<section id="limited-tort">
<h2>Can you still sue if you have limited tort?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes, but only for a qualifying injury, and that test is where most minor-injury claims fail.</strong> Of 13 Appellate Division threshold rulings we logged from 2018 to 2025, 7 held the injury fell short.</p>
<p>The threshold was met in 2 rulings, both 2025 Burlington County jury verdicts the Appellate Division affirmed. The other 4 rulings turned on different issues.</p>
<p>Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">verbal threshold appeals rulings</a> page lists every case, and the <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">appeals settlement and verdict figures</a> page covers the dollar amounts. Whether you are on limited tort at all, including the default when nobody chose in writing, is covered in <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">what no-fault means in New Jersey</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="not-your-fault">
<h2>Should I get a lawyer for a car accident that wasn't my fault?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Get help when you were hurt and you reject the adjuster's written fault percentage, threshold view or offer.</strong> Fault here is a percentage, not a yes or no.</p>
<p>Your recovery drops by your share of fault. Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">real fault splits in New Jersey appeals</a> shows how juries divided it, and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-evidence-helps-prove-fault-after-an-nj-crash">evidence that helps prove fault</a> covers what to collect.</p>
<p>If you disagree with the percentage, use the insurer's internal appeal first, then DOBI's Office of Insurance Claims Ombudsman. DOBI says neither step removes your right to sue.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="fees">
<h2>What percentage do lawyers take from a car accident settlement in NJ?</h2>
<p>At most 33 1/3 percent of the first $750,000 of the net recovery, under Court Rule 1:21-7, with lower caps on larger amounts.</p>

<ul>
<li>The fee is figured after case costs, but medical liens are not deducted first.</li>
<li>A contingent fee is a share of the recovery, so with no recovery there is no percentage to take. Ask in writing who pays case costs if the claim fails.</li>
<li>The lawyer must first offer an hourly or reasonable-value fee instead.</li>
<li>The agreement must be written and signed, and you get a signed closing statement at the end.</li>
<li>For a minor, a settlement before a jury is chosen is capped at 25%.</li>
<li>An intake caller is not the lawyer who decides on your case. Check a lawyer's license through an official New Jersey source.</li>
</ul>

</section>
<section id="minor-injury">
<h2>Is it worth claiming for a minor accident?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">For car damage alone, a property claim with your insurer is usually enough and no lawyer is needed.</strong> An injury claim is worth a look only while symptoms or unpaid losses remain.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>You recovered and PIP paid the bills.</strong> With no fault dispute and no open treatment, you can deal with the insurer yourself. Still read any release first.</li>
<li><strong>The fight is over a PIP payment.</strong> That goes to DOBI's arbitration program, not to a claim against the other driver; see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and medical bills</a>.</li>
<li><strong>The other driver carried only the minimum.</strong> Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">NJ minimum insurance limits for 2026</a> page shows the bodily injury floor by policy date.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></li>
</ul>

</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Common questions</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>How long does it take to settle a car accident lawsuit in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>No public source tracks NJ settlement times. In 11 NJ crash injury lawsuits that reached the Appellate Division, the median was 4 calendar years from filing to the appeal decision. See <a href="/new-jersey/how-long-car-accident-settlement-takes-nj">how long NJ car accident settlements take</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How likely is it to get sued after a car accident?</h3></summary>
<p>If the other side sues, your bodily injury liability coverage answers the claim. DOBI warns that without liability coverage, as on a Basic Policy that does not add it, the insurer will not defend you.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can you sue someone if they hit you in a parking lot?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes. The same fault and limited tort rules apply. Our guide on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-do-after-a-parking-lot-accident-in-new-jersey">what to do after a parking lot accident</a> covers the steps.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What is the minimum bodily injury coverage in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>A Standard Policy issued or renewed on or after 1 January 2026 must carry at least $35,000 per person and $70,000 per accident. Older policies may show a lower floor.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Does PIP paying my bills mean I have no other claim?</h3></summary>
<p>No. A claim against the at-fault driver for pain, suffering and other losses is separate from PIP.</p>
</details></section>
