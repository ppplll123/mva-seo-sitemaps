<h1>Morris County car accident help: I-80 records name a State Police station, Route 46 records almost never do</h1>

<section id="answer">
<p>In 2024 and 2025, 640 of 658 Morris County I-80 accidents where someone was hurt sit in NJDOT records that name a State Police station. On Route 46 the count is 2 of 607.</p>
<p>Most I-287 records name one too (394 of 423, 93 percent), as do 160 of 163 on Route 24 and all 51 on I-280. Records on Route 10, Route 15 and Route 202 name no State Police station, and town-street records almost never do. So the town police most likely wrote them.</p>
<p>That second point is an inference. The station field is not documented by the data project, so confirm with the officer's card or the agency that came. A trooper's report goes through the online portal below; a town officer's report goes through that town's records unit. If you are hurt, medical attention comes before any paperwork.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2024 and 2025 (2025 preliminary) as normalized by hudcostreets/nj-crashes; our count of records whose station field names a State Police station.</p>

<h3 id="state-police-report">Trooper at the scene: ordering the report online</h3>
<p>Reports written by New Jersey State Police troopers are ordered through the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and Turnpike Authority crash report portal</a>. According to the portal, the NJTR-1 becomes downloadable after it is ready and approved. If yours is not listed, the portal suggests repeating the search a couple of days later.</p>
<p>A report for a non-toll road such as I-80 or I-287 costs $13.00. Reports stay on the portal for six years from the accident year. For questions, the Criminal Justice Records Bureau lists NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and (609) 882-2000, extension 2234.</p>
<p class="source">Sources, both retrieved 2026-11-10: https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/ and the State Police records bureau page.</p>

<p>I-80, I-287 and I-280 all cross the township. As a result, 359 of its 797 injury accidents in 2024 and 2025 have a State Police station in the record, about 45 percent. On an interstate, start with the State Police portal above. Route 46, Route 10, Route 202 and township street records almost never name a State Police station, so start there with the <a href="#town-police-report">Parsippany-Troy Hills Police Department</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Morris County. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
<h2>Morris data notes: exports, road rule and the station field</h2>
<p>All counts come from NJDOT crash records for 2023 to 2025, normalized by the open-source project hudcostreets/nj-crashes. The 2023 records come from the NJDOT per-table archive. The 2024 and 2025 records come from the NJDOT Crash Data Portal. Fatal accidents are supplemented from the New Jersey State Police feed (file downloaded 2026-11-06). The 2023 and 2024 files are complete; the 2025 file is still preliminary.</p>
<p>Our "hurt or killed" rows are Morris records with one or more people injured or dead. Each town total follows the NJDOT municipality code. Street names were recorded differently in the 2023 export, so intersection and cross-street rankings cover 2024 and 2025 alone.</p>
<p>A road row counts records whose route number matches and whose road name starts NJ, US or I-, whatever road-system code the record carries. Raw name variants such as "I-80" and "I-80 SECONDARY" fold into one row. This matters because many Route 202 records carry a County road-system code.</p>
<p>We call a record a State Police record when its station field names a State Police station, such as Netcong or Totowa. The data project does not document that field, so a blank station is only a sign that a local agency handled it. The field is thin in the 2023 export, so the agency share uses 2024 and 2025 only.</p>
<p>These are frequencies, not risk rates: no traffic volume is used. We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file; the method notes on this page describe the selection.</p>
