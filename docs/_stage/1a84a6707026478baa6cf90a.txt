<h1>NJ hit and run accident claim help: who pays when the driver drives off</h1><nav class="df-jump" aria-label="Jump to" data-df="added"><span>Jump to:</span><a href="#next-steps">What to do next</a><a href="#faq">Questions</a><a href="#sources">Sources</a></nav>
<p class="byline">By Sandra H. Brown, Injuries Cases Autos research. Updated <time datetime="2026-11-11">11 November 2026</time></p>
<section id="answer" class="df-answer">
<p class="df-lead"><strong class="df-k">After a New Jersey hit and run, your own PIP pays your medical bills and pain and suffering goes to your own uninsured motorist (UM) coverage.</strong> That UM pays only up to its limit for the vanished driver's share: $15,000 of a $200,000 verdict in one appeal we coded.</p>
<aside class="key-facts"><ul>
<li data-origin="original"><strong>$15,000</strong>: all a UM carrier paid for a phantom car's share of a $200,000 verdict</li>
<li><strong>$0</strong> of UM coverage on New Jersey's Basic Policy</li>
<li><strong>$500</strong>: the first damages a UM or UIM claim excludes</li>
<li><strong>10</strong> days to send your own crash report to the MVC if no officer files one</li>
</ul></aside>
<p>What most pages skip: the police report can decide the claim. Without one, DOBI warns, your insurer could deny a hit-and-run claim.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>

</section>

<section id="medical-bills">
<h2>Who pays my medical bills after a hit and run in NJ?</h2>
<p>Your own PIP pays them, up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay.</p>
<p>If you chose health insurance as primary, your health plan pays first: see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">how PIP and health insurance split medical bills</a>. With no auto insurance in the household, PIP may come from the state's Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund, covered in our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">guide to PIP limits and medical bills</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="uninsured-motorist">
<h2>Does uninsured motorist cover a hit and run in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes, if your policy has UM: New Jersey courts have treated an unidentified "phantom vehicle" as a UM matter.</strong> Your policy's UM section sets the notice and proof terms.</p>

<p>Basic Policy holders have no UM at all, and UM cannot exceed your own liability limits: see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">what UM covers and its limits</a>. Ask your insurer in writing whether UM applies when the driver was never identified, and what it needs from you.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="phantom-vehicle-cases">
<h2>What did NJ appeals courts do with phantom vehicle claims?</h2>
<p>They paid the phantom car's share only up to the UM limit, and still required proof of a lasting injury.</p>

<p>Koukounias shows a UM claim against your own carrier still needs proof of a lasting injury. More rulings are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">New Jersey UM and UIM appeals decisions</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="report">
<h2>How do I report a hit and run in NJ?</h2>
<p>Call police right away; if no officer files a report, send the MVC your own written SR-1 report within 10 days.</p>
<p>Where to get the finished report, and what it costs, is in our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">getting your New Jersey car accident report</a>. The rest of the first day is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-do-in-the-first-24-hours-after-a-new-jersey-car-accident">what to do in the first 24 hours after a crash</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

<h3>How do I find out who hit me?</h3>
<p>Police trace the driver from what you hand them, so give the officer these:</p>
<ul>
<li>A full or partial plate, and the vehicle's color and type</li>
<li>Business or home cameras facing the road</li>
<li>Witness contacts, the direction it went and the time</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="police-next">
<h2>Will police do anything about a hit and run?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">You can find the police report without the other driver's details.</strong> Once it is complete and approved, which can take 14 business days from the crash, the NJ.gov portal finds it by your own last name or plate.</p>
<p>Crashes on the Turnpike, the Parkway or Port Authority crossings are handled by those agencies, with their own fees; each route is in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="parked-car">
<h2>What if someone hit my parked car and drove off?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Report it to police, then claim under your own collision coverage if you bought it.</strong> The steps are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-property-damage-claim-help-and-steps-guide">New Jersey property damage claim guide</a>.</p>
<p>The driver who hit it had the same duty to notify police if the damage passed $500. Our count of NJDOT records shows 58,460 crashes in 2023 and 2024 where a driver struck a parked vehicle; the records do not say how many drivers left.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="if-found">
<h2>What if police find the hit and run driver?</h2>
<p>Then you may claim against that driver's bodily injury liability coverage, which pays pain, suffering and economic losses such as lost wages.</p>
<p>How much that coverage must hold depends on the policy year: see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">New Jersey minimum insurance limits for 2026</a>. If the driver's limit is below your UIM limit, your UIM may pay the difference.</p>
<p>Fault shares and your lawsuit option then matter: see <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what happens if you were partly at fault</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">New Jersey verbal threshold appeals rulings</a>. Filing deadlines are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="next-steps">
<h2>What should I do this week after a hit and run?</h2>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>Say "hit and run"</strong> when you open the claim with your insurer, and ask in writing what its UM section requires.</li>
<li><strong>Get the police report</strong> to your insurer, since without one the claim could be denied.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></li>
</ol>

<p>Calls from the adjuster are covered in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>, and any other step in our <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident guide</a>. Hit while walking or cycling? See <a href="/new-jersey/hit-by-car-pedestrian-cyclist-nj-appeals">New Jersey pedestrian and cyclist appeals</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Hit and run questions people also ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Do I have to pay anything after a hit and run?</h3></summary>
<p>Some. You pay your PIP deductible and co-pay, your collision deductible if you claim for the car, and the first $500 of damages, which a UM claim excludes.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>

</details></section>
