<p><strong>What went wrong:</strong> recovery took longer than anyone around her expected, and two years is also New Jersey's deadline to sue.</p>
<p><strong>The NJ rule:</strong> a lawsuit for an injury must be started within two years of the crash.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>
<p><strong>What to do:</strong> do not wait to feel better before asking whether you have a claim. Therapy notes from those two years are your record.</p>

<section id="can-i-claim">
<h2>Can I claim for anxiety or PTSD after a crash in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">It depends on one choice on your car policy: the lawsuit option.</strong> Find it on the page that lists your coverages (the declarations page).<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
<div class="table-wrap">
<div class="l2-tablewrap" tabindex="0" role="region" aria-label="Scrollable table"><table class="df-stack">

<p>Most NJ drivers who never ticked a box have the limitation option, because it is the default. What that option means is explained in <a href="/new-jersey/what-is-the-verbal-threshold-nj">what the verbal threshold is in NJ</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="ptsd-threshold">
<h2>Does PTSD count as a permanent injury in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The law does not name it.</strong> It calls an injury permanent when "the body part or organ" has not healed to work normally and will not with more treatment.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
<p>The doctor's certification must rest on objective clinical evidence, and testing may not be "dependent entirely upon subjective patient response," as a 2001 NJ ruling stressed.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> Many anxiety diagnoses start from what you report, so PTSD alone is harder to certify. We found no NJ appeals ruling on PTSD alone in the sources we read.</p>
<p>When a physical injury does qualify, such as a displaced fracture, the nightmares and fear of driving count as part of your pain and suffering.</p>
<p>One rule people mix up: the old NJ law let you sue if you could not do most of your daily life for 90 of the first 180 days. The 1998 reform removed that category.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>
</section>
<section id="who-pays">
<h2>Who pays for therapy after a crash in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Your own PIP, first, whoever caused the crash.</strong> It covers you and relatives living with you.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>
<ul>
<li>Send counseling bills to PIP like any other treatment, and ask the adjuster in writing what it needs before it pays.</li>
<li>If you chose health insurance as primary, those bills go to your health plan first; see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP or health insurance first</a>.</li>
<li>If you bought the income continuation part of PIP and cannot work, it pays part of lost wages; more in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-can-you-claim-lost-wages-after-a-car-accident-in-nj">lost wages after a NJ crash</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>The lawsuit option never limits bills and lost pay. Those stay claimable from the driver who caused it.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
</section>
<section id="write-it-down">
<h2>How do I prove PTSD or driving anxiety?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">With records made at the time, by doctors, in your words.</strong> Cases fall apart when there is no medical record: in 76 signed car cases a lawyer later handed back, 4 ended because the person had no injury on record or would not see a doctor.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>At every visit, say what you can no longer do: drive at night, take the highway, sit in the passenger seat.</li>
<li>Ask for a referral to a therapist and keep going; tell your doctor before you stop.</li>
<li>Keep a short log: date, what set off the panic, missed work or trips.</li>
<li>Save the names of people who saw the change, such as a partner or a coworker.</li>
</ol>
<p>Other proof that holds up is covered in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-10-ways-to-prove-pain-and-suffering-after-an-accident">how to prove pain and suffering in New Jersey</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>How long does anxiety last after a car accident?</h3></summary>
<p>There is no fixed timeline. In the group we read, one member was still in therapy two years on and driving short trips again. Track it with a doctor and mind the two-year deadline in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">NJ deadline table</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>It was my fault. Why do I feel so guilty?</h3></summary>
<p>Guilt showed up in 21 of the 387 posts, 9 of them from drivers who had caused their own crash, one of which hurt nobody. It is a common reaction, and your own PIP still pays your treatment whoever was at fault.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What if my therapist makes me feel blamed?</h3></summary>
<p>You can change therapists. One member wrote several times about therapists who blamed her for a crash she did not cause. A therapist who believes you also writes better notes.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How much compensation do you get for PTSD after a car accident?</h3></summary>
<p>There is no set amount, and we have no NJ figures for PTSD alone. It turns first on your lawsuit option and whether a qualifying injury applies. Therapy bills and lost pay are claimed separately.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What should my family know?</h3></summary>
<p>That this can take months or years and is not a choice. Our guide for <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-families-should-know-after-a-serious-car-accident-in-nj">families after a serious NJ crash</a> covers the rest.</p>
</details></section>
