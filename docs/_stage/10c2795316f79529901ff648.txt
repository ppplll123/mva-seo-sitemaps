<section id="lawsuit-time">
<h2>How long does it take to settle a car accident lawsuit in NJ?</h2>
<p>Lawsuits that went all the way to an appeal took a median of 4 calendar years from filing to the appeal decision in our count.</p>
<p>We read 76 New Jersey crash-related court decisions and kept the 11 injury lawsuits that name both a trial-court docket and an appeal docket. <section id="appeal-time">
<h2>How much time does an appeal add?</h2>
<p>Across 72 NJ crash appeals with an appeal docket number, the median was 2 calendar years from docket year to decision, ranging from 1 to 4.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>
<p>How the 72 appeals spread out:</p>
<ul>
<li>1 calendar year: 12 appeals</li>
<li>2 calendar years: 41 appeals</li>
<li>3 calendar years: 16 appeals</li>
<li>4 calendar years: 3 appeals</li>
</ul>
<p>The same 2-year median holds for the 11 injury lawsuits above.</p>
<p>One case with exact dates: in the Hacker case from Ocean County, a jury found for the plaintiff on January 19, 2023. The appeal was decided on June 13, 2025, almost two and a half years later, and the verdict was reversed.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
</section>
<section id="longest">
<h2>What is the longest a personal injury case can take?</h2>
<p>In our count, 5 calendar years from filing to the appeal decision, which 5 of the 11 injury lawsuits reached.</p>
<p>The Malzberg case from Essex County was filed in 2017 and decided on appeal in September 2022. The Hassan case from Ocean County was filed in 2016 and decided in April 2021, and that appeal reversed the trial court.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>
<p>Every case, with its docket and outcome, is in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-appeals-index">searchable index of NJ car accident court decisions</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="waits">
<h2>What legal waiting periods can slow a NJ claim?</h2>
<p>Two rules build fixed waits into some New Jersey claims: a claim against a public entity, and the doctor's certification in a limited-tort lawsuit.</p>
