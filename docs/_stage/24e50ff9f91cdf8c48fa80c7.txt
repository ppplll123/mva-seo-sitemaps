<h1>Route 33 NJ accident records, Trenton to Neptune: 655 accidents hurt someone in 2023 and 2024</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">New Jersey police reported 2,428 car accidents on Route 33 in 2023 and 2024. In 655 of them someone was hurt or killed. Those accidents hurt 936 people and killed 8.</p>
  <p>Route 33 crosses 15 towns in Mercer, Middlesex and Monmouth counties. Police records give it three different names along the way: Greenwood Avenue in Trenton and Hamilton, the Theodore J Narozanick Highway through western Monmouth, where deer accidents cluster, and Corlies Avenue in Neptune.</p>
  <p>Monmouth County had 358 of the 655 injury accidents, about 55 percent. Mercer County had 275, and Middlesex County had 22, all in Monroe Township.</p>
  <p>Everything here comes from police accident records filed for 2023, 2024 and 2025, so an accident from this week will not appear. Treat every 2025 figure as preliminary.</p>

<section id="deer">
  <h2>Deer accidents bunch up between Millstone and Howell</h2>
  <p>Freehold Township comes first: police coded a deer as the first harmful event in 39 of its 145 Route 33 records for 2023 and 2024, about one in four.</p>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Getting the report: a records window in Trenton, and a State Police check for Millstone</h2>
  <p>Your report sits with whichever agency investigated. The accident records do not state that agency outright, so treat the Millstone note below as a pointer, not proof.</p>
  <h3>Millstone Township: search the State Police portal first</h3>
  <p>The Hamilton station of the New Jersey State Police is named on 72 of the 75 Millstone Township Route 33 records for 2024 and 2025. So a Millstone report may be with the State Police. If the portal search finds nothing, ask Millstone Township who took the report. Elsewhere on Route 33 a State Police station is rarely named: 6 of 91 records in Monroe Township, one or none in each other town.</p>
  <p>State Police crash reports are requested online through the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report service</a>. The portal will not find a report until the report is completed and approved, so an early search can come back empty. A non-toll road report costs $13.00. For help with the online service, the portal gives the email njsprecords@njsp.gov. It lists (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234 for questions about non-toll road reports, and asks you to call about a serious or fatal records request only after one month has passed.</p>
  <p class="source">Sources: our station count (run 2026-11-10; the station field is undocumented, so read it as "State Police station named on the record"); <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a> and the NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page (both retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <h3>Trenton: the Trenton Police records office</h3>
  <p>Trenton says reports can be picked up in person at the records office at Trenton Police headquarters, 225 N Clinton Avenue. The office is open 9 am to 4:30 pm, Monday through Friday. Bring identification, plus either the report number or the report's date.</p>
  <p>At the window, a motor vehicle accident report costs $0.50 a page for pages 1 to 10. By mail the fee is $5. Individuals must pay it by cashier's check, while attorneys and insurance companies may send an ordinary check. Mail requests are accepted only from the person named in the report, their attorney or their insurance company.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.trentonnj.org/402/Police-Report">City of Trenton, "Police Report"</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <h3>Every other town on Route 33</h3>
  <p>For Hamilton, Neptune, Wall, Howell and the other towns, ask the police department of the town where the accident happened. Request procedures and fees vary by department, and we did not verify each one. Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a> explains the general routes.</p>
  <p>Only an officer can fill out the NJTR-1 crash report. When no officer investigated, the driver files New Jersey's SR-1 self-report instead.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT, Police Resources: crash reports</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 33. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
