<h1>Franklin NJ car accident data: three corridors carry about half the injury crashes</h1>

<p>In Franklin Township, Somerset County, about half of all car accidents where someone was hurt happen on just three corridors. NJDOT records show 978 such accidents in 2023 and 2024, and 484 of them (49%) were on Route 27, Route 514 (Hamilton Street and Amwell Road) or Route 527 (Easton Avenue). Across the township, 1,437 people were hurt in them and 8 people died.</p>
<p>Franklin leads Somerset County. It had 23% of the county's injury accidents, the most of its 21 municipalities, and ranks 19th of 573 in New Jersey. The largest share is on county roads, not interstates: I-287 accounts for only 6% of the township's injury accidents.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the NJDOT records for the township and county, and our own corridor count. NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by hudcostreets/nj-crashes, fatal crashes supplemented from NJ State Police. Raw counts with no traffic-volume adjustment. <a href="#franklin-method">Method below</a>.</p>

<section id="franklin-pedestrians">
<h2>Pedestrian crashes: preliminary 2025 already matches the two previous years combined</h2>
<p>NJDOT coded 15 pedestrian crashes in Franklin in 2023 and 19 in 2024. The preliminary 2025 count is 34, the same as both earlier years together. One pedestrian was killed in 2025; none were in 2023 or 2024.</p>
<p>The rise is local. Across Somerset County, pedestrian crashes went from 107 in 2024 to 94 in preliminary 2025. Of Franklin's 34 in 2025, 19 were on Route 27, Route 514 or Route 527. New Jersey's statewide pedestrian picture is on our <a href="/new-jersey/pedestrian-crash-new-jersey">pedestrian crash page for New Jersey</a>.</p>

<section id="franklin-report">
<h2>DeMott Lane or the Somerville station: whose report you need</h2>
<p>In Franklin, the road you crashed on is the best early clue to which agency has your report. In 2024 and 2025, 482 of the 502 Franklin crash records on I-287 name the New Jersey State Police Somerville station. On Route 27, only 4 of 818 records name any State Police station, and on Route 514 only 2 of 529. The officer at the scene or the exchange slip you were given is the surest guide.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our count (2024 and 2025). A blank station field means no State Police station was recorded; it does not by itself name the agency.</p>

<h3>Crashes handled by the Franklin Township Police Department</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The department says a report can take about 3 to 10 business days to complete, and it cannot be accessed until then. The Records Bureau answers status questions at 732-873-5533 ext. 1020, Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 3:45 pm.</li>
  <li>Online: through BuyCrash (LexisNexis) for a $12 fee. Choose New Jersey, the Franklin Township Police Department, and one of: the report number, your last name and the date, or your last name and the location.</li>
  <li>In person: at the Police Records Window, 495 DeMott Lane, Somerset, NJ 08873, Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 3:45 pm. The department says there is no fee in person.</li>
  <li>By mail: a $5 check made out to Franklin Township, with the case number, your name, address and phone number, sent to the Franklin Township Police Records Bureau at the same address.</li>
  <li>If your copy needs a correction, the department has an online statement form. The officer who wrote the report decides whether to change it.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Source: Franklin Township Police Department, Traffic Safety Bureau, <a href="https://www.franklintwpnj.org/departments/police-department/patrol-division/traffic-safety-bureau/crash-reports">Crash Reports</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>

<h3>Crashes handled by the State Police, such as on I-287</h3>
<p>Because the Somerville station wrote nearly every I-287 record in Franklin, an I-287 crash report is usually a State Police document. You order it on the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash report portal</a>, not from the township. I-287 counts as a non-toll road there, so the fee is $13.00. The portal keeps reports for six years, counted from the crash year, and says to search again in a couple of days if yours is not posted yet. For help, email NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov; for serious or fatal crash records, the portal gives (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234 after one month.</p>
<p class="source">Fee, six-year window and contacts: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). Somerville share of I-287 records: our count (2024 and 2025).</p>

<h3>When neither Franklin police nor troopers came to the scene</h3>
<p>Only police fill out New Jersey's crash report form, the NJTR-1. A driver whose crash no officer investigated uses the SR-1 self-report instead, and the Franklin police page notes that the SR-1 is sent to NJDOT rather than to the township.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Franklin Township. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="franklin-method">
<h2>How the Franklin numbers were built</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Source: NJDOT crash records for 2023 (per-table archive) and 2024 and 2025 (NJDOT Crash Data Portal), as normalized by the open-source hudcostreets/nj-crashes project, with fatal crashes supplemented from the NJ State Police feed. Scope: every crash NJDOT assigns to the municipality Franklin Twp, Somerset County.</li>
  <li>We count a Franklin crash record toward the injury totals when the officer coded anyone in it as injured or dead. The people-hurt and people-killed columns add up the persons on those records.</li>
  <li>2023 and 2024 are complete years in the data. The 2025 file is preliminary even though Franklin's already runs 1.021 times its 2024 size.</li>
  <li>The 978 headline and the corridor shares rest on the two complete years. The intersection list and the Somerville station split rest on 2024 and 2025 instead: the 2023 export names streets differently and rarely fills in the station field.</li>
  <li>Corridors combine every name variant of a route number in the records, both directions. Intersections are the street-name pairs in the records; variants are not merged, so counts are a floor.</li>
  <li>No traffic volumes were applied, so a busy corridor such as Route 27 can top these tables simply because more vehicles use it. Fault is not part of any figure on this page.</li>
