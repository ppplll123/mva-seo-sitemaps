<h1>NJ uninsured motorist claim help: how UM and UIM coverage work after a car accident</h1><nav class="df-jump" aria-label="Jump to" data-df="added"><span>Jump to:</span><a href="#faq">Questions</a><a href="#sources">Sources</a></nav>
<p class="byline">By Sandra H. Brown, Injuries Cases Autos research. Updated <time datetime="2026-11-11">11 November 2026</time></p>
<section id="answer" class="df-answer">
<p class="df-lead"><strong>If the other insurer denies its driver was at fault, uninsured motorist (UM) coverage does not apply, but a written coverage denial can make an insured driver count as uninsured.</strong> UM pays what you would have claimed from a driver with no insurance; underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage pays the gap above a too-low limit.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>

<aside class="key-facts"><ul>
<li data-origin="original"><strong>$65,000</strong> or $75,000: the most a $100,000 UIM limit adds, against a 2026 or a 2023 to 2025 at-fault policy</li>
<li data-origin="original"><strong>2</strong> of 2 step-down appeals in our coded table kept the declared UM/UIM limit</li>
<li><strong>$500</strong>: excluded from every UM and UIM claim, per DOBI's 2026 guide</li>
<li><strong>$300,000</strong>: the most common UM/UIM limit in DOBI's 2026 options chart</li>
</ul></aside>
</section>

<section id="denial">
<h2>Does uninsured motorist coverage pay if the other insurer denies fault?</h2>
<p>No. DOBI treats a liability denial as a fault dispute, not missing coverage, so UM does not step in.</p>
<p>A coverage denial works the other way. Once an insurer denies coverage in writing, for example because the car was driven without the owner's permission, that driver can count as uninsured for your UM claim.</p>
<p>Either way, your insurer "stands in the shoes" of the other driver. Under New Jersey's comparative negligence law you recover only if you are not more at fault, and your own share reduces the payment. See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how New Jersey splits fault in real cases</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="uim-math">
<h2>How much does underinsured motorist coverage pay?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">UIM pays up to the difference between your UIM limit and the other driver's liability limit.</strong> It does not stack on top. DOBI gives this example:</p>

<p>The at-fault policy's date sets the ceiling. With a $100,000 UIM limit, the most UIM can add is $65,000 against a 2026 policy at the $35,000 minimum, or $75,000 against a 2023 to 2025 policy at $25,000.</p>
<p>Which minimum applies to your accident date is covered in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">NJ minimum car insurance limits for 2026</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="mandatory">
<h2>Is uninsured motorist coverage mandatory in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes, on a Standard Policy, which must include a minimum amount of UM and UIM coverage.</strong> The Basic Policy and the Special Automobile Insurance Policy (SAIP) include none.</p>

<section id="how-much-coverage">
<h2>How much UM/UIM coverage should you carry in New Jersey?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">A Standard Policy must carry at least the required minimum, and you can buy more up to, but not above, your own liability limits.</strong> The minimum itself is listed on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">NJ car insurance minimum limits for 2026</a> page.</p>
<p>The limit you pick sets your ceiling. In DOBI's worked example, Jane carries $100,000 UIM, Sam's policy pays its $35,000 limit on $45,000 of damages, and Jane's UIM pays the other $10,000, less the $500 exclusion.</p>
<p>DOBI's 2026 options chart shows UM/UIM choices of $95,000 / $100,000, $300,000 and $500,000, with $300,000 the most common. A Basic Policy has no UM or UIM at any level.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="not-covered">
<h2>What does uninsured motorist coverage not cover?</h2>
<p>It does not pay when the other driver was not legally at fault, and it leaves out these losses:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The uninsured driver.</strong> UM pays you, never the driver who had no insurance.</li>
<li><strong>Vehicle damage from a car that fled without touching you.</strong> That property damage claim needs the vehicle identified; see the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-hit-and-run-accident-claim-help">New Jersey hit-and-run guide</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Your first medical bills.</strong> PIP pays those whoever was at fault; see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP coverage limits and medical bills</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>An injury claim can also fail on the permanent-injury question. How that played out in UM and UIM appeals is on our page about <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions#threshold">UM claims and the permanent-injury rule</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="no-insurance">
<h2>What happens if you drive with no insurance in NJ and get hit?</h2>
<p>You cannot sue. DOBI says "if you lack coverage and someone hits you, you cannot sue," a rule known as the uninsured-owner bar.</p>
<p>The bar has an edge case, explained in our ruling on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions#uninsured-owner">the uninsured-owner bar on appeal</a>. If no one in your household has auto insurance, medical benefits may come from the state's Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund.</p>
<p>What coverage New Jersey requires in the first place is on the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">2026 mandatory coverage page</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="other-driver">
<h2>Can your insurer cut your UM/UIM limit for a different driver?</h2>
<p>Not in the appeals we coded: both step-down rulings kept the limit on the declarations page.</p>
<p>The clauses, the dollar amounts and the commercial truck exception are set out in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">NJ uninsured and underinsured motorist appeals: what 13 decisions show</a>. Five of those 13 came from Middlesex County, whose local numbers are on <a href="/new-jersey/middlesex-county-car-accident-help">Middlesex County car accident help</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="steps">
<h2>How do you sue an underinsured driver?</h2>
<p>Claim the driver's own policy first, then claim the rest as UIM from your insurer, with its permission before any release.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>Keep every letter in full.</strong> Save the other insurer's denial or limits letter with its date, claim number and stated reason.</li>
<li><strong>Pull the policy for the accident date.</strong> Use the declarations page and endorsements in force that day, not a later renewal.</li>
<li><strong>Get permission before you sign a release.</strong> Most insurers require it. Read the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-8-questions-to-ask-before-accepting-a-settlement-offer">questions to ask before accepting a settlement offer</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Tell your own insurer early</strong> and send it only what the other insurer leaves unpaid.</li>
</ol>
<p>Step 3 is where claims die: a release signed without reserving rights can end the UIM claim, as the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions#release">release ruling on appeal</a> shows.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="who-to-call">
<h2>What is the phone number for an NJ uninsured motorist claim?</h2>
<p>Call your own insurance company's claims line, not the MVC: UM and UIM are claims on your own policy.</p>
<p>If it refuses or delays, your policy's arbitration comes first, then the insurer's internal appeal, then DOBI's Insurance Claims Ombudsman at 1-800-446-7467. A bad-faith claim usually waits until the UIM claim is decided; see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions#bad-faith">suing a UIM carrier for bad faith</a>.</p>
<p>More guides are on the <a href="/new-jersey">New Jersey car accident hub</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">

<p>DOBI's 2026 guide says UM and UIM claims "exclude the first $500 in damages." In its UIM example, Jane's insurer pays the remaining $10,000 less that $500.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What does 100-300 uninsured motorist coverage mean?</h3></summary>
<p>DOBI's 2026 chart lists UM/UIM limits as $95,000 / $100,000, $300,000 or $500,000. How split per-person and per-accident figures work is explained on the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">2026 minimum limits page</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What is the statute of limitations for uninsured motorist claims in New Jersey?</h3></summary>
<p>The deadlines for the injury suit, minors and public vehicles are on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">statute of limitations for uninsured motorist claims</a> page. DOBI warns that you must settle or file within the limitation period.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup></p>

</details></section>
