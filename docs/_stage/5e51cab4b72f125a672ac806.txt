<h1>Mount Laurel car accidents: Burlington County's highest count, with more than a quarter on I-295 and the Turnpike</h1>

<p>Mount Laurel Township had 663 police-reported car accidents where someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024. They hurt 920 people and killed 6. No other Burlington County town comes close: Burlington Township is next with 354, and Mount Laurel alone holds 12.7% of the county's total. Statewide it ranks 41st of the 563 New Jersey municipalities with at least one injury accident in these records. What sets it apart is the highway share. 185 of the 663 were on roads NJDOT codes as Interstate or State Authority, almost all of them I-295 and the New Jersey Turnpike. That is 28%, against about 12% for the county and for New Jersey as a whole. It also decides where you get your report: on those two highways the records point to the State Police, and on most other roads to the township's own police.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by hudcostreets/nj-crashes, fatal crashes supplemented from NJ State Police; <code>lane2-content/data/profiles/mount-laurel-nj-car-accidents.json</code>, <code>data/counties/burlington.json</code> and our cut <code>data/mount-laurel-nj-car-accidents/mount-laurel-cuts.csv</code>. Headline counts are 2023 and 2024, both complete. <a href="#method">Method below</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Mount Laurel. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
