<section id="when-not">
<h2>When should you not accept a settlement offer yet?</h2>
<p>Hold off while any of these is still open, because each is something the release can close for good.</p>
<ul>
<li>A test result, specialist visit or work restriction is still pending, so nobody knows the full injury yet.</li>
<li>Your own insurer has not been told about the offer, and you may need its underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage.</li>
<li>The release names people, policies or claims beyond the one driver you meant to settle with.</li>
<li>The offer is cut for your share of fault, and nobody has explained the percentage in writing.</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="release-first">
<h2>What happens to your UIM claim if you sign the release first?</h2>
<p>It can end it. In three New Jersey appeals we coded, the injured person settled with the at-fault driver first, and the release decided what was left.</p>

<p>The difference between Nesby and Motil was not the dollar amount. It was what the paperwork preserved and which policy actually covered the injured driver.</p>
<h3>Before you sign: the UIM steps from these cases</h3>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Tell your own insurer about the offer before you sign; DOBI's UM/UIM guide stresses this notice before certain settlements.</li>
<li>Confirm which policy covers you. In Nesby, the driver was not a named insured or resident under the relatives' policies.</li>
<li>Have the release reserve your UIM claim. Nesby reserved no rights, and every later claim was dismissed.</li>
<li>File the UIM claim on time. In Chenel, a $99,000 settlement came first and the late UIM suit failed.</li>
</ol>
<h3>What should the release say before you sign?</h3>
<ul>
<li>Who is released: only the driver and policy you meant to settle with, not other people or other policies.</li>
<li>Which claims end: the claim against that driver, not your claims against your own insurer.</li>
<li>What is kept: a written reservation of your UIM claim, the point Nesby lacked and Motil kept alive.</li>
</ul>
<p>UIM pays only the gap between your UIM limit and the other driver's limit, as our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">guide to UM and UIM coverage in New Jersey</a> works through. All 13 coded coverage rulings are in the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">NJ uninsured and underinsured motorist appeals decisions</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="good-offer">
<h2>What are the signs of a good settlement offer?</h2>
<p>It is measured against the coverage behind it: Motil settled at a $15,000 limit, yet a $100,000 UIM limit was still there.</p>
<p>Ask the adjuster for the other driver's limit in writing. Which minimum applied on your crash date is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">NJ minimum car insurance limits for 2026</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="still-treating">
<h2>What if an MRI or follow-up visit is still pending?</h2>
<p>Then the liability offer prices only the injury known today, while your medical bills keep running through your own PIP, a separate claim.</p>
<p>If your policy has the limitation-on-lawsuit option, a pending MRI or specialist result can decide whether the injury meets the lawsuit threshold. How that option works is in <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">whether New Jersey is a no-fault state</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<p>Booked injections or therapy belong on the same dated list of pending care. PIP limits and its own deadline are on our page about <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">NJ PIP coverage limits and medical bills</a>. To gather the results a reviewer asks for, use the checklist for <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">medical records in a NJ injury claim</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="how-long">
<h2>How long does someone have to accept a settlement offer?</h2>
<p>The insurer's letter sets its own response date; the legal clock is the 2-year deadline to file an injury lawsuit in New Jersey.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<p>Every other deadline is on our page of <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">every deadline after a NJ car accident</a>. How the insurer's side of the claim runs is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster in NJ</a>, and court records on contested claims are in <a href="/new-jersey/how-long-car-accident-settlement-takes-nj">how long a NJ settlement takes</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="fault-cut">
<h2>What if the insurer denies the claim or cuts it for fault?</h2>
<p>Ask for the decision and the fault percentage in writing, then use DOBI's appeal route, set out step by step in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">NJ insurance adjuster guide</a>.</p>
<p>In DOBI's example, a driver 10 percent at fault on $1,000 of damage collects $900. How New Jersey juries actually split fault is in <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">7 real fault splits from NJ appeals</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="what-you-keep">
<h2>How much of a $25,000 or $50,000 settlement would you keep?</h2>
<p>The offer minus any lawyer's fee, case costs and liens; for a $20,000, $50,000 or $100,000 offer alike, the fee can be at most a third after costs.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

<p>The full fee steps and real NJ amounts are on <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">whether $50,000 is a good settlement in NJ</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="before-signing">
<h2>What should you bring to a review before signing?</h2>
<p>The full release, the offer letter, both drivers' policy limits, and a dated list of every pending visit, test and missed shift.</p>
<p>Add any letter disputing fault or coverage, and any written notice you sent your own insurer. First steps after a crash are in our <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident help guide</a>.</p>
</section>
