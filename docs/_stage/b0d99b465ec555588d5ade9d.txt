<section id="answer" class="df-answer">

<section id="recorded-statement">
<h2>Should I give a recorded statement to the other insurance company?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">You may, but no New Jersey law sets what you must tell the other driver's insurer.</strong> The common advice to never talk to them goes too far.</p>
<p>DOBI says giving enough to support your claim is in your interest, and not cooperating fully could lead to a denial. Before any recording, ask who will hear it, what it covers and whether written answers would do.</p>
<h3>Why is my insurance asking for a recorded statement?</h3>
<p>Your own policy requires you to cooperate with your insurer's investigation. It can also ask for a proof of loss and, in some cases, an examination under oath with your lawyer present.</p>
<h3>Can I refuse a recorded statement to an insurance company?</h3>
<p>Refusing your own insurer can breach your policy duties. With the other driver's insurer there is no set list, but DOBI warns that not cooperating fully could also lead to a denial.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="what-not-to-say">
<h2>What not to say to a claims adjuster?</h2>
<p>The rules differ by insurer: you owe your own company cooperation, while the other driver's company is owed only what supports your claim.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your own insurer:</strong> do not refuse its questions or paperwork. Its PIP and collision adjusters work under your policy.</li>
<li><strong>The other driver's insurer:</strong> DOBI says its primary obligation is to its policyholder, not to you. Give the facts your claim needs and stop there.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the adjuster pushes for a quick release or disputes treatment, check the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-7-signs-you-may-need-an-accident-lawyer-after-a-crash">signs you may need an accident lawyer</a> before the next call.</p>
</section>

<section id="which-adjuster">
<h2>Who pays for medical bills, and which adjuster handles what?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Your own insurer's PIP adjuster pays eligible medical bills, whoever caused the crash.</strong> Other adjusters handle your car, the other driver's share and uninsured drivers.</p>

<p>Medical bills above or outside your PIP limit can be claimed against the at-fault driver; amounts PIP will pay cannot. No-fault is not the same as not at fault: see <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">what no-fault means in New Jersey</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="fault">
<h2>How does the adjuster decide who was at fault?</h2>
<p>The adjuster sets a fault percentage from the police report, driver and witness statements, the traffic laws and the road layout.</p>
<p>Here the usual advice is wrong: a ticket for the other driver does not make them automatically 100% at fault. Your share cuts what you get, rental costs included; see <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what you get at 10, 50 or 80 percent fault</a>.</p>
<p>When the two stories differ and nothing else persuades, DOBI warns the insurer will side with its own policyholder. That is why <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-evidence-helps-prove-fault-after-an-nj-crash">evidence that proves fault</a> matters before the adjuster decides.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="timelines">
<h2>How long does an insurer have to accept or reject a claim?</h2>
<p>On car damage, your own insurer has 30 calendar days from notice to settle and the other driver's insurer 45, unless it tells you in writing why it needs longer.</p>
<p>These DOBI limits cover vehicle damage only, not injury claims. Contact, inspection and storage deadlines are in the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-property-damage-claim-help-and-steps-guide">DOBI deadlines for car damage claims</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="disagree">
<h2>How do you deal with an insurance adjuster who says no?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Use the insurer's internal appeal, then ask the state's claims Ombudsman to review it.</strong> A PIP medical denial goes to arbitration instead. Every route, from arbitration to a lawsuit, is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-claim-denied">what to do when a claim is denied</a>.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>File the internal appeal.</strong> Every New Jersey auto insurer must have an Internal Appeal Process and tell you where to send it.</li>
<li><strong>Ask the DOBI Insurance Claims Ombudsman to review it</strong> by phone at 1-800-446-7467 or by email at ombudsman@dobi.nj.gov.</li>
<li><strong>For a PIP denial, file with Forthright:</strong> $200 on the papers, $225 for an in-person hearing. The full route is in our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey#pip-arbitration">PIP arbitration guide</a>.</li>
</ol>
<p>None of these steps takes away your right to sue. Court rulings on PIP arbitration sit in the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-appeals-index">New Jersey appeals case index</a>.</p>
<p>If the first offer looks low, work through the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-8-questions-to-ask-before-accepting-a-settlement-offer">questions to ask before accepting a settlement offer</a> and compare <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">New Jersey appeals settlement figures</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="sue">
<h2>Can someone sue you personally after a car accident in NJ?</h2>
<p>Yes, and your liability insurer's adjuster handles that claim and your defense up to your limits, but a Basic Policy without liability coverage means no defense.</p>
<p>The 2026 limits are on the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">New Jersey minimum liability limits</a> page, and suit and public-entity deadlines on the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey accident deadlines</a> page.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>What else do people ask about adjusters?</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Should I give the other driver my insurance information?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes. At the scene, exchange names, licence, plate and insurance details, and photograph the cards. The <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-do-in-the-first-24-hours-after-a-new-jersey-car-accident">first 24 hours checklist</a> covers the rest.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long do you have to report an accident to your insurance company in New Jersey?</h3></summary>
<p>Our sources verify no fixed deadline for telling your insurer, so we give none. The DOBI settlement clocks above start only from notice, so report promptly; court deadlines are on the accident deadlines page.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What not to tell your insurance company after an accident?</h3></summary>
<p>A claim for future treatment needs only to be brought within a reasonable time if, at its last payment, your PIP insurer knew more treatment was probably needed (NJ Supreme Court, 2026). Saying you are done treating cuts against that. How future bills split is in our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">guide to bills PIP and health insurance leave unpaid</a>.</p>
<p>On the police report, DOBI says to check details such as how many passengers are listed, and to tell your insurer and the police department that responded about any inconsistency. To ask for a correction, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">how to read a New Jersey crash report</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>

</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How do I file a complaint against an insurance company in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>File the insurer's internal appeal first. Then contact the DOBI Insurance Claims Ombudsman at 1-800-446-7467 or ombudsman@dobi.nj.gov to review how the claim was handled.</p>
