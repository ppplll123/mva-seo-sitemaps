<h1>Cumberland County car accident help: who has your police report, and where accidents happen</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p>After a car accident in Cumberland County, get medical attention first, then find out which police agency wrote the report. Inside Vineland, Millville and Bridgeton, that is usually the city police department. In the ten townships and Shiloh Borough, it is almost always the New Jersey State Police.</p>
  <p>That split shows up clearly in the crash records. In 2024 and 2025, between 94 and 100 percent of crash records in each of those eleven towns name a State Police station, mostly Bridgeton or Port Norris. In Vineland, Millville and Bridgeton, only 1 to 5 percent do. The table below gives the share for every town and the place to ask for the report.</p>

The station field is not documented by NJDOT; we read a filled value as a State Police unit reporting the crash. Check the name of the agency on the card or paper the officer gave you.</p>

  <h3 id="vineland-report">Vineland</h3>
  <p>The Vineland Police Department website links to CrashDocs.org for crash reports, a records requests contact and the city's online OPRA request form. Headquarters is at 620 East Plum Street, Vineland, phone 856-696-1212.</p>

  <h3 id="millville-report">Millville</h3>
  <p>Millville police say crash reports are "generally" available five days after the accident, though some take longer.   <h3 id="bridgeton-report">Bridgeton</h3>
  <p>The Bridgeton Police Department website has a "Request Crash Reports" link that goes to the BuyCrash search page run by LexisNexis. The department's listed phone number is 856-451-0033. The city's own departments page links to this police site.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: Bridgeton Police Department, <a href="https://bpdops.com/">bpdops.com</a> (resource link "Request Crash Reports"), linked from City of Bridgeton, <a href="https://www.cityofbridgetonnj.gov/101/Departments">cityofbridgetonnj.gov/101/Departments</a>, both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

  <h3 id="state-police-report">Upper Deerfield, Maurice River, Shiloh and the other State Police towns</h3>
  <p>A crash worked by the Bridgeton or Port Norris State Police station is not on file at any Cumberland city hall. Its report comes through the State Police online crash report portal, where a non-toll road report costs $13.00. A report becomes downloadable only once it is "ready and approved," so a Fairfield or Downe Township crash from this week may not show yet; try again after a couple of days.</p>
  <p>The portal holds reports for six years from the crash year. Its listed contacts are NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. The $13.00 fee is the State Police price only; Vineland, Millville and Bridgeton handle their reports their own way, as set out above.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: New Jersey State Police, Crash Reports portal (fee, retention, "ready and approved," email and phone), <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/</a>; NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page, <a href="https://njsp.njoag.gov/identification-amp-information-technology-section/criminal-justice-records-bureau/">njsp.njoag.gov</a>; both retrieved 2026-11-10. Police complete the NJTR-1 crash report; drivers self-report with the SR-1 only when police did not investigate (<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT Police Resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>Whichever agency wrote it, check the report against what happened. The state insurance department's consumer guide says to "review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed." If something is wrong, it says to tell your insurance company and the responding police department. For reading the codes, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">how to read a New Jersey crash report</a>.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: NJ DOBI, <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Cumberland County. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
