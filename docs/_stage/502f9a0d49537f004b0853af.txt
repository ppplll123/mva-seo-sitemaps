<h1>Cherry Hill car accidents: the town that holds a third of Route 70's injury crashes</h1>

<p>Police in New Jersey reported 1,358 Cherry Hill Township car accidents in 2023 and 2024 that left at least one person hurt or dead. In those two years 1,852 people were hurt and 9 were killed. No other Camden County town comes close except Camden City, and statewide the township ranks 12th of 573 municipalities. Route 70 is the main story: its 265 injury accidents inside the township are almost one in three of the road's statewide total. Rear-end collisions made up half of Cherry Hill's injury accidents, a share beaten by only one other large New Jersey town, Paramus. Cherry Hill Police usually hold the report, except on I-295 and the New Jersey Turnpike, where it is usually the State Police.</p>
<p class="source">Where these figures come from: NJDOT police crash records for 2023 to 2025, cleaned and published by the open-source project hudcostreets/nj-crashes, with NJ State Police fatal data added. The opening counts use 2023 and 2024 only, both full years. The rank orders all 573 municipalities by accidents with someone hurt or killed. Details in <a href="#cherry-hill-data-notes">our Cherry Hill data notes</a>.</p>

<section id="cherry-hill-report">
<h2>Who has your Cherry Hill accident report</h2>
<p>Which road you were on predicts which agency wrote your report. Cherry Hill's records include a station field, and it holds a State Police station or unit name in some records and is blank in others. In 2024 and 2025 that field splits cleanly by road.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Route 70, Route 38, Route 41 and county or township roads.</strong> A State Police station appears on only 13 of 1,047 Route 70 records and 1 of 587 Route 38 records. Expect the Cherry Hill Police Department to hold these reports.</li>
<li><strong>I-295.</strong> A State Police station appears on 477 of the township's 581 I-295 records (82%), and 467 of those name Bellmawr. A State Police report is likely, but confirm the agency from whatever details the officer gave you at the scene.</li>
<li><strong>New Jersey Turnpike.</strong> The State Police Moorestown station appears on 98 of the 101 Turnpike records in the township.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Station split: NJDOT records for 2024 and 2025 (2025 preliminary), hudcostreets/nj-crashes, field station, in <code>lane2-content/data/cherry-hill-nj-car-accidents/cherry-hill-njsp-station-by-route-2024-2025.csv</code> and <code>cherry-hill-turnpike-i295-stations-2024-2025.csv</code>. Treating a filled station field as a State Police report is our own reading of the field, based on the names it contains. We skip 2023 because the 2023 archive rarely fills this field.</p>

<h3>Cherry Hill Police Department reports</h3>
<p>According to the township, copies of police reports "are available to involved persons and can be picked up during regular business hours at the Police Records window." You will find the Police Records Office on the Municipal Building's first floor. The Records Unit answers at 856-488-7822. No crash report fee or online ordering is mentioned on the township's page, so phone ahead.</p>
<p class="source">Township records procedure: <a href="https://www.chnj.gov/138/Police-Records">Cherry Hill Township, Police Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h3>I-295 and Turnpike reports</h3>
<p>For an I-295 or Turnpike accident in Cherry Hill, order the report on the State Police crash report portal, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>, which also handles Turnpike Authority reports. A report you cannot find yet may simply not be approved, so try the search again a couple of days later. An I-295 report counts as a non-toll road report at $13.00, and a Turnpike report as a toll road report at $5.00. If you need help using the portal itself, it asks you to email njsprecords@njsp.gov. Its contact list sends questions about non-toll road reports such as I-295 to the State Police at (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. Questions about Turnpike reports go to the Turnpike Authority at (732) 442-8600 ext. 2431. For serious or fatal crash records, allow up to a month for delivery; the portal says to phone ext. 2234 about such a request only after that month.</p>
<p class="source">State Police and Turnpike route: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash report portal</a>, fetched live 2026-11-10 (fees, delivery times, email and phone contacts), consistent with research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8.</p>

<h3>No police at the scene in Cherry Hill</h3>
<p>From its Forms and Reports page, the township links to New Jersey's self-complete crash form. NJDOT describes the SR-1 as the form "used to report a crash that was not investigated by the police and it is the only form that can be used for this purpose." Officers alone complete the NJTR-1, which is the police crash report.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.chnj.gov/134/Forms-Reports">Cherry Hill Township, Forms and Reports</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10; NJDOT self-reporting crash form page, archived copy dated 2026-09-18 (<a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20260918164037/https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">Wayback Machine</a>; the live dot.nj.gov page was blocked to us on 2026-11-10); <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources</a>, confirmed live by the coordinator 2026-11-10.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Cherry Hill. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="cherry-hill-data-notes">
<h2>Cherry Hill data notes</h2>
<p>Every count on this page comes from one file of NJDOT police crash records, downloaded on 2026-11-06. It joins NJDOT's per-table archive for 2023 with its Crash Data Portal exports for 2024 and 2025, as cleaned by hudcostreets/nj-crashes, and adds State Police fatal crash data. We kept records with NJDOT municipality "Cherry Hill Twp" in Camden County.</p>
<p>An accident counts as "hurt or killed" here when the police record lists at least one injured or dead person. We treat 2023 and 2024 as final and mark every 2025 figure as preliminary. Corner rankings start in 2024, since NJDOT changed how it records street names after the 2023 export. Spelling variants of a corner stay separate, which keeps corner counts low. Road totals, by contrast, merge spellings under the NJDOT route number. Nothing here is a rate: the records hold no traffic volumes, so a busy road can show more accidents simply because more cars use it.</p>
<p>Code and outputs: <code>lane2-content/data/build_place_profiles.py</code> builds the profile, and <code>lane2-content/data/cherry-hill-nj-car-accidents/cherry_hill_cuts.py</code> writes the Cherry Hill CSV files in that folder. The general method is in <code>lane2-content/data/README.md</code>.</p>
