<h1>Car accident in Passaic, NJ: one in six injury crashes in the city involves a pedestrian</h1>

<p>Police crash reports from the City of Passaic cover 3,852 car accidents over 2023 and 2024, the two latest complete years. 729 of those reports list at least one person hurt or killed: 994 people hurt and 7 killed in all.</p>
<p>The figure that stands out is pedestrians. 123 of those 729 accidents were pedestrian crashes, or 16.9 percent. That is the highest share of any town in Passaic County, and the second highest of the 50 New Jersey towns with at least 500 such accidents, behind only Bayonne.</p>
<p>If Passaic police investigated your accident, the report comes from the Passaic Police Department Records Bureau, covered further down this page.</p>

<h2 id="deaths">Every fatal crash in Passaic since 2023 was recorded after dark</h2>
<p>Eight people died in eight separate crashes in the city from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 still preliminary. Six were pedestrians. All eight crashes were coded as happening in darkness, seven of them on streets with lights on and one with street lights off.</p>
<p>2024 stands apart: five pedestrians were killed, after none in 2023. Four of those five deaths came between August and October 2024.</p>

<p>Parked cars tell a different story when you count every accident, not only the ones where someone was hurt. 994 of Passaic's 3,852 accidents, or 25.8 percent, were coded as a vehicle striking a parked vehicle. <p>Timing follows its own pattern too. Monday had the most accidents where someone was hurt (128 in 2023 and 2024), while Friday leads for Passaic County as a whole. The busiest three-hour window in the city was 3 pm to 6 pm, with 141.</p>

<h2 id="monroe">Monroe Street: a city street with 65 injury crashes in 2024 and 2025 (2025 preliminary)</h2>
<p>Monroe Street is coded as a municipal street, not a county or state road. Even so, 26 Passaic corners had 3 or more accidents where someone was hurt in 2024 and 2025 (2025 preliminary), and Monroe Street forms four of them. Those are its corners with Hoover Avenue and Hope Avenue (5 each), and with 3rd Street and Columbia Avenue, which sit in an 18-way tie at 3.</p>
<p>Counting every record that names Monroe Street as the road or the cross street gives 406 accidents in 2024 and 2025 (2025 preliminary). 65 of them hurt someone, 90 people were hurt, and 9 involved a pedestrian. No one was killed.</p>

<h2 id="route-21">Route 21 inside city limits</h2>
<p>Route 21 is the McCarter Highway. In 2023 and 2024 it had 279 accidents inside Passaic, 72 of them with someone hurt or killed. That is 9.9 percent of the city's injury accidents, on one state highway. 103 people were hurt and 2 were killed.</p>
<p>In 2024 and 2025 records (2025 preliminary), River Drive is the cross street named most often on Route 21 in Passaic, with 17 accidents where someone was hurt among 63 in total. Hope Avenue follows with 8, then Passaic Avenue with 6, Paulison Avenue with 4 and Monroe Street with 3. Another 81 Route 21 records in those two years name no cross street at all, so the real spread is wider than this list.</p>

<h2 id="report">How to get the police report for a Passaic car accident</h2>
<p>If the Passaic Police Department investigated your accident, the report comes from its Records Bureau. The city lists crash reports there.</p>
<ul>
  <li>The city's "Motor Vehicle Crash Report" listing links to an online request form for police reports and gives the Records Bureau phone number, 973-365-3910. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.cityofpassaic.com/Directory.aspx?DID=43">City of Passaic, Motor Vehicle Crash Report</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
  <li>The Records Bureau page lists hours of Monday through Friday, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm, and fax 973-365-3915. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.cityofpassaic.com/221/Record-Bureau-Resources">City of Passaic, Record Bureau Resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
  <li>Police headquarters is at 330 Passaic Street, Passaic, NJ 07055, non-emergency 973-365-3900. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.cityofpassaic.com/200/Police">City of Passaic Police</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
</ul>
<p>The city's page does not state a fee or a waiting time, so ask the Records Bureau when you request the report.</p>
<p>The agency that investigated decides where the report is. If you are unsure, for example after a Route 21 crash, check the agency named on any paperwork from the scene or call the Records Bureau. If New Jersey State Police investigated, request the report through the State Police crash report portal at <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>; questions go to NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov or (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
<p>The police crash report, form NJTR-1, is completed by police only. If no officer investigated, the driver's own report is the SR-1 self-reporting form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
<p>When the report arrives, New Jersey's insurance regulator suggests you "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed" and, if you see inconsistencies, "let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25)</span></p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Passaic. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
