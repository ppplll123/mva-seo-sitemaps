<h1>Union County car accident data: the Parkway tops the roads, and Union Township holds more than half of Route 22's injury accidents</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <h2>Parkway, county police or town department: three report routes in Union County</h2>
  <p>The Garden State Parkway logged 719 Union County accidents where someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024, ahead of Route 22 (597) and Route 1 (519). A Parkway or Turnpike report is ordered online through the State Police portal. A crash on a Union Township or Springfield street goes through that township's police, and a Union County Police case through the county's Records Division.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: our count of NJDOT crash records 2023 and 2024 by route number, <code>data/union-county-car-accident-help/union-county-roads-by-route-2023-2024.csv</code> (method below).</p>

  <h3 id="report-parkway-turnpike">Garden State Parkway, New Jersey Turnpike and State Police cases</h3>
  <p>The Parkway and the Turnpike are toll roads, so their Union County NJTR-1 reports come from one State Police crash reports portal, as do reports from State Police cases on other roads. The download opens once the officer's report is finished and approved; a report not yet listed may show up after a couple of days. Union County Parkway or Turnpike copies are priced at $5.00, a non-toll State Police report at $13.00, and each report stays downloadable for six years counted from the accident year.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: New Jersey State Police, Crash Reports portal, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. The same portal page lists the contacts: Turnpike and Parkway report questions go to the New Jersey Turnpike Authority, (732) 442-8600 ext. 2431; non-toll State Police report questions go to (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234; help with the online service itself goes to njsprecords@njsp.gov. Union Township and Springfield send people to crashdocs.org instead, and we did not check that vendor's prices.</p>
  <p>NJDOT codes 1,160 of the county's 9,235 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024, or 12.6 percent, to its State Authority road system.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: our count by NJDOT road system, <code>data/union-county-car-accident-help/union-county-road-system-2023-2024.csv</code>.</p>

  <h3 id="report-county-police">Union County Police</h3>
  <p>The Union County Police say their Patrol Division handles traffic enforcement and accident investigations throughout the county and assists local police departments. Their contact page lists the Records Division at (908) 654-9809 and headquarters at 400 North Avenue East, Westfield. If a county officer took your report, start there.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: County of Union, Division of Police, <a href="https://ucnj.org/public-safety/division-of-police/patrol-division/">Patrol Division</a> and <a href="https://ucnj.org/public-safety/division-of-police/contact-the-union-county-police/">Contact the Union County Police</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

  <h3 id="report-town-police">Town police departments</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Union Township:</strong> the department's site links "Get Your Accident Report" to crashdocs.org, an outside report service. Details are in the <a href="#union-township">Union Township section</a> below.</li>
    <li><strong>Springfield Township:</strong> the police department's page links "Accident Reports" to crashdocs.org, filtered to the Springfield Police Department. The department is at 100 Mountain Avenue. <span class="source">(Township of Springfield, <a href="https://springfield-nj.us/police/">Police Department</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
    <li><strong>Elizabeth, Linden and Plainfield:</strong> each town page covers its own report route: <a href="/new-jersey/elizabeth-nj-car-accidents">Elizabeth car accident records and report route</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/linden-nj-car-accidents">Linden car accident data and police report</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/plainfield-nj-car-accidents">Plainfield car accident records and police report</a>.</li>
    <li><strong>Rahway, Roselle, Hillside, Cranford, Westfield and the other towns:</strong> we did not confirm how each department releases accident reports or what it charges. Ask the department whose officers responded to the scene.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>No officer at the scene means no NJTR-1 to request: NJDOT says only police fill out that form. Drivers in that situation report the accident themselves on the SR-1.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: NJDOT, <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">Police Resources</a> and <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">Self-Reporting Crash Form</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. Statewide steps: <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey accident report, agency by agency</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Union County. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

  <h2>How the Union County figures were built</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Records: NJDOT's 2023 per-table archive plus 2024 and 2025 from the <a href="https://njdot.aashtowaresafety.net/njdot-crash-data-dashboard#/">NJDOT Crash Data Portal</a>, cleaned up by the open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a> project, with deaths filled in from the NJ State Police fatal feed. Our copy was downloaded 2026-11-06.</li>
    <li>The 9,235 figure counts each police-reported Union County accident with one or more people hurt or killed once; the 12,444 people hurt and 76 killed add up the people in those accidents.</li>
    <li>NJDOT has not finalized 2025. Here it appears only in the year tables and the two corner tables, each time marked preliminary; every other Union County table rests on the finished 2023 and 2024 files.</li>
    <li>The corner tables leave 2023 out: that year's file writes street names in a different style from 2024 and 2025, which would split one corner into two entries.</li>
    <li>Road tables group records by road class and route number (raw labels such as "US 22", "NJ 27 (ST GEORGES AVENUE)" and "GARDEN STATE PARKWAY SECONDARY" are combined by route). Scripts and CSVs: <code>data/union-county-car-accident-help/union_cuts.py</code>, <code>data/build_place_profiles.py</code>.</li>
    <li>No traffic volumes are used, so the Parkway and Route 22 rows are totals, not a measure of how risky each mile is. The records do not show who caused any accident.</li>
