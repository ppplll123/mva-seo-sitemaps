<section id="ten-ways">
<h2>How do you prove pain and suffering, step by step?</h2>
<p>Build a record that answers the two questions the appeals turned on: is the injury permanent, and did this crash cause it?</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>Check your lawsuit option first.</strong> Your declarations page shows whether the injury list applies to you at all.</li>
<li><strong>Get treated early and keep the dates.</strong> Tell every provider each symptom, and record why any visit was missed.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the objective findings, not only pain scores.</strong> Imaging reports, test results and measured range of motion are what a certification rests on.</li>
<li><strong>Ask your treating doctor the permanency question directly.</strong> Has the injured part healed to work normally, and will more treatment fix it? Ask for the answer in writing.</li>
<li><strong>Do not stop at the MRI label.</strong> Herniations appear in 4 of the 7 losses and in both wins; the proof around the herniation decided the result.</li>
<li><strong>Disclose old injuries.</strong> Joo v. Corless failed because no medical evidence said the crash made the old condition worse.</li>
<li><strong>Get aggravation stated in medical terms.</strong> Presbery v. Willitts cleared the threshold with aggravation of earlier degeneration that had caused no symptoms before.</li>
<li><strong>Link the injury to this crash.</strong> Keep the crash report, photos and first medical notes together. Two of the four cases below failed on causation.</li>
<li><strong>Match the injury to a listed type.</strong> Significant scarring is on the list, so photograph scars over time and keep the dated files.</li>
<li><strong>Record daily-life change in your own and others' words.</strong> A dated journal, a before and after list, and accounts written by people who saw the change.</li>
</ol>
<p>For the full document list, see <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">which medical records to keep for an injury claim</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="worth-suing">
<h2>Is it worth suing for pain and suffering?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">It can be, but only with the medical proof in hand.</strong> In our table, 7 of the 9 rulings that reached the threshold question went against the injured person.</p>
<p>Those losses turned on missing proof of permanence and causation, which is why the steps above come before any lawsuit. For value questions, see <a href="/new-jersey/is-50k-good-car-accident-settlement-nj-appeals-figures">what a fair amount to ask for pain and suffering looks like</a>.</p>
</section>

<p>Yes, but what you must prove depends on the lawsuit option on your auto policy.</p>

<section id="exceptions">
<h2>What are the exceptions to the verbal threshold?</h2>
<p>An out-of-state driver outside the New Jersey PIP system may not be able to use the threshold against you.</p>
<p>The threshold also never blocks a claim for economic loss. Your own share of fault still reduces what you recover; see <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how fault was split in real New Jersey cases</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="certification">
<h2>What does the doctor's certification have to show?</h2>
<p>It must state, on objective clinical evidence, that the injured body part has not healed to work normally and will not with more treatment.</p>
<p>Daily pain alone does not meet that test. You do not have to show a "serious life impact": the Supreme Court dropped that older test in DiProspero v. Penn (2005).<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

<p>For the filing window, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">when the doctor's certification is due</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="not-pain-and-suffering">
<h2>What does pain and suffering not cover?</h2>
<p>It does not cover medical bills or lost wages; those run through <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and medical bills</a>, <a href="/new-jersey/nj-can-you-claim-lost-wages-after-a-car-accident-in-nj">claiming lost wages after a crash</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">how New Jersey no-fault insurance works</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>What is the verbal threshold law in New Jersey?</h3></summary>
<p>It is the Limitation on Lawsuit option. You can claim pain and suffering only for death, dismemberment, significant disfigurement or scarring, a displaced fracture, loss of a fetus, or a permanent injury.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What is a lawsuit threshold?</h3></summary>
<p>It is the injury test you must pass before suing for pain and suffering. In New Jersey it applies only on the Limited Right to Sue option.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What does zero threshold mean?</h3></summary>
<p>It is a common name for No Limitation on Lawsuit: no injury test applies before you claim pain and suffering. DOBI calls it the "Unlimited Right to Sue."</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How much money can I sue for pain and suffering?</h3></summary>
<p>There is no formula, and another person's verdict says nothing about yours.
