<h1>Had a car accident in Newark? At least 7,436 people were hurt here in two years, and 826 accidents hit someone on foot.</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>If you were hurt in a car accident in Newark (Newark City, Essex County), you are in the town with more injury accidents than any other in New Jersey.</strong> 5,305 accidents here left someone hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024, about 7.3 a day. 7,436 people were hurt and 63 died. Route 21 (McCarter Highway) has the most of any numbered road. The busiest corners in 2024 and 2025 are North 5th Street at Orange Street and 15th Avenue at South 13th Street, tied at 8 each.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by hudcostreets/nj-crashes, fatal crashes supplemented from the NJ State Police feed; our analysis, files <code>data/profiles/newark-nj-car-accidents.json</code> and <code>data/newark-nj-car-accidents/routes.csv</code>. Newark ranks 1st of 573 municipalities by injury accidents in 2023 and 2024.</p>
<p>These are floors, not exact totals. Newark's own records thin out sharply from September 2024, which we explain <a href="#records-gap">below</a>.</p>

<section id="pedestrians">
<h2>One in ten New Jersey pedestrian accidents happens in Newark</h2>
<p>Newark had 826 police-reported pedestrian accidents in 2023 and 2024. That is 9.7 percent of the state's 8,521, while Newark had 4.7 percent of the state's injury accidents. Pedestrian accidents make up 14 percent of Newark's injury accidents, against 7 percent statewide.</p>
<p>24 of the 63 people killed in Newark in those two years were pedestrians, 38 percent. Statewide the share was 32 percent (409 of 1,294). Newark also had 290 accidents involving a cyclist.</p>
<p>The mix of other accident types differs too. Rear-end accidents are 23 percent of Newark's injury accidents, against 31 percent statewide. Right-angle accidents (25 percent) and same-direction sideswipes (12 percent) are more common here than across New Jersey (22 and 9 percent).</p>
<p class="source">Source: <code>data/profiles/newark-nj-car-accidents.json</code> (crash_type_mix_2023_2024) and <code>data/statewide.json</code>; shares computed in <code>data/newark-nj-car-accidents/summary.json</code>. Counts of crashes, not rates per person or per mile driven.</p>

<section id="after-dark">
<h2>Two in three Newark road deaths happened after dark</h2>
<p>42 of Newark's 63 deaths in 2023 and 2024 happened in accidents recorded as dark, 67 percent. Statewide the figure was 51 percent (658 of 1,294). Most Newark injury accidents still happen in daylight, 62 percent of them.</p>
<p>The small hours stand out as well. 11 percent of Newark's injury accidents happened between midnight and 6 am, against 8 percent statewide. The busiest stretch is 3 pm to 6 pm, with 1,090 injury accidents in the two years.</p>
<p class="source">Source: light condition and hour fields, NJDOT crash records 2023 and 2024; <code>data/newark-nj-car-accidents/summary.json</code> and <code>data/profiles/newark-nj-car-accidents.json</code> (time.by_3h_window). Hour-by-hour patterns for the whole state are on our <a href="/new-jersey/when-do-most-car-accidents-happen">when car accidents happen in New Jersey</a> page.</p>

Their accident reports come from a different place than city-street reports; see <a href="#getting-the-report">getting the report</a>.</p>

<section id="getting-the-report">
<h2>Getting the report for a Newark car accident</h2>
<p>Start with the agency named on the card or slip the officer gave you. Where you crashed decides which route applies.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>On the Turnpike or the Garden State Parkway inside Newark:</strong> request the report on the state's NJ.gov Crash Reports Request portal, toll road option, for $5. The same portal handles State Police reports for non-toll roads at $13. Reports stay available for six years from the year of the crash. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
<li><strong>On a city street, if Newark police took the report:</strong> the request goes to the Newark Police Division. Its published address is 480 Clinton Avenue, Newark, NJ 07108, non-emergency phone (973) 733-6000. We could not read the city's records request page on 2026-11-10, so we do not state a fee or wait time. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.newarkpublicsafety.org/contact/">Newark Department of Public Safety, Contact Us</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
<li><strong>If no officer came:</strong> the Division's "File a Police Report" page links New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting crash form. Its own online reporting program covers other incidents, such as theft or lost property, and excludes incidents on state highways and inside Newark Liberty International Airport. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.newarkpublicsafety.org/how-do-i/file-a-police-report/">Newark Police Division, File a Police Report</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
</ul>
<p>Every agency's route, including the State Police and other towns, is on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a>.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Newark. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
  <p>Next steps: <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey accident report, agency by agency</a>, and <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">how New Jersey no-fault (PIP) insurance works after an accident</a>. This is general information, not legal advice.</p>

<section id="method">
<h2>Method and sources</h2>
<p>An injury accident here is a police-reported crash in which at least one person was hurt or killed, as coded on the New Jersey police crash report (NJTR-1). Scope: every crash NJDOT assigns to the municipality Newark City, Essex County, on every road type.</p>
<p>Records: NJDOT crash records for 2023 (per-table archive) and 2024 and 2025 (<a href="https://njdot.aashtowaresafety.net/njdot-crash-data-dashboard#/">NJDOT Crash Data Portal</a>), normalized by the open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">nj-crashes</a> project, with fatal crashes filled in from the <a href="https://www.nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/index.shtml">NJ State Police fatal crash statistics</a>. File downloaded 2026-11-06. 2023 and 2024 are complete statewide, but Newark's late 2024 looks incomplete (see above). 2025 is preliminary.</p>
<p>Road rankings use 2023 and 2024 and group on the route number. Corner rankings use 2024 and 2025 and do not merge spelling variants. All figures are counts, not risk rates: a busier road has more accidents partly because more cars use it.</p>
<p>Scripts and outputs: <code>lane2-content/data/build_place_profiles.py</code> and <code>lane2-content/data/newark-nj-car-accidents/newark_cuts.py</code>, with method notes in <code>lane2-content/data/README.md</code>.</p>
