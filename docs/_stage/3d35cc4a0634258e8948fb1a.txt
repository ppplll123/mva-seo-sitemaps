<h2 id="deaths">Who died on I-78, and why were so many outside a moving car?</h2>
<p>From 2023 to 2025, 28 fatal accidents on I-78 killed 31 people, and 13 of them died on foot or in an accident that began at a stopped vehicle.</p>
<p>Eight of those killed were pedestrians, on foot on or beside an interstate. Five fatal accidents began with a moving vehicle hitting a parked one, and they killed 6 people. In 2024 alone, four of the 11 fatal accidents began that way.</p>
<p>Darkness stands out too. 22 of the 28 fatal accidents happened in the dark, against about 36 percent of the injury accidents in 2023 and 2024. Ten were on stretches coded "dark, no street lights".</p>

<h2 id="appeals">Have New Jersey appeals courts ruled on crashes at a stopped vehicle?</h2>
<p>Yes, and two of the four such decisions in our file turned on PIP, the no-fault medical cover, rather than on who hit whom.</p>
<p>In Bell v. Hardy (2025, Essex), a driver whose parked vehicle was struck was held "statutorily barred" from suing the other driver. The other three, including a fatal rear-end crash into a tractor-trailer parked on a highway shoulder, were affirmed and are listed in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-appeals-index">New Jersey car accident appeals index</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

<h2 id="west">Is the western end of I-78 safer?</h2>
<p>It has far fewer injury accidents, not fewer deaths: Hunterdon and Warren counties had 7 of the 21 deaths on I-78 in 2023 and 2024.</p>
<p>Deer are the western hazard. <h2 id="when">When do I-78 accidents happen, and what kind are they?</h2>
<p>Most often on the evening commute: 5 pm to 6 pm had the most injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 (101).</p>
<p>The three hours from 3 pm to 6 pm held 260, about 22 percent. October had the most of any month (135) and February the fewest (72). Tuesday was the worst day (189) and Wednesday the quietest (152).</p>
<p>Rear-end accidents are 43 percent of injury accidents (518 of 1,195), sideswipes 24 percent and fixed objects such as guide rails 21 percent. See our <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">rear-end crash guide</a> and the <a href="/new-jersey/when-do-most-car-accidents-happen">statewide hour-by-hour page</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

<h2 id="report">Who takes the report for an I-78 crash?</h2>
<p>Mostly a reporting station, not a town: records name Somerville, Perryville or Newark station for 66 percent of I-78 accidents in 2023 and 2024.</p>
<p>Hudson County is the exception. Only 32 percent of records there name a station, and 99.5 percent code the road "State Authority". Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">guide to reading a New Jersey crash report</a> explains codes like these.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

<section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us.</strong> They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on I-78. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup></li>
</ul>
