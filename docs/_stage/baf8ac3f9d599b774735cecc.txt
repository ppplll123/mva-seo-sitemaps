<h1>New Jersey PIP Coverage Limits: Medical Bills After a Car Accident</h1><nav class="df-jump" aria-label="Jump to" data-df="added"><span>Jump to:</span><a href="#faq">Questions</a><a href="#sources">Sources</a></nav>

<section id="answer" class="df-answer">
<p class="df-lead"><strong>New Jersey PIP pays your medical bills whoever caused the crash, up to the limit you chose, from $15,000 to $250,000.</strong> A 2023 appeals ruling means your $250 deductible and co-pay sit on top of the limit, so a $15,000 limit covers about $16,200 of bills.</p>

<li><strong>$1,200</strong>: your share of a $10,000 medical bill with the $250 deductible</li>
<li><strong>$250,000</strong>: still paid for brain, spinal cord and certain trauma injuries, even on a $15,000 limit</li>

<section id="deductible-copay">
<h2>Do the PIP deductible and co-pay reduce your limit?</h2>
<p>No. In Birmingham v. Travelers (2023), the Appellate Division held that the insurer owes the full limit, and your deductible and co-pay are paid on top of it.</p>
<p>The insurer had counted a $250 deductible and a $950 co-pay against a $15,000 limit. The court said no, so that limit can cover about $16,200 of bills before it runs out.</p>
<p>The other PIP rulings, on arbitration, rideshare policies and fee schedule disputes, are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-pip-appeals-decisions-permanent-injury">review of New Jersey PIP appeals decisions</a>.</p>
<h3>How do the deductible and 20% co-pay work?</h3>
<p>You pay the deductible first, then 20% of bills between the deductible and $5,000. Above $5,000 the insurance company pays everything up to your limit.</p>

<section id="limit-options">
<h2>What are the PIP limits in New Jersey?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">On the Standard Policy you choose a medical limit of $15,000, $50,000, $75,000, $150,000 or $250,000.</strong> DOBI says most drivers pick $250,000.</p>

<p>Do not assume you have $250,000. The limit that counts is the one on your declarations page on the date of the crash.</p>
<h3>Do I need personal injury cover on car insurance in NJ?</h3>
<p>Yes, every Standard and Basic Policy includes PIP. Our <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">explainer on whether New Jersey is a no-fault state</a> shows how it fits with fault. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">which coverages are mandatory in New Jersey</a>.</p>
<h3>Does a $15,000 limit still pay for serious injuries?</h3>
<p>Yes, up to $250,000 for permanent or significant brain injury, spinal cord injury or disfigurement. It also covers trauma center or acute care treatment of other permanent or significant injuries right after the crash, until the patient is stable.</p>
<p>Those payments use up the lower limit you chose first. The Basic Policy has a $15,000 PIP limit with the same rule.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="hospital-bill">
<h2>Who pays a hospital bill after a car accident in NJ?</h2>
<p>Your own PIP pays it first, after your deductible and co-pay, unless you chose your health plan as primary.</p>
<h3>What is PIP primary?</h3>
<p>It is the default: auto PIP pays before your health plan. Our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">guide on whether PIP or health insurance pays first</a> explains the other choice.</p>
<h3>Does a provider balance mean PIP has run out?</h3>
<p>DOBI's medical fee schedule sets how much PIP pays for each listed service, including home care, ambulance and medical equipment. A provider balance does not prove your PIP has run out, so check which of three numbers someone is quoting.</p>

<p><strong class="df-k">Only if you kept the Extra PIP Package, which DOBI says most consumers choose but you can decline.</strong> A medical-only selection pays treatment and medical equipment, nothing else.</p>

<section id="above-the-limit">
<h2>What happens when medical bills go over your PIP limit?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">You can claim bills above your limit, and bills PIP does not cover, from the driver who caused the crash.</strong> The limitation on lawsuit option does not block this, as our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">verbal threshold rulings review</a> shows.</p>
<p>How future treatment costs split between PIP and that claim after the 2026 Murray v. Punina ruling is in our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">guide to bills PIP and health insurance leave unpaid</a>.</p>
<p>If you were partly to blame, read <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what happens when you are partly at fault</a>. If the driver had no insurance, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured and underinsured motorist claims</a>, and for every other step the <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident help guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="settlement-payback">
<h2>Will PIP take money back from my settlement?</h2>
<p>Bills PIP paid or can still pay are kept out of your claim against the at-fault driver.</p>
<p>That is New Jersey's rule against double recovery. Your claim against that driver covers bills above your PIP limit or outside PIP, plus pain and suffering where your lawsuit option allows it.</p>
<p>In Liberty Mutual v. Penske (2019), a PIP reimbursement dispute, including whether the other insured was at fault, had to go to arbitration. That case was between companies, not about an injured person's settlement.</p>
<p>Our sources set no single rule on whether an insurer can claim part of your own settlement, so ask before you sign. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-7-signs-you-may-need-an-accident-lawyer-after-a-crash">signs you may need an accident lawyer</a>. Under the court rule, a lawyer's contingent fee is worked out before any medical lien is deducted.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="treatment-review">
<h2>What can you do if PIP denies a bill or treatment?</h2>
<p>Ask for the written reason, then use the insurer's internal appeal and, if needed, binding PIP arbitration.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Find your declarations page: note your PIP limit, deductible and whether you have the Extra PIP Package.</li>
<li>Give every provider your PIP claim number so they bill the PIP insurer (or your health plan, if primary).</li>
<li>Your doctor requests approval through decision point review and precertification, on the Uniform Attending Provider Treatment Plan Form.</li>
<li>If a bill is denied, get the reason in writing and ask your provider to file the insurer's internal appeal.</li>
<li>If that fails, the dispute can go to binding PIP arbitration, explained in our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP denial and arbitration guide</a>.</li>
<li>Get your <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey car accident report</a> for any claim against the other driver. PIP arbitration does not handle that claim.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></li>
</ol>

</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>What else do people ask about NJ PIP?</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Do I have to pay PIP back if I win my case?</h3></summary>
<p>Our sources set no general rule. Bills PIP paid, or can still pay, are not part of your claim against the at-fault driver, so the settlement is not meant to cover them twice. Whether an insurer can claim a share depends on your policy and facts, so ask a <a href="/new-jersey/nj-7-signs-you-may-need-an-accident-lawyer-after-a-crash">New Jersey accident lawyer</a> first.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What PIP deductible should I choose?</h3></summary>
<p>DOBI lists $250 as the deductible most drivers choose; $500 to $2,500 cost less. In DOBI's $10,000 example, the $250 deductible leaves you paying $1,200 and the $2,500 deductible $3,000.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can you increase PIP coverage?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes. The Standard Policy goes up to $250,000, and DOBI says some insurers offer more.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long does PIP pay for treatment in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Until your dollar limit runs out, subject to the insurer's review rules. A suit for PIP benefits generally must start within two years of the expense, or within two years of the last PIP payment. See the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">PIP suit deadline versus other NJ deadlines</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Does PIP cover you as a pedestrian?</h3></summary>
<p>Usually yes, from your own household auto policy. A 2026 ruling also required pedestrian PIP on a rideshare policy; our <a href="/new-jersey/hit-by-car-pedestrian-cyclist-nj-appeals">review of pedestrian and cyclist appeals</a> covers who pays. See <a href="/new-jersey/hudson-county-rideshare-accident-help">Uber and Lyft injury claims in Hudson County</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></p>

</details></section>
