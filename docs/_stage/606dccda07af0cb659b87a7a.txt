<h1>Lakewood NJ accident records: New Hampshire Road corners, people on foot and the Lakewood PD report</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Lakewood had more car accidents where someone was hurt or killed than any other town in Ocean County: 1,617 in 2023 and 2024. That is 27 percent of the county total, and the 8th highest of 573 municipality codes in New Jersey's crash records.</p>
  <p>Those accidents hurt 2,136 people and killed 13. Two things set Lakewood apart. New Hampshire Road shows up at more of the town's busy corners than any other street, and almost half of Ocean County's pedestrian crashes happened here.</p>
  <p>Most Lakewood crash reports are held by the Central Records unit of the Lakewood Police Department on 3rd Street. Garden State Parkway crashes in town are State Police reports, with their own portal and help line, set out further down.</p>

  <p>Police reported 183 pedestrian crashes in Lakewood in 2023 and 2024, out of 374 in all of Ocean County. That is 49 percent of the county's pedestrian crashes, from a town with 27 percent of its injury accidents.</p>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Ordering a crash report from Lakewood Central Records or the Parkway portal</h2>
  <h3>Lakewood Police Department</h3>
  <p>The Lakewood Police Department's Central Records page says report requests can be emailed to records@lakewoodpolicenj.com or mailed to PO Box 540, Lakewood, NJ 08701. Include a copy of a valid picture ID and a phone number. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.lakewoodpolicenj.com/records/">Lakewood Police Department, Central Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <p>The same page says reports can be picked up at the department, 231 3rd Street, and that accident reports are also available online through crashdocs.org or lexisnexis.com. Public records (OPRA) requests go through the Township Clerk's office instead. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.lakewoodpolicenj.com/records/">Lakewood Police Department, Central Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <p>Pickup hours differ between two official pages. The police records page gives 8:15 am to 4:00 pm, while the township's police FAQ gives 10:00 am to 5:00 pm, Monday to Friday. Call the non-emergency number the township lists, 732-363-0200 ext 5712, before you go. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.lakewoodnj.gov/department/police">Township of Lakewood, Police Department page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <p>If you think the report contains an error, the township FAQ says to call 732-363-0200 extension 5360 and ask the Watch Commander to have the investigating officer call you back. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.lakewoodnj.gov/department/police">Township of Lakewood, Police Department page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <h3>Garden State Parkway: State Police</h3>
  <p>In the preliminary 2025 records, 140 of Lakewood's 4,367 crash rows name a State Police station, and 136 of those are on the Garden State Parkway. For 2023 and 2024 the station field is almost empty, so earlier years cannot be split this way.</p>
  <p>Parkway crash reports are ordered on the NJ State Police crash report portal at <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>. The portal lists a $5.00 fee for toll road reports and keeps reports for six years from the year of the crash. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <p>For a question about a Parkway report, the portal sends you to the New Jersey Turnpike Authority at 732-442-8600 ext. 2431. The State Police line, 609-882-2000 ext. 2234, is for State Police reports on roads without tolls, boat crashes and serious or fatal crash records. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Lakewood. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
