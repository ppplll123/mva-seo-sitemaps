<h1>Route 35 NJ accident today? What the police records show, Rahway to Berkeley</h1>

<p>This page is not a live traffic feed, so it cannot tell you about a crash that happened today. If you were in a car accident on Route 35, get medical attention first. Your police report will come from the department that responded, which on Route 35 is usually the local police department.</p>

<p>What we can show is the record. In 2023 and 2024, police reported 5,257 car accidents on Route 35. In 1,315 of them someone was hurt or killed: 1,815 people hurt and 13 killed. Seven of those 13 people were pedestrians.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>, fatal crashes supplemented from <a href="https://nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/">NJ State Police fatal crash statistics</a>; our analysis of the NJDOT records, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h2>More than half the people killed on Route 35 were on foot</h2>

<p>Pedestrian crashes are a small slice of Route 35 records: 74 of 5,257 in 2023 and 2024, about 1.4 percent. Yet those crashes killed 7 of the 13 people who died on the road in those two years. Statewide, pedestrians were 409 of 1,294 people killed in the same period, about 32 percent.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our count of the Route 35 and statewide records (NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes, NJSP fatal supplement), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<p>Adding the preliminary 2025 records, 9 of 16 people killed on Route 35 from 2023 to 2025 were pedestrians. Eleven of the 16 fatal crashes happened in the dark or at dusk. These are small numbers, so one more or one fewer death moves the share a lot.</p>

<p>Ocean Township in Monmouth County stands out. All four Route 35 deaths recorded there were pedestrians, and all four happened after dark or at dusk. Two of them were recorded at Sunset Avenue, in April 2024 and November 2025.</p>

<h2>A summer road on the barrier island</h2>

<p>South of Brielle, Route 35 runs through the Ocean County shore towns. There the crash count rises sharply in summer. In 2023 and 2024, the Ocean County stretch had 338 police-reported accidents in June, July and August, against 77 in December, January and February.</p>

<p>Accidents where someone was hurt show the same swing: 71 in the summer months against 14 in winter. July was the busiest month, with 124 accidents over the two years. Farther north the season matters much less. Monmouth County's stretch had 943 summer accidents against 742 in winter.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our counts by season, county and month, NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes, retrieved 2026-11-10. These are counts, not rates: summer traffic volume is not in the data.</p>

<h2>Rear-end crashes, the 3 pm to 6 pm peak and Monmouth's top state route</h2>

<p>Rear-end crashes were 37 percent of Route 35 accidents where someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024, against 31 percent statewide. Right-angle crashes were 24 percent, close to the statewide 22 percent. Crashes into a fixed object were only 6 percent, about half the statewide share of 12 percent.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the Route 35 and statewide records, NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<p>Accidents where someone was hurt peaked between 3 pm and 6 pm, with 321 of the 1,315. May had the most of any month, 150, and January the fewest, 55. Route 35 is also Monmouth County's busiest state route for these accidents: 727 in two years, against 389 on Route 9 in the county.</p>
<p class="source">Source: time block of the Route 35 profile; our ranking of state routes by county (all name variants of each route number combined), retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>

<h2>Route 35 is mostly town police territory: where each report sits</h2>

<p>On Route 35 the report is usually written by the local police department of the town where the crash happened. One rough signal is an undocumented field naming a State Police station. It is almost empty statewide in 2023 and 2024, so only 2025 is useful. In preliminary 2025 records, 5,669 of 8,028 Garden State Parkway crashes (71 percent) name a station, against 2 of 2,457 on Route 35. The police report itself is the NJTR-1, which only police complete.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 35. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<h2>Which records this page treats as Route 35</h2>
<p>A record counts here when NJDOT codes it to route number 35 and its road name carries a state route prefix. Almost all of them read "NJ 35", including those marked SECONDARY (the other direction or roadway as NJDOT codes it) and NJ 35 Z. Twelve records carry another state road string instead: NJ 36 Secondary (4), NJ 36 (2), NJ 138 (2), and NJ 440, NJ 66, NJ 34 and NJ 27 (St Georges Avenue) once each. One of those 12, the NJ 27 record, involved someone hurt; they stay in the counts, as in the profile. That is 7,714 records from 2023 to 2025 across 32 towns. 2023 and 2024 are complete; 2025 is preliminary. Ten records coded to parking lots along the highway are included, as in the profile; none involved an injury.</p>
