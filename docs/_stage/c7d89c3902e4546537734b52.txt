<h1>Car accident in Bridgewater, NJ: Route 22, I-287 and which police report you need</h1>

<p>Most car accidents where someone gets hurt in Bridgewater Township happen on highways, not at local corners. In 2023 and 2024, NJDOT records show 703 accidents in Bridgewater where someone was hurt or killed. They hurt 972 people and killed 5. Route 22 and I-287 alone carried 304 of the 703, or 43%. Only Franklin Township (978) had more injury accidents in Somerset County, and Bridgewater ranks 39th of 573 New Jersey municipalities.</p>
<p>The road also tells you where to look for your police report. In the 2024 and 2025 records, almost every Bridgewater crash on I-287 or I-78 names a State Police station. Crashes on Route 22, Route 202, Route 28 and local streets almost never do. <a href="#bridgewater-report">The report steps for both cases are below.</a></p>

<section id="bridgewater-side-swipe">
<h2>Side-swipe accidents run at almost twice the Somerset share</h2>
<p>Police coded 103 of Bridgewater's 703 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 as "Same Direction (Side Swipe)". That is 14.7% of the town total. Across Somerset County the share is 7.5%, and statewide it is 8.5%. Route 22 (38) and I-287 (32) hold 70 of the 103.</p>
<p>Two of the five people killed in 2023 and 2024 died in side-swipe-coded accidents, both on I-287. By contrast, right-angle accidents are a smaller share here than elsewhere: 13.4% in Bridgewater, 19.0% in the county and 22.4% statewide.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the NJDOT records for Bridgewater, Somerset County and New Jersey.</p>
<p>A side-swipe code describes how the vehicles met. It does not say which driver caused the accident. More on these crashes is in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-side-impact-collision-claim-support-help">side-impact and sideswipe accident guide</a>.</p>

<section id="bridgewater-when">
<h2>Weekday afternoons hold most of the injury accidents</h2>
<p>Weekdays carried 552 of Bridgewater's 703 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 (79%), more than the five-sevenths share an even spread would give. Thursday (120), Tuesday (119) and Wednesday (116) were the busiest days. Saturday had 71. Between noon and 6 pm there were 317 (45%), and the 5 pm hour had the most, 60.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the NJDOT records (injury accidents 2023 and 2024). The statewide pattern is on our <a href="/new-jersey/when-do-most-car-accidents-happen">hour-by-hour New Jersey accident page</a>.</p>

<section id="bridgewater-report">
<h2>Which police agency has your Bridgewater accident report</h2>
<p>Start with the road. In 2024 and 2025, 957 of 994 Bridgewater crash records on the interstates named a State Police station, 952 of them Somerville. By road name that was 846 of 870 records on I-287 and 110 of 116 on I-78; the other 8 interstate records named neither road. On state highways such as Route 22, Route 202, Route 206 and Route 28, only 12 of 1,550 did. County roads had none of 484, and municipal streets 1 of 453.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes, "station" field (State Police station on the record; blank when none). 2023 is left out because the field is mostly blank statewide that year. This is a strong pattern, not a rule. The agency named on your report number is what counts.</p>

<h3>I-287 and I-78: the State Police crash report portal</h3>
<p>A trooper-handled crash on I-287 or I-78 is a non-toll crash, so its report comes through the State Police portal on njportal.com. There the basic NJTR-1 can be downloaded only after it is finished and approved. A search that comes back empty usually means waiting a couple of days and trying again. The fee the portal lists for a non-toll road report is $13.00. It keeps these reports on offer for six years, counted from the crash year, and a delivered report can be downloaded for up to 14 days. For trouble with the online request itself, the portal gives the email address njsprecords@njsp.gov. It lists (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234 for questions about non-toll road reports, and asks that you call about a boat report or a serious or fatal crash records request only after one month has passed.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash reports, njportal.com</a> (fee, approval, six-year retention, 14-day download window, email and phone conditions, retrieved 2026-11-10); NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>

<h3>Route 22, Route 202, Route 28 and local streets: Bridgewater Police Records Bureau</h3>
<p>When Bridgewater police investigated, the report comes from the department's Records Bureau at 100 Commons Way. Its page asks you to allow five business days for a report to be completed, and to call ahead to check it is ready. Have your case or incident number with you. The department gives no information from police reports over the phone, and it charges a fee it calls nominal.</p>
<ul>
  <li>Email: records@bridgewaternj.gov</li>
  <li>Phone: (908) 725-4044 or (908) 722-4111 ext. 5; calls taken 9:00 am to 3:45 pm</li>
  <li>Office hours: Monday through Friday, 8:30 am to 4:00 pm, closed on major holidays; after-hours pick-up can be arranged at the Police/Communications window</li>
</ul>
<p>The same page links to the LexisNexis BuyCrash site and to the township clerk's Open Public Records Act (OPRA) page.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.police.bridgewaternj.gov/divisions/records">Bridgewater Township Police Department, Records</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). The page does not state the fee amount, so we do not give one.</p>

<p>The NJTR-1 is filled in by police only. If no officer investigated your accident, drivers report it themselves on the SR-1 form (<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10). Bridgewater's own records spell one corner up to eight ways, so look hard at the road and cross street written on your copy. A checklist item in the state insurance department's 2026 guide reads: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed." Where something does not match, that guide says to "let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene" (<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25).</p>
<p>The Bridgewater department also posts press releases on serious and fatal crashes, including several in 2026 (<a href="https://www.police.bridgewaternj.gov/">Bridgewater Township Police Department</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10). 2026 accidents are not in the data on this page.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Bridgewater Township. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
