<h1>Paramus car accidents: three highways carry most of them, but the worst corners are on county roads</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>Paramus had 876 police-reported car accidents in 2023 and 2024 in which someone was hurt or killed.</strong> They hurt 1,194 people and killed 8. No other Bergen County town had more. Statewide, Paramus ranks 21st among the 573 municipality codes in the state crash records.</p>
<p>Six in ten of those accidents were on Route 17, Route 4 or the Garden State Parkway. Yet every one of the town's busiest intersections for injury accidents is a county road crossing. Forest Avenue appears in more of them than any other street. Rear-end accidents make up 55 percent of the total here, against 35 percent across Bergen County.</p>
<p>For 2025, the records show 411 accidents in which someone was hurt. That year is still <strong>preliminary</strong>. Paramus's 2025 total is 98 percent of its 2024 total, so it does not look visibly incomplete, but it can still change.</p>

<h2>Route 17, Route 4 and the Parkway: how much of Bergen's highway toll falls inside Paramus</h2>
<p>Route 17 is the single biggest source of injury accidents in Paramus, with 264 in 2023 and 2024. That is 35 percent of all such accidents on Route 17 anywhere in Bergen County. Route 4 is even more concentrated here: 174 of Bergen's 387 Route 4 injury accidents, or 45 percent, were inside Paramus.</p>
<p>County roads as a group, led by Forest Avenue, Paramus Road, Fairview Avenue and Oradell Avenue, come nearly level with Route 17 at 248. Borough streets and the Parkway each account for about one in ten.</p>

<section id="paramus-report">
<h2>Borough police, State Police or the Turnpike Authority: who to ask for a Paramus report</h2>
<p>Because so many Paramus accidents happen on state highways and the Parkway, the report may not sit with the borough. It is held by whichever police agency responded. The officer's paperwork or card shows the agency.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Paramus Police Department</strong>, 1 Carlough Drive, Paramus, NJ 07652, (201) 262-3400. Its Police Reports page offers copies in person, Monday to Friday, 8 am to 4 pm. Online, it links to the CrashDocs report service set to the Paramus Police Department. <span class="source">(<a href="https://paramuspolice.org/police-reports">Paramus Police Department, Police Reports</a> and <a href="https://paramuspolice.org/contact-us">Contact Us</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10; the site is linked as "Paramus Police Department" from the official <a href="https://www.paramusborough.gov/">Borough of Paramus</a> site)</span></li>

<li><strong>No officer came.</strong> The police crash report (NJTR-1) is filled out by police only. Drivers report a crash police did not investigate on the SR-1 self-reporting form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT Police Resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>

<p>Once you have the report, read it closely. New Jersey's insurance regulator suggests you "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed." If you see inconsistencies, it says to "let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">reading the codes on a New Jersey crash report</a> explains the boxes, including the crash type used in the tables on this page.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Paramus. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
<h2>Paramus data: records, scope and limits</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Records.</strong> NJDOT crash records: 2023 from the NJDOT per-table archive, 2024 and 2025 from the NJDOT Crash Data Portal, normalized by the open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a> project. Fatal crashes are supplemented from the NJ State Police fatal crash feed. Records file downloaded 2026-11-06.</li>
<li><strong>Scope.</strong> Every crash NJDOT assigns to the municipality "Paramus Boro" in Bergen County, on every road type. Bergen comparisons use every crash in Bergen County.</li>
<li><strong>Rank.</strong> The statewide rank of 21st compares Paramus with the 573 county and municipality codes in the 2023 and 2024 crash file. That is more codes than New Jersey's municipalities, so the rank is among codes in the records.</li>
<li><strong>Years.</strong> 2023 and 2024 are complete. 2025 is preliminary; Paramus's 2025 total is 98 percent of 2024. Intersections use 2024 and 2025 only, because NJDOT changed how it writes street names after 2023.</li>
<li><strong>Definitions.</strong> "Hurt or killed" means a police-reported accident with at least one person injured or killed. Road groups use the NJDOT road system and route fields: NJDOT state highway routes 17 and 4, State Authority route 444 (the Parkway), county, municipal and private property. Raw names such as "NJ 17" and "BERGEN COUNTY 59" are shown as Route 17 and County Route 59.</li>
<li><strong>Limits.</strong> Counts, not risk rates: there is no traffic volume in the data, and busier roads see more accidents. Street-name spelling variants are not merged, so intersection counts are a floor. Nothing in these records shows who caused an accident.</li>
<li><strong>Counts.</strong> We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file; the method notes on this page describe the selection. Our counts cover road groups, Paramus vs Bergen, deaths by road group, intersections with 2 or more injury accidents and the share of Bergen highway accidents.</li>
</ul>
