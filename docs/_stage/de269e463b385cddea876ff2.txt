<h1>Hamilton NJ accident data: injury crashes in Hamilton Township, Mercer County, and which police hold the report</h1>

<section id="answer">
  <p class="lead">Hamilton Township had 1,344 police-reported car accidents in 2023 and 2024 where someone was hurt or killed. That is more than any other town in Mercer County, Trenton included. In those two years 1,891 people were hurt and 18 were killed.</p>
  <p>Which police wrote your report depends on the road. In 2024, 373 of the 378 accidents recorded on the township's interstates (I-295 and I-195) carry a New Jersey State Police station. Accidents on township and county streets almost never do.</p>
  <p>These are counts from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 still preliminary. They are not live traffic, and they say nothing about who caused any accident.</p>
  <p class="source">Hamilton figures come from our own tally of NJDOT crash records (2023 to 2025), using the cleaned copy kept by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>, with State Police fatal data added (retrieved 2026-11-10). Working files: the Hamilton profile and hamilton-cuts-2023-2025.csv; see "Where the Hamilton counts come from" below.</p>

<section id="hamilton-vs-trenton">
  <h2>More injury crashes than Trenton, far fewer pedestrian crashes</h2>
  <p>Trenton recorded slightly more accidents overall in 2023 and 2024 (5,712 against Hamilton's 5,672). Hamilton still had more accidents where someone was hurt or killed: 1,344 against 1,182. Hamilton alone holds about 28 percent of Mercer County's injury and fatal accidents.</p>
  <p>The pedestrian picture runs the other way. Trenton recorded 175 accidents involving a pedestrian in those two years, Hamilton 50. Statewide, Hamilton ranks 13th of the 573 municipality names in the crash records by injury and fatal accidents.</p>

<section id="which-police">
  <h2>Who has your Hamilton accident report: State Police or the township</h2>
  <p>Hamilton's records include accidents on I-295, I-195 and the New Jersey Turnpike. On those roads the records nearly always name a State Police station. On township and county streets they almost never do. We use 2024 here because the station field is filled in most completely that year.</p>

  <h3>If State Police took the report (I-295, I-195, the Turnpike)</h3>
  <p>For a crash on I-295 or I-195 through Hamilton, or on a New Jersey Turnpike segment counted in Hamilton's records, you order the report yourself at <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com/njsp/crashreports</a>. An I-295 or I-195 report costs $13.00 there; a Turnpike report costs $5.00. Reports can be ordered for six years from the year of the crash. If yours is not ready, the portal says to search again in a couple of days. For questions about I-295 or I-195 reports, the portal lists the State Police at (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. For Turnpike reports, it lists the Turnpike Authority at (732) 442-8600 ext. 2431. Requests for serious or fatal crash records can take up to a month, and the portal asks you to call the State Police only after that month has passed.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: NJSP crash reports portal, <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/</a> (fees, six years, phone numbers and the one-month wait, retrieved 2026-11-10); research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8.</p>

  <h3>If Hamilton's own police took the report (local and county streets)</h3>
  <p>Reports written by a local police department are requested from that department, not from the State Police portal. Ask for the case number at the scene, then contact the township police records unit. We could not reach the township's website on 2026-11-10, so we do not list its records process, fees or wait time here. If no officer investigated, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the driver's form; the police NJTR-1 report is completed by police only.</p>
  <p class="source">Sources: research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8 (municipal procedures vary and were not verified); <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT Police Resources</a> (NJTR-1 is police-only, retrieved 2026-11-10).   <h2>Where the 18 deaths happened</h2>

  <p>Accidents involving a pedestrian killed five people in Hamilton in 2023 and 2024, all of them in 2024. Two of those fatal accidents were on municipal streets, two on state highways and one on a county road. Deaths overall went from 6 in 2023 to 12 in 2024; the preliminary 2025 count is 5.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Hamilton Township. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
