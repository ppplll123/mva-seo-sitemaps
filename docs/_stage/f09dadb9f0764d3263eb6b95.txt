<h1>Car accidents in Woodbridge: more on toll roads than in any other New Jersey town</h1>

<p>If you had a car accident in Woodbridge Township (Middlesex County), NJDOT crash records show 2,448 accidents where someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024, about 3.3 a day. Those accidents hurt 3,438 people and killed 13. More of them happened on the Garden State Parkway than on any other road in the township, and one in four happened on a toll road (the Parkway or the New Jersey Turnpike). The corners with the most such accidents in 2024 and 2025 are two on Route 35 (Amboy Avenue), at Main Street and at Grove Street, with 7 each.</p>

<section id="woodbridge-toll-roads">
<h2>No New Jersey town has more injury accidents on toll roads</h2>
<p>NJDOT tags every crash record with the kind of road it happened on. In Woodbridge, 638 of the 2,448 accidents where someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024 were on roads run by a state authority, almost all of them the Garden State Parkway and the New Jersey Turnpike. That is the highest count of any municipality in the state, and 8.3% of all such toll-road accidents statewide (7,668). The next town, Fort Lee, had 392.</p>
<p>The Parkway alone accounts for 458 accidents where someone was hurt in Woodbridge in those two years. That is 13% of every such accident on the whole Parkway (3,461), and more than twice the next town on the road, Bloomfield (221). Five of Woodbridge's 13 deaths in 2023 and 2024 were on the two toll roads, including a single 2024 Parkway crash in which three people died.</p>

<section id="woodbridge-report">
<h2>Which police report to ask for, and where</h2>
<p>Because so many Woodbridge accidents are on the Parkway and the Turnpike, the report you need may not be held by the township at all.</p>
<h3>Parkway or Turnpike accident</h3>
<p>New Jersey State Police and NJ Turnpike Authority crash reports, including the Garden State Parkway and the Turnpike, are requested online through the State Police crash report portal. The basic report (NJTR-1) can be downloaded once it is ready and approved; if it is not there yet, the portal says to search again in a couple of days. The portal lists a $5.00 fee for toll road crash reports and $13.00 for non-toll road reports, and keeps reports for six years from the year of the crash. A fatal Turnpike or Parkway NJTR-1 is requested through the Turnpike Authority's OPRA form instead.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10, as summarized in research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8.</p>
<h3>Accident handled by Woodbridge Police</h3>
<p>Woodbridge Police Department reports are picked up at the Records Bureau on the ground floor of the municipal building, 1 Main Street, Woodbridge. The township lists its hours as Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:15 p.m., and Tuesday 8:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m., excluding holidays. Vehicle crash reports cost $0.50 per page. Questions go to the Records Bureau at 732-602-7390. The page does not give a waiting time, so a report for an accident from today or this week may not be ready yet. The township also links an online records (OPRA) request form from its home page.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.twp.woodbridge.nj.us/225/Police-Reports">Woodbridge Township, Police Reports</a> (hours, location, fees, phone); <a href="https://www.twp.woodbridge.nj.us/Directory.aspx?DID=12">Woodbridge Township directory, Police Department</a> (1 Main Street address); <a href="https://www.govpilot.com/forms/main/e9db3133-6d9/Open-Records-Request/Township-of-Woodbridge/NJ/7221/1">Township of Woodbridge OPRA request form</a> (linked from twp.woodbridge.nj.us). All retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>If you are not sure which agency came to your accident, the agency name is on the report itself; our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">guide to New Jersey accident reports, agency by agency</a> covers the other routes. State Police patrol of non-toll roads in Woodbridge was not checked for this page.</p>
</section>

<section id="woodbridge-amboy-avenue">
<h2>Route 35 (Amboy Avenue) holds six of the township's busiest corners</h2>
<p>Corner counts in Woodbridge are small next to road totals: the busiest corner has 7 accidents where someone was hurt in two years, the Parkway has hundreds. But one pattern stands out. <section id="woodbridge-types">
<h2>Rear-end accidents are a bigger share here than statewide</h2>
<p>Rear-end accidents were 42% of Woodbridge accidents where someone was hurt in 2023 and 2024 (1,034), against 37% in Middlesex County and 31% statewide. Right-angle accidents were a smaller share here (15%, against 22% statewide), and so were pedestrian accidents (4%, against 7%). The police code describes how the vehicles met. It does not say who caused any particular accident. Our <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">rear-end car accident guide</a> covers that kind of accident in New Jersey.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Woodbridge. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
