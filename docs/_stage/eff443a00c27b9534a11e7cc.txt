<h1>Wall NJ car accident records: rear-end crashes on Routes 35, 34 and 138</h1>

<p>After a car accident in Wall Township, your police report usually comes from the Wall Township Police Records and ID Bureau, which asks you to allow 3 to 5 business days. A crash on the Garden State Parkway is a toll road report instead, with its own route. <a href="#wall-report">Both routes are set out below.</a></p>
<p>The records behind this page show a town with a clear pattern. In 2023 and 2024, Wall had 620 police-reported car accidents where someone was hurt or killed, more than any other Monmouth County municipality. Those accidents hurt 815 people and killed 4. Nearly half of them, 299 of 620 (48%), were coded as rear-end accidents. Across Monmouth County and across New Jersey, the rear-end share is about 31%.</p>

<h2 id="rear-end">Where Wall's rear-end accidents are recorded</h2>
<p>Wall's high rear-end share is concentrated on its state highways, not its local streets. On Route 138, 48 of the 62 accidents where someone was hurt were rear-end, or 77%. Route 34 and Route 35 sit between 54% and 61%. On county and local roads in Wall, the share is 29%, close to the county figure.</p>
<p>About seven in ten of Wall's injury accidents were on roads NJDOT lists as state highways, interstates or a state authority road such as the Parkway: 444 of 620. County roads carried 102 and municipal streets 74.</p>

<h2 id="easy-street">Easy Street at Herbertsville Road: eight injury accidents, all coded right angle</h2>
<p>One Wall corner stands well apart from the rest. Easy Street at Route 549 (Herbertsville Road) had 14 police-reported accidents in 2024 and 2025, and 8 of them hurt someone. All 8 injury accidents were coded "Right Angle", and so were 13 of the 14 accidents overall. They hurt 16 people, and nobody was killed.</p>
<p>No other Wall corner had more than 3 injury accidents in those two years. Six corners tie at 3, so a "second worst corner" cannot honestly be named. They are New Bedford Road at Route 35, Allenwood Road at Belmar Boulevard, Allenwood Road at County Route 30, Meeting House Road at Route 35, Route 35 at Route 524 Spur (Atlantic Avenue), and 17th Avenue at K Street. Another 19 corners tie at 2.</p>

<h2 id="wall-report">Allaire Road records bureau or State Police: who holds your Wall crash report</h2>
<h3>Crashes Wall Township Police worked</h3>
<p>Wall police handle report requests through the Records and ID Bureau at 2700 Allaire Road, Wall, NJ 07719, phone (732) 449-4500. The bureau processes requests from 8:00 to 4:00, Monday to Friday, and asks you to call ahead. Have the case or event number, the time and location of the accident, and the people involved ready.</p>
<ul>
  <li>The NJTR-1 crash report is requested online through the link on the department's <a href="https://www.wallpolice.org/accident-reports-online">Accident Reports Online page</a>. The department says to allow 3 to 5 business days. Reports involving serious injury, a death or further investigation take longer.</li>
  <li>By mail, the department asks for a legal-size self-addressed stamped envelope, the names of the parties, the date, the location, the case number, and a $5.00 check or money order payable to Wall Township Police, sent to the Records Division.</li>
  <li>Some details are blanked out by law, such as Social Security numbers and dates of birth, and all juvenile information.</li>
</ul>
<p class="source">Source: Wall Township Police, <a href="https://www.wallpolice.org/records-and-id-bureau/">Records and ID Bureau</a> and <a href="https://www.wallpolice.org/accident-reports-online">Accident Reports Online</a> (both retrieved 2026-11-10). The $5.00 mail fee is Wall's own; it does not apply to other departments.</p>
<h3>Wall's Parkway and I-195 stretches: the State Police route</h3>
<p>Wall's Parkway and I-195 crashes are largely State Police work, so the Allaire Road bureau is usually the wrong place to ask. In the 2024 and 2025 records, 186 of 499 Parkway accidents in Wall name the State Police Holmdel station, and 31 of 68 on I-195 name the Hamilton station. The rest name no station, so check the agency on any paperwork the officer gave you.</p>
<p>State Police reports are ordered on the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">njportal.com crash report portal</a>, which charges $5.00 for a toll road report, the Parkway included, and $13.00 for a report from a State Police road without tolls. The portal keeps reports for six years from the crash year, and a Wall Parkway report that has not posted yet can be searched for again a few days later. The NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau takes questions at NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov or (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234. Wall's own records page also sends Parkway crashes to an outside agency rather than its bureau, but it uses an older agency name, so start with the State Police portal.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a> and the NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page, both per <code>research/nj-primary-sources.md</code> section 8 (retrieved 2026-11-10); <a href="https://www.wallpolice.org/records-and-id-bureau/">Wall Township Police Records and ID Bureau</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10); station counts from <code>wall_cuts.csv</code> (station_2024_2025).</p>
<h3>A Wall crash with no police report: the SR-1</h3>
<p>Only police fill in the NJTR-1. When no officer investigated a crash in Wall, the driver uses New Jersey's SR-1 form instead, and Wall's Accident Reports Online page links to the online SR-1. The same Wall page notes that the receipt from the online form can go to your insurance company.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10); <a href="https://www.wallpolice.org/accident-reports-online">Wall Township Police, Accident Reports Online</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
<p>Once the Wall or State Police report arrives, compare its passenger count and other details with what you remember. DOBI's 2026 consumer guide suggests telling your insurance company about any mismatch and contacting the department that came to the scene, which here means either the Allaire Road bureau or the State Police station on the report. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>

<h2 id="when">Weekday commutes, July, and the unlit Parkway</h2>
<p>Wall's injury accidents lean toward weekdays and the evening commute. In 2023 and 2024, 491 of 620 (79%) happened Monday to Friday, against 75% countywide. The 3 pm to 6 pm window held 177 (29%), against 25% countywide, and 5 pm to 6 pm was the busiest single hour with 65.</p>
<p>July stands out more in Wall than in the county. Wall had 80 injury accidents across the two Julys and 28 across the two Januarys. Monmouth County had 685 and 460. These are small monthly numbers, so treat the gap as a pattern to watch, not a rule.</p>
<p>Darkness without street lights shows up more often here too. 44 of Wall's 620 injury accidents (7.1%) were coded "Dark (no street lights)", against 4.5% in Monmouth County and 3.8% statewide. 19 of those 44 were on the Garden State Parkway.</p>

<h2 id="wall-method">How these Wall figures were counted</h2>
<p>Data: NJDOT crash records for 2023 (per-table archive) and 2024 and 2025 (NJDOT Crash Data Portal), as normalized by the open-source project hudcostreets/nj-crashes, with fatal crashes supplemented from the NJ State Police feed. File <code>aashto_supplemented_crashes.parquet</code>, downloaded 2026-11-06. Rows: county Monmouth, municipality Wall Twp.</p>
<ul>
  <li>Wall's injury-accident figure counts every Wall Twp crash where at least one person was hurt or killed, and adds up people hurt and killed crash by crash.</li>
  <li>Wall's 2023 and 2024 rows are complete. Its 2025 rows are preliminary, and each 2025 figure above carries that label.</li>
  <li>The Easy Street corner and the tied corners use 2024 and 2025 only, since the 2023 export wrote Wall's street names differently. Differently spelled versions of the same Wall street stay separate, so a corner may have had more accidents than shown.</li>
  <li>Each road row adds both directions of that route, keyed on the route-number field with a state-style road name. A few records are misfiled or left out by that rule, so the road totals are approximate.</li>
  <li>Nothing here is divided by traffic volume. Route 35 or the Parkway can lead the table simply because more cars use them, and no figure says who caused any Wall accident or whether anyone has a claim.</li>
</ul>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Wall Township. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
