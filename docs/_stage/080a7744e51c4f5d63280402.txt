<section id="shape">
<h2>Is a merge that ends in a rear-end worse than a sideswipe?</h2>
<p>Yes, on these two roads. Rear-end crashes hurt someone in 178 of 762 cases, about 23 percent; sideswipes did in 62 of 498, about 12 percent.</p>
<p>So the usual picture of a merge crash as a scraped door is incomplete. A late merge that ends with one car braking in front of another hurts people about twice as often, and it is the harder one to argue, because the struck car is the one behind.</p>
<p>The claim sides of each shape are in our <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">rear-end crash guide for New Jersey</a> and the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-side-impact-collision-claim-support-help">side-impact collision claim guide</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="fault">
<h2>Who is at fault when a car merges into you?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Each driver gets a percentage, and you recover from the merging driver only if your share is not greater than theirs.</strong> Worked examples are in <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">partly at fault NJ accident splits</a>.</p>
<p>What decides the share is proof of who left their lane: dashcam video, witnesses and the lane each car held on the report. Our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-evidence-helps-prove-fault-after-an-nj-crash">what evidence proves fault in NJ</a> lists what weighs most.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="phantom">
<h2>What if the driver who cut you off kept going?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">A driver who forced you out of your lane and left can still be an uninsured motorist claim on your own policy, even without contact.</strong> The reporting steps are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-hit-and-run-accident-claim-help">hit-and-run and phantom vehicle claims</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="report">
<h2>Who has the police report for an I-80 or I-287 crash in Parsippany?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Usually the State Police: on I-287, 314 of the 439 township crash records name the Netcong station.</strong> Township police cover Route 46, Route 202, Route 10 and local streets.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Check the officer's card for the agency and station before you search; I-80 records split between two stations, mapped in our <a href="/new-jersey/i-80-nj-accidents">I-80 accident data</a>.</li>
<li>For a State Police report, search the NJSP and Turnpike portal by date and name. Fees and wait times for every agency are in the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey car accident report guide</a>.</li>
<li>For a township report, use the OPRA records request on the Parsippany-Troy Hills Police Department forms page.</li>
</ol>

<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>What else do drivers ask after a merge crash?</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Who pays for car damage in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>PIP never pays for your car. You claim through your own collision coverage minus the deductible, or through the merging driver's insurer, which pays only its driver's share. Most sideswipes here hurt no one, so see the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-property-damage-claim-help-and-steps-guide">property damage claim steps</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Who is most at fault in a rear-end collision?</h3></summary>
<p>Not always the driver behind. New Jersey gives a share to every driver whose conduct caused the crash, so a car that cut in and braked can carry part of the fault.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How do you read the police report code in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Read each code only inside its box. In Box 105, 01 is a same-direction rear-end and 02 a same-direction sideswipe, and Vehicle 1 is an identifier, not a fault finding. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-to-read-a-new-jersey-crash-report-after-an-accident">how to read an NJ crash report</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How much can the merging driver's insurance pay?</h3></summary>
<p>At least the state minimum, which rose on 1 January 2026; your own underinsured motorist coverage can add the difference up to your limit. See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">New Jersey minimum insurance limits for 2026</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits for medical bills</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long do I have to sue after a Parsippany crash?</h3></summary>
<p>Two years from the crash for an injury suit; see the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

</details></section>
