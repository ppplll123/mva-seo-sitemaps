<h1>Plainfield NJ car accident records: four in ten injury accidents are right-angle collisions</h1>

<p>In Plainfield, the typical car accident that hurts someone is two vehicles meeting at a right angle on the city's street grid. Right-angle collisions made up 41% of the 839 Plainfield accidents where someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024. Across Union County the share was 24%, and statewide it was 22%.</p>
<p>Those 839 accidents hurt 1,161 people and killed 7. By this count Plainfield ranked 27th among New Jersey municipalities, and 4th in Union County.</p>

<h2 id="accident-mix">Plainfield's accident mix is the reverse of the state's</h2>
<p>Statewide, rear-end collisions are the most common accident where someone gets hurt. In Plainfield they are only 11%, and right-angle collisions lead by a wide margin. More than half of Plainfield's injury accidents were coded at an intersection, against about a third statewide.</p>

<h2 id="parked-cars">One in four reported Plainfield accidents hit a parked car</h2>
<p>Of 3,640 police-reported Plainfield accidents in 2023 and 2024, 925 (25.4%) involved a moving vehicle striking a parked one. The share is 13.4% countywide and 11.4% statewide. Most of these hurt no one: 70 of the 925 did.</p>
<p>In 2024 and 2025, the most parked-car accidents were recorded on West Front Street (Route 28) with 42, East Front Street with 39, Park Avenue with 38 and West 7th Avenue with 37.</p>

<h2 id="timing">Sunday is Plainfield's busiest day, and evenings run late</h2>
<p>Union County as a whole has its fewest injury accidents on Sunday (1,176 in 2023 and 2024). Plainfield does not. Its 132 Sunday injury accidents were the most of any day of the week, narrowly ahead of Friday's 131.</p>
<p>Evenings also weigh more here. From 6 pm to midnight, Plainfield had 31% of its injury accidents, against 24% countywide. The single busiest hour was 5 pm to 6 pm, with 73.</p>

<h2 id="report">Getting the police report for a Plainfield accident</h2>
<p>If an officer came to your Plainfield accident, that officer's agency wrote the NJTR-1, a form no driver can fill in. If nobody from the police investigated, the record you make yourself is the SR-1.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT Police Resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>Request the report from the agency that came to the scene. We could not verify a Plainfield police records page, so we do not list one. If the New Jersey State Police handled your accident, its reports are requested online through the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">State Police online report portal</a>, with questions to NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov or (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page and njportal.com crash report service, retrieved 2026-11-10; research/nj-primary-sources.md section 8.</p>


<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Plainfield. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
