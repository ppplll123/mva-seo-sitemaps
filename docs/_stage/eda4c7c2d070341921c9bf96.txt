<section id="claim-against-driver" class="df-answer">
<h2>Can you sue the at-fault driver for lost wages in NJ?</h2>
<p class="df-lead"><strong class="df-k">Yes, even on the limitation on lawsuit option.</strong> DOBI says that option "does not affect your ability to sue for economic damages such as medical expenses and lost wages."</p>
<h3>The NJ Transit case: wages recovered after a failed threshold</h3>
<p>In a 2018 Appellate Division case, an NJ Transit employee hurt in a work crash failed the permanent-injury threshold. The court still let NJ Transit pursue <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-appeals-index">$22,949 in wage loss and $33,625 in medical costs</a> from the at-fault drivers.</p>
<p>The threshold limits only pain and suffering, as our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">review of NJ verbal threshold rulings</a> shows.</p>
<h3>PIP wages are not paid twice</h3>
<p>Amounts paid, or collectible, under PIP stay out of the case against the at-fault driver. So that claim covers wage loss above your income continuation limit, or all of it if you have no income continuation.</p>
<h3>Your share of fault cuts the amount</h3>
<p>Your wages drop by your fault percentage, and you recover nothing if your share is greater than the other drivers'. See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how fault splits worked in real New Jersey cases</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="pip-income-continuation">
<h2>Does PIP pay lost wages regardless of fault?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes, if your policy includes income continuation.</strong> DOBI says it "pays lost wages, less Temporary Disability Benefits you may receive," no matter who caused the crash. Three things follow:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>You may not have it.</strong> Income continuation sits in the Extra PIP Package on the Standard Policy. You can decline the package, though most drivers choose it.</li>
<li><strong>The cap is yours.</strong> The limit is the amount you selected, not a fixed state figure. DOBI says higher amounts may be available, so ask your insurer before renewal.</li>
<li><strong>Temporary Disability Benefits come off.</strong> The two run together: PIP pays your lost wages minus those state benefits.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></li>
</ul>

<p>Check your declarations page or Coverage Selection Form for the Extra PIP Package and the amount you chose. For deductibles and medical limits, see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">what NJ PIP covers and its limits</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="work-crash">
<h2>What if you were hurt driving for work?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Workers' compensation, not only PIP, may be paying your wages.</strong> In the NJ Transit case, the employer covered a work crash and then pursued the at-fault drivers for the wage loss it paid.</p>
<p>So in a work crash, the at-fault driver can face a claim from the payer as well as from you. Tell both your employer and your auto insurer about the crash.</p>
</section>
<section id="compare">
<h2>PIP or the other driver: who pays which wages?</h2>
<p>PIP pays first, up to your chosen amount; the at-fault driver's insurer answers for the rest, reduced by your fault share.</p>

<section id="records">
<h2>How does insurance calculate lost wages?</h2>
<p>Each payer subtracts what another source already covered: PIP takes off Temporary Disability Benefits, and the driver's insurer takes off what PIP paid or could pay.</p>

<section id="pip-disputes">
<h2>What if PIP refuses to pay your lost wages?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">DOBI's PIP dispute resolution program covers income continuation too, not only medical bills, so bring your wage records.</strong> The filing steps are in our guide to <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits and denied claims</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="deadlines">
<h2>How far back can you submit an insurance claim for lost wages?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">PIP benefit suits and suits against the driver run on separate clocks, and a crash with a public vehicle, such as NJ Transit, needs written notice within 90 days.</strong> Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">table of every NJ car accident deadline</a> has the dates.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>What else do people ask about lost wages and PIP?</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Does PIP coverage have to be paid back?</h3></summary>
<p>Amounts paid or collectible under PIP are kept out of your case against the at-fault driver. The driver's insurer does not pay those wages a second time, so the claim covers only what PIP left unpaid.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Is PIP income continuation coverage worth it?</h3></summary>
<p>Without it, PIP pays no lost wages, and only the claim against an at-fault driver is left. DOBI says most drivers choose the package.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Does New Jersey have state disability insurance?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes, <a href="https://myleavebenefits.nj.gov/worker/tdi/">Temporary Disability Benefits</a>. If you receive them, PIP income continuation pays your lost wages minus those benefits.</p>
</details></section>
