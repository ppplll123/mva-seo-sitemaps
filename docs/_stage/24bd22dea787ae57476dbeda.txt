<h1>Jersey City car accidents: the most bicycle crashes in New Jersey, and the streets where walkers and riders get hurt</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
<p><strong>If you were hurt in a car accident in Jersey City, you are in the New Jersey town with more bicycle crashes than any other.</strong> In 2023 and 2024, NJDOT classed 440 police-reported crashes here as bicycle crashes. Newark, second, had 290. Jersey City holds about 1 in 12 of the state's bicycle crashes, yet only about 1 in 44 of its injury accidents.</p>
<p>Overall, 2,561 accidents in Jersey City hurt or killed someone in those two years, 3rd of 573 New Jersey municipalities. 3,316 people were hurt and 21 died. Of those injury accidents, 28 percent are crashes NJDOT classes as pedestrian or bicycle crashes, against about 10 percent statewide. A crash with a rider or walker present can carry another crash type, so these shares are floors.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records for 2023, 2024 and 2025 (the open nj-crashes dataset from hudcostreets, with fatal crashes filled from State Police data); our analysis of the NJDOT records. Bicycle = NJDOT crash type "Pedalcyclist"; walker = crash type "Pedestrian". Shares: 726 of 2,561 Jersey City injury accidents; 11,747 of 112,966 statewide.</p>

<p>Jersey City's own project page gives rare, measured detail about one West Side corridor. The corridor links the Mallory Avenue protected bikeway to the West Side Avenue light rail station.</p>

<section id="police-report">
<h2>Getting the police report for a Jersey City car accident</h2>
<p>Which office holds your report depends on who came to the scene. NJTR-1 crash reports are written by police only. If no officer investigated, drivers report the crash themselves on the SR-1 form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT Police Resources</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
<h3>Jersey City Police Department</h3>
<p>The city lists "Accident Reports &amp; Police Records" among the services at Two Jackson Square (356 Martin Luther King Dr.), Jersey City NJ 07305, 2nd floor. Its payment note says: "Accident Reports and other Police Records can only be paid for using cash or money orders." The page does not state the fee or how long a report takes. Check the page for current office hours before you go.</p>
<p class="source">Source: City of Jersey City, <a href="https://www.jerseycitynj.gov/cityhall/public_safety/parking">Division of Parking</a> (contact (201) 547-5538), and <a href="https://www.jerseycitynj.gov/cityhall/public_safety/Police">Jersey City Police Department</a> page (non-emergency (201) 547-5477), both retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<h3>I-78 crashes: check which agency came to the scene</h3>
<p>NJDOT codes 1,045 of Jersey City's 2023 to 2025 crash records on I-78 as a State Authority road. Do not assume the State Police wrote your report. Most of these records (700 of 1,045) name no State Police station, and some are intersection crashes where I-78 meets Grove Street, where city police may have responded. Look at the agency named on the paperwork the officer gave you, and ask that agency.</p>
<p>If State Police or the Turnpike Authority handled it, request the report on the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJ Turnpike Authority crash report portal</a>. It lists $5.00 for toll road reports and $13.00 for non-toll road reports. For questions about Turnpike and Garden State Parkway reports, the portal names the Turnpike Authority at 732-442-8600 ext. 2431. State Police at 609-882-2000 ext. 2234 handle questions on non-toll road reports and on serious or fatal crash records. The email njsprecords@njsp.gov is for help using the online service.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: our analysis of the NJDOT records (station field of the same records: 700 of 1,045 blank, rechecked on 2026-11-10); <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJTA crash report portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10.</p>
<p>When the report arrives, read it closely. New Jersey's insurance regulator advises: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed. If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene." <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance, 2026</a>, p. 25)</span> Our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a> covers other agencies.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Jersey City. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
<h2>Where the Jersey City numbers come from</h2>
<p>We counted police-reported crashes that NJDOT assigns to the municipality Jersey City, Hudson County, on every road type. On this page, "injury accident" means the police record lists at least one person hurt or killed. The records are NJDOT crash data for 2023, 2024 and 2025, normalized by the open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">nj-crashes project</a>, which also fills fatal crashes from the <a href="https://www.nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/index.shtml">NJ State Police fatal crash feed</a>. The 2023 and 2024 years are complete statewide; 2025 is preliminary.</p>
<p>Corners, streets and the Claremont counts use 2024 and 2025 only, because street naming changed between the 2023 and 2024 exports. The same street can appear under several spellings, and we did not merge them, so those counts are floors. Bicycle and pedestrian counts follow the NJDOT crash type field, so they are floors too. Route totals group records by NJDOT route number. All numbers are counts, not risk rates. They say nothing about who was at fault in any accident or what any claim is worth. We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file, data downloaded 2026-11-06.</p>
