Families can read <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-wrongful-death-accident-claim-support-guide">what New Jersey families can do after a fatal accident</a>.</p>
</section>

<h2>What else do drivers ask about Route 33?</h2>
<p>Fridays and the 3 pm to 6 pm window are the worst times, and Route 33 has far fewer deaths than Route 1 or Route 9.</p>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Was there an accident on Route 33 today?</h3></summary>
<p>Not on this page, which counts police records. The preliminary 2025 file already has 1,185 Route 33 records, close to the 1,189 for 2024. For first steps after a fresh accident, see the <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident help hub</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What day and time see the most Route 33 injury accidents?</h3></summary>
<p>Friday had 127 of the 655, about 19 percent, against about 16 percent statewide. The 3 pm to 6 pm window had 161, about one in four. The statewide pattern is on <a href="/new-jersey/when-do-most-car-accidents-happen">when most New Jersey accidents happen</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Where does Route 33 meet other numbered highways?</h3></summary>
<p>In Neptune Township it meets <a href="/new-jersey/route-35-nj-accidents">Route 35</a>, with 32 accidents at Stokes Avenue, and <a href="/new-jersey/route-18-nj-accidents">Route 18</a>. It crosses <a href="/new-jersey/route-130-nj-accidents">Route 130</a> in East Windsor and Robbinsville, <a href="/new-jersey/route-9-nj-accidents">Route 9</a> in Freehold Township and <a href="/new-jersey/route-1-nj-accidents">Route 1</a> in Trenton.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Is Route 33 the deadliest road in New Jersey?</h3></summary>
<p>No. Route 33 had 8 deaths in 2023 and 2024, against 50 on <a href="/new-jersey/route-1-nj-accidents">Route 1 in NJ</a>, 27 on Route 9 and 13 on Route 35. The <a href="/new-jersey/most-dangerous-intersections-nj">statewide intersection ranking</a> compares single corners.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

<section class="place-common df-rules" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
<h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us.</strong> They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 33. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
<h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays eligible medical expenses "no matter who caused the auto accident," up to your policy's limit and after its deductible and co-pay. If you chose your health insurance as primary, it pays first and PIP pays second. A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate: it depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></li>
<li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's, or the combined share of all the drivers you claim against (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></li>
<li>If someone is hurt or killed, or property damage to any one person is over $500, the driver must tell the police right away and send a written report to the Motor Vehicle Commission within 10 days, unless a police officer files one. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></li>
<li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-11">11</a></sup></li>
</ul>
