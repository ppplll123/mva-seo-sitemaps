<section id="how-common">
<h2>How often do crashes involve three or more vehicles in NJ?</h2>
<p>About one New Jersey crash in 17 involves three or more vehicles, but those crashes produce one in nine of the crashes where someone is hurt or killed.</p>
<p>Our count of 514,320 police-reported crashes from 2023 and 2024 shows the jump clearly. Each added vehicle raises the share of crashes with an injury and the number of people hurt per crash.</p>

<section id="where-when">
<h2>Where and when do chain-reaction crashes happen?</h2>
<p>Mostly on the Garden State Parkway and the big commuter routes, and mostly between 3 pm and 6 pm.</p>
<ul>
<li>The Parkway had 1,566 crashes with three or more vehicles, more than any other road. That is 9.3 percent of all Parkway crashes, against 5.8 percent statewide.</li>
<li><a href="/new-jersey/route-1-nj-accidents">Route 1 crash data</a> shows the next highest count, 1,207, then Route 95 (the Turnpike and I-95) with 1,049 and <a href="/new-jersey/i-80-nj-accidents">I-80 crash data</a> with 740.</li>
<li>25 percent of these crashes fell between 3 pm and 6 pm; the 5 pm hour alone held 9.3 percent.</li>
<li>Toll roads carried 10.3 percent of 3-plus vehicle crashes, against 7.4 percent of all crashes.</li>
</ul>
<p>That toll-road share is why a pile-up report is often on the NJ.gov State Police and Turnpike portal rather than with a town. Fees and every other agency are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get a New Jersey crash report</a>.</p>
<p>Parkway mileposts with the most crashes are on our <a href="/new-jersey/garden-state-parkway-accidents">Garden State Parkway crash data page</a>, and hour patterns for every crash type are in <a href="/new-jersey/when-do-most-car-accidents-happen">when most car accidents happen in New Jersey</a>. First steps for any crash are in our <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident help guide</a>.</p>

<section id="who-is-at-fault">
<h2>Who is most at fault in a chain-reaction rear-end crash?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Not automatically the last car.</strong> A jury sets a percentage for every driver, so the driver who started the chain can carry the largest share, even one who never touched you.</p>
<p>A middle driver pushed into the car ahead is in the same position: the push counts against whoever caused it. In each case below, the injured driver sued more than one party.</p>

<section id="describe">
<h2>How do you describe a crash with several impacts?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Describe only what you felt and saw, in order, and say plainly where you do not know.</strong> A guessed sequence is the statement insurers quote back later.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Write what your car was doing just before the first contact: speed, lane, braking.</li>
<li>Count the jolts you felt and number them. If you cannot tell which car caused one, say so.</li>
<li>Note where each car ended up and which part of your car each one hit: front, rear or side.</li>
<li>Collect each driver's plate, insurer and policy number.</li>
<li>Mark photos taken after the cars were moved and keep dashcam files in their original form; <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-evidence-helps-prove-fault-after-an-nj-crash">evidence that helps prove fault</a> lists the rest.</li>
</ol>
<p>Tell the clinician about every contact, including hitting the wheel, window or seat. Delayed symptoms are covered in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-how-long-car-accident-symptoms-can-take-to-show-up">how long car accident symptoms can take to show up</a>.</p>
<p>A worked lane-by-lane example is our <a href="/new-jersey/i280-east-orange-crash-lane-sequence-guide">I-280 East Orange crash sequence guide</a>.</p>

<h2>How many insurance claims does a pile-up create?</h2>
<p>Expect one claim number per vehicle; none of them means fault was accepted.</p>
<p>Your medical bills still go to your own PIP, as set out in <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP or health insurance first</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">is New Jersey a no-fault state</a>. What each caller may ask is in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>.</p>
<p>Car repairs follow the <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-property-damage-claim-help-and-steps-guide">property damage claim steps</a>, and filing deadlines are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">the New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.</p>

<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Can someone sue you personally after a car accident in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes. If a jury gives you a share of the chain, the drivers you hit can claim against you. Your bodily injury liability coverage pays those claims up to its limit; the Basic Policy does not include it as standard.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Do I need to call the cops after a pile-up?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes, if anyone is hurt or any one person's property damage tops $500, and 43 percent of these crashes hurt someone. New Jersey law says to notify police by the quickest means. The rule is set out in <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey#police">calling police after a rear-end crash</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What if the driver who started the chain drove off?</h3></summary>
<p>Your own uninsured motorist coverage can stand in for the missing driver, as in Carbajal. The steps are in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-hit-and-run-accident-claim-help">hit-and-run claim help in New Jersey</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Should I get a lawyer for a car accident that wasn't my fault?</h3></summary>
<p>Consider it when you reject an insurer's written fault percentage or offer, which is more likely with several insurers.
