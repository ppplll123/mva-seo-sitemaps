In one NJ case, a jury awarded $200,000, and UM paid only its $15,000 limit for the driver who fled.</p>

<section id="police-never-came">
<h2>They took off and the police never came. Do I still have a claim?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Often yes, but the proof is now on you.</strong> Report it yourself the same day at the police station in the town where it happened, get a report number, and tell your insurance company "hit and run". Your own UM covers a car that hit you and drove off.</p>
<h3>When the police were called but never showed up</h3>
<aside class="story-quote"><img alt="Illustrative sketch, not the real person" height="96" loading="lazy" src="https://sitemap.injuriescases.com/images/portraits/nj-hit-and-run-accident-claim-help-R001.webp" srcset="https://sitemap.injuriescases.com/images/portraits/nj-hit-and-run-accident-claim-help-R001@2x.webp 2x" style="width:96px;height:96px;border-radius:50%;float:left;margin:0 12px 6px 0" width="96"/><p>What they told us, in short: he said it was a hit and run, and the police were called but never came.</p><p><strong>E., Gloucester County resident, crash in Pennsylvania, 2023</strong></p><p style="font-size:12px;opacity:.75">Illustrative sketch to protect privacy. Want to share your story? sandrahbrown@injuriescases.autos</p></aside>
<p>What went wrong: no officer came, so there was no scene report, and no witness was named. The crash was across the river, so the NJ lawyer couldn't take the case and passed it to a Pennsylvania firm.</p>
<p>Crashed in another state? Its rules usually decide who can take it: see <a href="/new-jersey/car-accident-in-another-state-nj-resident">what changes for a NJ driver hit in another state</a>.</p>
<p>In NJ, if anyone was hurt, call police "by the quickest means"; if no officer files a report, send your own written one within 10 days.</p>
<h3>What people hit by a driver who fled were missing</h3>
<ul>
<li><a href="/new-jersey/check-uninsured-motorist-coverage-nj">Check your UM before you call</a>, on your policy's coverage page: only 1 of the 7 hit and run callers among 584 NJ calls for help could say they had it, and 42% of all callers didn't know.</li>
<li>Get a witness's name and number: a witness or a camera is often the only way to find the car, yet only 28% of people who call have one.</li>
<li>Crashed outside NJ? Say so on the first call: it is a common reason a NJ lawyer gives a case back (25 of 39 such cases with a known place; 10 Pennsylvania, 9 New York).</li>
</ul>
<h3>No car insurance at home? The state fund needs notice in 180 days</h3>
<p>If nobody in your household has car insurance, a state fund may pay: the <a href="/new-jersey/what-is-the-ucjf-nj">Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund</a>. Send it written notice within 180 days of the crash, and show you made "all reasonable efforts" to find the driver and car. You cannot use it if you owned an uninsured car yourself.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

<section id="medical-bills">
<h2>Who pays my medical bills after a hit and run in NJ?</h2>
<p>Your own PIP does, up to your limit, after you pay its deductible and co-pay.</p>
<p>If you picked your health plan to pay first, it pays first: see <a href="/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">how PIP and health insurance split medical bills</a>. No car insurance at home? PIP may come from the state's <a href="/new-jersey/what-is-the-ucjf-nj">Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund</a> (UCJF); see our <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">guide to PIP limits and medical bills</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="uninsured-motorist">

<p><strong class="df-k">Yes, if your policy has UM.</strong> NJ law covers a hit-and-run car under UM, and courts treat a car that drove off and was never found (a "phantom vehicle") the same way. Read your policy's UM section this week: it sets how fast to tell them and what proof they need.</p>

</div>
<p>These are 2 of the 76 NJ car crash appeals we read. In Carbajal, a second driver was found and paid the rest; with no second driver, your UM limit is the most you get.</p>
<p>Koukounias lost because the jury found no lasting injury, so keep a record of each doctor visit. Want more? Read <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-underinsured-motorist-appeals-decisions">New Jersey UM and UIM appeals decisions</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="report">
<h2>How do I report a hit and run in NJ?</h2>
<p>Call police right away; if no officer files a report, send the MVC your own written SR-1 report within 10 days.</p>
<p>Need the finished report? See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">getting your New Jersey car accident report</a>, and for the rest of day one, <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-do-in-the-first-24-hours-after-a-new-jersey-car-accident">what to do in the first 24 hours after a crash</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-6">6</a></sup></p>

<h3>How do I find out who hit me?</h3>
<p>Police trace the driver from what you give them, so hand the officer these:</p>
<ul>
<li>A full or partial plate, and the vehicle's color and type</li>
<li>Business or home cameras facing the road</li>
<li>Witnesses' phone numbers, which way the car went, and the time</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="police-next">
<h2>Will police do anything about a hit and run?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">You can find the police report without the other driver's details.</strong> Once it is finished and approved, which can take 14 business days from the crash, the NJ.gov website finds it by your own last name or plate.</p>
<p>Turnpike, Parkway or Port Authority crash? Those agencies have the report, with their own fees: see our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey accident report guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-7">7</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="parked-car">
<h2>What if someone hit my parked car and drove off?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Report it to police, then claim under your own collision coverage if you bought it.</strong> Next, get it fixed: see our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-property-damage-claim-help-and-steps-guide">New Jersey property damage claim guide</a>.</p>
<p>The driver who hit it had to tell police if the damage passed $500. Police recorded 58,460 crashes into parked cars in NJ in 2023 and 2024, though the records don't say how many drivers left.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-8">8</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="if-found">
<h2>What if police find the hit and run driver?</h2>
<p>Then you may claim from that driver's insurance for people they hurt (bodily injury liability), which pays for pain, suffering and lost wages.</p>
<p>How much they must carry depends on the policy year: see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-insurance-minimum-limits-2026">New Jersey minimum insurance limits for 2026</a>. If the driver had only a little insurance and your UIM limit is higher, your UIM may pay the rest.</p>
<p>Partly to blame, or on the limited lawsuit option? See <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">what happens if you were partly at fault</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">New Jersey verbal threshold appeals rulings</a>. Watch the clock: our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></p>

</section>
<section id="next-steps">
<h2>What should I do this week after a hit and run?</h2>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li><strong>Say "hit and run"</strong> when you open the claim with your insurance company, and ask in writing what its UM section requires.</li>
<li><strong>Get the police report</strong> to your insurance company, since without one the claim could be denied.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-10">10</a></sup></li>
</ol>

<p><strong class="df-k">Insurance company calling?</strong> See <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>; for anything else, our <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">New Jersey car accident guide</a>.</p>
<p>Hit while walking or on a bike? See <a href="/new-jersey/hit-by-car-pedestrian-cyclist-nj-appeals">what NJ appeals courts decided for people like you</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Hit and run questions people also ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Do I have to pay anything after a hit and run?</h3></summary>
<p>Some. You pay your PIP deductible and co-pay, your collision deductible if you claim for the car, and the first $500 of damages, which a UM claim does not pay.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-9">9</a></sup></p>
