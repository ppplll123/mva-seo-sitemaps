<h1>Elizabeth NJ car accident records: pedestrians, one right-angle corner, and the Turnpike</h1>

<section id="direct-answer">
<p><strong>Elizabeth (Elizabeth City, Union County) had 2,287 police-reported car accidents where someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024.</strong> 3,109 people were hurt and 24 died. That is the 5th highest count of 573 New Jersey municipalities and the highest in Union County, about a quarter of the county's total.</p>
<p>Three things set Elizabeth apart in these records. People on foot are hit far more often here than elsewhere in the county. One corner, East Grand Street at Reid Street, has more injury accidents than any other in the county, and nearly all of them are right-angle collisions. And the New Jersey Turnpike runs through the city, so the agency that holds your report depends on which road you were on.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by hudcostreets/nj-crashes, fatal crashes supplemented from the NJ State Police feed; our analysis, <code>data/profiles/elizabeth-nj-car-accidents.json</code> (years, rank) and <code>data/counties/union.json</code>. Elizabeth's share of Union County injury accidents: 2,287 of 9,235, 24.8 percent.</p>

<section id="people-on-foot">
<h2>Pedestrian crashes in Elizabeth run far ahead of the city's size</h2>
<p>Elizabeth had 351 police-reported pedestrian crashes in 2023 and 2024. Union County as a whole had 847, so 41 percent of the county's pedestrian crashes happened in this one city. Elizabeth's share of all county injury accidents was 25 percent.</p>
<p>The same gap shows against the state. 315 of the 351 pedestrian crashes hurt or killed someone, and 315 of Elizabeth's 2,287 injury accidents is 13.8 percent. Measured the same way, the share is 8.2 percent in Union County and 6.6 percent statewide. Elizabeth had 4.1 percent of New Jersey's pedestrian crashes but 2.0 percent of its injury accidents.</p>
<p>11 of the 24 people killed in Elizabeth in those two years were pedestrians. Statewide, pedestrians were 409 of 1,294 deaths, about 32 percent. 131 of Elizabeth's 351 pedestrian crashes were recorded in the dark.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <code>data/elizabeth-nj-car-accidents/summary.json</code> (eliz_ped_crashes, union_ped_crashes, state_ped_crashes, eliz_ped_killed_pk, eliz_ped_crashes_dark), <code>data/profiles/elizabeth-nj-car-accidents.json</code> (crash_type_mix_2023_2024), <code>data/counties/union.json</code> and <code>data/statewide.json</code>. NJDOT crash records 2023 and 2024. Counts of crashes, not rates per resident or per mile walked.</p>
<p>More than half of the pedestrian crashes, 198 of 351, were on city streets with no route number. Route 27 had 43 and Route 439 had 37. <section id="east-grand-reid">
<h2>East Grand Street at Reid Street: 47 of 53 crashes were right-angle</h2>
<p>East Grand Street at Reid Street had 24 accidents where someone was hurt in 2024 and 2025, with 35 people hurt. No other intersection in Union County reached 18 in the same period. The next Elizabeth corner, Magie Avenue at Stiles Street, had 11.</p>
<p>The crash type at this corner is unusually uniform. 47 of its 53 police-reported crashes were coded "Right Angle", and so were 21 of the 24 where someone was hurt. Citywide, right-angle crashes were 25 percent of injury accidents in 2023 and 2024. The count rose from 9 injury accidents in 2024 to 15 in 2025, though 2025 is preliminary.</p>
<p>A crash type describes how the vehicles met. It does not say who had the right of way or who caused any accident. Our page on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-side-impact-collision-claim-support-help">side-impact and T-bone collisions in New Jersey</a> explains how these accidents are documented.</p>

<section id="after-dark">
<h2>More of Elizabeth's injury accidents happen after dark</h2>
<p>854 of Elizabeth's 2,287 injury accidents in 2023 and 2024 were recorded in the dark, 37 percent. Statewide the share was 28 percent (31,491 of 112,966). 15 of the 24 deaths happened in the dark, against 51 percent of deaths statewide (658 of 1,294).</p>

<section id="summer-2024">
<h2>A thin summer in Elizabeth's 2024 records</h2>
<p>Elizabeth's 2024 total, 4,699 crashes, is lower than both 2023 and preliminary 2025. Most of the drop sits in four months. From May to August 2024 the records hold 1,058 Elizabeth crashes, against 1,989 in the same months of 2023 and 1,852 in 2025.</p>
<p>The rest of New Jersey shows no such dip: 85,985 crashes statewide in May to August 2023, 87,850 in 2024 and 88,573 in 2025. So the 2024 Elizabeth figures are best read as a floor. We found no official explanation, and we do not read the change as a real fall in accidents.</p>

<section id="method">
<h2>Where these Elizabeth figures come from</h2>
<p>Our scope is each police-reported crash in NJDOT's records coded to Elizabeth City, Union County, on any road. "Hurt or killed" means the crash injured or killed one or more people. Totals, roads, pedestrians and darkness cover 2023 and 2024, the two complete years. Corners are ranked on 2024 and 2025 instead, since the 2023 export records street names differently from the later ones.</p>
<p>The underlying records are NJDOT's: its per-table archive for 2023 and the <a href="https://njdot.aashtowaresafety.net/njdot-crash-data-dashboard#/">NJDOT Crash Data Portal</a> for 2024 and 2025. <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>, an open-source project, put both into one format and added fatal crashes from the NJ State Police feed. We downloaded its file on 2026-11-06, and its 2023 statewide fatal crash and death totals agree exactly with the State Police.</p>
<p>The Elizabeth-only cuts were run by <code>data/elizabeth-nj-car-accidents/elizabeth_cuts.py</code>, and the rest come from <code>data/profiles/elizabeth-nj-car-accidents.json</code>. A big count on a busy road such as the Turnpike reflects traffic as much as danger. Nothing in the records speaks to fault, claims or settlements.</p>
</section>

<section id="your-report">
<h2>Which agency has your Elizabeth accident report</h2>
<p>The report sits with the police agency that investigated, and in Elizabeth that depends on the road. The basic police crash report in New Jersey is the NJTR-1, which only police complete. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>On the New Jersey Turnpike (I-95).</strong> The Turnpike had 255 of Elizabeth's injury accidents in 2023 and 2024, and its crash reports are requested from a state site, not from the city. The Turnpike Authority and the State Police run one shared request site, charging $5.00 per toll road report. A fatal Turnpike crash is the exception: for that NJTR-1, the site sends you to the OPRA form on the Turnpike Authority's website instead. For questions about a Turnpike report, the site lists the Turnpike Authority's number, 732-442-8600 ext. 2431. Save njsprecords@njsp.gov for trouble with the online site itself. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJTA crash report request site</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
<li><strong>On city streets.</strong> The Elizabeth Police Department says its Records Division "maintains all police department records" and processes OPRA requests. The department is at 1 Police Plaza, Elizabeth, NJ 07201, phone 908-558-2000. The city page does not list a crash report fee or wait time, so ask when you call. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.elizabethnj.org/480/Property-And-Records">City of Elizabeth, Property and Records</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
<li><strong>Not the city's online reporting form.</strong> Elizabeth's Citizens Online Police Reporting System is for incidents with "no injured persons", and its damaged-property category "does not include motor vehicle collisions". Call the department or 9-1-1 instead. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.elizabethnj.org/539/Citizen-Incident-Reports">City of Elizabeth, Citizen Incident Reports</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></li>
<li><strong>On Route 1, Route 27, Route 439, I-278 or Route 81.</strong> We could not confirm from an official source which agency patrols each of these inside the city. Use the agency named on the card or case number the officer gave you.</li>
</ul>
<p>If no police officer investigated, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
</section>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Elizabeth. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
