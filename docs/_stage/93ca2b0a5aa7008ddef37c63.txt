<section id="what-counts">
<h2>Is it a head-on crash if the cars hit at an angle?</h2>
<p>Usually yes in the police code: New Jersey's crash report puts head-on and angled opposite-direction impacts in one category.</p>
<p>The NJTR-1 manual also explains that a same-direction crash can end front to front after a car spins. An oncoming car turning across you is coded Left Turn/U Turn instead, and glancing contact is an opposite-direction sideswipe.</p>
<p>So describe both paths before impact, not the damaged ends. Turning and crossing crashes are covered on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-side-impact-collision-claim-support-help">T-bone and sideswipe claims page</a>.</p>
<p>For other crash types see <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">rear-end crashes in New Jersey</a> and the <a href="/new-jersey/new-jersey-car-accident-help">statewide guide to what to do after a crash</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

<section id="fault">
<h2>Who is at fault in a head-on collision in NJ?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">No label decides it: New Jersey splits fault by percentage, and the insurer assigns each driver a share case by case.</strong> The rule itself is on our page on <a href="/new-jersey/partly-at-fault-nj-car-accident-real-fault-splits">how NJ splits fault by percentage</a>.</p>
<p>A spin after an earlier impact can put a car in your lane without its driver being the main cause. A defendant 60 percent or more responsible can be made to pay all the damages; below 60 percent, only its share.</p>
<p>That 60 percent line matters when a third car pushed someone across the center line. When both drivers say the other crossed it, see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-evidence-helps-prove-fault-after-an-nj-crash">what evidence helps prove fault</a>; wrong-way and impaired drivers are covered in <a href="/new-jersey/nj-drunk-driving-accident-help">drunk driving accident claims</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<tr><th scope="row" data-label="Case">State v. Liepe (2018)</th><td data-label="What happened">A rear-end impact sent a car into an oncoming vehicle; one death</td><td data-label="Result on appeal">Convictions affirmed, sent back for resentencing</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row" data-label="Case">Diero v. Kim (2025, Mercer)</th><td data-label="What happened">A left turn started a three-car crash that pushed a car into oncoming traffic</td><td data-label="Result on appeal">The 60-day certification was missed; dismissal with prejudice affirmed</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table></div>
</div>

<p>The lesson an injured driver can act on is Diero v. Kim: the 60-day certification was missed, and the dismissal with prejudice was affirmed. More rulings of this kind are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-verbal-threshold-appeals-rulings">verbal threshold appeals rulings</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="who-pays">
<h2>Who pays your bills after a head-on crash?</h2>
<p>Your own PIP pays your medical bills whoever crossed the line; the at-fault driver's insurer covers what PIP does not.</p>
<p>If the driver who came into your lane had no or too little insurance, your uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage applies, which the Basic Policy leaves out. See our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-uninsured-motorist-claim-help">uninsured motorist claim help</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup></p>

<p>The full split is on our page on <a href="/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">who pays after a crash in NJ</a>; limits are in <a href="/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">PIP limits for medical bills</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="first-steps">
<h2>What should you do in the days after a head-on crash?</h2>
<p>Lock down how each car approached before the scene changes, then keep the proof of your injuries.</p>
<ol class="df-steps">
<li>Tell the clinician where you sat, every symptom and any second impact, and keep the records listed in <a href="/new-jersey/medical-records-injury-claim-new-jersey">medical records for an injury claim</a>.</li>
<li>Order the report through <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">the New Jersey crash report guide</a> and check the collision code and lane diagram.</li>
<li>Keep full-length dashcam or doorbell video showing the lanes before impact, not just the hit.</li>
<li>Check your filing deadline on our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">NJ car accident statute of limitations</a> page.</li>
</ol>
</section>
<section id="faq" class="df-faqs">
<h2>Questions people ask</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Should I give a recorded statement to the other insurance company?</h3></summary>
<p>You may, but no New Jersey law sets what you must tell the other driver's insurer, and statements feed the fault share it assigns. Ask who will hear it and what it covers first; see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-expect-from-an-insurance-adjuster-after-an-accident">what to expect from an insurance adjuster</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can I refuse a recorded statement to an insurance company?</h3></summary>
<p>Refusing your own insurer can breach your policy duties. With the other driver's insurer there is no set list, but DOBI warns that not cooperating fully could lead to a denial.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What happens if the other driver is at fault?</h3></summary>
<p>Your PIP still pays your medical bills first. Their liability insurer covers what PIP does not, such as pain and suffering if your lawsuit option allows it and your car damage.</p>
</details></section>
