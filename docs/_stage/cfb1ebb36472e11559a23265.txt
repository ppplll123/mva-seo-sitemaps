<h1>Camden County car accidents: 2024 counts, the roads that lead, and who holds your report</h1>

<p>Camden County's 2024 crash records list 4,075 car accidents with at least one person hurt or killed: 5,620 people hurt in all, and 37 killed. Route 30 (the White Horse Pike) had more of these accidents than any other road in the county.</p>
<p>Your police report sits with the agency that came to the scene. In Camden City that is the Camden County Police Department. Cherry Hill, Pennsauken and Bellmawr each publish their own records route. On I-295, Route 42, I-76, I-676 and the New Jersey Turnpike, most records name a State Police station, so start with the State Police report portal. Atlantic City Expressway reports come from the Expressway's own authority. <a href="#report">The full list by agency is below</a>.</p>

<h2 id="years">Why this page leads with 2024 and not a two-year total</h2>
<p>The 2023 records for Camden County look incomplete. They hold 2,243 accidents where someone was hurt, against 4,075 in 2024. Statewide, 2023 held 90% of the 2024 figure. Camden County held only 55%.</p>
<p>The gap is uneven. Camden City (714 in 2023, 751 in 2024) and Cherry Hill (704 and 654) look steady. Pennsauken shows 20 such accidents in 2023 against 364 in 2024. Berlin Borough shows 2 against 86, and Lindenwold 15 against 132. A jump that size is far more likely a records gap than a real change on the roads.</p>
<p>Deaths hold up better. The fatal records carry a State Police top-up, and the county's death count stays level across the three years. So this page uses 2024 for accident counts and 2023 plus 2024 for deaths.</p>

<h2 id="winslow">Winslow Township: fifth for accidents, first for deaths</h2>
<p>Winslow Township had 301 accidents where someone was hurt in 2024, fifth in the county. But 14 people died in Winslow accidents in 2023 and 2024, more than in any other Camden County town. Camden City, with more than twice as many injury accidents, had 8 deaths.</p>
<p>Eight of Winslow's 14 deaths came in fixed-object accidents, where a vehicle hit something like a tree or pole. That is 8 of the county's 21 fixed-object deaths. Ten of the 14 deaths happened in the dark. Five of them were on the Atlantic City Expressway.</p>
<p class="source">Source: our analysis of the NJDOT records (fatal accidents supplemented from NJ State Police) and our count by town. A death count shows what happened, not why, and it says nothing about who was at fault in any one accident.</p>
<p>Across the county, pedestrians were the largest group of people killed: 26 of 78 deaths in 2023 and 2024. In 2024, Camden City had 72 of the county's 179 pedestrian accidents, about 40%. Yet only one of its eight deaths in 2023 and 2024 was a pedestrian. See <a href="/new-jersey/camden-nj-car-accidents">car accident data for Camden City and its pedestrian accidents</a> and <a href="/new-jersey/pedestrian-crash-new-jersey">help after a pedestrian accident in New Jersey</a>.</p>

<h2 id="types">Right-angle collisions ran well above the state share</h2>
<p>In 2024, 1,219 of Camden County's 4,075 injury accidents were coded "Right Angle," where vehicles on crossing paths collide. That is 29.9%, against 21.9% statewide. Rear-end accidents were the largest group here too, at 33.8%, close to the state's 30.9%.</p>
<p>Two types ran below the state share. Accidents coded "Left Turn/U Turn" were 1.9% here against 5.0% statewide, and pedestrian accidents 3.9% against 6.7%. The data cannot say why the mix differs. The code describes how the vehicles met, not who caused it.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Camden County. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<h2 id="method">Where the Camden County figures come from</h2>
<p>Every count here starts from NJDOT's own crash records. The 2023 rows come from NJDOT's per-table archive and the 2024 and 2025 rows from its Crash Data Portal, combined into one file by the open-source hudcostreets/nj-crashes project. That project tops up the fatal records with State Police data. Our copy of the file is dated 2026-11-06.</p>
<p>We count an accident as one "where someone was hurt" when the police record lists at least one person injured or killed. Each town is an NJDOT municipality code inside Camden County. The road table merges the record names that share one NJDOT route number, so Route 30's several spellings add up together. That is why our road totals run higher than the road list in the county profile, which keeps the spellings separate.</p>
<p>Corner counts cover just 2024 and 2025, since the 2023 export spells street names differently and cannot be paired reliably. Only the Gibbsboro Road corner has its spellings merged, so every other corner count may be low. Anything from 2025 carries a "preliminary" tag on this page because that year's records are not final. Without traffic volumes, a high count shows a busy place, not a more dangerous one. We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file; the method notes on this page describe the selection.</p>
