<h1>Wayne NJ car accidents: Route 202 leads once its seven local street names count as one road</h1>

<p>Wayne Township ranks 24th of New Jersey's 573 municipalities for car accidents where someone was hurt or killed. It had 848 of them in 2023 and 2024, out of 3,828 police-reported accidents. Those 848 accidents hurt 1,159 people, and 10 people died. Within Passaic County, only Paterson and Clifton had more.</p>
<p>Route 202 accounts for 151 of the 848, more than any other road in Wayne. A street-name count hides that, because the records spread Route 202 across seven local street names plus plain "US 202". By our reading of the records' station field, the Wayne Police Department wrote most reports on Wayne's local and numbered roads, and the New Jersey State Police wrote most I-80 reports.</p>

<h2 id="roads">Wayne's roads by route number, not by street name</h2>
<p>A road-name count splits Route 202 into many small pieces. In Wayne's 2024 and 2025 records it appears as Black Oak Ridge Road, Mt View Boulevard, Paterson-Hamburg Turnpike, Terhune Drive, Fayette Avenue, Boonton Turnpike, Ramapo Valley Road and plain "US 202". Ramapo Valley Road shows up on only 2 records, both with someone hurt. NJDOT also files part of Route 202 under the county road system and part under the state highway system.</p>
<p>We added the state and county system records back together by route number. Grouped this way, Route 202 has more accidents where someone was hurt than any other road in Wayne, in both periods below.</p>

<h3 id="hamburg-turnpike">Four numbered roads with Hamburg Turnpike in the name</h3>
<p>In Wayne's records, "Hamburg Turnpike" is part of the name of four numbered roads. They are County Route 504, County Route 689, Route 202 (as "Paterson-Hamburg Turnpike") and County Route 673 (as "Paterson-Hamburg Turnpike (Passaic County 673)", on 17 records from 2023 to 2025). The two busiest intersections in the next table sit on two different Hamburg Turnpikes.</p>
<p>So if you describe where your accident happened, to the police or your insurance company, the route number and the nearest cross street are clearer than the street name alone. The same goes for checking that the location on your report is right.</p>

<h2 id="deaths">Where Wayne's 10 deaths happened in 2023 and 2024</h2>
<p>Wayne had 10 people killed in 2023 and 2024, one more than Clifton, which had more than twice as many accidents where someone was hurt (2,195). Four of Wayne's 10 deaths were on I-80, although I-80 holds only 63 of the township's 848 accidents where someone was hurt.</p>
<p>Five of the 10 deaths were in fixed-object accidents, where a vehicle hits something like a pole, tree or barrier. Four deaths were on wet roads, which make up about a fifth of Wayne's accidents where someone was hurt.</p>

<h2 id="report">Getting your Wayne accident report: Wayne Police or State Police</h2>
<p>Which agency wrote your report mostly depends on the road. In 2024 and 2025 records, 319 of 409 I-80 accidents in Wayne list the State Police Totowa station. On Route 202, Route 23, Route 46 and County Route 504, almost none list a State Police station, so those reports usually come from the Wayne Police Department.</p>
<p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes, "station" field, 2024 and 2025 (2025 preliminary). Reading a listed station as a State Police report is our interpretation, not an NJDOT definition. If you are unsure, the officer's card or the report number will show the agency.</p>

<h3 id="wayne-police">Wayne Police Department reports</h3>
<p>The township says its Records and Identification Bureau handles all police reports. "Police reports are available within three working days after the officer takes the report, including accident reports." Copies can be picked up from 8:30 am to 4:30 pm on Mondays, Wednesdays, Thursdays and Fridays, and until 7 pm on Tuesdays.</p>
<p>The same page lists copy fees of 75 cents a page for pages 1 to 10, 50 cents for pages 11 to 20, and 25 cents after that. It also says accident reports are online "in partnership with CARFAX" at crashdocs.org, where they "are normally approved and posted within 7 days from the date the accident occurred." The non-emergency number is 973-694-0600.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://waynetownship.com/public-safety/wayne-police/police-operations/">Wayne Township Police, Operations Division, Records and Identification Bureau</a> and <a href="https://waynetownship.com/public-safety/wayne-police/">Wayne Police Department page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10. The online report link on that page is a CARFAX service; we did not test it.</p>

<h3 id="state-police">State Police reports for I-80</h3>
<p>If a trooper from the Totowa station handled your I-80 accident, order the report yourself on the NJSP crash report portal. I-80 has no tolls, so the $13 non-toll fee applies. The NJTR-1 becomes downloadable after the State Police mark it ready and approved. Mind the age limit: the portal keeps a report for six years, counted from the crash year.</p>
<p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash reports portal</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10 (. Questions: NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov or (609) 882-2000 ext 2234, per the NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page. The NJTR-1 is filled in by police only; drivers self-report with the SR-1 form (<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
<p>Before you send a copy to your insurance company, compare its location line with where you actually were. Four numbered roads here carry "Hamburg Turnpike" in their names and Route 202 runs under seven local names, so a wrong road is an easy slip. DOBI's consumer guide gives the general advice: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed. If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene."</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened in Wayne. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
