<section id="when">
<h2>When are shopping-trip crashes most likely on these roads?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">In the holiday season and the late afternoon.</strong> December had more crashes than any other month, and the 3 pm to 6 pm window had more than any other.</p>
<ul>
<li>December: 248 crashes, about a third above the monthly average of 188.</li>
<li>November and December together: 478 crashes, 21 percent of the year in two months.</li>
<li>3 pm to 6 pm: 617 crashes, 111 of them with someone hurt.</li>
<li>Saturday: fewer crashes than any weekday (303), but 25 percent hurt someone, against 18 percent on weekdays.</li>
<li>Sunday: 145 crashes, less than half the total of any weekday.</li>
</ul>
<p>Hour and month patterns for the whole state are in <a href="/new-jersey/when-do-most-car-accidents-happen">when most car accidents happen in New Jersey</a>.</p>

<section id="movements">
<h2>Who is at fault when you are rear-ended or cut off on Route 4 or Route 17?</h2>
<p>Fault is split by percentage among every driver whose conduct caused the crash, and you can still recover if your share is 50 percent or less.</p>
<p>Rear-end crashes made up 1,224 of the 2,252 on the two roads, and two out of three of those that hurt someone. Same-direction sideswipes, typical of lane changes toward an exit or store entrance, added 764.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Rear-end, retail lane.</strong> In Lamothe v. Huggins (2025), a driver hit three times from behind in a drive-thru won $930,500 for permanent neck injuries. The appeals court upheld it.</li>
<li><strong>Lane change, no contact.</strong> In Williams-Siraj v. Schwartz (2025), a driver swerved onto a curb to avoid a lane-changer. She lost because she could not prove that driver caused her injuries.</li>
</ul>
<p>Our <a href="/new-jersey/rear-end-crash-new-jersey">rear-end crash guide for New Jersey</a> covers fault splits behind you. The <a href="/new-jersey/nj-new-jersey-side-impact-collision-claim-support-help">side-impact collision guide</a> covers sideswipes.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>

<section id="report">
<h2>Do I need to call the cops if I get rear-ended on Route 4 or Route 17?</h2>
<p><strong class="df-k">Yes if anyone is hurt or killed, or if damage to any one person's property tops $500.</strong> The driver must tell police right away.</p>
<p>On Route 17, only 10 of 3,855 crash records from 2023 and 2024 name a State Police station, so in Paramus the report usually sits with Paramus Police. They take requests on their <a href="https://paramuspolice.org/police-reports" rel="noopener">police reports page</a>. Wait times and every other agency are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">New Jersey crash report guide</a>.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>

<h2>Paramus shopping-trip crash questions</h2>
<details class="df-faq" open><summary><h3>Who is most at fault in a rear-end collision?</h3></summary>
<p>Often the driver behind, but not always. New Jersey splits fault among every driver whose conduct caused the crash. In Lamothe, the driver hit from behind kept a $930,500 verdict on appeal.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>What if the other driver cut me off but never touched my car?</h3></summary>
<p>You can still claim, but you must prove that driver caused your injuries. In Williams-Siraj, a driver who swerved onto a curb to avoid a lane-changer lost on exactly that point.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>Can you sue someone if they hit you in a parking lot?</h3></summary>
<p>Yes, if their carelessness caused your injury. Lot crashes often have no police report; see <a href="/new-jersey/nj-what-to-do-after-a-parking-lot-accident-in-new-jersey">what to do after a parking lot accident in New Jersey</a>.</p>
</details><details class="df-faq"><summary><h3>How long after a car accident can you sue in NJ?</h3></summary>
<p>Usually two years for an injury lawsuit against the other driver. The exceptions are in our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-statute-of-limitations">New Jersey statute of limitations guide</a>.</p>
