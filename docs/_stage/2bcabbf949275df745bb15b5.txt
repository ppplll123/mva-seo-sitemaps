<h1>Route 37 car accidents in Toms River: what the police records show</h1>
<p class="micro" data-editorial-byline="publisher">By <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/about">Injuries Cases Autos</a></p>
<p class="lead">Route 37 is mostly a Toms River road in the accident records. Of 258 Route 37 car accidents where someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024, 235 were in Toms River Township. From 2023 to 2025, 6 pedestrians were killed on Route 37, and every one of those deaths was in Toms River.</p>
<p>The cross street named most often where someone was hurt is Route 166 (Main Street), in Toms River. If you were hurt on Route 37, the usual order is medical attention first, then the police report from the agency that came, then your own PIP claim for medical bills.</p>

<section id="timing">
  <h2>Afternoons, Wednesdays and early summer</h2>
  <p>In 2023 and 2024, 60 of the 258 Route 37 accidents where someone was hurt happened between 3 pm and 6 pm. The single busiest hour was 3 pm, with 23. Noon and 2 pm tied for second with 22 each.</p>
  <p>Wednesday had the most, 47, while Friday and Saturday had the fewest, 31 each. June had 29 and May 28, against 12 in December. About 24 percent, 63 of 258, happened in the dark. Two were on a dark stretch with no street lights, and one of those was fatal. Police recorded a wet road surface in 33.</p>
  <p class="source" data-file="profiles/route-37-nj-accidents.json (time, light_2023_2024, surface_2023_2024)">Source: NJDOT crash records via nj-crashes; our count of accidents where someone was hurt or killed, 2023 and 2024 only. <section id="bridges">
  <h2>The Mathis and Tunney bridges over Barnegat Bay</h2>
  <p>Route 37 crosses Barnegat Bay between Toms River and Seaside Heights on two bridges. The eastbound span is the Mathis Bridge and the westbound span is the Tunney Bridge. NJDOT's project page is historical background, not a record of current lane closures.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.nj.gov/transportation/commuter/roads/rte37mathisbridge/">NJDOT, Route 37 Mathis Bridge project page</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>In the police records, Seaside Heights had 4 Route 37 accidents in 2023 and 2024 and none hurt anyone. Our data does not mark which accidents happened on a bridge span, so we cannot count bridge accidents.</p>
  <p>If your car accident was near the bay, write down your direction of travel and where the first contact happened. Was it on the approach, on the span, or at a driveway or turn before it? Where the cars came to rest can be a different place. Being close to a bridge does not by itself mean the bridge or its condition caused anything.</p>
</section>

<section id="report">
  <h2>Which police department has your Route 37 report</h2>
  <p>The report comes from the police agency that responded. If that was the Toms River Police Department, its Records Division handles the request. Its published process:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>No report information is given out by phone.</li>
    <li>In person, bring proof of identification. Reports are reviewed and approved before release, so the department asks you to allow 3 business days before you come.</li>
    <li>By email, certain reports can be requested at PoliceRecords@trpolice.org with a copy of your driver's license.</li>
    <li>By mail, the department's page lists a $5.00 fee payable to "Toms River Township" and a self-addressed, stamped envelope, sent to Toms River Police Department, Records Division, PO Box 876, Toms River, NJ 08754.</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="source">Source for every item above, including the fee: <a href="https://trpolice.org/administration-bureau/records/police-reports-investigation-and-accident-reports/">Toms River Police Department, Police Reports / Investigation and Accident Reports</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). Fees for other municipal departments differ and are not stated here.</p>
  <p>For a Route 37 car accident in Manchester, Lakehurst, Berkeley, Island Heights or Seaside Heights, ask that town's police department. We did not verify their procedures. If the New Jersey State Police responded, use the <a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP crash report portal</a>. For questions, the portal lists NJSPRecords@NJSP.gov and (609) 882-2000 ext 2234. The NJTR-1 report is filled in by police. If no police investigated, drivers report on the SR-1 form themselves.</p>
  <p class="source">Sources: NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page and NJSP crash report portal (retrieved 2026-11-10); <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). Agency-by-agency detail: <a href="/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-report">how to get your New Jersey accident report</a>.</p>
  <p>When the report arrives, DOBI advises: "Review report for details, such as how many passengers were listed... If you see inconsistencies, let your insurance company know and contact the police department that responded to the scene."</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, p. 25 (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>

<section id="if-hurt">
  <h2>If you were hurt on Route 37</h2>
  <h3>Medical attention and symptoms that appear later</h3>
  <p>Get medical attention and describe every symptom, including ones that started hours or days later. After a blow to the head, the CDC lists seizures, confusion and being unable to wake up as danger signs that need emergency care.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/signs-symptoms/index.html">CDC, concussion signs and symptoms</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <h3>Medical bills go to your own PIP first</h3>
  <p>Your own auto policy's PIP handles your medical bills whatever caused the car accident, as the box below explains. If you chose the option that makes your health insurance primary, your health plan pays first and its own rules apply. See <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/pip-health-insurance-medical-bills-new-jersey">whether PIP or health insurance pays first</a> and <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/pip-coverage-limits-medical-bills-new-jersey">how PIP limits work</a>. For how this differs from a claim against the other driver, read <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/is-nj-a-no-fault-state">what New Jersey's no-fault system means</a>.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/pipoption.htm">NJ DOBI, PIP and health insurance option</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <h3>Fault in a rear-end or turning accident</h3>
  <p>Rear-end and right-angle collisions are the most common Route 37 accidents where someone was hurt, but the collision code does not settle fault. DOBI says the insurance company decides fault from all the facts, such as police reports and statements. Insurance companies must also offer an internal appeal for disputes about the fault percentage.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/everythingauto2026.pdf">NJ DOBI, Everything You Wanted to Know About Auto Insurance (2026)</a>, pp. 26 to 27 (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>If another driver turned across your lane, write down only what you saw: the movement, the signal if you saw it, and any witnesses or cameras nearby. Leave out speeds or signal colors you are guessing at. For how New Jersey appeals courts have handled fault and coverage disputes, see our <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/nj-car-accident-appeals-index">searchable index of 76 New Jersey appeals</a>. Statewide corner counts are in our <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/most-dangerous-intersections-nj">New Jersey intersection injury analysis</a>.</p>
  <h3>Work, repairs and a settlement offer</h3>
  <p>Keep a dated list of the days you could not work because you were hurt, and the medical notes behind them. The <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/nj-can-you-claim-lost-wages-after-a-car-accident-in-nj">lost wages guide</a> covers which records to keep. A check for car repairs and a settlement for your injury can cover different losses. Before signing a release, go through the <a href="https://injuriescases.autos/new-jersey/nj-8-questions-to-ask-before-accepting-a-settlement-offer">questions to ask before accepting a settlement offer</a>, and ask a lawyer about any term you do not understand.</p>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 37. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>
