<h1>Route 70 NJ accident today? Who has the police report, town by town, and what the crash records show</h1>

<section id="short-answer">
  <p class="lead">We do not post live crash alerts. If your car accident on Route 70 happened today, the report will come from the police agency that came to the scene. In most Route 70 towns, the crash records point to that town's own police department.</p>
  <p>The clearest exception in the records is Southampton Township in Burlington County, with Woodland Township next door close behind. In Southampton, 215 of 253 Route 70 crashes from 2023 to 2025 carry the name of the State Police Red Lion Station, and in Woodland 29 of 45 do. For a crash in either town, the State Police crash report portal is the place to look.</p>
  <p class="source">Red Lion Station (1722 US-206, Vincentown) is listed as a Troop C station on the <a href="https://njsp.njoag.gov/troop-c/">New Jersey State Police Troop C page</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10).</p>
  <p>The records also show where Route 70 hurts people. Cherry Hill alone had 265 of the road's 830 crashes in which someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024. Burlington County had about a quarter of those crashes but 8 of the 16 deaths.</p>
  <p class="source">Source: NJDOT crash records 2023 to 2025 as normalized by the open-source <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a> project (retrieved 2026-11-10), fatal crashes supplemented from <a href="https://www.nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/index.shtml">NJ State Police fatal crash statistics</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). Our analysis; 2025 is preliminary. How we read the station field is explained in the method section.</p>

  <h2>Fourteen towns on one milepost line, Pennsauken to Wall</h2>
  <p>Once three 2023 coding slips are corrected (explained below the table), the crash records place Route 70 crashes in 14 municipalities across Camden, Burlington, Ocean and Monmouth counties. Ordered by the NJDOT milepost the police recorded, they run from Pennsauken (about milepost 0) to Wall Township (about milepost 59). The records name the road both as "NJ 70" and as the "John Davison Rockefeller Memorial Highway".</p>
  <p>Three things stand out in the table. Cherry Hill has the most crashes by far. Medford and Manchester have the highest share of crashes in which someone was hurt among the larger towns, about one in three. Brick has many crashes but the lowest such share, about one in ten. The records do not explain these differences, and they may partly reflect how each department codes minor injuries.</p>

<section id="rear-end">
  <h2>Half of Route 70's injury crashes were rear-end collisions</h2>
  <p>Of the 830 Route 70 crashes in which someone was hurt or killed in 2023 and 2024, police coded 416 as rear-end collisions, 50 percent. Across all New Jersey roads the rear-end share was 31 percent. Right-angle crashes came second on Route 70 with 158, or 19 percent.</p>
  <p>In the hours of 3 pm to 6 pm, Route 70 had 203 crashes in which someone was hurt, the busiest three-hour window. Saturdays (91) and Sundays (80) had fewer than any weekday (126 to 136).</p>

<section id="get-the-report">
  <h2>Getting the report: town police, or the State Police portal</h2>
  <p>The police crash report in New Jersey is form NJTR-1, and only police complete it. If no officer investigated, drivers report the crash themselves with the SR-1 form. <span class="source">(<a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/policeres.shtm">NJDOT police resources page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span></p>
  <p>For Route 70, the first question is which agency came. Check the card or paperwork the officer gave you. The records give a good guess by town:</p>
  <h3>Southampton, and much of Woodland: State Police</h3>
  <p>In the records, 215 of 253 Southampton crashes and 29 of 45 Woodland crashes carry the Red Lion station name. Red Lion is a State Police Troop C station on US 206 in Vincentown. <span class="source">(<a href="https://njsp.njoag.gov/troop-c/">NJSP Troop C page</a>, retrieved 2026-11-10)</span> State Police and Turnpike Authority crash reports are requested on the state portal. The basic NJTR-1 can be downloaded once it is "ready and approved", and if it is not found yet the portal says to "search again in a couple days".</p>
  <p>The portal lists "Non-Toll Road Crash Reports" at $13.00. A Red Lion report does not vanish after a few months: the portal keeps each one for six years past its crash year. Investigation files for a serious or fatal crash are not public records and need a subpoena or court order. Questions go to njsprecords@njsp.gov or (609) 882-2000 ext. 2234.</p>
  <p class="source"><a href="https://www.njportal.com/njsp/crashreports/">NJSP and NJTA Crash Report Requests portal</a> (fetched 2026-11-10); <a href="https://njsp.njoag.gov/identification-amp-information-technology-section/criminal-justice-records-bureau/">NJSP Criminal Justice Records Bureau page</a> (retrieved 2026-11-10). The $13.00 fee is for State Police reports only; town departments set their own procedures.</p>
  <h3>Cherry Hill: the Police Records window</h3>
  <p>Cherry Hill Township says copies of police reports "are available to involved persons and can be picked up during regular business hours at the Police Records window". The Police Records Office is on the first floor of the Municipal Building and lists 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m, Monday through Friday, for filing incident reports.</p>
  <p class="source"><a href="https://www.chnj.gov/138/Police-Records">Cherry Hill Township, Police Records</a> (fetched 2026-11-10). For more on this stretch, see <a href="/new-jersey/route-70-cherry-hill-crash-guide">our Route 70 in Cherry Hill guide to treatment, bills and claims</a>.</p>
  <h3>Lakewood: email, mail, in person or online</h3>
  <p>The Lakewood Police Department takes report requests at records@lakewoodpolicenj.com or by mail to PO Box 540, Lakewood, NJ 08701, with a copy of a valid picture ID and a phone number. Reports can be picked up at 231 3rd Street from 8:15 am to 4:00 pm. It says accident reports are also available online via crashdocs.org or lexisnexis.com.</p>
  <p class="source"><a href="https://www.lakewoodpolicenj.com/records/">Lakewood Police Department, Central Records</a> (fetched 2026-11-10).</p>
  <h3>Toms River: Records Division</h3>
  <p>The Toms River Police Department recommends "allowing 3 business days" before trying to pick up a report in person. Requests can also go by email to PoliceRecords@trpolice.org with a copy of your driver's license, or by mail with a $5.00 fee and a self-addressed stamped envelope to PO Box 876, Toms River, NJ 08754.</p>
  <p class="source"><a href="https://trpolice.org/administration-bureau/records/police-reports-investigation-and-accident-reports/">Toms River Police Department, Police Reports / Investigation and Accident Reports</a> (fetched 2026-11-10).</p>
  <h3>Every other Route 70 town</h3>

<section class="place-common" id="what-these-numbers-mean">
  <h2>What these numbers can and cannot tell you</h2>
  <p>The counts on this page are police-reported car accidents from NJDOT crash records, analyzed by us. They show where and when accidents where someone was hurt happened on Route 70. They do not show who was at fault in any accident, and they say nothing about whether you have a claim or what it could be worth.</p>
  <h3>Rules that apply anywhere in New Jersey</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Your own auto policy's PIP pays your medical bills "no matter who caused the auto accident." A claim against the other driver for pain and suffering is separate and depends on fault and on the lawsuit option on your policy. <span class="source">(<a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/pdf/autoguide2026.pdf">NJ DOBI Auto Insurance Buyer's Guide 2026</a>, p. 2)</span></li>
    <li>Being partly at fault does not stop you from recovering, as long as your share is not greater than the other driver's (the "51 percent" rule). Your damages are reduced by your share. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:15-5.1; <a href="https://www.nj.gov/dobi/ins_ombudsman/ombuds_acnsfaq.html">NJ DOBI comparative negligence FAQ</a>)</span></li>
    <li>If someone is hurt, the driver must tell the police right away. If police did not investigate, New Jersey's SR-1 self-reporting form is the form for that. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 39:4-130; <a href="https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/refdata/accident/selfreporting.shtm">NJDOT self-reporting crash form</a>)</span></li>
    <li>An injury lawsuit generally must be filed within two years, and a claim against a public body (a town, the state, NJ Transit) needs a written notice within 90 days. These are general rules with exceptions; check your own dates with a lawyer. <span class="source">(N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2; N.J.S.A. 59:8-8)</span></li>
  </ul>

<section id="method">
  <h2>How the Route 70 counts were made</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Records.</strong> NJDOT crash records for 2023 (per-table archive) and 2024 and 2025 (NJDOT Crash Data Portal), as normalized by <a href="https://github.com/hudcostreets/nj-crashes">hudcostreets/nj-crashes</a>, with fatal crashes supplemented from <a href="https://www.nj.gov/njsp/info/fatalacc/index.shtml">NJ State Police fatal crash statistics</a>. File downloaded 2026-11-06; both sources retrieved 2026-11-10.</li>
    <li><strong>Which crashes count as Route 70.</strong> Route number 70 with a state road name ("NJ 70", "NJ 70 SECONDARY", and the same with "John Davison Rockefeller Memorial Highway"). County roads that share the number are excluded. That gives 5,312 crashes for 2023 to 2025.</li>
    <li><strong>Years.</strong> 2023 and 2024 are complete. 2025 is preliminary; Route 70's 2025 total is 1.10 times its 2024 total, so it is not visibly incomplete, but it can still change.</li>
    <li><strong>"Someone hurt or killed"</strong> means a police-reported crash with at least one person injured or killed. People hurt and killed are counted per person.</li>
    <li><strong>Cross streets</strong> use 2024 and 2025 only, because street naming changed between the 2023 and 2024 files. Name variants are not merged, so counts are a floor. Ties at the cutoff are all shown.</li>
    <li><strong>State Police station field.</strong> Statewide, this field holds only State Police station names, such as Totowa, Bellmawr, Red Lion and "Atlantic City Expressway", and it is filled mostly on interstate and Parkway crashes. We read a filled value as a State Police investigation. It is filled more often in later years (55 Route 70 crashes in 2023, 92 in 2024, 132 in 2025), so treat it as a pointer. The agency named on your own report is what counts.</li>
    <li><strong>Town codes.</strong> In the 2023 file only, 37 Route 70 records carry a Burlington municipality code one below the real town: Maple Shade (code 19) for Medford (20), Shamong (32) for Southampton (33) and Pemberton Borough (28) for Pemberton Township (29). Their cross streets and mileposts place them in the neighbouring town, and none of the three names appears on Route 70 in 2024 or 2025. We fold them into Medford, Southampton and Pemberton Township; county and stretch totals are unchanged.</li>
    <li><strong>Deer</strong> counts use the police-coded "first harmful event". <strong>Mileposts</strong> are medians of the mileposts officers recorded.</li>
    <li><strong>Counts, not rates.</strong> There is no traffic volume in these records, so a busier stretch will have more crashes. Nothing here shows who was at fault in any crash.</li>
    <li>We ran the counts ourselves from the NJDOT crash records file; the method notes on this page describe the selection.</li>
  </ul>
