<section id="two-years">
<h2>How long after a car accident can you sue in NJ?</h2>
<p>Generally two years from the day the claim accrued, under N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup></p>
<p>The checker counts those two years from your crash date, because that is when most claims accrue. The statute itself counts from accrual, and the start can move on the facts. Our sources do not settle when it moves, so the checker never shifts the date for you.</p>
<p>A crash on 29 February is the odd case: two years later there is no 29 February. The checker shows 28 February and flags that 1 March is the other reading.</p>
</section>
<section id="public-body">
<h2>What changes if a town, the state or NJ Transit was involved?</h2>
<p>A much shorter clock starts: written notice of the claim must reach the public body by the 90th day after the claim accrued.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Missed the 90 days?</strong> A court may still allow notice within one year, but only for extraordinary circumstances and where the public body is not substantially harmed by the delay.</li>
<li><strong>Waiting period:</strong> no lawsuit until six months after the public body receives the notice.</li>
<li><strong>Outer limit:</strong> never later than two years from accrual.</li>
</ul>
<p>The six months run from when the notice arrives, so the checker cannot date it. Which bodies count and what the notice says are on <a href="/new-jersey/nj-tort-claims-notice-car-accident-public-entity">the tort claim notice after a crash with a public body</a>.</p>
</section>
<section id="ten-days">
<h2>How long do you have to report an accident in NJ?</h2>
<p>Police first, by the quickest means, when someone is hurt or killed or one person's property damage is over $500; then a written MVC report within 10 days.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup></p>
<p>The written report is only due when no police officer files one. Knowingly skipping it can cost a $30 to $100 fine and a possible suspension. If no officer investigated at all, the record of the crash is the SR-1 self-report, and our <a href="/new-jersey/nj-crash-report-finder">crash report finder</a> shows who holds a police report by town or road.</p>
</section>
<section id="minors">
<h2>Does a child get more time to sue after a crash in NJ?</h2>
<p>Possibly, but our sources only confirm one rule: the public-body notice law lets a minor bring the claim within its limits after turning 18.<sup class="df-fn"><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup></p>
<p>General rules that may pause the two-year clock for children exist but are not verified in our sources. So the checker shows "special rules may apply" for a minor and gives no extra date.</p>
</section>
